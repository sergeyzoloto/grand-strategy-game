A single large workshop concentrating many specialized workers under one
roof or one coordinating authority, producing at a volume and consistency
no household or small independent workshop can match — the organizational
precursor to the factory (COM-170) that arrives before mechanized power
does.

## Timing

Jingdezhen's imperial porcelain kilns organize at manufactory scale from
around 1004; the Venice Arsenal's specialized shipbuilding production lines
develop from the 1104 founding of the Venetian state shipyard; comparable
European manufactories (the Gobelins tapestry works, various state
armories) proliferate through the 1600s-1700s, before mechanized factory
production (COM-170) begins displacing the model.

## Mechanism

Concentrating many specialized workers in one location under one
coordinating authority achieves consistency and volume no dispersed
household-production network can match, by breaking production into
sequential specialized stages each worker repeats rather than requiring
every worker to master a whole product end to end — this is a purely
organizational innovation, achievable with existing hand-tool technology and
requiring no mechanized power source, which is what distinguishes it from
the later factory model that adds mechanization on top of the same
underlying division-of-labor logic.

## Social consequence

Manufactory work concentrates a large wage-dependent labor force in one
location under direct supervision, a meaningfully different daily life and
labor relationship than household production or independent craft
workshops, where a worker owned their tools and set their own pace — the
manufactory worker's rhythm and output are set externally, a preview of the
factory discipline (COM-170, COM-200) that later mechanization intensifies
rather than invents.

## Regional specifics

Jingdezhen's specialized sequential-stage porcelain production predates
comparable European manufactory scale by centuries, organizing what may be
history's largest and most sustained specialized-labor production complex
before industrialization. The Venice Arsenal and Ottoman state
manufactories reach comparable organizational sophistication independently,
showing the underlying logic (concentrate and specialize labor for
consistency and volume) is generic to any sufficiently demanding, high-value
production need rather than tied to one region's institutions. South Asian
textile production's continued reliance on decentralized household
production despite massive export success demonstrates the manufactory
model was a choice, not a requirement, for achieving world-market-scale
output.

## Hook

Concentrating and specializing labor under one roof achieves consistency and
volume beyond dispersed household production using only existing hand-tool
technology — an organizational innovation entirely separable from
mechanization, which the factory (COM-170) later adds on top of the same
underlying logic rather than inventing it from scratch.
