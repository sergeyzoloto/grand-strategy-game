Mechanized power applied on top of the manufactory's existing division-of-
labor logic (COM-020) — breaking production into narrow, repetitive,
machine-paced tasks that require far less individual skill per worker than
craft production ever did, at the cost of the worker's control over their
own pace.

## Timing

Arkwright's water-powered cotton mill (1771) is an early paradigm case;
factory production scales rapidly across British textile manufacturing
through the early 1800s, with the model spreading internationally
(including Meiji Japan's deliberate adoption from 1872) through the
mid-1800s.

## Mechanism

Where a manufactory (COM-020) divides labor into specialized stages using
hand tools at a pace workers themselves still substantially control, a
factory adds a mechanized power source (water, then steam) that sets the
production pace externally — worker skill requirements drop further (tending
a machine requires less training than hand craftsmanship) while output per
worker rises sharply, converting labor cost from a function of skill into a
function of machine-tending endurance and attendance discipline.

## Social consequence

Factory labor imposes an externally-set, clock-governed work rhythm
entirely disconnected from the task-completion pacing that governed both
craft work and agricultural labor, requiring a genuinely new habitus (design
principle 2) — regular attendance, clock-time punctuality, sustained
attention to a machine's pace — that factory owners had to actively instill
in a workforce, often recently arrived from rural areas (feeding directly
into COM's migration-adjacent labor supply), rather than one workers
already possessed.

## Regional specifics

Meiji Japan's deliberate, state-sponsored wholesale adoption of the factory
model compresses into a few decades a transition that took Britain multiple
generations to reach organically, consistent with the broader pattern of
compressed institutional adoption already seen in this record's other Meiji
cases. Colonial India's slower factory development, shaped directly by
tariff and trade policy favoring British manufactured imports over Indian
industrial capacity, shows the factory model's spread depends on trade
policy access as much as on capital or technical knowledge being available
locally.

## Hook

Mechanized power imposed on top of divided labor (COM-020) converts work
pace from worker-controlled to externally machine-set, requiring a new
`time discipline` habitus factory owners must actively instill in a
workforce — and the model's international spread depends as much on trade
and tariff access (COM-018) as on capital or technical knowledge alone.
