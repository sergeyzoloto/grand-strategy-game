A temporary, legally-protected, periodically-recurring gathering lets
merchants from many otherwise-unconnected regions converge to set prices
and clear credit all at once, policed for the fair's duration by an
on-the-spot merchant court rather than by the host territory's ordinary
legal system.

## Timing

The Champagne fairs reach their classic organized form from the 1100s,
running on a fixed annual cycle across several host towns through the
1200s-1300s before losing ground to permanent city-based trade (COM-015)
and maritime alternatives; comparable fair institutions recur elsewhere
across this broad window, with the underlying mechanism's most ancient
documented instance (Arabian sacred-truce fairs) considerably predating
the European case.

## Mechanism

A fair concentrates in one place, for a fixed and advertised period,
enough merchants from enough different regions that genuine multilateral
price discovery becomes possible — no single merchant needs to seek out
every trading partner separately, since the fair itself guarantees
everyone worth trading with will be there at the same time. The fair's
own merchant court (the historical "law merchant," administering
dispute resolution literally on the spot — "piepowder," from the dusty
feet of itinerant traders) extends `contract enforcement` for the fair's
duration through a jurisdiction independent of and faster than the host
town's ordinary courts, which is what lets merchants from mutually
unfamiliar and mutually distrustful legal systems trade with each other
at all.

## Social consequence

A fair's host town gains substantial, if seasonal, prosperity and
prestige from hosting a recognized fair cycle, giving would-be host
rulers a real incentive to grant and protect a fair charter, while
merchants gain a periodic, reliable opportunity to clear accumulated
credit and inventory that doesn't depend on the year-round market depth
COM-015's permanent city confederation requires to function.

## Regional specifics

Arabian sacred-truce fairs solve the same multilateral-gathering problem
through a calendrical religious rather than secular royal guarantee,
predating the Champagne case by well over a millennium and showing the
periodic-fair mechanism isn't tied to any one legal tradition. Chinese
periodic rural marketing systems apply the identical staggered-schedule
logic at a much smaller, local scale — a fixed rotation of nearby
villages through market days lets one trader or town serve a whole rural
area without needing daily transaction volume anywhere. South Asian
maritime trade's reliance on continuously-operating resident port
communities rather than temporary fairs shows periodicity itself is a
specific institutional choice, not a necessary stage on the way to
sustained long-distance trade.

## Hook

A periodic fair converts scattered bilateral bargaining into scheduled
multilateral price discovery, with its own merchant-court jurisdiction
extending `contract enforcement` across parties whose home legal systems
share no other basis for mutual trust — a civic institution that exists
only while the fair is in session, distinct from both COM-015's permanent
confederation and COM-027's earlier, more general stranger-trust
mechanism this entry specializes into a scheduled, wholesale-scale form.
