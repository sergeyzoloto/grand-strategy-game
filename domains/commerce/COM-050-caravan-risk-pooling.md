This entry owns the caravan as its own commercial institution — how a
long-distance overland crossing gets financed, guarded, and provisioned
at all — distinct from COM-080's later, network-level analysis of tolls
and political fragmentation along an already-functioning route.

## Timing

Organized caravan crossings recur wherever a valuable trade good sits on
the far side of a desert, steppe, or other crossing too costly for an
individual merchant to survive alone, from at least -1000 in Arabia
through the trans-Saharan trade's medieval peak and into the 1850s, before
railways (COM-200) begin displacing the pack-animal caravan for the
routes a rail line can reach.

## Mechanism

No individual merchant can afford the guards, water and fodder logistics,
and pack animals a long desert or steppe crossing requires alone, and the
risk of losing everything to raiders, thirst, or simple bad luck is
catastrophic rather than merely costly — pooling many merchants' goods
and capital into one large, jointly-guarded caravan converts an
individually unbearable fixed cost and catastrophic risk into a shared,
survivable one. Below some minimum scale of pooled participation, the
crossing isn't viable at any price; above it, each additional merchant's
marginal cost to join drops sharply, which is why caravans cluster at a
few large, infrequent departures rather than continuous small ones.

## Social consequence

Caravan terminus cities grow directly out of the institution's own scale
requirements rather than out of any local agricultural or resource base
— Timbuktu's prominence is a function of being where caravans large
enough to cross the Sahara safely assemble and disperse, not of what the
surrounding land itself produces, the same logistics-determines-
prosperity pattern COM-080 documents for Silk Road oasis cities.

## Regional specifics

Trans-Saharan gold-salt caravans are this record's starkest case of the
mechanism: a crossing wide and dry enough that the pooling requirement is
absolute, not merely economical, with camel caravans numbering in the
hundreds or thousands the only viable unit of organization. Arabian
caravan trade layers a sacred-truce protection system on top of the same
underlying pooling logic, drawing directly on COM-010's neutral-ground
mechanism to secure passage through territory the caravan doesn't
control. The Gangetic plain's dense river network shows the caravan
institution is a response to a specific transport problem, not a stage
every long-distance-trading region eventually reaches — where boats
already solve the problem, the caravan's pooling mechanism simply isn't
needed.

## Hook

Caravan formation is a pooling gate rather than a smooth cost curve: below
a `caravan viability threshold` of participating merchants and capital,
a crossing isn't viable at any price, and above it marginal participants
join cheaply — converting catastrophic individual risk into a shared,
survivable cost, and explaining why long-distance overland trade clusters
into large infrequent caravans rather than continuous small shipments.
