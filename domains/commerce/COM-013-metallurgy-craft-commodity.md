The smelting chemistry itself is ENG's territory, and the bronze-to-iron
shift's military and social consequence is WAR-002's; this entry owns
metalworking as an organized craft trade and its output as a tradeable
commodity, through the much later commercial event that finally makes steel
cheap.

## Timing

Bronze Age metalworking guilds organize as specialized craft trades from
roughly -3300; South Asian wootz steel export flourishes from around -300;
steel remains a comparatively expensive, craft-scale product everywhere
until the Bessemer process (1856) converts it into a cheap bulk commodity
within a few decades.

## Mechanism

Metalworking requires enough specialized, non-transferable skill that it
organizes into guild-like craft structures wherever it's practiced at scale,
converting raw ore availability into a value-added tradeable commodity whose
price reflects skilled labor and fuel input as much as raw material cost —
the Bessemer process breaks this pattern for steel specifically by
mechanizing the labor-intensive decarburization step, collapsing steel's
price relative to wrought iron by an order of magnitude within a generation
and converting it from an expensive specialty good into cheap bulk
infrastructure material.

## Social consequence

Where metalworking organizes as hereditary or guild-restricted craft
knowledge, it confers durable social standing and often ritual or
quasi-magical status on smiths across many independent traditions — a
recurring pattern for a craft whose product (weapons, tools) is both
economically valuable and physically transformative in a way that impresses
onlookers regardless of the smith's actual social class origin.

## Regional specifics

South Asian wootz steel's export as a high-value finished good, feeding
directly into the Middle Eastern Damascus steel tradition, shows metallurgical
craft skill itself functioning as an exportable commodity independent of
raw ore trade. West African ironworking guilds' comparable social
organization and ritual status, developed with no contact with Old World
guild traditions, suggests craft-guild structure is a close-to-inevitable
social response to concentrated specialized skill rather than a culturally
specific institution. Europe's steel staying craft-scale and expensive until
the comparatively very late Bessemer breakthrough shows commodification
timing depends on a specific process innovation, not simply on how long a
region has practiced metallurgy.

## Hook

Metalworking commodifies as a guild-organized craft trade wherever it's
practiced at scale, and a single process innovation (Bessemer) can collapse
a specific metal's price by an order of magnitude almost overnight —
converting a centuries-old expensive specialty craft into cheap bulk
infrastructure material within a single generation.
