A merchant-capitalist supplies raw material and collects finished goods
from many dispersed rural households, financing and coordinating
production without concentrating workers under one roof the way COM-020's
manufactory does — a distinct organizational form sitting between
independent craft production and centralized manufactory labor, not a
weaker version of either.

## Timing

The putting-out system develops across English wool textile production
from around 1400, reaching its fullest extent before mechanized factory
production (COM-023) begins displacing it from the late 1700s; the
Bengali case runs on a broadly overlapping timeline through the 1800s,
ending far more abruptly under colonial-era machine-import competition
(COM-021) than the English case's own gradual internal displacement.

## Mechanism

A merchant who owns raw material (wool, raw cotton, raw silk) but not
production capacity advances that material, and often working capital,
to rural households who spin or weave it on their own equipment and
their own schedule, then collects the finished goods against the
advance — the merchant captures the value of coordinating a large,
dispersed production network and accessing wider markets than any single
household could reach alone, while the household keeps control over its
own work pace and rhythm in a way factory labor (COM-023) later removes
entirely.

## Social consequence

Putting-out lets rural households supplement agricultural income with
piece-work wages without requiring anyone to relocate to a production
center, embedding textile production into the existing rhythm of
agricultural households' seasonal labor calendar rather than requiring a
wholly separate wage-labor identity — a genuinely different social
position from both the independent guild craftsman (who owns his own
material and tools) and the later factory worker (who owns neither and
works someone else's schedule).

## Regional specifics

Bengali putting-out organizes textile production on the same underlying
logic as the English case, entirely independently, and is specifically
the organizational structure that gets dismantled by machine-made British
imports and colonial tariff policy (COM-021) — not a generic craft
tradition displaced incidentally, but this entry's own institution meeting
its own displacement event. Chinese household production's persistence
around household-owned material and direct market sale, rather than
merchant-advanced credit, shows putting-out is one specific organizational
choice among several viable ways to organize dispersed household textile
production, not the default one.

## Hook

Putting-out converts household labor into wage-adjacent piece-work
without requiring relocation or loss of pace-control — a distinct labor-
supply mechanic from both independent craft production and factory
concentration (COM-023), and the specific organizational form COM-021's
displacement mechanic actually operates on in the Bengali case rather
than craft production in general.
