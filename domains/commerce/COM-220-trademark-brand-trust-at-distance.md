Once mechanized factories (COM-170) make goods anonymous and mass-
produced, a consumer can no longer personally know or inspect who made
what they're buying — the marketplace trust problem COM-010 first solves
through neutral ground and market judges recurs at industrial scale and
needs a new solution: a legally protected, portable brand identity a
producer builds once and spends repeatedly.

## Timing

Britain's 1875 Trade Marks Registration Act (Bass Brewery's red triangle
becoming the first registered mark, 1876) establishes the paradigm legal
form; comparable trademark law and branded consumer-goods practice spread
across major industrializing economies through the following four
decades, including Meiji Japan's 1884 trademark law.

## Mechanism

A trademark converts a producer's reputation into a legally defensible,
transferable asset independent of any single transaction or personal
relationship — a buyer who has never met the producer and cannot inspect
the factory can still rely on the mark itself as a signal, since
counterfeiting or misrepresenting it carries legal consequence the
producer can enforce. This extends `market trust radius` (COM-010's
stat) to cover anonymous, mass-produced goods sold at a distance
entirely divorced from any face-to-face or neutral-ground guarantee,
substituting a durable legal asset for a one-time or personal one.

## Social consequence

Building and defending a trademark requires capital and legal access a
small or newly-established producer often lacks, meaning the mechanism's
benefit accrues disproportionately to already-capitalized producers —
converting brand trust into a competitive advantage that compounds over
time for whoever can afford to build it first, in a way pure product
quality alone doesn't guarantee.

## Regional specifics

Coca-Cola's brand-building shows the mechanism operating at full
consumer-goods scale, substituting a consistent name and mark for any
consumer's ability to personally verify the anonymous factory behind the
product. Meiji Japan's adoption of trademark law specifically to help
domestic manufacturers compete credibly in unfamiliar export markets
shows the mechanism solving a genuine market-access problem, not merely
protecting incumbents. Colonial South Asia's asymmetric practical access
to the same nominally available legal protection shows the mechanism's
formal availability and its actual usability aren't the same thing.

## Hook

Trademark law extends `market trust radius` to cover anonymous, mass-
produced goods sold to buyers who will never meet the producer — a
modern, industrial-scale answer to the same stranger-trust problem
COM-010 first solves through neutral ground, substituting a durable,
legally-defensible brand asset for a one-time transactional guarantee,
with real, unequal access to the mechanism itself as its own separate
variable.
