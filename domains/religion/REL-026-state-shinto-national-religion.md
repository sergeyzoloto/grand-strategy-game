A modern, self-aware instance of REL-013's confessional-state logic applied to a tradition deliberately reshaped for the purpose — the Meiji state converting a diverse, locally-variable set of shrine practices into a unified national ideology centered on imperial divinity.

## Timing

The Meiji government begins systematically reorganizing Shinto shrine practice into a state-administered national institution from 1868, formally separating it from Buddhism (with which it had been closely intertwined for centuries) and centering it on the emperor's own divine status; State Shinto persists as compulsory civic ideology through 1945, formally disestablished under Allied occupation.

## Mechanism

Where REL-013's early modern confessional states select among already-existing rival confessions within one broader tradition, Meiji Japan does something more constructive: taking a genuinely diverse, locally-variable set of shrine practices and traditions and administratively reorganizing them into a single, state-defined national ideology, framed not as one religion among others but as a supra-religious civic duty compatible with, and in later practice functionally required alongside, whatever else a subject believed.

## Social consequence

Because State Shinto is framed as civic obligation rather than confessional choice, it applies uniformly to subjects of every actual religious background — Buddhist, Christian, or otherwise — in a way REL-013's confessional states, which explicitly favor one confession over rival versions of the same tradition, don't attempt, converting religious-nationalist consolidation into something closer to REL-011's parish-registration legibility mechanic than to ordinary confessional persecution.

## Regional specifics

The deliberate administrative separation of Shinto from its centuries-long institutional entanglement with Buddhism, and its reconstruction around a specifically modern claim to imperial divine descent, shows a state can construct a "traditional" national religion nearly from scratch using genuinely old material — a close cousin of the same colonial-administrative-invention dynamic (creating rigid categories out of actually plural practice) documented elsewhere in this record's personal-status-law history.

## Hook

A modern, self-aware instance of REL-013's confessional-state logic: a state administratively reorganizes diverse existing practice into a single national ideology framed as civic duty rather than confessional choice, applying uniformly across subjects of every actual religious background rather than favoring one confession over its rivals.
