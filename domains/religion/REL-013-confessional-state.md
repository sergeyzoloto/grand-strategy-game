A state formally adopts one religious confession as its official identity,
using state power to promote, enforce, and sometimes violently impose
that specific confession over rival versions of the same broader religious
tradition, not merely over other religions entirely.

## Timing

The Safavid confessionalization of Iran begins with the dynasty's 1501
founding; the parallel European pattern of confessional-state formation
following the Reformation's confessional fragmentation is formalized at
the Peace of Augsburg (1555) and reaffirmed at Westphalia (1648).

## Mechanism

Once a broader religious tradition fragments into rival confessions
(Sunni-Shia, Catholic-Protestant), a state can use official adoption of
one specific confession as a legibility and loyalty-sorting device —
declaring one confession the state religion converts ambiguous internal
religious diversity into a clearer, more legible sorting of subjects into
loyal-confession and potentially-suspect-other-confession categories,
which is a powerful tool for internal consolidation but also a durable
source of internal friction wherever a significant minority holds the
non-favored confession.

## Social consequence

Confessional-state formation frequently triggers active persecution or at
minimum second-class status for adherents of the non-adopted confession
within the same broader tradition — a Sunni minority in Safavid Iran or a
Protestant minority in a Catholic-confessional state faces a qualitatively
different, often much harder, position than either an adherent of the
favored confession or an adherent of an entirely unrelated religion the
state has no comparable rivalry-driven interest in suppressing.

## Regional specifics

The Safavid case shows confessionalization functioning explicitly as
inter-state geopolitical differentiation — adopting Shia Islam
specifically distinguishes and helps unify the new Iranian state against
its immediate Sunni Ottoman rival, confessional identity doing real
diplomatic and military work beyond internal religious conviction alone.
The European cuius regio, eius religio settlement is a solution to a
specifically intra-Christian conflict-management problem, formalized as
sovereign territorial religious choice specifically to end a devastating
round of confessional warfare — a narrower, conflict-resolution-driven
origin than the Safavid case's more offensively-oriented state-
differentiation motivation.

## Hook

Confessionalizing a state converts ambiguous internal religious diversity
into a legible loyalty-sorting device and, often, a tool of inter-state
differentiation — concentrating `religious authority` behind one specific
confession within a broader tradition, at the durable cost of creating a
second-class or actively persecuted status for any significant minority
holding the non-favored confession.
