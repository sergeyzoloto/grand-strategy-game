Where forced migration (MIG-013) or colonial imposition brings two
religious systems into forced, unequal contact, the result is rarely
either tradition's clean replacement of the other — practitioners instead
build a working blend that lets core practice continue under a permitted
or safer outward form.

## Timing

Enslaved Africans transported to the Americas from 1526 onward (directly
continuing MIG-013's forced-migration mechanic) begin developing syncretic
traditions almost immediately upon arrival, with the resulting practices
(Vodou, Santeria, Candomble among many named traditions) continuing to
develop and diversify through the 1800s.

## Mechanism

Where practicing an African deity system openly is forcibly suppressed but
practicing Catholicism is required or strongly incentivized, associating
each African deity with a Catholic saint sharing recognizable attributes
lets practitioners continue substantive worship of the original deity
system while presenting an outwardly compliant Catholic form — the
syncretism is functionally a concealment and survival strategy under
coercion as much as it is organic religious blending, which is why it
develops so consistently across otherwise unconnected enslaved communities
facing the same structural pressure.

## Social consequence

Syncretic traditions become a durable marker of specifically diasporic
community identity, distinct from both the African traditions their
deity systems trace to and from orthodox Catholicism itself — a genuinely
new religious formation rather than an incomplete or corrupted version of
either source tradition, one that persists and continues developing long
after the specific coercive conditions that originally produced it.

## Regional specifics

Vodou, Santeria, and Candomble each develop independently across
different enslaved communities and colonial contexts, converging on a
broadly similar dual-layer structure (African deity, Catholic saint
outward form) without necessarily being in direct contact with each other
— convergent adaptation to a shared underlying coercive pressure rather
than one tradition simply copying another. Syncretism within Africa itself
runs in something closer to the opposite direction — selective
incorporation of Christian or Islamic elements into a still-recognizably-
African framework — since practitioners there weren't typically operating
under the same coercive suppression forcing concealment specifically.

## Hook

Under forced migration (MIG-013) and religious suppression, syncretism
functions as a concealment strategy that lets suppressed `religious
authority` and practice continue under a permitted outward form — a
mechanism that produces a genuinely new, durable religious formation
rather than a temporary or incomplete blend, and one that runs in a
structurally different direction wherever the same coercive pressure isn't
present.
