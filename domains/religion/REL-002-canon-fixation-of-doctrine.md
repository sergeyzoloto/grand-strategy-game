This entry owns canon-fixation as an institutional and political
process — who convened the deciding body, what got included or excluded,
and why — not the theological content of the resulting doctrine itself,
which is PHI's fuller territory.

## Timing

The Third Buddhist Council's canon-fixing work dates to around -250; the
Council of Nicaea (325) and subsequent councils progressively fix
Christian doctrinal canon; the Quran's standardized written codification
under Caliph Uthman dates to around 650; the Wycliffite Bible controversy
and comparable vernacular-canon disputes continue into the 1380s.

## Mechanism

Before a canon is fixed, a tradition's teachings typically circulate in
multiple, sometimes conflicting oral or written versions, which makes
doctrinal consistency across a large or growing community increasingly
hard to maintain — convening an authoritative body to formally decide
which texts and readings count as canonical converts scattered variant
tradition into a single citable reference, at the direct cost of
whoever's preferred version doesn't make the final cut being formally
declared non-canonical rather than merely a minority view.

## Social consequence

Canon-fixation is inherently a power-allocating act: whoever controls or
dominates the deciding council gets their preferred texts and
interpretations declared authoritative for the entire tradition going
forward, converting a theological disagreement into a permanent
institutional winner and loser — which is exactly why canon councils are
so often contested, political events rather than neutral scholarly
exercises.

## Regional specifics

The Third Buddhist Council's canon-fixation and missionary dispatch in one
sponsored act shows the two processes (REL-002's fixation and REL-014's
later missionary expansion) can be directly and deliberately linked rather
than occurring independently generations apart, as in the Christian case.
Chinese Buddhism's sustained choice never to fully close its canon, instead
continuously incorporating new material over centuries, demonstrates an
open canon can be a stable long-term institutional strategy in its own
right rather than an unstable intermediate state every tradition
eventually resolves into closure.

## Hook

Canon-fixation converts scattered variant tradition into one authoritative,
citable reference — a power-allocating institutional act riding on
whoever's `religious authority` dominates the deciding council, with an
open, never-closed canon (the Chinese Buddhist case) representing a
genuine stable alternative to eventual fixation rather than an
unfinished version of the same process.
