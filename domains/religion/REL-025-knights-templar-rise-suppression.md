A specific, vivid case combining REL-009's monastic-order mechanic with REL-001's temple-economy scale, before a state suppression that previews REL-019's own dissolution mechanic on a smaller scale and two centuries earlier.

## Timing

The Knights Templar are founded in 1119 to protect pilgrims to Jerusalem, growing over the following two centuries into a major international banking and landholding institution; King Philip IV of France, heavily indebted to the order, engineers its suppression through coordinated arrests and heresy charges from 1307, culminating in the order's formal dissolution by Pope Clement V in 1312.

## Mechanism

The Templars combine REL-009's monastic vow structure (poverty, obedience, communal property) with an international network of holdings sophisticated enough to function as a proto-banking system — pilgrims and crusaders could deposit funds in one Templar location and draw an equivalent sum elsewhere, a service resting on exactly the kind of trans-regional institutional trust REL-001 describes temple economies providing. This scale of accumulated wealth and cross-border reach is precisely what makes the order an attractive target once a heavily indebted monarch has sufficient political motive and legal pretext (heresy charges, largely fabricated under torture) to move against it.

## Social consequence

The suppression's speed and coordination — simultaneous dawn arrests across France on Friday, October 13, 1307 — shows a sufficiently motivated and organized state can move against even a well-established, internationally-connected religious-military order faster than that order's own dispersed structure can mount a coordinated defense, a preview of the same asymmetry REL-019's later, larger-scale dissolutions exploit.

## Regional specifics

Philip IV's debt to the order and his systematic use of heresy accusation as legal pretext for what was substantially a fiscally-motivated seizure previews the same conviction-versus-material-interest ambiguity REL-007 already tracks for heresy prosecution generally — a documented case where the material-interest component is unusually well-evidenced relative to the doctrinal one.

## Hook

A smaller-scale, two-centuries-earlier preview of REL-019's dissolution mechanic: a religious-military order's international financial network becomes exactly the target that makes it attractive for a debt-motivated monarch to suppress, using doctrinal accusation as legal cover for what is substantially a fiscal seizure.
