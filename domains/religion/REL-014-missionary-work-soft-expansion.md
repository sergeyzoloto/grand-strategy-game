A universalist religion (REL-003) extends its reach not through military
conquest but through deliberate, organized persuasion — a soft-power
expansion mechanism that can travel well beyond, and independent of, any
accompanying military or political power.

## Timing

Ashoka's dispatched Buddhist missions (REL-002) date to around -250;
Islamic expansion along merchant networks runs from around 700 onward;
Jesuit missions to Asia and the Americas operate intensively from the
1500s through the Rites Controversy's resolution against them by the 1650s
in China specifically.

## Mechanism

Missionary expansion converts religious universalism's abstract growth
potential (REL-003) into an organized, resourced institutional program —
trained missionaries, translated texts, and often deliberate cultural
accommodation strategies aimed at making the new religion legible and
acceptable to a specific target culture — succeeding or failing based on
how well that accommodation is calibrated, not merely on the sending
institution's resources or the sending religion's own internal conviction.

## Social consequence

Successful missionary conversion at scale reshapes a society's religious
landscape without necessarily requiring military conquest or even formal
political influence, giving the sending religious institution durable
cultural and diplomatic reach into regions no accompanying army or
state ever directly controlled — a genuinely different mechanism of
influence-projection than territorial conquest, operating on cultural
and institutional persuasion instead.

## Regional specifics

Islamic expansion along existing merchant trade routes shows missionary-
style religious spread can happen organically through commercial
relationship and gradual conversion rather than requiring an organized,
centrally-directed mission program at all — the same underlying diaspora-
trade-network mechanic (MIG-009) doing double duty as a religious-
expansion channel. Jesuit missions to China illustrate the opposite
extreme: a highly organized, deliberately calibrated elite-accommodation
strategy achieving real but ultimately limited and politically precarious
reach, constrained by an internal doctrinal dispute (the Rites
Controversy) over how much of the target culture's own practice a convert
could retain — showing missionary success depends as much on the sending
institution's own internal doctrinal flexibility as on missionary skill in
the field.

## Hook

Missionary expansion converts a universalist claim (REL-003) into an
organized soft-power reach mechanism independent of military conquest —
succeeding or failing based on cultural accommodation calibration and the
sending institution's own doctrinal flexibility about that accommodation,
and capable of riding entirely on existing trade networks (MIG-009) rather
than requiring a dedicated mission program at all.
