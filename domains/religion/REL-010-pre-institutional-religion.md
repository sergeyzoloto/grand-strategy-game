Before any temple accumulates reserves or any priesthood claims institutional trust, religious practice already functions as a real, socially organizing force — the pre-institutional starting condition every later entry in this domain silently assumes is already available to be organized.

## Timing

Ritual practice with no plausible non-religious explanation — deliberate burial with grave goods, monumental construction with no evident practical function — is attested from at least -20000; Göbekli Tepe's carved enclosures date to roughly -9600; Neolithic Chinese ancestor-directed burial practice is attested from roughly -7000, both well before REL-020's earliest temple-economy cases at -3000.

## Mechanism

A shamanic or ancestor-cult ritual specialist derives authority from personal reputation — demonstrated ability to mediate with the unseen, successfully interpret omens, or maintain continuity with venerated ancestors — rather than from any institutional office, land grant, or accumulated treasury. This personal-reputation basis for authority is what REL-020's later temple economy converts into something durable and transferable: an institution that outlives any single practitioner's own reputation, but one that has to have something to institutionalize in the first place.

## Social consequence

Because pre-institutional religious authority rests on personal demonstrated capacity rather than office, it is inherently harder to inherit, transfer, or scale beyond a single community's direct relationship with a specific practitioner — a structural limitation later institutionalized religion (canon, hierarchy, endowment) specifically solves, but one that doesn't prevent pre-institutional ritual practice from organizing real, large-scale collective action, as Göbekli Tepe's monumental construction — requiring coordinated labor from multiple hunter-gatherer bands with no evident state or surplus-driven compulsion behind it — demonstrates directly.

## Regional specifics

Göbekli Tepe's construction preceding any local evidence of settled agriculture inverts the usual assumption that monumental ritual architecture follows from agricultural surplus — here, organized ritual practice and the communal labor it could mobilize plausibly precede and help motivate the shift to settled farming instead. Neolithic Chinese ancestor-directed burial practice establishes a ritual thread running continuously from deep prehistory into the well-documented Shang and Zhou ancestral state religion, showing a specific case where later institutionalized practice really can be traced back toward, if not conclusively to, pre-institutional roots.

## Contested

How much continuity exists between specific Paleolithic and Neolithic ritual practices and the much later institutionalized religions this domain otherwise covers is genuinely disputed, given how thin the evidentiary bridge is and how much interpretation of ambiguous remains as religious rather than practical rests on inference. Model pre-institutional ritual practice as real and socially organizing in its own right, while treating any specific claimed continuity to a later named tradition as a separate, weaker, source-dependent claim rather than an established lineage.

## Hook

An origin gate: establishes sacred authority and a recognizable ritual-specialist role as a pre-institutional social fact, resting on personal reputation rather than office — the starting condition REL-020's temple economy converts into something durable, transferable, and institutional for the first time.
