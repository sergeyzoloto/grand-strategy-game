Distinct from REL-015's extraction of a household's income, a religious tradition's calendar extracts a standardized share of a household's time — mandated rest days, fasting periods, and festival cycles that structure economic and social rhythm independent of any state labor policy.

## Timing

Sabbath observance is attested in Judaic practice from at least -600; the Christian liturgical calendar (Sunday rest, Lenten fasting, a cycle of feast days) formalizes progressively through late antiquity and the medieval period; the Islamic calendar and its Ramadan fasting obligation are established from the religion's founding in 610, continuing to structure practice through 1600 and well beyond.

## Mechanism

A mandated day of rest or a fasting period removes a fixed portion of the calendar from ordinary productive labor or ordinary consumption regardless of what a given week's economic conditions would otherwise call for — this is a real, recurring cost imposed uniformly across an entire observant population, converting the calendar itself into a shared, non-negotiable resource-allocation constraint no individual household can simply opt out of without visible social consequence.

## Social consequence

Because the entire observant community stops or slows productive activity simultaneously, the sacred calendar becomes a powerful, low-cost coordination device — shared festival timing, market closures, and travel patterns synchronize across an entire population without requiring any state administrative order to produce that synchronization, a form of coordinated social rhythm that predates and operates independently of deliberately-imposed factory time discipline.

## Regional specifics

The Islamic lunar calendar's deliberate decoupling from the solar agricultural year means Ramadan cycles gradually through every season over a 33-year period, a distinctive design choice that recurringly changes the fast's practical difficulty — a summer Ramadan's daylight fasting hours are far longer and more physically demanding than a winter one — in a way no fixed-date solar or lunisolar tradition's festivals experience. Chinese lunisolar calendar administration by the imperial state itself, rather than by an independent religious institution, shows sacred-time-setting can be a function of state cosmological legitimacy rather than of religious institutional authority specifically.

## Hook

A modifier: converts a fixed portion of the calendar into a non-negotiable, uniformly-observed constraint on productive activity, structuring economic and social rhythm as a low-cost coordination device independent of any state labor policy — distinct from and prior to deliberately-imposed factory time discipline.
