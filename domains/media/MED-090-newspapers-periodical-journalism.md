A book is read whenever. A newspaper arrives on a schedule — and that
single difference, periodicity, is what makes a periodical a different
medium from the press that prints it rather than an application of it.

## Timing

From the Strasbourg *Relation* of 1605, through eighteenth-century
licensing regimes, the penny press from the 1830s, the wire services from
the 1850s, and the vernacular and treaty-port presses of the colonial
world, to 1900.

## Mechanism

**Periodicity synchronises attention.** MED-080's press makes text cheap to
reproduce; that is a production fact and says nothing about when anyone
reads. A periodical fixes the *when*. Everyone who takes the morning paper
reads roughly the same material in roughly the same few hours, which means
each reader knows — without being told, and without meeting any of them —
that thousands of strangers are doing the same thing at the same moment.
`simultaneity of attention` is the variable this entry introduces and it is
distinct from reach: a book with a million readers over fifty years and a
paper with a million readers on one morning are not the same object.

That is the nation-formation mechanism in Anderson's account. A population
that cannot possibly meet nonetheless experiences itself as a body, because
it is repeatedly and visibly doing one thing together. The entry should
carry it as a contributing mechanism rather than the sole route, since
compulsory schooling and conscription (WAR-200) run alongside it and are
hard to separate from it.

**The funding model determines the institution**, exactly as MED-130 finds
for broadcast — and the finding arrives here first, so this entry
establishes it and the radio entry inherits it. Subscription produces a
paper answerable to propertied readers; advertising produces one answerable
to circulation, where the reader becomes the product sold rather than the
customer served; party subsidy produces an organ; state ownership produces
a gazette. Identical technology, four institutions, and the difference is
entirely in who pays.

**Licensing is the control instrument, not censorship of content.** A state
that cannot un-invent the press controls the periodical by controlling
permission to publish — which is cheaper than reading everything, harder to
characterise as suppression, and operates in advance rather than after the
fact. It is the same mechanism LAW-225 later describes for spectrum, and it
recurs wherever a medium requires a standing permission to operate.

**The wire services standardise both content and voice.** After the
telegraph (MED-100), an agency can sell one report to papers of every
political complexion, which is only commercially possible if the report is
written so as to offend none of them. The flat, attributed, claim-light
style called objectivity has a plausible origin in that constraint — a
commercial requirement that became a professional virtue. The same cable
that destroyed the merchant's information premium created the news agency.

## Social consequence

A new occupational class appears whose authority comes from being first and
from being believed, and which answers to a proprietor rather than to a
guild, a church, or a state. The editor becomes a figure of real political
weight without holding office — the intermediary layer that MED-130 later
describes broadcast as bypassing is constituted here, which is why this
entry has to exist for that one to make sense.

Reading also changes shape. A periodical trains a habit of consuming
current and disposable text at a fixed time, which is a different practice
from reading a book — and it makes the daily paper a component of household
routine rather than an occasion. For readers, the effect is that events
elsewhere become continuously present rather than episodically reported.

And a literate public becomes politically legible to itself. Before
periodicals, a population's opinion existed but had no visible aggregate
form; after them, it has a daily one, which is why press regulation becomes
a standing political question rather than an occasional one.

## Regional specifics

The Vernacular Press Act of 1878 is the entry's sharpest case and belongs
at the centre rather than in a colonial appendix. By imposing prior
restraint on Indian-language papers while exempting English-language ones,
it stated the mechanism in its own text — the threat was not publication
but publication the administration could not read. Nothing in the European
record puts the point so plainly.

The Arabic press of the *nahda* shows a readership constituted as a
language community rather than as a state's population, circulating across
provinces and into diaspora. The Chinese treaty-port press shows
independence arising from extraterritoriality rather than from any
principle, which is a very different foundation from the American or
British case and produces a press with different vulnerabilities. The penny
press supplies the advertising model. Europe supplies licensing.

Both absences are the same conjunction failing — a periodical needs a dense
enough readership, reliable enough distribution, and enough revenue inside
that circle to fund the next issue. Where delivery cannot be kept to a
schedule the medium collapses back into the book, because a paper that
arrives whenever has lost the only thing that made it a newspaper.

## Contested

Anderson's argument is contested on three grounds — that it understates
pre-print collective identity, that its causal weight cannot be separated
from the schooling and conscription running alongside it, and that it fits
European and creole-American cases better than others. Model simultaneity
as one contributing mechanism among three and require more than one to be
present before a national population forms, which keeps this entry
consistent with the schooling and conscription entries instead of competing with them for the
same effect.

Separately, whether objectivity emerged from the wire services' commercial
need or from a later professionalising project with its own interests is
unresolved. The commercial account is tidier and should not be adopted for
that reason; model the norm as over-determined.

## Hook

Introduces `simultaneity of attention` — the share of a population
consuming the same content inside the same short window, which is a
different variable from reach and is what converts an audience into a
public. Periodicity produces it, and the entry should be gated on a
distribution system reliable enough to keep a schedule rather than on
literacy alone, so a dispersed or slowly-served population gets a book
market and no press. The funding model should determine the institution
outright — subscription, advertising, party subsidy and state ownership
producing four different papers from identical technology, with advertising
converting the reader from the customer into the product. Control should
run through licensing rather than content censorship, operating in advance
and cheaply. And it constitutes the editor as an intermediary class, which
is the layer MED-130 later bypasses — so `intermediary dependence` rises
here before it falls there.
