Fixing a canon freezes the language it is written in. Because the canon is
authoritative, that language becomes the medium of all higher learning —
and because it cannot change, it stops evolving while everyone's speech
moves on. The gap that opens is the second gate on literacy, and it is
larger than the first.

## Timing

From the fixing of the Pali canon around -250 and the Confucian classics
around -100, through the Latin, Arabic and Geez cases, to the displacement
of canon languages by vernacular learning from roughly 1800.

## Mechanism

REL-070 owns canon-fixation as a doctrinal and political act — who
convened, what was excluded, who lost. **This entry owns what happens to
the language afterwards**, which is a separate mechanism with separate
consequences and operates identically whether or not the canon is
religious.

**The language stops moving and speech does not.** A fixed text cannot be
updated, so its grammar and vocabulary are held at the moment of fixation
while vernaculars drift as they always do. The gap widens by itself, with
nobody deciding it should, until after some centuries the language of
learning is one nobody speaks natively and everyone must acquire
deliberately. **Diglossia is not imposed; it accumulates.**

**That is a second gate on literacy, and it is the expensive one.** MED-020
lowers the cost of knowing letters. This raises the cost of reading
anything that matters, because knowing letters is no longer enough — a
reader must acquire a second language first, and the texts that would teach
it are in it. `literacy training cost` rises at exactly the point where the
alphabet had cut it, which is why cheap script and a reading public are so
often separated by a thousand years. `intermediary dependence` rises with
it: a population that can read its own vernacular still cannot read the
law, the scripture, the medicine or the philosophy, and continues to reach
all of them through someone who can.

**The compensation is a scholarly community larger than any polity.** A
shared written language means a scholar's peers are wherever the language
is read rather than wherever the ruler governs — so correspondence,
citation, travel and dispute run across borders that armies do not cross,
and `intellectual authority` accrues to a transnational body of readers
rather than to a court. **Latin, Arabic, Sanskrit and Literary Chinese are
four co-equal instances of one mechanism.** None is the template and the
others variants: the Sanskrit world reached from Kashmir to Java and the
Arabic one across three continents, both larger than Latin Christendom,
and the East Asian case ran on a canon nobody treated as revelation.

**The gate can be attacked, and only one way.** Because the barrier is a
language rather than an institution, translating the canon dissolves it,
which is the whole of the vernacular-translation mechanism downstream and
the reason that entry takes this one as its prereq.

## Social consequence

The scholar becomes a distinct social type, defined by a possession that is
purely acquired and cannot be inherited, confiscated or conferred. That
makes the class unusually permeable in principle — anyone who does the
years of study is in — and unusually closed in practice, since the years
must be paid for by someone. Entry is means-tested by default rather than
by rule, which is a harder barrier to attack than a formal one because
there is no rule to abolish.

The distance between learned and spoken language also changes what
authority sounds like. Law, liturgy and medicine are conducted in a
register the subject cannot follow, so the practitioner's competence is
unverifiable by the person relying on it — a structural condition, not a
moral failing, and one that recurs wherever this arrangement holds. It is
also why vernacular translation is experienced as an attack rather than a
convenience by the people whose standing depends on the gap.

And because the canon language is shared across polities, an educated
person's strongest professional ties frequently run outside their own
state. That makes the scholarly class chronically suspect to rulers and
chronically useful to them, since the same network that makes a scholar
hard to control is what makes them worth employing.

## Regional specifics

The four principal instances are co-equal and the entry is built so that no
one of them frames the others. Sanskrit was fixed by grammarians rather
than a council, which shows the mechanism does not require an institution
to convene. Arabic was fixed by codification and held by a doctrine about
the text's own language, which is what later blocks translation entirely.
Latin outlived both its native speakers and its church, ending as the
medium of a secular scholarly network — the clearest evidence the mechanism
belongs to the language rather than the doctrine. Literary Chinese ran on a
non-revelatory canon and was tied to office by examination (LAW-100), which
is the strongest single case that the effect is not religious at all.

Geez shows a small polity sustaining the whole arrangement alone, and
Southeast Asia shows canon languages travelling to courts that had no hand
in producing them. The Americas absence is the one worth keeping: knowledge
there was held in the language people spoke, so the access gate was
institutional membership rather than grammar — which cannot be dissolved by
translation, and equally cannot be climbed by private study.

## Hook

A second and larger gate on literacy, arriving after MED-020 has lowered
the first. `literacy training cost` rises again because knowing letters
stops being sufficient — a reader must acquire a second language whole
before anything authoritative becomes accessible — and `intermediary
dependence` rises with it, since a population literate in its own
vernacular still reaches law, scripture and medicine through someone else.
The gap should widen on its own over centuries rather than being imposed,
since the canon cannot change and speech always does. In compensation it
should create a scholarly network larger than the polity, accruing
`intellectual authority` to a transnational body of readers and giving
educated people their strongest ties outside their own state — useful to a
ruler and impossible to control. The barrier is made of language rather
than membership, which means exactly one thing dissolves it — translating the canon out of
it, which the next entry in this domain does.
