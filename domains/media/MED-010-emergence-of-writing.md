Writing originates as accounting, not as expression. The earliest large
corpus anywhere is a set of ration lists, and everything this domain
contains descends from the moment a quantity became checkable by someone
who was not there when it was counted.

## Timing

Proto-cuneiform at Uruk from around -3300; Egyptian hieroglyphs within a
century or two; Shang oracle bones from around -1200; Zapotec and then Maya
writing in Mesoamerica from around -500, with no Old World contact; Andean
khipu in use well before the Inca state and scaled by it. The window closes
around 900, by which point every major script family in use for the next
thousand years is established.

## Mechanism

**A record makes a quantity checkable by someone who did not witness it.**
That is the whole of the mechanism and everything else follows. Before
writing, verifying a claim means finding someone who was present and
trusting them — so the reach of any authority is bounded by the number of
people it can personally know and the reliability of what they remember. A
sealed tablet has neither limit. It can be produced years later, in front
of an official with no connection to the transaction, and it says the same
thing it said when it was made.

This detaches verification from both presence and memory, and it is the
precondition for `legibility` as FIN-020 defines it — a state's ability to
see and measure what it governs. A census is a written artefact before it
is an administrative one. So is a tax assessment, a land register, and a
law (LAW-020).

**The medium is inseparable from the access gate.** Early scripts carry
several hundred to several thousand signs, which puts `literacy training
cost` high enough that reading is a profession rather than a skill — years
of schooling, an apprenticeship, and a career. That produces a scribal
class holding a monopoly on records, positioned between the ruler who
needs the count and the population being counted, and answerable to
neither in the way either would prefer. Every later entry in this domain is
in some sense about that gate rising or falling.

**The accounting origin is strong but not universal, and the entry should
say so.** Mesopotamia supports it plainly. The Shang and Maya cases do not —
both record legitimacy rather than inventory, divination and dynasty
rather than grain. The mechanism survives the correction: a divination
record makes a past king's question checkable by a later one, and a
dynastic inscription makes a succession claim checkable by someone who was
not alive for it. What is being made countable differs; that it is made
countable by someone absent does not.

## Social consequence

The scribe is the first occupational class whose power comes from
controlling a medium rather than from land, force, or lineage, and the
position is structurally odd — high skill, real leverage, and low formal
status, dependent on a patron who cannot check the work. PHI-010's wisdom
literature is scribal-school material and shows the class instructing
itself in how to occupy that position.

Writing also changes what an obligation is. An oral debt exists in the
memory of the parties and their witnesses and dies with them; a written one
outlives everybody involved and can be enforced by people who never met
either party. Obligations become durable, transferable, and inheritable,
which is the substrate every later instrument in FIN rests on. It also
makes them harder to renegotiate — a remembered debt can be quietly
adjusted by mutual understanding, and a recorded one has to be formally
cancelled.

And it changes who can lie about the past. A written record can be forged,
destroyed, or selectively kept, but it cannot be gradually misremembered
into a more convenient shape, which is what oral tradition does by default.
Writing does not make the record honest. It makes dishonesty require a
deliberate act.

## Regional specifics

Four independent inventions, and **no European instance among them** — the
alphabet reaches Europe later, from the Levant, in MED-020. Mesopotamia and
Egypt are close enough in time that the relationship between them is a real
question rather than a settled sequence. The Shang and Maya cases are the
analytically valuable ones precisely because they disagree with the
Mesopotamian pattern and agree with each other, both recording authority
rather than inventory, on opposite sides of the world with no contact.

The Andean case is where the entry's own definition gets tested. Khipu
administered an empire on knotted cord, with a specialist class, a durable
record, and delegated verification — every functional element the
Mesopotamian case has. Treating that as an absence would require defining
writing as language-encoding, which would make this a different entry about
a different question. FIN-020 already states the principle from the
administrative side: state legibility does not require a script, only a
sufficiently structured record.

The absences isolate what the technology actually needs. The steppe has no
fixed referent to count. Oceania has hierarchy and no durable record, and
therefore a hard ceiling on auditable scale.

## Contested

Egyptian independence versus stimulus diffusion is unresolved; model it as
a branch. The token-to-cuneiform lineage is disputed and AGR-050 already
carries that dispute, so this entry defers rather than restating it.

The khipu question is the one that shapes the entry. Under a glottographic
definition — a system must encode language — khipu are excluded, and
whether any encode connected narrative is still open. Under the functional
definition this entry's mechanism actually uses — a durable external record
permitting verification by someone absent — they qualify without
difficulty. This entry adopts the functional definition and treats khipu as
a region_variant, because the glottographic one would make this an entry
about linguistics rather than administration. Model the two definitions as
alternative gates: under the strict gate a khipu-using polity lacks writing
and still achieves imperial `legibility`, which is itself the interesting
result.

## Hook

The origin of `legibility` as a capability rather than an assumption.
A durable external record detaches verification from presence and memory,
so an authority's reach stops being bounded by how many people it can
personally know — which is the gate every administrative mechanic in FIN
and LAW sits behind. It should be available from a storable surplus
(AGR-050) and should not require the surplus to be grain, since the Shang
and Maya cases record authority instead. It arrives with a high
`literacy training cost` built in, because early scripts carry hundreds or
thousands of signs, so the capability comes bundled with a scribal class
that holds it as a monopoly and stands between ruler and ruled. And it
should be reachable without a language-encoding script at all — a
sufficiently structured record does the administrative work, which is what
the Andean case demonstrates and what makes the strict definition a
modelling choice rather than a fact.
