A ruler grants a town a legal charter establishing rights of self-taxation,
self-jurisdiction, and self-defense — trading direct royal control over
daily urban administration for reliable revenue and, often, military
support from a now-self-interested town government.

## Timing

Chartered communal self-government scales across European towns from
around 1080, with the pattern of grant-and-negotiation continuing through
the following two centuries as urban centers grow wealthy and
administratively capable enough to make self-government both desirable to
seek and practical to grant.

## Mechanism

A charter converts a town from directly administered royal or noble
territory into a semi-autonomous legal entity with its own courts, tax
authority, and often militia, in exchange for fixed payments or specific
service obligations to the granting lord — this trades the lord's direct
day-to-day control for administrative capacity the lord doesn't have to
build or fund himself, letting urban self-interest (a chartered town
generally wants its own commerce, courts, and defense to function well)
substitute for direct oversight.

## Social consequence

Chartered self-government creates a genuinely distinct legal status for
urban residents relative to the surrounding countryside's feudal
obligation (LAW-080) — a serf who reaches a chartered city and resides
there for a specified period gains free status in several such systems, a
concrete legal escape route from rural feudal bondage that draws migration
toward chartered towns independent of any specific economic opportunity
there.

## Regional specifics

Islamic waqf endowments achieve a functionally comparable durable urban
institutional autonomy through an entirely different legal mechanism —
religious-charitable trust structure rather than a royal grant of
self-governing political rights — funding and protecting urban
institutions in perpetuity independent of any single ruler's ongoing
favor. European chartered rights themselves varied enormously in practice,
from extensive self-governing communes to much more limited concessions,
showing "chartered" describes a wide negotiated range rather than one
standard bundle of rights granted uniformly.

## Hook

A city charter trades direct royal administrative control for reliable
revenue and self-interested urban capacity, creating a distinct legal
status (and, via serf-emancipation clauses, an escape route from rural
feudal obligation, LAW-080) that draws migration toward chartered towns —
extending `contract enforcement` and self-governance into exactly the
commercial centers (see COM-110) that most need it to function.
