The state takes custody of children for a fixed portion of every day, for
years. Structurally this is the same instrument as conscription — a legal
obligation reaching every household, requiring a register, adjudicating
exemptions, and enforced against evasion — and the two were built by the
same administrations out of the same apparatus.

## Timing

From Prussia's 1763 school regulation, with Sweden's household examination
system from 1686 as the non-school precursor, through Massachusetts in
1852, Meiji Japan's 1872 system, and the Soviet *likbez* campaign from
1919, to roughly 1950.

## Mechanism

**It is conscription's twin and should be modelled from the same
components.** WAR-200 establishes that conscription is gated on
`legibility` — a birth register, a name that stays attached to a person, a
residence record, and a bureaucracy that adjudicates exemptions and pursues
evasion. Compulsory schooling needs all four, plus one more: it must know
where a child is on a given *morning*, not merely which district they
live in. It is therefore the more demanding of the two instruments, and it
is no accident that states which built one generally built the other, often
within a decade and using the same registers.

**What the state actually takes is time, not children.** This matters for
the mechanic, because it is what makes the instrument expensive to the
household rather than merely inconvenient. A child at school is a child not
working, and in a poor agrarian or artisanal household that is a real
income loss, concentrated on exactly the families least able to absorb it.
**The removal of children from the household labour force is the main
historical source of resistance**, and it explains why truancy enforcement
is the costly half of the system while the legislation itself is cheap.

**The outputs arrive bundled.** Schooling produces `literacy`, but it also
produces `time discipline` — the internalised submission to a timetable
that COM-170 identifies in the factory, here installed in childhood — and a
standardised national language, a common account of the past, and the
experience of other regions' children as countrymen. A state cannot buy one
of these without the others. That bundling is what makes the instrument a
nation-building device rather than an educational one, and it is why the
same measure appears in the programmes of states with no other political
similarity.

**`literacy training cost` shifts rather than falls.** The expense of
producing a reader does not change; who pays it does. Under every earlier
arrangement in the record the cost fell on the learner or their family, and
literacy distributed itself accordingly. Socialising the cost onto the state
is what finally converts literacy from a distribution shaped by wealth into
something approaching a universal, which no reduction in script complexity
(MED-020) ever managed on its own.

## Social consequence

Childhood is redefined. A phase of life previously distinguished by
graduated entry into work becomes a distinct legal status with its own
institution, its own authority figures, and an age-graded cohort structure
that has no precedent — children spend their days with fifty others of
exactly their own age, which is an arrangement almost no society before
this produced. Age-peer identity is an artefact of the school timetable.

The household loses authority in a specific and permanent way. The state
asserts a direct relationship with the child that the parent cannot
override, which is the same assertion conscription makes about an adult son
and is resisted for the same reason. Where the state and the household
disagree about what a child should learn — language, religion, history —
the school is where that disagreement is settled, by the state.

That power is also available for other purposes, and the entry should carry
the worst case rather than only the civic one. The custody the mechanism
requires is exactly what makes it usable for assimilation, and the
residential schools imposed on Native American and comparable indigenous
populations used it to sever language and kinship rather than to add
literacy. The same instrument, in the same decades, with the custody
provision doing the work.

## Regional specifics

Two cases complicate the standard story and both should be load-bearing.
**Sweden's household examination from 1686** produced near-universal
reading with no state schools at all — the parish tested household members
and marriage required passing, which is a legal lever applied through an
existing religious institution (REL-210) rather than a new state one. It
falsifies any model in which schooling is the only route to mass literacy.
**The Chinese examination system** (LAW-100, from 605) generated sustained
elite literacy demand for a millennium with no compulsion whatever, by
making literacy the sole path to office — demand-side rather than
supply-side, and arguably the strongest pre-modern literacy-driving
institution anywhere.

Meiji Japan is the clearest statement of the twin-instrument reading, with
education legislated in 1872 and conscription in 1873 as one programme.
The colonial cases run it in reverse: South Asian policy funded the
schooling that produced clerks and declined the schooling that produced a
population, and most African administrations left primary education to
missions entirely — because a state that does not intend to recruit its
subjects into citizenship has no use for the apparatus that produces
citizens.

## Contested

Whether compulsory schooling extended citizenship or manufactured governable
subjects is disputed, and the evidence supports both because states pursued
both, often in one statute. The civic reading points to literacy, mobility
and an informed electorate; the control reading points out that the
curriculum's most reliable outputs are punctuality, timetable obedience and
a standardised national identity rather than any specific knowledge.

Model the outputs as **a bundle that cannot be separated at adoption** — a
state that legislates schooling gets literacy, time discipline and
nationalisation together, and cannot select among them. That represents the
record better than letting the outcome be chosen, and it keeps the mechanic
inside design principle 2, which forbids direct levers on population
disposition.

## Hook

Conscription's twin, built from the same components and gated on the same
`legibility` — but more demanding, because it must know where a child is on
a given morning rather than which district they live in. What it takes is
time, so its real cost falls on households that needed the child working,
which makes truancy enforcement the expensive half and household resistance
the predictable response. `literacy training cost` does not fall here; it
is transferred to the state, which is what finally makes literacy universal
where cheaper scripts never did. Outputs arrive bundled and unselectable —
`literacy`, `time discipline` and national identification together — so a
state cannot buy the reading without the timetable or the nation. And the
custody the mechanism requires is available for other purposes: the same
provision that permits teaching permits assimilation, which is not a misuse
of the instrument but the instrument working as designed.
