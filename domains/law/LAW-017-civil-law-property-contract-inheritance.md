Distinct from LAW-001's public and political law, private law governs everyday transactions between individuals — who owns what, what makes an agreement enforceable, how property passes at death — the exact substrate this wiki's own commercial-trust and contract-enforcement mechanics silently assume already exists.

## Timing

Roman private law matures into a sophisticated, systematically taught body of doctrine well before its eventual codification under Justinian (529-534); Islamic fiqh develops its own detailed property, contract, and inheritance jurisprudence from the 700s; the Napoleonic Code (1804) achieves the first modern, comprehensive, and deliberately exportable systematic codification of an entire nation's private law in one internally consistent text, a model widely adopted or imitated through the following century.

## Mechanism

Private law converts an informal expectation between two parties — that a sale is final, that a debt will be repaid, that an estate passes to specified heirs — into a claim a court can actually adjudicate and enforce, by fixing in advance what counts as valid ownership, what makes an agreement binding, and how an inheritance is divided. Without this substrate, the "contract enforcement" any commercial or financial trust mechanic depends on has no legal object to actually enforce — a court can only rule on a contract dispute if a body of property and contract law already defines what a valid contract and a valid property claim are.

## Social consequence

A codified, publicly known private-law regime lets strangers transact with each other on the strength of the law itself rather than requiring personal trust, reputation, or a shared community's informal enforcement (the diaspora-network trust substitute MIG-009 describes for exactly the situations where this legal substrate isn't reliably available) — converting commercial and property relationships from something that requires an ongoing personal or communal relationship into something a court-enforceable legal claim can secure between total strangers.

## Regional specifics

Islamic fiqh's fixed Quranic inheritance shares represent a detailed, independently developed private-law tradition considerably predating comparable systematic European codification. The Napoleonic Code's deliberate internal consistency and export design converts private law from a patchwork of regional custom into a portable institutional package other states could adopt wholesale, the same institutional-borrowing pattern seen elsewhere in this domain (LAW-004's examination system, LAW-009's UDHR drafting). Qing China's embedding of private-law provisions within a unified penal code rather than a separate civil-law category shows the same underlying transactions (property, contract, inheritance) can be governed effectively without adopting the Roman/European civil-versus-criminal law architectural split at all.
