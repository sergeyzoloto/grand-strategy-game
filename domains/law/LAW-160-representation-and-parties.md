Once a representative assembly (LAW-140) exists, its members reliably
organize into durable competing factions around shared interest or
ideology — parties are not a separate later invention so much as what any
sufficiently large representative body eventually organizes itself into.

## Timing

The Whig-Tory division in English politics organizes recognizably from
around 1679; comparable party organization emerges wherever representative
assemblies persist long enough for durable factional alignment to form,
including Japan's Meiji-era Diet politics from 1890.

## Mechanism

A representative assembly large enough that no single member can
individually decide outcomes creates strong incentive for members with
aligned interests to coordinate voting and strategy — this coordination,
repeated across successive sessions and issues, hardens into durable party
identity and organization, converting representation from a collection of
individually-elected members into a competition between organized
coalitions with their own platforms and discipline.

## Social consequence

Party organization gives voters a legible shortcut for evaluating
candidates (a party's general platform and reputation) that individual
candidate assessment alone doesn't provide, at the cost of subordinating
individual representatives' independent judgment to party discipline and
strategy — a tradeoff between electoral legibility for voters and
individual representative autonomy that every party system manages
differently.

## Regional specifics

Japan's Meiji Diet and its factional politics develop as a deliberate
institutional package adopted alongside the era's other constitutional and
bureaucratic reforms, rather than emerging from a comparable centuries-
long estate-bargaining history (LAW-140) the European cases build on — the
representative form was imported quickly even though the slow underlying
process that produced it organically in Europe wasn't. The Ottoman First
Constitutional Era's introduction of an elected parliament without
sufficient time for a durable party system to take root before suspension
shows the representative institutional form and sustained competitive-
party practice are separable, and the first can be introduced without the
second following automatically.

## Hook

Any sufficiently large and durable representative assembly (LAW-140)
organizes itself into competing parties as a close-to-inevitable
consequence of members needing to coordinate voting — a legible shortcut
for voters traded against individual representative autonomy, and an
institutional form that, per the Ottoman case, can be imported without
the sustained practice needed to make it actually function as intended.
