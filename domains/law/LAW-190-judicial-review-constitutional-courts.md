The specific institutional mechanism LAW-180's own Hook identifies as the missing variable: a court gains the power to strike down a legislative or executive act as unconstitutional, converting a written constitution from a structural or aspirational text into one with a concrete, court-centered enforcement mechanism.

## Timing

The US Supreme Court establishes the power of judicial review over federal legislation in Marbury v. Madison (1803); the diffuse American model, in which any court can rule on a constitutional question, spreads unevenly through the 1800s; Austria's centralized constitutional-court model, designed by Hans Kelsen, is established in 1920 and becomes the dominant continental European pattern after 1945.

## Mechanism

Judicial review supplies exactly the enforcement gap LAW-180 identifies but doesn't resolve: rather than relying on the political branches to police their own compliance with a written constitution, a court is given (or asserts, as the US Supreme Court did in Marbury) the authority to declare a law or executive act void for conflicting with the constitutional text, converting the document from a statement of intended structure into one with a specific, citable enforcement mechanism independent of whichever branch the constitution is meant to constrain.

## Social consequence

Once a court holds this power, it becomes a genuinely independent political actor capable of overturning decisions made by elected legislatures and executives, which raises a recurring legitimacy question distinct from anything LAW-180 alone poses: an unelected judiciary now has a real, citable claim to override elected officials' decisions, a tension every judicial-review system has to manage through its own rules for how judges are selected, how long they serve, and how their decisions can be revisited.

## Regional specifics

Austria's centralized constitutional-court model, hearing only constitutional questions in a single specialized body, is a structurally distinct institutional answer to the same enforcement problem the diffuse American model solves differently — both convert a written constitution into an enforceable document, but through genuinely different institutional architecture, later widely adopted in different combinations across the world. The United Kingdom's complete absence of judicial review over primary legislation, resting instead on unwritten constitutional practice and parliamentary sovereignty, shows a written constitution and court-enforced review are historically bundled in cases like the American one but are logically separable institutions that don't have to travel together.

## Hook

A gate that closes the exact enforcement gap LAW-180 names but doesn't resolve: a court's power to void an unconstitutional act converts a written constitution into an actually enforceable document, at the cost of raising a durable legitimacy question about an unelected judiciary overriding elected officials that every judicial-review system has to manage on its own terms.
