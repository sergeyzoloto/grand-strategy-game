A shift in the underlying theory of what punishment is for — deterrence and proportionality rather than spectacle and retribution — converting criminal sentencing from a display of sovereign power into a calculated, increasingly incarceration-centered system, distinct from LAW-001's codification of what's illegal or LAW-012's street-level enforcement of order.

## Timing

Cesare Beccaria's "On Crimes and Punishments" (1764) argues systematically against torture, arbitrary and disproportionate punishment, and the routine use of capital punishment; the argument's institutional consequence, the penitentiary built around isolation and reflection rather than public physical punishment, matures from Pennsylvania's 1790s reforms and spreads across Europe and the Americas through the 1800s.

## Mechanism

Beccaria's core argument is that punishment's purpose is deterring future crime through certainty and proportionality, not displaying sovereign power through spectacle or satisfying retributive impulse through severity — a punishment disproportionate to the offense, he argues, actually undermines deterrence by eroding the perceived legitimacy of the law imposing it. This theoretical shift directly motivates a new institutional form, the penitentiary, built around sustained isolation and enforced reflection intended to reform an offender's character rather than simply inflict pain or death as public demonstration.

## Social consequence

Converting punishment's stated purpose from retributive spectacle to calculated deterrence and reform changes what the state's exercise of penal power is supposed to look like in public — a public execution or corporal punishment staged for maximum visible deterrent effect gives way to a punishment carried out privately, behind institutional walls, whose reformative claim (successfully reforming the offender) becomes a new, separate, and often unmet standard the institution can be judged against.

## Regional specifics

Pennsylvania's Walnut Street Jail and the resulting "Pennsylvania system" translate Beccaria's largely theoretical European argument into a specific, exportable American institutional design — solitary confinement structured explicitly around reflection and reform — showing a philosophical argument and its concrete institutional implementation can develop in different places even when the underlying theory travels quickly. Qing China's continued reliance on a graduated, explicitly codified corporal and capital punishment schedule without a comparable proportionality-and-reform theoretical break shows systematic codification of punishment (which China achieves fully) and this specific Enlightenment-driven shift in punishment's underlying purpose are separable achievements.

## Hook

A branch/modifier: converts punishment philosophy itself — deterrence and proportionality rather than spectacle and retribution — from a display of sovereign power into a calculated, increasingly incarceration-centered system, with the penitentiary as its durable institutional consequence.
