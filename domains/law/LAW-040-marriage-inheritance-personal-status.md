Law governing marriage validity, divorce, inheritance shares, and guardianship operates at a household scale no other entry in this domain touches, and is frequently administered through a separate legal track from general civil or criminal law entirely.

## Timing

Personal-status provisions appear within the earliest written codes (Hammurabi's code already regulates marriage and inheritance alongside its other provisions) from -1754 onward; the Ottoman millet system formalizes religiously-differentiated personal-status administration from 1517; British colonial codification of separate "Hindu" and "Muslim" personal-status categories in India dates from 1772; European civil marriage jurisdiction secularizes gradually from the Reformation through the 1900s.

## Mechanism

A polity can govern marriage, divorce, and inheritance either as part of one unified civil-law code applying equally to every subject, or as a separate track administered differently by religious or customary community — and the second option lets a single state run multiple, simultaneously valid personal-status regimes for different subject populations at once, a structural option unified civil codes (LAW-070) don't offer. Which model a state adopts shapes whether personal-status law changes uniformly through general legislative reform or requires separate negotiation with each recognized community.

## Social consequence

Personal-status law reaches into a household's most intimate legal relationships — who can marry whom, how a divorce is granted, which children inherit what — in a way LAW-050's citizenship or LAW-220's suffrage never does, meaning a subject's actual legal experience of the state can differ enormously by religious or communal category even where general civil and criminal law applies uniformly to everyone.

## Regional specifics

The Ottoman millet system treats religious legal pluralism in personal-status matters as the empire's normal governing structure, not an exception. British colonial codification in India instead converts an actually fluid, locally-variable set of customary practices into two rigid, administratively convenient categories ("Hindu law," "Muslim law") that didn't previously exist in that fixed form — a case of colonial administrative convenience actively inventing the very legal tradition it claimed only to be recording, with consequences persisting well past independence. European secularization of marriage jurisdiction runs in the opposite institutional direction from the Ottoman case over the same broad era, consolidating personal-status law under unified state civil authority rather than formalizing religious plurality.

## Hook

A gate at the household and individual scale this domain otherwise entirely lacks — frequently run as a separate, religiously or communally differentiated track parallel to general civil law rather than unified under one code, and, per the colonial Indian case, capable of inventing the very "traditional" category it claims only to be recording.
