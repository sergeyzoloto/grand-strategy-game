Before any standing international institution could enforce an agreement
between sovereign polities, treaties relied entirely on sworn oath, divine
witness, and reputational consequence for violation — inter-polity
`contract enforcement` with no court above the parties to appeal to.

## Timing

The Egyptian-Hittite Treaty of Kadesh (-1259) is the earliest surviving
complete peace treaty text; Chinese meng covenant practice is attested
from around -650; comparable oath-based treaty traditions persist and
develop independently across many polities through at least 1142.

## Mechanism

Without an enforcing authority standing above both treaty parties, a
treaty's binding force depends entirely on invoking a consequence each
party takes seriously enough to actually constrain their future behavior
— divine witness and oath (the threat of supernatural retribution for
violation), physical ritual and shared symbolic investment (the wampum
belt, the buried covenant text), or simply each party's own reputational
stake in being seen as an oath-keeper by other polities watching how this
particular agreement gets treated.

## Social consequence

A ruler's personal oath-keeping reputation becomes a real strategic asset
or liability independent of their military strength, since other polities
calibrate their own willingness to negotiate future agreements based on
observed past compliance — a ruler known for breaking sworn treaties
faces a durable diplomatic cost even if no single violation triggers
immediate military consequence.

## Regional specifics

Chinese meng covenant ceremonies and the Egyptian-Hittite treaty both
invoke divine or ancestral witness against violation despite arising from
entirely unconnected traditions, suggesting oath-based enforcement is a
close-to-inevitable solution wherever inter-polity agreement needs to bind
without a superior enforcing authority. Haudenosaunee wampum treaty-
recording achieves comparable durable, mutually-referenced agreement
memory through a physical exchanged-object technology rather than
written text, a solution requiring no shared literacy at all between
treaty parties.

## Hook

Absent any enforcing authority above the treaty parties themselves, a
treaty's binding force rests entirely on invoked consequence — divine
witness, shared ritual investment, or accumulated reputational stake in
`contract enforcement` — a genuinely different foundation for inter-polity
obligation than the domestic courts LAW's territory can eventually rely
on.
