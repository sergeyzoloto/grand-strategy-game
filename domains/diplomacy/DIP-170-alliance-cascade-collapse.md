The same standing-commitment architecture that reduces everyday friction
between great powers (DIP-130's concert diplomacy) removes each individual
power's ability to contain a crisis once the first commitment triggers the
next — converting a localized bilateral dispute into a rapid, largely
automatic cascade that destroys in weeks a coordination structure decades
in the building.

## Timing

The July Crisis runs from the June 28, 1914 assassination of Archduke Franz
Ferdinand through the cascading mobilizations and declarations of war that
produce general European war by early August 1914, closing the Concert-era
diplomatic system (DIP-130) this record dates from 1815.

## Mechanism

A system of standing bilateral and multilateral commitments — built up
precisely to make each individual conflict less likely by giving every
major power a stake in every other's security — has the reverse effect once
a triggering crisis is severe enough to activate the first commitment:
each subsequent power's mobilization is no longer a fully independent
choice but a contractually and reputationally constrained response to the
prior power's move, converting what begins as a bilateral dispute between
two lesser powers into a general war between every major signatory in
turn, with no single power's leadership fully choosing the outcome any of
them individually end up in.

## Social consequence

Because the cascade operates through prior standing commitment rather than
any single decision-maker's fresh judgment, populations and even most of
the involved governments experience the resulting general war as something
that happened to the system rather than something any one actor
deliberately chose — a sharply different experience of agency than DIP-130's
own everyday concert diplomacy, where each negotiated adjustment is a
visible, deliberate great-power choice.

## Regional specifics

The crisis originates in the Balkans, a region DIP-130's own spheres-of-
influence arrangement had never fully stabilized, and cascades through the
existing alliance architecture (Austria-Hungary and Germany, France and
Russia, and Britain's own less formal but ultimately binding commitments)
in a matter of weeks — the same commitment structure that had successfully
contained several prior Balkan crises without general war finally producing
one once the triggering dispute involved a great power's own direct
security guarantee rather than a peripheral territorial adjustment.

## Hook

A pool-drain event: the accumulated inter-power trust and coordination
capacity concert diplomacy (DIP-130) and later collective-security
institutions (DIP-180) both depend on is not just something that erodes
gradually as interests diverge, but something a single sufficiently severe
triggering crisis can catastrophically zero out through the very alliance
architecture built to prevent exactly that outcome — the direct, explicit
provocation for DIP-180's own collective-security redesign.
