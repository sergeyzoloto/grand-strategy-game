Where imperial oceanic diplomacy's multilateral partitions (DIP-090) settle
colonizing powers' claims among themselves with the colonized party absent
throughout, an unequal treaty instead negotiates — under direct military
coercion — bilaterally with the target government itself, which remains a
nominal treaty party even as the terms it's forced to accept strip away
much of its actual sovereign control.

## Timing

The 1842 Treaty of Nanking, concluding the First Opium War (COM-210's own
commercial angle), establishes the paradigm case; the resulting treaty-port
and extraterritorial system (DIP-150) expands through further coerced
treaties with China and Japan across the following decades, with China's
last such unequal treaty provisions formally renounced by the Western
powers in 1943.

## Mechanism

An unequal treaty is negotiated and signed following a direct military
defeat, with the losing government retaining nominal sovereign standing —
it is still the party that signs, and the treaty's legal form is
indistinguishable from an agreement between equals — while its actual
substantive terms (forced port openings, fixed low tariffs it cannot
independently revise, extraterritorial jurisdiction for the victor's
citizens) are dictated by the victor with no real negotiating room on the
losing side, converting the treaty form itself into a legal fiction of
equality covering a substantively coercive, one-sided outcome.

## Social consequence

Because the coerced government remains the nominal signing party rather
than being formally annexed or bypassed entirely, it also remains formally
responsible for enforcing terms imposed against its own interest — a
uniquely corrosive position domestically, since the same government has to
be seen administering its own population's subjection to foreign
extraterritorial privilege (DIP-150) it never actually agreed to in any
meaningful sense, fueling the specific brand of nationalist grievance
("the century of humiliation," in the Chinese case) that later drives
sustained diplomatic efforts to renegotiate or abrogate the treaties
entirely.

## Regional specifics

The First Opium War's outcome establishes the treaty-port template that
subsequent coerced treaties with China and, briefly, Japan (before Japan's
own rapid modernization let it renegotiate its unequal treaties on
favorable terms decades ahead of China) extend and elaborate — a
diplomatic-legal category that, unlike DIP-090's multilateral colonial
partitions, always retains the coerced government as at least a nominal
party, giving it a continuous, if severely constrained, legal standing from
which to eventually contest and reverse the terms once its own relative
power recovers.

## Hook

A modifier: converts a military defeat into a durable legal category — the
losing government remains a nominal treaty party even as it loses real
control over tariffs, ports, and jurisdiction over foreign residents
(DIP-150) — retaining, unlike DIP-090's multilateral partitions, a
continuous legal standing from which the coerced terms can eventually be
renegotiated once the underlying power imbalance narrows.
