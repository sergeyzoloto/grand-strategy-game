Before any tribute relationship, sworn treaty, or resident embassy can
exist, two polities need a way to safely make contact at all — a norm,
independently arrived at across unconnected traditions, that a messenger
carrying words rather than weapons is not to be killed for the words they
carry.

## Timing

Near Eastern diplomatic correspondence (the Amarna letters prominent among
the earliest surviving evidence) already presupposes safe, repeated envoy
travel between rival courts by the mid-second millennium BCE; the Greek
kerux tradition is attested from around -800; comparable Chinese practice
matures alongside the meng covenant system (DIP-020) from around -700.

## Mechanism

A herald or envoy operates under a claimed inviolability — sometimes
religiously sanctioned (the kerux's Hermes-marked staff), sometimes bound up
with the same ritual-covenant logic securing a treaty oath itself — that
holds even when the sending and receiving polities are otherwise at open
war: this converts inter-polity contact from something that cannot safely
be initiated at all (any message-bearer is simply an enemy combatant to be
killed) into a repeatable, low-risk channel, which is the actual
precondition every later diplomatic mechanism in this domain — tribute
negotiation, oath-swearing, hostage and gift exchange, resident
ambassadors — silently assumes is already available.

## Social consequence

Because the norm is a shared expectation rather than an enforceable law, a
polity that violates it pays a real reputational cost independent of any
immediate military consequence — other polities recalibrate their own
willingness to send envoys to, or receive them from, a known
envoy-violator, converting a single act of envoy-killing into a durable
diplomatic liability that can outlast whatever short-term advantage the
violation was meant to secure.

## Regional specifics

The Chinese case ties envoy inviolability directly to the same ritual-oath
logic (DIP-020) securing inter-state covenants generally, making a
violation a breach of the same sacred framework binding treaties themselves
rather than a separate norm. The Greek kerux's religious sanction under
Hermes achieves comparable protection through an independent mythological
framework. The comparative absence of clearly documented, formally-named
envoy-immunity norms in the pre-contact Americas is most plausibly an
evidentiary gap rather than a real absence, since the underlying contact
these norms exist to protect demonstrably occurred there too.

## Contested

How uniformly envoy immunity was actually honored versus violated across
the traditions that professed it is a real, case-by-case historical
question — the norm's recurring violation is itself evidence of its real
weight, since killing an envoy functions as a recognized, deliberately
provocative escalation precisely because it breaks so widely shared an
expectation. Model envoy immunity as a strong default norm whose violation
is a modelable, escalatory act in its own right, not an absolute rule
without exception.

## Hook

A gate: establishes safe, repeatable inter-polity contact as this domain's
actual starting condition — the precondition tribute diplomacy (DIP-040),
oath-bound treaties (DIP-020), hostage and gift exchange (DIP-060), and
resident embassies (DIP-080) all silently assume is already available, with
its violation modelable as its own real, escalatory diplomatic event.
