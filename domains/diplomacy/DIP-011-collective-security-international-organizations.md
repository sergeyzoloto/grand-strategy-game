Rather than relying entirely on ad hoc alliance and concert diplomacy
(DIP-009) case by case, states construct standing international
institutions with defined membership, procedure, and — at least in
principle — a collective obligation to respond to aggression against any
member.

## Timing

The League of Nations is founded in 1920; its failure to prevent renewed
great-power war leads to the United Nations' 1945 founding with a
strengthened (if still great-power-constrained) collective-security
mechanism; the Non-Aligned Movement establishes an alternative
international organizational model from 1961.

## Mechanism

Collective security substitutes a standing, rules-based institutional
commitment for the ad hoc, case-by-case calculation concert diplomacy
(DIP-009) relies on — member states agree in advance to treat aggression
against any one member as a concern for all, which in principle deters
aggression more reliably than a system where each potential aggressor must
separately guess how other powers might react — but the mechanism only
functions as well as member states' actual willingness to honor that
advance commitment when a real crisis tests it, which is exactly where
the League's practical failures concentrated.

## Social consequence

Membership and voice within these institutions becomes a real marker of
international standing independent of a state's raw power — a founding
UN Security Council seat carries permanent structural advantage over
ordinary General Assembly membership, converting institutional design
choices made at founding into durable, decades-long inequality among
formally equal member states.

## Regional specifics

The Non-Aligned Movement represents newly-independent and developing
states building their own alternative collective institution rather than
simply joining a great-power-constructed system on offer, a genuine
instance of diplomatic institutional agency exercised by exactly the states
usually treated as objects rather than architects of the international
order elsewhere in this record (compare DIP-007's Berlin Conference,
DIP-009's spheres of influence). The League mandate system's supervised-
but-not-fully-member status for former Ottoman Middle Eastern territories
shows collective security institutions can themselves reproduce a
hierarchy of standing among the very populations and territories they
claim to help order, rather than extending equal participation
automatically to everyone affected by their decisions.

## Hook

Collective security substitutes a standing rules-based commitment for
concert diplomacy's (DIP-009) ad hoc calculation, but institutional design
choices made at founding (permanent council seats, mandate-system
subordinate status) can lock in durable inequality among members for
decades — and, per the Non-Aligned Movement, states excluded from shaping
that original design can and do construct their own alternative
institutions rather than simply accepting the terms on offer.
