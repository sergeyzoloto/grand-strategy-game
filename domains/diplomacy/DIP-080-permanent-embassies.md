A resident ambassador stationed continuously in a foreign capital, rather
than a temporary envoy dispatched for one specific negotiation, converts
diplomatic relations from episodic contact into continuous, real-time
intelligence-gathering and relationship management.

## Timing

Italian city-states (Milan and Venice prominent among them) develop the
practice of maintaining permanent resident embassies abroad from around
1450; the practice spreads across European powers and formalizes further
through the Peace of Westphalia settlement (1648).

## Mechanism

A temporary envoy can negotiate a specific matter but then departs,
leaving the sending polity dependent on the next dispatched mission for
any further information or negotiation — a permanent resident ambassador
instead maintains continuous local relationships, ongoing intelligence-
gathering, and the ability to react and negotiate in real time as
circumstances change, converting `information speed` about a foreign
court's intentions and conditions from occasional and delayed into
continuous and current.

## Social consequence

Professional resident ambassadors require reciprocal legal protection
(diplomatic immunity) to function at all, since a host court could
otherwise simply detain or coerce a permanently-resident foreign
representative — this reciprocal-immunity norm, once established, becomes
a durable convention any power has strong incentive to honor even toward
a rival, since violating it would immediately endanger their own
ambassadors resident elsewhere.

## Regional specifics

Byzantine diplomatic institutions achieve real, sustained professional
sophistication considerably earlier than the Italian case, but organized
around one imperial center managing its own relationships with a variety
of foreign powers rather than around the specifically reciprocal practice
of formally equal sovereign states each stationing permanent ambassadors
in the others' capitals. Ming China's Honglu Si achieves comparable
institutional permanence and ritual sophistication purely as a receiving
institution for tributary envoys (DIP-040), never developing the practice
of China itself maintaining resident ambassadors abroad on a reciprocal
basis — a genuine institutional difference tied directly to the
tributary system's inherently hierarchical rather than formally-equal
framing of foreign relations.

## Hook

Permanent resident embassies convert diplomatic relations from episodic,
delayed contact into continuous, current `information speed` — a
capability requiring reciprocal diplomatic immunity to function safely at
all, and one whose reciprocal, formally-equal form specifically depends on
a diplomatic culture (like the Italian city-state system) that doesn't
frame foreign relations through an inherently hierarchical lens the way
tributary-system diplomacy (DIP-040) does.
