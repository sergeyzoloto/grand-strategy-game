Replacing alchemy's transmutation-oriented framework with a systematic
theory of elements and conservation of mass — converting centuries of
accumulated practical chemical knowledge (including the gunpowder
manufacturing lineage in ENG-008) into a predictive science.

## Timing

Boyle's systematic case against Aristotelian element theory (1661) is an
early step; Lavoisier's identification of oxygen's role in combustion and
formulation of mass conservation (through the 1770s-1780s) mark the
decisive break from the phlogiston theory that had organized chemical
thinking for the preceding century.

## Mechanism

Phlogiston theory explained combustion as the release of a fire-like
substance from burning material, a framework that worked reasonably well
for qualitative prediction but ran into serious quantitative trouble once
careful measurement showed some combustion products gain mass rather than
losing it — Lavoisier's application of precise measurement (applying
NAT-007's experimental rigor specifically to weighing reactants and
products) and his oxygen-based alternative resolves the anomaly by
reframing combustion as combination with atmospheric oxygen rather than
loss of an internal substance, converting chemistry into a science
organized around conserved, measurable quantities.

## Social consequence

Reframing chemistry around measurable, conserved quantities converts it
from a craft tradition organized around qualitative recipes and
transformations into a quantitative science capable of genuine prediction
— a shift that professionalizes chemistry as an institutional discipline
distinct from both alchemy's esoteric tradition and simple artisanal craft
knowledge.

## Regional specifics

Islamic and Chinese alchemical traditions accumulate enormous practical
chemical knowledge over many centuries — the saltpeter refining underlying
gunpowder's manufacturing chain (ENG-008) being a direct, consequential
example — without formally replacing transmutation-oriented theory with a
systematic elemental framework, showing extensive working knowledge and
correct theoretical framework are separable achievements that can be
centuries apart even within a single tradition's own history.

## Hook

Applying precise, systematic measurement (NAT-007's rigor) specifically to
weighing reactants and products is what breaks phlogiston theory and
converts centuries of accumulated practical chemical craft knowledge
(including gunpowder's own manufacturing lineage, ENG-008) into a
predictive, quantitative science — a reframing of existing knowledge more
than an entirely new body of observation.
