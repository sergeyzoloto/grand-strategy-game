The general theory explaining why a steam engine (ENG-009) works, what
limits how efficient it can ever become, and why heat flows one direction
rather than another — theory developed largely in response to an already-
working technology rather than preceding and enabling it.

## Timing

Sadi Carnot's analysis of ideal heat-engine efficiency (1824) is the
foundational work; Clausius's formulation of entropy and the second law
(1850-1865) completes the theoretical framework, developed substantially
after Watt's steam engine (ENG-009) was already commercially widespread.

## Mechanism

Carnot's key insight is that any heat engine's maximum possible efficiency
is set by the temperature difference it operates across, not by any
particular mechanical design detail — a theoretical ceiling that exists
regardless of engineering cleverness, which reverses the usual technology-
then-theory sequence in the opposite direction from most of this record's
science-and-engineering pairs, since here the practical steam engine
substantially motivates and precedes the general theory explaining and
bounding it.

## Social consequence

Knowing the theoretical efficiency ceiling redirects engineering effort
productively — rather than searching indefinitely for a mechanical
improvement that could exceed a limit that provably doesn't exist,
engineers can instead focus effort on approaching the known achievable
ceiling as closely as practical, a more efficient allocation of scarce
technical talent once the theoretical bound is established.

## Regional specifics

This is a genuinely Europe-concentrated theoretical achievement in this
window, developed by French and German physicists specifically working to
explain an already-successful British engineering technology — a case
where theory follows and explains practice rather than the more common
theory-enables-practice sequence elsewhere in this record. American
engineering's comparative strength in applying and scaling technology
(ENG-005's interchangeable parts, COM-023's assembly line) rather than in
originating foundational theoretical physics during the same period shows
theoretical and applied engineering leadership can concentrate in
different places even within the same broader industrial era.

## Hook

Thermodynamic theory follows and explains an already-working technology
(ENG-009) rather than enabling it in advance — the reverse of the usual
science-then-engineering sequence — and the resulting known efficiency
ceiling redirects engineering effort toward approaching a provable limit
rather than searching indefinitely for an improvement that can't exist.
