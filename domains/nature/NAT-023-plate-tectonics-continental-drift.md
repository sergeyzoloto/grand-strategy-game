A scientifically correct theory can be professionally rejected for
decades not on religious or cultural grounds but for a genuinely
scientific reason — lacking a plausible physical mechanism — until a
later, independent line of evidence finally supplies one.

## Timing

Wegener proposes continental drift in 1912, based on the fit of
continental coastlines and matching fossil distributions across now-
separated continents; the theory is widely rejected or ignored by the
geological establishment for decades for want of a plausible mechanism
capable of actually moving continents; mid-century seafloor-spreading
evidence, gathered substantially through Cold War-funded oceanographic
mapping, supplies that mechanism and the theory is broadly vindicated as
plate tectonics by 1965.

## Mechanism

Wegener's evidence for continental drift — matching coastlines, shared
fossil species across continents now separated by ocean, matching rock
strata — is genuinely strong, but he cannot explain how anything as large
as a continent could physically move through solid ocean floor, and the
mechanism he does propose (continents plowing through the seabed) is
correctly judged physically implausible by contemporary geologists.
Seafloor-spreading evidence — new ocean floor forming at mid-ocean ridges
and spreading outward, carrying the continents along passively rather
than requiring them to plow through anything — finally supplies a
physically plausible mechanism for the same phenomenon Wegener had
already correctly described from different evidence.

## Social consequence

Plate tectonics' delayed acceptance is a case, distinct from
heliocentrism's (NAT-006) or evolution's (NAT-010) pattern of religious
and cultural resistance, of correct empirical observation being properly
withheld institutional acceptance on legitimate scientific grounds — a
professional community declining to accept a theory it cannot yet explain
mechanistically is behaving correctly by its own standards, even though
the underlying observation later turns out to have been right all along.

## Regional specifics

Wegener's original proposal and its early European reception belong to
this record's broader early-twentieth-century European theoretical
tradition (NAT-013, NAT-014); the decisive vindicating evidence instead
comes substantially from mid-century American oceanographic research,
much of it Cold War-funded naval seafloor mapping, shifting the theory's
evidentiary center of gravity across the Atlantic within a single
scientific generation.

## Hook

A branch distinct from NAT-006's and NAT-010's religious-resistance
pattern: correct evidence without a plausible causal mechanism can be
professionally rejected for decades on legitimately scientific grounds
alone, until an independent later line of evidence supplies the missing
mechanism and the original, already-correct observation is vindicated
rather than revised.
