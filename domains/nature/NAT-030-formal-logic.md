This entry owns formal logic specifically as a tool for validating
knowledge claims and inference — its use as an instrument feeding into
NAT-020's frameworks and NAT-090's empirical method; logic's fuller
treatment as philosophical argument in its own right belongs to PHI.

## Timing

Aristotelian syllogistic logic develops from around -350; Nyaya logical
method matures independently from around -300; Mohist logical analysis in
China dates to a comparable era (from around -400); Islamic logicians
preserve and extend the Aristotelian tradition through roughly 1000.

## Mechanism

Formal logic provides a method for checking whether a conclusion actually
follows from given premises independent of whether the conclusion is one a
reasoner wants to be true — this validity-checking function is what lets a
knowledge-generating tradition catch invalid inferences before they
propagate into accepted belief, a distinct and necessary complement to
having good raw premises (NAT-020's frameworks) or good empirical
observations (NAT-090) in the first place.

## Social consequence

A tradition with a well-developed formal logic can subject rival claims
and arguments to a shared, mechanical standard of validity independent of
either party's authority or rhetorical skill — this is part of why formal
logic becomes especially elaborated in traditions with active institutional
debate culture (Buddhist monastic debate, Islamic theological disputation,
Greek dialectic), where being able to demonstrate an opponent's argument is
formally invalid carries real institutional stakes.

## Regional specifics

Nyaya and later Buddhist logical systems reach a formalization comparable
to or in some respects more systematic than Aristotelian syllogistic logic,
developed with no contact between the traditions — independent convergence
on rigorous inference-validation suggests the underlying need is close to
universal for any sufficiently developed intellectual culture. Mohist
logical analysis addresses comparable problems in the same broad era as
Aristotle but doesn't sustain continuous institutional transmission the way
the Greek tradition (via Islamic preservation) does, showing formal
logic's long-term influence depends on institutional continuity as much as
on the logic's own original sophistication.

## Hook

Formal logic supplies the validity-checking function that catches invalid
inferences a metaphysical framework (NAT-020) or empirical observation
(NAT-090) might otherwise let through — a tool multiple traditions develop
independently to broadly comparable sophistication, whose lasting influence
then depends heavily on which tradition's institutions actually sustain and
transmit it across generations.
