Special and general relativity replace Newtonian mechanics' assumption of
absolute space and time with a framework where measurements of space,
time, and gravity depend on the observer's own motion and the surrounding
mass-energy distribution — a revision of NAT-004's foundational mechanics
rather than a separate theory alongside it.

## Timing

Special relativity (1905) addresses the behavior of objects moving at
constant high speed; general relativity (1915) extends the framework to
acceleration and gravity, reframing gravity itself as the curvature of
spacetime rather than a force; the 1919 solar eclipse observation
confirming general relativity's predicted light-bending brings the theory
to wide public and scientific attention.

## Mechanism

Newtonian mechanics (NAT-004) assumes space and time are a fixed,
universal backdrop the same for every observer; relativity shows this
assumption breaks down at speeds approaching light or in strong
gravitational fields, where different observers genuinely measure
different elapsed times and distances for the same events — not because
of measurement error, but because space and time themselves are relative
to the observer's own reference frame, a conceptual revision rather than a
mere refinement of Newton's framework.

## Social consequence

Relativity's confirmation displaces Newtonian mechanics' two-century status
as the unquestioned final word on space, time, and gravity, demonstrating
even a spectacularly successful, thoroughly-validated scientific framework
remains open to revision under new theoretical and observational
pressure — a demonstration of science's own revisability as much as a
specific finding about the universe.

## Regional specifics

This is a genuinely Europe-concentrated theoretical achievement in its
origin. American physics's essentially absent role in the theory's
original development, followed by American institutions becoming central
to the field only once European physicists relocate amid the following
decades' political upheaval, is a case of scientific leadership migrating
through institutional relocation rather than emerging independently in a
new location — the capability moved with the people who held it, not
through separate local development.

## Hook

Relativity revises NAT-004's foundational assumption that space and time
are a fixed backdrop the same for every observer — a genuine conceptual
overturn of a two-century-old, thoroughly-validated framework, and a
demonstration that even the most established scientific consensus remains
open to being shown fundamentally incomplete under new pressure.
