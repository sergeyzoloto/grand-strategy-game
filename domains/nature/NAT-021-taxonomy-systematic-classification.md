A systematic, hierarchical naming and classification scheme converts
scattered individual natural-historical observation into a shared,
comparable, cumulative record any later observer can extend without
renegotiating the whole system from scratch.

## Timing

European natural history moves from ad hoc regional herbals and
bestiaries toward systematic classification from the mid-1500s, reaching
its most durable and widely-adopted form in Linnaeus's binomial
nomenclature, standardized by the tenth edition of his Systema Naturae
(1758); Li Shizhen's independently-developed Chinese materia medica
classification (1578) falls within the same broad window.

## Mechanism

Before a shared classification system exists, natural-historical
observation accumulates as scattered, locally-organized description —
useful to whoever compiled it, but not easily compared or extended by a
later observer working from different regional or organizational
assumptions. A systematic hierarchical scheme (Linnaeus's genus-species
binomial; Li Shizhen's property-and-use-based materia medica categories)
fixes a shared structure any later observer can slot a new specimen or
substance into without having to renegotiate the whole classification
from scratch, converting natural history into a genuinely cumulative
rather than merely additive body of knowledge.

## Social consequence

A stable, extensible classification system is what makes natural-historical
knowledge portable between observers who never meet and never coordinate
directly — a specimen described and classified in one place becomes
immediately comparable to one described anywhere else using the same
system, a precondition for the kind of large-scale comparative pattern
Darwin later needs (NAT-010) to notice similarity and variation across
species systematically enough to explain it.

## Regional specifics

Li Shizhen's Bencao Gangmu achieves a systematic classification of
comparable scope and rigor to the later European system, organized under
a substantially different logic — medicinal property and use rather than
reproductive structure — and developed with no connection to the European
tradition, reaching this practical achievement over a century before
Linnaeus.

## Hook

A modifier on how comparable and extensible natural-historical knowledge
becomes across different observers and generations: a systematic
classification scheme is what lets Darwin's later pattern-recognition
across species (NAT-010) work from a shared, cumulative record rather
than from scattered, incompatible regional description.
