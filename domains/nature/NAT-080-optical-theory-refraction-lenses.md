A mathematical account of how a curved surface bends light — the
knowledge-side precondition for converting the unaided eye's view of the
world into whatever a constructed instrument can additionally reveal,
owned here as theory and kept distinct from the grinding and mounting of
an actual lens, which belongs to a future engineering entry this record
does not yet have.

## Timing

Ibn Sahl's Baghdad treatise (completed by 984) states a correct
mathematical law of refraction for both simple and compound lenses;
Kepler's Dioptrice (1611), written within two years of Galileo's first
telescopic observations, works out the general theory of image formation
through lens combinations that later instrument design builds on
deliberately rather than by trial and error.

## Mechanism

Ibn Sahl's law specifies exactly how much a ray of light bends when it
crosses the boundary between two transparent materials, which is the
single piece of mathematics a lens-grinder needs to know in order to
shape glass that reliably focuses or magnifies an image rather than
merely distorting one — six centuries before European optics arrives at
an equivalent statement, usually credited to Snell. Kepler's later
contribution is not the refraction law itself but the theory of how two
lenses in sequence combine their effects, which is what makes a
telescope's design improvable by calculation rather than only by
grinding another lens and testing it.

## Social consequence

Converting lens-making from an empirical craft (grind, polish, and see
what the glass happens to do) into an applied mathematical theory is what
lets instrument quality improve predictably across successive
generations of makers rather than depending on any one craftsman's
personal accumulated skill — the same shift from tacit craft knowledge to
transmissible formal theory this record tracks elsewhere, applied here
specifically to the instruments that let heliocentrism (NAT-120) and germ
theory (NAT-130) each move from mathematically or logically plausible to
directly observationally confirmed.

## Regional specifics

Ibn Sahl's refraction law is a clear case, in the pattern of several
others in this record, of a foundational mathematical result achieved in
the Islamic world centuries before its later, differently-credited
European restatement — and one with no confirmed transmission chain
connecting the two, unlike the Maragha-Copernicus case (NAT-120), making
independent rediscovery the more likely reading here. Kepler's dioptric
theory follows within two years of the telescope's first practical
astronomical use, theory catching up to an instrument that had briefly
outrun its own explanation.

## Hook

A gate on what counts as observable evidence at all: heliocentrism's
telescopic confirmation (NAT-120) and germ theory's microorganism
identification (NAT-130) both require a working theory of how a
constructed lens extends the unaided eye to exist first, not merely the
general commitment to controlled observation NAT-090 already establishes.
