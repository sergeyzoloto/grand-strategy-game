Deliberately testing a claim against controlled observation rather than
relying on authority or pure reasoning alone — the specific method that
converts NAT-001's frameworks and NAT-003's logical validity into claims
checked against the world itself.

## Timing

Ibn al-Haytham's Book of Optics (completed by 1021) systematically applies
controlled experiment to test hypotheses about light and vision,
predating the European "Scientific Revolution" by roughly six centuries;
Francis Bacon's methodological argument for induction (1620) and the
following institutionalization of experimental science through the 1600s
mark the method's later systematic codification and collective
institutionalization in Europe.

## Mechanism

Ibn al-Haytham's specific innovation is methodological as much as
substantive: rather than reasoning from accepted authority (Euclid's and
Ptolemy's optical theories, both of which he tests and finds wanting) or
from pure logical deduction alone, he designs controlled experiments
specifically to distinguish between competing hypotheses about how vision
works — checking a claim against constructed observation rather than
against consistency with prior authority is the actual methodological leap,
independent of which specific culture or century first demonstrates it.

## Social consequence

Once experimental testing becomes institutionalized rather than an
individual scholar's personal practice — as with the Royal Society's peer
review and reproducibility expectations — claims about the natural world
become subject to a shared, checkable community standard rather than
resting on any one investigator's individual authority or reputation,
converting knowledge validation from a personal to a collective and
cumulative process.

## Regional specifics

Ibn al-Haytham's experimental optics predates the European "Scientific
Revolution" narrative's usual starting point by roughly six centuries,
demonstrating the core experimental method well before its later European
systematic codification — a case, similar to several others in this
record, where a foundational modern achievement's usual attribution
understates real prior achievement elsewhere. Chinese natural inquiry's
extensive careful observation without an accompanying explicit
experimental-method argument shows systematic empirical documentation and
formalized experimental philosophy are separable achievements — a
tradition can generate enormous careful observational data without framing
controlled experimentation as its own privileged, generalizable method.

## Hook

Testing a hypothesis against constructed, controlled observation rather
than against prior authority is the actual methodological leap this entry
tracks — demonstrated by Ibn al-Haytham centuries before its later
European systematic codification and collective institutionalization,
which is a real but distinct achievement (turning individual method into
shared, checkable community practice) from the method's original
invention.
