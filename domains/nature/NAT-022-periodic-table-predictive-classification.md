A classification system organized correctly enough not merely to sort
what is already known, but to predict the existence and properties of
things not yet observed at all.

## Timing

Mendeleev publishes his periodic table in 1869, arranging the elements
known at the time by atomic weight and recurring chemical behavior; over
the following two decades several elements he predicted but had not
himself observed (gallium, scandium, germanium) are discovered with
properties closely matching his calculations.

## Mechanism

Arranging the known elements by atomic weight and chemical behavior
(building on NAT-008's elemental framework) reveals a periodic, repeating
pattern with specific gaps in it — Mendeleev's decisive move is treating
those gaps not as errors or omissions in the available data but as
positive predictions: an undiscovered element with calculable atomic
weight and specific chemical properties must exist to fill each one,
converting a descriptive classification into a generator of falsifiable
claims about the unobserved.

## Social consequence

A classification whose gaps generate correct, specific, checkable
predictions carries a different kind of scientific authority than one
that only organizes what is already known — each subsequent confirmed
discovery (gallium in 1875, scandium in 1879, germanium in 1886) further
validates the underlying system itself, not merely the individual new
element found, converting the periodic table from a convenient organizing
device into strong evidence that the pattern it captures reflects a real
underlying structure in matter.

## Regional specifics

This is a genuinely Europe-concentrated achievement in both its origin
and its early confirming discoveries, developed and verified entirely
within the same nineteenth-century European chemical research community
that NAT-008's chemical revolution had already established a century
earlier.

## Hook

A distinct predictive-gate mechanic: a correctly-structured classification
does not only organize existing knowledge, it generates falsifiable
claims about what has not yet been found — a mechanism this record does
not otherwise have, and a stronger form of validation than the
descriptive classification systematics (NAT-021) manages.
