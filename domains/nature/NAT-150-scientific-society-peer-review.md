The standing, ongoing machinery — submission, critique, and publication
through a community of specialists — that converts an individual's claim
about the natural world into one collectively validated or rejected,
distinct from NAT-090's one-time argument that this kind of checking
should happen at all.

## Timing

The Royal Society (founded 1660) and the roughly contemporary French
Academy of Sciences (1666) institutionalize experimental philosophy as an
ongoing collective enterprise; the practice of adversarial peer critique
and publication-based priority claims matures over the following two
centuries, with the Newton-Leibniz calculus dispute (beginning in the
1690s and running for decades) an early, prominent test of the system's
own internal credit-assignment machinery.

## Mechanism

Where NAT-090 establishes that a claim about nature should be tested
against controlled observation rather than accepted on authority, a
scientific society adds a second, distinct layer: a standing community
that reviews, publishes, and disputes claims against each other on an
ongoing basis, converting the validation of any single claim from a
one-time act of individual verification into a repeatable, adversarial,
and cumulative institutional process that keeps functioning generation
after generation regardless of which individual scientist is involved.

## Social consequence

Once a claim's validity depends on surviving a standing community's
critique rather than resting on any one investigator's personal
authority or reputation, priority and credit become a resource the
institution itself allocates and can allocate wrongly or contentiously —
the Newton-Leibniz dispute shows the society's own credit-assignment
process can become a nationally-charged fight only loosely tracking the
actual, independently-real fact of who developed what and when.

## Regional specifics

The specific combination this entry tracks — open publication, adversarial
peer critique, and priority claimed by publication date — is a genuinely
European institutional innovation in this period. The Islamic world's
ijaza system and Chinese imperial academies each sustain serious,
long-running scholarly communities through substantially different
institutional logics (personal teacher-to-student transmission license;
state-supported academy without competitive priority-by-publication),
making this a case of a real institutional difference rather than an
unmet aspiration toward the European model.

## Contested

Institutional peer validation and actual scientific priority are related
but separable: the Newton-Leibniz dispute over calculus's invention shows
a scientific society's own internal credit-assignment machinery can
become a contested, nationally-inflected fight that only loosely tracks
who genuinely did what first. Model the society's ongoing validation
function as one process and its internal priority disputes as a second,
only loosely coupled process, rather than treating institutional credit
as a reliable proxy for actual priority.

## Hook

A civic mechanic: this entry creates and sustains a knowledge-validating
institution whose own internal disputes over credit and priority — the
Newton-Leibniz case being the paradigm example — are as mechanically real
and modelable as the scientific validation the institution otherwise
performs, since the two can diverge from each other.
