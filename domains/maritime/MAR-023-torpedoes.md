A self-propelled underwater explosive charge, aimed and released to travel
independently to its target — the single weapon whose emerging threat
reshapes naval doctrine across this entire domain's late-19th and 20th
century entries.

## Timing

The Whitehead self-propelled torpedo (from 1866) is the foundational
design; range, speed, and reliability improve steadily through the following
decades, with the weapon proving decisively effective in major fleet actions
by the Russo-Japanese War (1904-1905).

## Mechanism

A reliable self-propelled torpedo lets a small, cheap vessel threaten
catastrophic damage to a large, expensive capital ship from underwater,
where armor plating (MAR-018) offers little protection — this single
capability is what makes the Jeune École's cheap-flotilla argument (MAR-019)
coherent in the first place, and what makes every subsequent capital ship
design (MAR-025, MAR-026) need dedicated underwater protection and escort
screening (MAR-021) it didn't previously require.

## Social consequence

The torpedo's cost-asymmetric threat potential (a cheap weapon threatening
an expensive ship) becomes a recurring source of naval strategic anxiety
disproportionate to its actual kill count in most engagements up to this
point, driving real doctrinal and shipbuilding investment (destroyers,
submarine development) well ahead of the weapon's fully proven battlefield
record — an anticipated threat shaping investment as much as a demonstrated
one.

## Regional specifics

Japan's early and decisive operational use of torpedo attacks at scale, both
opening the Russo-Japanese War and finishing damaged ships at Tsushima,
gives it a claim to the weapon's first major decisive fleet-action
validation, ahead of comparable European combat use. The United States'
reliance on licensed and observed European torpedo designs rather than
independent development shows technology leadership in this domain doesn't
track general industrial capacity in any simple way — a state can lag
significantly in one specific weapons technology while leading in others.

## Hook

The torpedo is the underlying weapon-technology unlock that every doctrinal
argument in this domain's ironclad-to-dreadnought era (Jeune École,
destroyers, submarines) is actually a response to the maturity level of —
model its range/reliability improving on its own timeline, with each
dependent doctrine's viability keyed to that underlying curve rather than
to its own independent story.
