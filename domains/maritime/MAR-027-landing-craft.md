Purpose-built shallow-draft vessels for landing troops and equipment
directly onto a defended beach, plus the logistics doctrine for sustaining
an invasion force after landing without a captured port — a capability that
converts marines (MAR-017) and naval transport into a genuine offensive
power-projection tool against a hostile shore.

## Timing

Develops through the late 1930s, proven at scale across both the Pacific
(from 1941) and European (culminating in the 1944 Normandy landings)
theatres of World War Two through 1945.

## Mechanism

Specialized landing craft solve the specific problem of transferring troops,
vehicles, and supplies from ocean-going transport directly onto a beach
without a captured port to unload at — a capability that removes port
capture as a hard prerequisite for a large-scale invasion, at the cost of an
extremely complex, purpose-built logistics chain (specialized vessel types,
beach organization, floating and temporary harbor construction) that only
a highly industrialized power can field at the scale a major invasion
requires.

## Social consequence

The scale of over-the-beach logistics required for a major amphibious
invasion (the Normandy landings' artificial Mulberry harbors being the
paradigm case) concentrates an enormous, temporary, highly-coordinated
industrial and organizational effort into a single operation, making
successful large-scale amphibious invasion as much a test of an entire
war economy's logistics coordination capacity as of the landing force's
combat performance on the beach itself.

## Regional specifics

Japanese amphibious doctrine's early-war effectiveness in Malaya and the
Philippines develops in genuine parallel with, rather than by copying, Allied
landing-craft design, showing the underlying capability was independently
arrived at by more than one industrial power facing the same operational
problem, before the vastly larger Allied industrial base overtakes Japan's
capacity to keep pace later in the war. North African landings being
conducted entirely by external Allied forces using their own imported
capability, with no local capability developed at all, is a clean case of a
theatre and a capability being entirely decoupled from each other.

## Hook

Landing craft and over-the-beach logistics remove port capture as a
prerequisite for invasion, converting marine capability (MAR-017) into a
genuine offensive power-projection tool against any defended coastline —
gated on an entire war economy's logistics-coordination capacity, not just
on the landing force's own combat strength.
