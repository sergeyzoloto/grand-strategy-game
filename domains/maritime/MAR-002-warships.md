A vessel purpose-built or purpose-adapted for combat rather than cargo — the
foundational category this entire domain's later warship-type entries
(Ship of the Line, Ironclads, Dreadnought) are all later chapters of.

## Timing

Purpose-built oared warships (Egyptian and later Phoenician, Greek, and
Roman galleys) are attested from roughly -1200; independent East Asian and
South Asian warship traditions develop on their own timelines from roughly
-500 and 1000 respectively, all predating the ocean-going and gunpowder-era
vessels (MAR-008, MAR-018) that eventually supersede them.

## Mechanism

A warship trades cargo capacity for speed, maneuverability, ramming or
boarding capability, and (once oar-and-sail galleys mature) a rowing crew
large enough to fight independent of wind conditions — a specialization
that only pays off once a state or city-state has enough surplus and enough
naval rivalry to justify building vessels that carry fighters and weapons
instead of goods.

## Social consequence

Galley warfare's dependence on large rowing crews (often several hundred per
vessel) makes naval power a direct function of a state's ability to feed,
pay, or coerce large numbers of oarsmen — tying naval capability to fiscal
and manpower capacity in a way that recurs across every independent warship
tradition covered here, whatever the specific hull design.

## Regional specifics

East Asian and South Asian warship traditions reach comparable purpose-built
military vessel concepts entirely independently of the Mediterranean galley
lineage, on different hull and propulsion technology, showing the underlying
military logic (a dedicated combat vessel outperforms an armed cargo ship) is
close to inevitable wherever naval rivalry exists, not a Mediterranean
invention. The Chola navy's Bay of Bengal expeditions are a striking early
case of oceanic-range force projection well before this becomes routine
elsewhere. Sub-Saharan Africa's comparative absence of a dedicated warship
tradition in this era tracks the Swahili coast's orientation toward trade
network integration (MAR-001) rather than inter-polity naval rivalry, not a
capability gap.

## Hook

A dedicated warship trades cargo capacity for combat capability, converting a
state's fiscal and manpower surplus directly into naval force — the
foundational mechanic every later warship-type entry in this domain (Ship of
the Line, Ironclads, Dreadnought) is a subsequent chapter of.
