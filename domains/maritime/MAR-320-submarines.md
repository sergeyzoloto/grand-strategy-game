A vessel that fights primarily by staying hidden underwater rather than by
armor or speed — the technology that finally makes the Jeune École's
cheap-asymmetric-threat logic (MAR-290) decisively effective at scale, a
generation after the doctrine itself fell out of favor.

## Timing

Practical military submarines mature from around 1900; unrestricted
submarine warfare campaigns in both World Wars (1914-1918 and 1939-1945)
are the type's most strategically significant application.

## Mechanism

A submarine's concealment, rather than armor or speed, is its primary
defense, letting a relatively cheap vessel threaten warships and merchant
shipping alike without needing to survive a direct engagement on
anything like even terms — this is the underlying logic the Jeune École
(MAR-290) argued for prematurely in the 1880s, now viable because torpedo
(MAR-280) and underwater-vessel technology finally caught up to the
strategic insight decades later.

## Social consequence

Unrestricted submarine warfare against merchant shipping deliberately
targets civilian crews and cargo rather than only military vessels,
provoking exactly the kind of neutral-power diplomatic backlash MAR-220
identifies as a recurring cost of aggressive sea-lane strategy — German
unrestricted campaigns in both World Wars are directly implicated in drawing
previously neutral powers into the conflict against the submarine-employing
side.

## Regional specifics

German Atlantic submarine campaigns are the paradigm case of the
technology's strategic potential and its diplomatic cost, in both wars.
Japan's comparative underuse of its own submarine force for systematic
commerce raiding, despite possessing capable vessels, is a doctrinal choice
consistent with its broader fleet-battle-over-trade-protection orientation
(MAR-220) rather than a technology gap — Japan had a capability its own
doctrine underused, at direct cost to itself given its own subsequent
vulnerability to the identical tactic used against it by Allied submarines.

## Hook

The submarine finally cashes in the Jeune École's cheap-asymmetric-threat
bet (MAR-290) once concealment and reliable torpedo technology (MAR-280)
mature enough to support it — the same weapon that provokes severe
diplomatic backlash from neutral powers in direct proportion to how
indiscriminately it's used against merchant shipping.
