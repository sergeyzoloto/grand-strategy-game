Enclosed, lock-gated wet docks that keep water level constant regardless of
tide, letting a vessel load and unload cargo without waiting for or racing
against the tidal cycle — a specific engineering response to a specific
problem (large tidal range) more than a universal port requirement.

## Timing

Enclosed wet-dock construction scales in major European tidal ports (London
especially) from around 1700, driven by tidal-range and increasing vessel
and cargo volume, continuing through the 1800s including colonial-port
construction on the same model.

## Mechanism

A lock-gated wet dock trades substantial fixed construction cost for
eliminating the tidal-window constraint on loading and unloading, directly
increasing a port's cargo throughput capacity per berth — the investment
only pays off where tidal range is large enough, and cargo volume high
enough, to make the constant availability worth the construction and
maintenance cost, which is why it doesn't appear everywhere ports exist.

## Social consequence

Large dock complexes concentrate enormous, seasonally-steady dockworker
labor forces in a small geographic area, creating a distinct urban
waterfront labor class and labor-organizing context (dock strikes recur
across major port cities once this concentrated workforce exists) that
smaller or natural-harbor ports never generate at comparable scale.

## Regional specifics

Colonial-era dock construction in South Asia is built specifically to serve
an export-oriented colonial trade pattern rather than emerging organically
from prior indigenous port development, tying the infrastructure's design
directly to the trade relationship — a case where port infrastructure
itself encodes a specific colonial economic function rather than being a
neutral general-purpose facility. Chinese treaty ports' reliance on simpler
wharf infrastructure and natural anchorages instead of the enclosed wet-dock
model reflects a genuinely different tidal and engineering-cost calculus
rather than a lesser capability — the European solution to the European
tidal problem wasn't the only viable design.

## Hook

Enclosed wet docks convert a fixed tidal-range engineering investment into
sustained cargo-throughput capacity independent of tide timing, concentrating
port-city labor and trade volume in specific harbors — a targeted response
to large tidal range, not a universal infrastructure upgrade every port
needs equally.
