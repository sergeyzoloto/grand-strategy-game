State-licensed private raiding under a letter of marque — legally
distinguished from piracy (MAR-004) only by that license, converting the
same predatory capability into a cheap, off-balance-sheet substitute for
regular naval capacity.

## Timing

Letters of marque are documented from around 1200, in continuous and
expanding use through the major European conflicts of the following six
centuries, until international agreement (the 1856 Declaration of Paris)
formally abolishes the practice among its signatories.

## Mechanism

Privateering lets a state extend its effective naval reach without bearing
the standing cost of a regular navy (MAR-005): private captains and
investors supply the ship, crew, and risk capital in exchange for a share of
captured enemy shipping, while the licensing state supplies only the legal
cover and captures a portion of the prize value — a way to convert existing
private predatory capability into state-aligned military effect at a small
fraction of the fiscal commitment MAR-005's model requires.

## Social consequence

Privateering creates a legitimate, socially respectable path to wealth
through what is otherwise indistinguishable from piracy in practice,
attracting capital and manpower toward state-aligned raiding during wartime
that reverts to ambiguous or outright illegal status the moment the license
lapses or a peace treaty is signed — the same individual can be a celebrated
privateer captain one year and a hunted pirate the next, with no change in
actual conduct.

## Regional specifics

The early United States' heavy reliance on privateering during the
Revolutionary War is a clear case of the mechanic substituting for a regular
navy a state cannot yet afford to build, rather than supplementing an
existing one. The Barbary regencies' corsairing sits in a genuinely
ambiguous zone between MAR-004's piracy and this entry's licensed
privateering for most of its multi-century operation — formally sanctioned
under Ottoman suzerainty, but functioning with enough autonomy that
contemporary treaty partners negotiated with and paid tribute to it as if it
were an independent predatory power rather than a clearly state-controlled
one.

## Hook

A letter of marque converts private raiding capability into state-aligned
naval reach at a fraction of a regular navy's fiscal cost, extracting the
same `protection rent` dynamic as piracy but with legal cover that evaporates
the moment the license or the war itself ends.
