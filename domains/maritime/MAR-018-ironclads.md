Iron or steel armor plating applied to a warship's hull, defeating the
existing generation of naval gunnery (and, in the Korean case, boarding
action) that wooden hulls could no longer reliably survive.

## Timing

Korean turtle ships demonstrate a combat-proven armored warship concept from
1592; the general European ironclad era, driven by explosive shell guns
outclassing wooden hulls, runs from the 1850s (Crimean War floating
batteries, the French Gloire) through the 1870s, by which point armor is a
standard capital-ship feature rather than a novelty.

## Mechanism

Iron armor directly defeats whatever offensive capability drove the previous
generation's naval combat calculus — boarding pikes and small arms in the
Korean case, explosive shell fire in the 19th-century European case —
forcing every existing warship design (MAR-002's warship concept generally,
MAR-010's line-of-battle ships specifically) into sudden obsolescence the
moment a rival power fields even a small number of armored vessels, since a
wooden hull's most valuable asset (large numbers of guns) becomes moot
against a target its shot can't penetrate.

## Social consequence

Ironclad adoption resets naval power rankings essentially overnight for
whichever powers can't quickly match the new capability, since decades of
accumulated wooden line-of-battle strength (MAR-005's standing-navy
investment) loses most of its deterrent and combat value in a single
technological shift — a rare case in this domain where naval power isn't a
slowly-accumulated stock but something a single technology transition can
largely zero out.

## Regional specifics

The Korean turtle ship's two-and-a-half-century head start on the concept of
armored warship hulls is a striking case that rarely factors into popular
"ironclad revolution" narratives centered on the 1860s European and American
ships — the Korean case is real, combat-proven, and decisive in its own
war, but stayed a specific tactical solution rather than generalizing into
a standing doctrine or ship class the way the later European ironclad does.
Ottoman naval modernization's reliance on purchasing rather than
domestically developing ironclad technology illustrates that adopting the
capability and building the industrial base to produce it independently are
separable achievements — a navy can field modern ironclads while remaining
dependent on a foreign industrial supplier for them.

## Hook

Armor plating resets the existing naval-power hierarchy by making the
previous generation's accumulated warship stock (MAR-005, MAR-010) suddenly
and sharply less effective — a technology transition that devalues existing
military capital instead of just adding to it, forcing a rebuild rather than
an upgrade for whichever power can't adopt it quickly.
