Grouping merchant shipping under naval escort — the trade-protection
mirror of MAR-210's line-of-battle formation logic, converting scattered,
individually-routed vessels into a defended formation specifically to
counter submarine commerce raiding (MAR-320).

## Timing

The convoy system is adopted by Britain in 1917 after unrestricted
German submarine warfare threatens to starve Britain's import-dependent
war economy, is largely dismantled between the wars, and is readopted
from the outbreak of World War Two, with the Battle of the Atlantic's
convoy-versus-U-boat contest turning decisively in the Allies' favor by
1943 through a combination of escort numbers, radar, and code-breaking.

## Mechanism

An unescorted merchant vessel sailing independently is a target a
submarine can engage without meaningful risk to itself; grouping many
merchant vessels into a convoy screened by escort warships converts
submarine hunting from picking off isolated targets at will into
attacking a defended formation, trading each individual ship's routing
efficiency — a convoy moves at its slowest member's speed and takes
longer to assemble — for a substantially better aggregate survival rate
across the whole group.

## Social consequence

The convoy system makes sea-lane defense (MAR-220) a continuously
contested numbers game — escort vessel availability, not merchant
tonnage alone, becomes the binding constraint on how much trade a
combatant can safely move, shifting wartime naval procurement priority
toward exactly the cheap, mass-producible escort types (destroyers,
MAR-310, and their smaller wartime variants) that a battleship-centered
navy doctrine might otherwise underweight.

## Regional specifics

The convoy system's initial adoption, abandonment between the wars, and
forced readoption is itself a case of institutional memory failing to
persist despite the underlying threat (submarine commerce raiding)
never having gone away — the doctrinal lesson had to be relearned rather
than simply retained, a smaller-scale version of the standing-capability
maintenance problem MAR-060 describes for regular navies generally.

## Hook

Grouping merchant shipping into escorted convoys converts submarine
hunting (MAR-320) from picking off isolated targets into attacking a
defended formation — the doctrinal countermeasure that makes MAR-220's
sea-lane contest a fight rather than a one-sided slaughter once
submarines mature, at the cost of the convoy's own slower, less flexible
routing.
