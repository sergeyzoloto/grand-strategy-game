A triangular, fore-and-aft rigged sail that can be trimmed much closer to
the wind than a square sail, unlocking the ability to sail across or even
somewhat against the wind rather than only running before it.

## Timing

Attested in the Mediterranean and Middle East from roughly the early first
millennium CE, in continuous and expanding use through the 1400s, when it
becomes a standard component (combined with square sails) on the multi-masted
ships that undertake the first Atlantic and global ocean crossings (MAR-008).

## Mechanism

A square sail is efficient only running downwind, which locks a vessel's
useful routes to wherever the prevailing wind happens to blow; a lateen
rig's fore-and-aft geometry lets a crew sail at a much sharper angle to the
wind, converting a fixed, wind-dictated route map into a much more flexible
one where a vessel can tack its way toward a destination the wind doesn't
directly favor — a direct multiplier on effective `route knowledge`'s value,
since more routes become physically reachable at all.

## Social consequence

The rig's greater flexibility (and, on smaller vessels, capacity for a
smaller crew to handle it relative to a large square sail) shifts economics
toward smaller, more maneuverable vessels able to serve a wider range of
routes and schedules than a fleet built exclusively around large
downwind-dependent square-riggers.

## Regional specifics

Indian Ocean dhow traditions develop lateen-type rigging on a parallel or
possibly independent track from the Mediterranean case, specifically adapted
to the monsoon-reversal sailing pattern, rather than as a simple copy of a
Mediterranean invention. East Asian shipbuilding instead solves the same
close-hauled-sailing problem with an entirely different technology (the
balanced lug and batten sail used on junks), achieving comparable windward
performance through convergent function rather than shared technique — a
clean case of two independent solutions to the same physical constraint.

## Contested

Whether the lateen rig originated in the Mediterranean and spread eastward,
or originated in the Indian Ocean/Arab world and spread into the
Mediterranean, is genuinely unresolved given sparse and ambiguous early
evidence. Model the innovation as emerging from a connected Mediterranean/
Middle Eastern maritime zone rather than committing the timeline to either a
Mediterranean-origin or Arab-origin scripted fact.

## Hook

The lateen rig converts a wind-direction-locked route map into a much more
flexible one, directly expanding the set of destinations reachable from any
given port — a `route knowledge` multiplier that becomes a required
component (alongside square rigging) once true ocean-crossing voyages
(MAR-008) demand handling a much wider range of wind conditions on a single
long passage.
