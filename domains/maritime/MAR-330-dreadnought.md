HMS Dreadnought's 1906 launch — an all-big-gun, turbine-powered battleship
so far ahead of the existing pre-dreadnought standard (MAR-300) that it
makes every previous battleship in every navy, including Britain's own,
instantly obsolete at a stroke.

## Timing

Dreadnought launches in 1906; the resulting international naval arms race,
most intensely contested between Britain and Germany, runs through the
outbreak of World War One in 1914.

## Mechanism

By combining uniform heavy-caliber main guns (rather than the mixed
calibers of pre-dreadnought designs) with turbine propulsion for superior
speed, Dreadnought resets the battleship-count status metric MAR-300
describes to zero for every navy simultaneously, including Britain's own —
a single ship design decision that erases the accumulated pre-dreadnought
fleet advantage Britain had spent decades building, forcing every naval
power (including Britain itself) to restart the battleship-count race from
a clean slate on the new standard.

## Social consequence

The resulting Anglo-German dreadnought arms race becomes a major driver of
prewar diplomatic tension and domestic political mobilization in both
countries (naval spending campaigns, public "dreadnought fever"), directly
illustrating MAR-300's point about battleships functioning as much as a
diplomatic and domestic-political instrument as a military one — the race
itself, independent of any actual battle, reshapes European alliance
anxiety in the years leading to 1914.

## Regional specifics

Japan's immediate entry into the dreadnought race, leveraging its recent
Tsushima-earned naval prestige (MAR-300) rather than being left behind by
the standard reset, shows a power can convert one status gain directly into
continued competitiveness on a completely new technological basis. Africa's
total exclusion from the race is the same structural absence noted in
MAR-300, unchanged by the standard's reset since the underlying industrial-
base gap the reset didn't touch.

## Contested

Whether Dreadnought was a strategically necessary response to naval
technology trends already converging elsewhere, or a needless British
self-inflicted reset of a race Britain was already winning under the old
standard, is disputed. Model the underlying all-big-gun technology as an
inevitable adoption by some major power regardless of British timing, while
treating the specific decision to build and dramatically publicize
Dreadnought as a separate, modelable choice carrying its own arms-race-
acceleration and diplomatic consequence, distinct from the technology's own
independent trajectory.

## Hook

A single capital-ship design leap can reset an entire naval-status metric
(MAR-300) to zero for every power simultaneously, including whoever built
it — converting accumulated fleet advantage into a fresh, all-parties-equal
arms race whose diplomatic tension (feeding DIP) accumulates independent of
any actual naval engagement occurring at all.
