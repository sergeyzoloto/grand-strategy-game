A small, fast warship originally built to counter torpedo boats (hence
"torpedo boat destroyer"), which rapidly absorbs the torpedo-armed
fast-attack role itself and becomes an essential multi-purpose screening
vessel for the battle fleet it was first designed to defend.

## Timing

Develops from 1893 specifically as a response to the torpedo-boat threat
(MAR-023) that partly motivated the Jeune École (MAR-019); the type matures
into an essential fleet component by the Russo-Japanese War (1904-1905) and
becomes indispensable in both world wars through 1945.

## Mechanism

The destroyer resolves the Jeune École's cheap-torpedo-flotilla challenge
(MAR-019) not by rejecting torpedo-armed fast attack craft but by absorbing
the concept into the conventional battle fleet as a screening and escort
role — a fast, cheap vessel that both defends the expensive capital ships
against enemy torpedo boats and carries torpedoes itself, converting what
Jeune École doctrine framed as a threat to battleship dominance into a
supporting component of it instead.

## Social consequence

The destroyer's relatively low cost and high production volume (compared to
capital ships) makes it the workhorse vessel type most navies can afford in
real numbers, producing a much larger and more broadly experienced junior
officer corps trained on destroyers than the much smaller battleship fleet
ever could, with lasting effects on naval career paths and promotion
patterns.

## Regional specifics

Japan's integration of torpedo-boat destroyers directly into its
conventional battle-fleet doctrine at Tsushima — screening the battle line
and delivering finishing torpedo attacks on already-damaged Russian ships —
demonstrates the type's absorption into mainstream fleet doctrine rather
than remaining a Jeune-École-style alternative to it, consistent with
Japan's broader rejection of that doctrine (MAR-019). The complete absence
of the type among African naval forces in this era reflects the broader
absence of any independent blue-water naval capability on the continent at
this point, not a rejection of the destroyer concept specifically.

## Hook

The destroyer converts the Jeune École's cheap-torpedo-threat logic
(MAR-019) into a supporting fleet role rather than a battleship-replacing
one — a resolution of that doctrinal argument through absorption rather than
victory for either original side, and the type every subsequent navy builds
in the largest numbers precisely because it's cheap enough to.
