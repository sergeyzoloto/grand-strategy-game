A ship built to launch, recover, and support aircraft at sea, which
displaces the battleship (MAR-300, MAR-330) as the capital ship whose
presence or absence actually decides a fleet action, by extending a fleet's
effective striking range far beyond gun range.

## Timing

Naval aviation experiments begin in the 1910s; the first purpose-built
carrier (Japan's Hosho) commissions in 1922, with carrier doctrine and
design maturing rapidly through the interwar period to become the decisive
capital ship type of the Pacific War by 1941-1945.

## Mechanism

A carrier's aircraft can locate, attack, and sink an enemy fleet from a
distance no battleship gun can match, converting naval combat's decisive
range from a function of gun caliber (the entire pre-dreadnought and
dreadnought lineage's core metric, MAR-300/MAR-330) to a function of aircraft
range and carrier air-group quality instead — a capital-ship-defining
metric change as sharp as ironclad armor's reset of wooden-hull naval power
(MAR-200), arriving roughly eighty years later.

## Social consequence

Carrier operations require an entirely new professional specialization
(naval aviators, deck crews, air-group commanders) layered onto the existing
naval officer hierarchy, creating fresh career-advancement pathways and
institutional rivalries between "big-gun" battleship admirals and the newer
carrier-aviation officer corps within the same navy — a doctrinal
transition fought as much internally within each navy's own command
structure as against any external rival.

## Regional specifics

Japan's early lead in purpose-built carrier design and, especially, in
carrier aviator training and doctrine going into the Pacific War is a case
where a non-Western power holds outright technological and doctrinal
leadership in a decisive weapons category — Pearl Harbor and the early
Pacific campaign are direct demonstrations of that leadership, not a
surprising anomaly. Britain's failure to convert its genuine early
naval-aviation pioneering into sustained interwar institutional priority,
entering the war with a comparatively smaller and less doctrinally central
carrier force than Japan or the United States, shows an early technological
lead doesn't automatically translate into sustained doctrinal investment —
the same gap MAR-060 identifies in the standing-navy context recurring here
for a specific ship type instead of an entire institution.

## Hook

The aircraft carrier resets the naval-power decisive-range metric from gun
caliber (MAR-300, MAR-330) to aircraft range and air-group quality, a
capital-ship-defining reset on the same scale as ironclad armor's reset of
wooden-hull dominance (MAR-200) — and, per the Japanese case, one a
non-Western power can hold outright first-mover advantage in.
