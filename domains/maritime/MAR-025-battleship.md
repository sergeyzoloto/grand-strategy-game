The "pre-dreadnought" battleship — the settled, mature ironclad capital ship
type (heavy but mixed-caliber armament, iron then steel armor, steam
propulsion) that becomes the mid-to-late 19th century's definitive measure
of great-power naval, and by extension general great-power, status.

## Timing

The type matures from roughly 1870 as ironclad design settles into a
standard capital-ship configuration, remaining the dominant naval-power
metric through the Russo-Japanese War (1904-1905) before the 1906 Dreadnought
(MAR-026) resets the standard again.

## Mechanism

A battleship fleet's size and quality become the single most legible,
internationally comparable proxy for a state's overall industrial and
naval capacity, converting shipbuilding output directly into a diplomatically
and strategically legible power ranking — a mechanism that in turn drives
naval arms-race dynamics, since a rival's new battleship class is both a
military threat and a status challenge simultaneously.

## Social consequence

Battleship-building programs absorb an enormous, visible share of state
fiscal capacity specifically because their value is partly performative — a
battleship signals great-power status to domestic and foreign audiences
alike in a way that's harder for less visible military investments to
match, making battleship fleets as much a diplomatic instrument (feeding
directly into DIP's territory) as a purely military one.

## Regional specifics

Japan's Tsushima victory is the era's clearest case of the battleship
functioning exactly as this status-signaling mechanic predicts: a single
decisive battle immediately and durably changes how every other great power
treats Japan's subsequent naval decisions, a direct, legible status
promotion earned through battleship-fleet performance. Africa's total
absence from battleship-power status in this era reflects an industrial-base
gap more absolute than the purchase-versus-develop distinction seen in
MAR-018's Ottoman case — building or even meaningfully operating a battleship
fleet requires an industrial and fiscal base no African polity possessed in
this period.

## Hook

Battleship fleet size and quality function as the era's most legible,
internationally-tracked great-power status metric, converting industrial
and fiscal capacity into a directly comparable naval-power ranking — a
metric that both drives and is driven by arms-race dynamics, since a rival's
new-class battleship simultaneously threatens militarily and demotes
status diplomatically.
