Paradox is universal. Crisis is not. A contradiction becomes a crisis only
in a tradition whose authority rests on derivation from a single axiomatic
base, because only there can one flaw at the foundation reach every result
built on it.

## Timing

From Cantor's set theory in 1874, through the paradoxes of the 1890s and
Russell's letter to Frege in 1902, to Zermelo's axiomatisation in 1908,
Brouwer's intuitionism, and the first volume of *Principia Mathematica* in
1910. The same mechanism operates in Greek mathematics around -400 and is
pursued without crisis in the Islamic world from 1077.

## Mechanism

**Proof-based knowledge has a failure mode that observational knowledge
does not.** A natural science absorbs an anomaly locally. A surprising
measurement revises one theory and leaves the rest standing, and knowledge
degrades gracefully. An axiomatic system cannot do this. In classical logic
a contradiction implies every statement, so a contradiction derivable at
the foundation does not damage one result — it makes the whole structure
prove anything, which is the same as proving nothing. **The very property
that makes proof permanent makes it brittle at the base.**

That is why the crisis is conditional rather than universal, and the entry
has to be built that way. Paradoxes of the infinite, of division, and of
identity appear in every mathematical and philosophical tradition this
record covers. They become a crisis only where two conditions hold together
— mathematics claims its authority from derivation, and the whole of it has
been made to rest on one foundation. Remove either condition and the
paradox is a puzzle, not a threat.

**The European case is the one where both conditions were maximised.**
Through the nineteenth century analysis was rebuilt on set theory
specifically in order to make it rigorous, which meant that by 1900
everything rested on one base. Russell's paradox then showed that the most
natural principle of that base — any definable collection forms a set —
derives a contradiction. The rigorisation had created the vulnerability.

**The responses branch, and they should be modelled as branches.**
Logicism (Frege, then Russell and Whitehead's *Principia*) tried to rebuild
mathematics on logic with the paradox excluded. Formalism (Hilbert) proposed
to prove, by finite means, that the axioms could never produce a
contradiction — the programme MTH-080 records being answered in the
negative. Intuitionism (Brouwer) rejected the offending forms of reasoning
about the completed infinite and accepted the loss of classical results.
Axiomatic set theory (Zermelo) restricted set formation and carried on. No
one of them won outright, and the working compromise was the last.

**A crisis of this kind is productive.** It forces a field to state its
foundations precisely enough to be examined, and a foundation stated that
precisely can be proved inadequate. The crisis is what made the question
Hilbert posed well defined, and a well-defined question is what MTH-080's
negative answers required.

## Social consequence

The cost fell on individuals in a way that is unusually well documented.
Frege received Russell's letter as the second volume of his life's work was
being printed, and appended an acknowledgement that its foundation had
collapsed. Cantor spent years under attack from Kronecker and others who
regarded his transfinite numbers as illegitimate, in a dispute that was
professional, personal and conducted through control of journals and
appointments. Foundational disputes are fought over authority, and people's
careers are the stakes.

For the field the effect was to split mathematicians into those for whom
the foundations were a live problem and those for whom they were not, and
the second group was far larger. Hilbert's declaration that no one would
expel mathematicians from the paradise Cantor had created is a statement
about authority rather than logic — a refusal to accept a repair that cost
too much of the existing structure — and it is the position most working
mathematicians effectively took.

## Regional specifics

The cases are comparative rather than a lineage with precursors, and the
comparison is the entry's argument. Greek mathematics met the mechanism
first, when incommensurability broke the assumption of common measure at
the base of geometry, and Eudoxus rebuilt the foundation. Khayyam and
al-Tusi doubted Euclid's parallel postulate with full rigour and proved
results that belong to non-Euclidean geometry, without crisis, because no
one had made mathematics as a whole rest on that postulate. Their work
reached Europe through Saccheri and underlies Lobachevsky and Bolyai, and
non-Euclidean geometry then waited nearly a century to become the geometry
of relativity (NAT-220). That is deferred applicability in its clearest
form — a doubt about an axiom, pursued rigorously in eleventh-century
Persia, supplying the mathematics of a physical theory in 1915.

The absences carry the conditional. Chinese thought produced paradoxes of
the infinite as sharp as Zeno's and no crisis, because its mathematics
derived authority from procedures shown correct case by case (MTH-020).
The Kerala school worked with infinite series and established each result
by its own demonstration, so there was nothing for a paradox to undermine —
while European calculus, working with comparable infinite processes, drew
Berkeley's attack because it claimed a single foundation. The two
traditions that avoided crisis were not less rigorous. They distributed
their rigour differently, and that distribution is exactly the variable.

## Contested

Whether *crisis* describes mathematics or only a foundations community is
disputed. Most working mathematicians were unaffected and analysis,
number theory and applied mathematics went on producing results
throughout. Against that, the foundations community set the next
half-century's agenda, including the programme whose failure produced
computability theory.

Model it as branching by audience — near-zero effect on the output of
working mathematics, large effect on the research agenda and on the
authority of foundations as a field. The Hippasus story is legend and
should carry only as the tradition's own account of the incommensurability
discovery.

## Hook

A failure mode unique to proof-based knowledge. A contradiction at an
axiomatic foundation propagates to every result built on it, where an
anomaly in observational knowledge stays local, so the permanence of proof
and its brittleness at the base are the same property. It should trigger
only when two conditions hold together — authority grounded in derivation,
and the whole body of results made to rest on one foundation — which means
a tradition that distributes justification across case-by-case
demonstration, as Chinese procedural mathematics and the Kerala school did,
meets the same paradoxes and suffers no crisis. Rigorisation should
therefore raise vulnerability as it raises certainty. Resolution branches
rather than resolves, among rebuilding on logic, proving consistency,
rejecting the problematic reasoning, or restricting the foundation and
carrying on. And the crisis should pay out as a research agenda — a
foundation stated precisely enough to fail is precisely enough to be
examined, which is what makes the negative results of MTH-080 possible.
It should cost `intellectual authority` for foundations as a field while
leaving the output of working mathematics nearly untouched.
