Before machines, computation was a job — and it was organised as a
factory. This entry is MTH-020's delegable procedure actually delegated, at
industrial scale, to people who did not need to understand it.

Placement is MTH under the boundary rule's computation clause. COM owns the
division of labour as a general institution (COM-170, whose Smithian
model Prony cited by name); labour organised specifically to perform computation is MTH,
because the thing being organised is procedural delegation.

## Timing

From the British Nautical Almanac's distributed computers in 1767 and
Prony's tables in the 1790s, through the Survey of India's computing office,
the Harvard computers from 1881, the WPA Mathematical Tables Project from
1938, and the computing pools at NACA and JPL, to the displacement of human
computing by electronic machines by about 1970.

## Mechanism

**MTH-020 makes calculation delegable; this entry organises the
delegation.** A procedure written precisely enough can be followed by
someone who does not understand it, and once that is true, calculation can
be divided the way pin-making was. Prony did it explicitly and cited Smith.
A few mathematicians chose the formulae. A small group of skilled
calculators reduced them to step-by-step procedures. A large bottom tier
performed only addition and subtraction and needed no mathematical training
at all. The procedure carried the expertise and the tier supplied accuracy
and volume.

The consequence is that **computation scales by hiring rather than by
training**, which is `execution delegability` realised as an institution.
Output is set by headcount, and quality is maintained by redundancy rather
than by skill — two computers working the same values independently, with a
comparator reconciling them, which is the Nautical Almanac's method and the
ancestor of every later error-checking scheme.

**This entry makes later mechanisation legible as substitution, not
invention.** When calculating machines and then electronic computers arrive,
they replace a job that already existed in precisely specified form — which
is why the early electronic machines were literally called computers, after
the people whose work they took. The skill of a computing pool is maximally
specific, so its displacement is `stranded capital` at its sharpest: a
workforce whose single competence becomes worth nothing within a few years,
on the machine's timescale rather than its own.

**Cheap computing labour also competes with the machine.** `labour-saving
incentive` runs here the way it runs for coerced labour in WAR-035: where a
skilled human workforce is cheap and fast, the incentive to mechanise is
weak, and mechanisation arrives late. The soroban offices of East Asia are
the clearest case.

## Social consequence

**The gendering is the mechanism, not colour.** The work was substantially
female and low-paid because it had been deskilled — classified as execution
rather than expertise — and MTH-020's social consequence, that expertise
separates from execution and the two acquire different social positions,
appears here as a workforce. Women were hired at a fraction of male wages
for work defined as clerical, and the definition was what made the wage
defensible.

The boundary did not hold, and the entry should carry the evidence that it
did not. Harvard's computers were assigned to classify stellar spectra and
produced results that reorganised astronomy — Cannon's classification
scheme, Leavitt's period-luminosity relation — from inside a role that
officially made no such contribution. Sikdar at the Survey of India
computed the survey's most famous figure. NACA's West Area Computers did
mathematics that aerospace programmes depended on under a double
classification, as clerical and as segregated. The role said execution; the
people in it frequently did more, and the institution was structured so
that it did not have to recognise it.

When electronic machines arrived, the first programmers were drawn from
exactly these workforces, because programming was initially classified as
clerical too. As programming acquired status it was redefined as
engineering and its workforce changed. The same work was feminised while it
was called execution and masculinised once it was called expertise.

## Regional specifics

The institution follows demand for tables, and that demand concentrated
where navies, surveys, insurers and artillery bureaucracies were
administered from — which is why the Atlantic cases are numerous, and why
the entry corrects for that rather than presenting it as the pattern. The
Survey of India's computing office was among the largest calculation
operations of the nineteenth century and ran in Calcutta and Dehradun.
Japanese soroban offices ran the same institution on a different
instrument, fast enough to beat an electric calculator in 1946, which made
the human workforce a competitor to mechanisation rather than a stage
before it.

The absences confirm the demand logic. African survey and census
computation was done in metropolitan offices, so no computing workforce
formed where the territory was measured. The steppe had no buyer for tables
of any kind.

## Contested

The hairdressers are the period's account of itself and should be carried
as such. Whether cheap computing labour delayed mechanisation is plausible
and unproven, and should be a conditional on relative wage and machine
cost. The direction of causation in the gendering is disputed — feminised
because deskilled, or classified as deskilled because feminised — and the
later reclassification of programming suggests it runs both ways. Model
classification and workforce composition as mutually reinforcing.

## Hook

Converts `execution delegability` into an institution: computation scales
by hiring rather than training, with output set by headcount and quality
held by redundancy — independent computers and a comparator — rather than
by skill. Demand for tables decides where it forms, so the institution
appears where navies, surveys and insurers are administered from and not
where the measured territory lies. A cheap skilled workforce should weaken
`labour-saving incentive` and delay mechanisation, and its displacement,
when machines win, should be `stranded capital` at its sharpest because the
competence is single-purpose. And classification should drive composition
— work defined as execution is paid and staffed as execution, whatever the
people in it actually produce — so the same task is recruited, paid and
recognised differently once it is renamed as expertise.
