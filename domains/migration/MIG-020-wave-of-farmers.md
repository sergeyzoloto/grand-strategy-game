Farming spreads geographically slower than any single farmer would choose to
walk — a genuine population-wave process (the "wave of advance" model) driven
by local population growth pushing a frontier outward roughly a kilometer a
year, not migration in the sense of anyone making a long journey.

## Timing

From the Fertile Crescent core outward from roughly -9000, reaching Central
Europe by around -5500 and the British Isles and Scandinavia by around -4000;
independent East Asian (from -7000) and African (from -3000) waves run on
unrelated timelines.

## Mechanism

Farming supports higher local population density than foraging, so a farming
population at its ecological carrying capacity generates a small, constant
surplus of people who settle just beyond the existing frontier — repeated over
many generations, this produces a slow, steady geographic wave with no single
long-distance migration event anywhere in it, an emergent macro-pattern from
purely local, short-range decisions.

## Social consequence

Wherever the wave passes through existing forager populations, some mix of
replacement, absorption, and cultural adoption occurs, and the mix itself
varies by region for reasons not fully understood — the frontier zone is where
farmer and forager social systems actually meet and negotiate that mix,
producing very different regional outcomes from what looks superficially like
the same process.

## Regional specifics

East Asian and African farming waves reproduce a comparable demic-diffusion
mechanism independently, on different crops, different timelines, and
different terrain — the Bantu expansion in particular runs over a much longer
span, plausibly because farming, ironworking, and disease-ecology adaptation
all had to advance together through highly varied environments. South Asia's
apparently larger share of cultural-diffusion (adoption without much
replacement) relative to the classic Anatolian-into-Europe case shows the two
mechanisms — demic and cultural diffusion — mix in different ratios by region
rather than one always dominating.

## Contested

The demic-versus-cultural-diffusion question is actively being revised by
ancient-DNA studies region by region, with some areas (much of Europe)
showing strong evidence for substantial population replacement and others
(parts of South Asia) showing more adoption with less replacement. Model the
wave's advance as driven by a blend of both mechanisms with a per-region
mixing ratio, rather than requiring the whole domain to commit to one
explanation.

## Hook

Farming's local population-density advantage generates a slow, self-propelled
geographic wave (`migration range` expanding by small increments every
generation, with no single migration event) — a mechanic that runs on
population growth pressure alone, not on any triggered movement decision.
