The campaign and political conquest are WAR's; this entry owns a distinct
migration mechanic the conquest enables — a very small, mobile founding
population entirely replacing an existing landholding elite while leaving the
underlying general population largely in place, structurally unlike either
folk migration or frontier settlement.

## Timing

From the 1066 conquest itself through the roughly two decades it takes for
the new Norman aristocracy to fully displace the prior English landholding
class, documented in detail by the 1086 Domesday survey.

## Mechanism

Elite-replacement migration requires only a small founding population —
enough armed retainers and administrators to physically hold key fortified
points (castles) and enforce new land grants — because the mechanism of
control is legal and military dominance over an existing administrative
system, not demographic weight or agricultural resettlement. This is a
sharply lower `settler footprint` than frontier colonization (MIG-005)
achieves a comparable or greater degree of political control with.

## Social consequence

The general population experiences a change of landlord, language of
administration, and law at the top of the social structure while continuing
much of its prior daily life and land-working arrangements underneath it — a
narrower, more concentrated social rupture than mass folk migration produces,
felt most acutely by exactly the prior elite being displaced and least by the
peasantry whose obligations shift target more than form.

## Regional specifics

The Domesday survey provides an unusually precise documentary snapshot of
just how total the elite replacement was (by landholding value, the
displacement of English lords is close to complete within twenty years)
alongside genetic evidence showing comparatively modest general-population
change — making this one of the best-documented instances anywhere in this
record of the elite-replacement migration type as mechanically distinct from
mass demographic replacement.

## Hook

Elite-replacement migration achieves durable political and legal control
with a very low `settler footprint` by capturing an existing administrative
system rather than demographically outnumbering or displacing the population
under it — a structurally cheaper, faster conquest-migration type than either
frontier settlement or mass folk migration, at the cost of depending entirely
on holding the captured administrative and military chokepoints.
