The domesticated animal and its use as a food/pack-transport source are
AGR's and COM's; this entry owns what a camel's specific physiology does
to how far, and across what kind of terrain, a population or trade
caravan can move — the desert-mobility parallel this domain's
steppe-mobility entry (MIG-050) has and currently lacks.

## Timing

The North Arabian saddle, letting a rider or heavy load be carried
effectively astride a camel rather than only on its back as pack cargo,
is in wide use by roughly -1000, converting the camel from a useful pack
animal into the basis of a full caravan mobility system; trans-Saharan
caravan crossing is attested from the early centuries CE and scales
substantially from around 800, remaining the dominant desert-crossing
mobility technology through 1500.

## Mechanism

A camel can travel several days between water sources and carry a heavy
load across true desert terrain — sustained heat, minimal forage,
multi-day waterless stretches — that no horse, ox, or human porter makes
economically survivable at comparable scale, converting the Sahara and
the Arabian interior from a near-impassable barrier into a repeatable, if
still genuinely dangerous, migration and trade corridor. This is the same
category of mobility-threshold unlock MIG-050 covers for the horse and
open steppe, applied to a structurally different terrain constraint:
water scarcity over distance rather than raw open-ground speed.

## Social consequence

Societies and merchant communities that master camel-caravan logistics
gain reliable access to markets and population centers a
non-desert-adapted mobility technology simply cannot reach at all, not
just reaches more slowly — converting the desert from a hard boundary
between regions into a controlled, toll-collecting corridor whoever
masters the logistics (watering points, guide knowledge, caravan-scale
risk-pooling) can profit from and defend.

## Regional specifics

Trans-Saharan caravan crossing is the specific mobility precondition the
Sahelian and West African diaspora trade networks (MIG-070) depend on, a
link this record's steppe-migration entries already draw explicitly
(MIG-120, MIG-110, MIG-130 all prereq MIG-050/MIG-040) but which the
desert case has lacked an equivalent foundational entry to supply. South
Asia's reliance on a mixed pack-animal tradition rather than a dedicated
deep-desert camel corridor reflects a genuinely different geography —
fewer comparably extreme, multi-week waterless crossings to solve for —
rather than any lesser adoption of the same technology where it would
actually help.

## Hook

A gate structurally parallel to MIG-050's horse but keyed to
water-scarcity-over-distance rather than open-terrain speed — gating
trans-Saharan and Arabian population and trade movement (including
MIG-070's Sahelian diaspora networks) the way the horse gates steppe
movement, and converting the desert from a hard barrier into a
controlled corridor for whoever masters the logistics.
