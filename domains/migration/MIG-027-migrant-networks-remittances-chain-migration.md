Two faces of one self-reinforcing network effect this domain otherwise
leaves unmodeled: a migrant sends earnings back to an origin household on
an ongoing basis, and the same migrant's presence at a destination lowers
the risk and cost of the next migrant's journey from the same village or
kin network — a standing tie between mover and stayer, not just a
one-time transport or settlement cost.

## Timing

Informal remittance-sending accompanies long-distance labor migration
throughout this record's history, but banked and wired remittance flows
at trackable scale develop alongside the mass-emigration era (MIG-015)
from the 1850s onward, becoming a major, sustained component of
sending-economy income by the late 20th century, with Gulf labor
migration (MIG-022) driving a further sharp scale-up from 1973.

## Mechanism

A migrant who has successfully settled and found work at a destination
routinely sends a portion of earnings back to family at the point of
origin, creating a durable income tie between migrant and origin
household independent of whether the migrant ever returns — and the same
migrant's presence, contacts, and accumulated local knowledge at the
destination substantially lower the risk, cost, and uncertainty of the
next migrant's journey from the same village or kin network, since a new
arrival has somewhere to stay, someone to vouch for them, and direct
information about where work actually is. These two flows compound:
remittance income can finance the next departure directly, and the
network effect concentrates successive waves of migration from the same
specific origin communities into the same specific destinations,
producing durable migration corridors between oddly specific place-pairs
that a purely wage-differential model (MIG-016) wouldn't predict.

## Social consequence

For a sending economy, aggregate remittance income can exceed formal
foreign aid or foreign direct investment, a transfer of resources that
moves entirely outside ordinary trade or investment accounting and is
directed by individual household ties rather than any state or
institutional allocation decision — a form of economic dependency on
migration that neither the sending nor the receiving state's official
statistics were originally built to track.

## Regional specifics

Gulf labor migrant remittances become one of the largest single
foreign-currency inflows for several major South and Southeast Asian
sending economies, at a scale exceeding both foreign investment and
official aid in some years — a direct, measurable case of the
household-to-household tie this entry tracks aggregating into genuine
macroeconomic significance. Sub-Saharan Africa's comparatively smaller
formal remittance volume in this era, despite substantial
intra-continental labor migration, reflects weaker wage differentials and
banking infrastructure on the dominant routes rather than the network
mechanic itself being absent.

## Hook

A standing income-and-information flow between migrant and origin
household that funds and directs further migration — the closest thing
to a genuine building pool (design principle 4, run in reverse) this
domain has, concentrating successive migration waves into specific
place-pairs a wage-differential model alone wouldn't predict, and moving
resources at a scale that can exceed formal foreign aid to the sending
economy.
