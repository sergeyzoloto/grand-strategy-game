A sudden, verified resource discovery triggers an extremely fast, largely
unplanned migration spike far exceeding what steady economic opportunity of
the same eventual magnitude would produce — speed and volatility, not just
scale, distinguish this from ordinary frontier settlement.

## Timing

California (from 1848), Australia (from 1851), and the Witwatersrand in
South Africa (from 1886) are the paradigm 19th-century cases, each drawing
in a large migrant population within a few years of the triggering discovery.

## Mechanism

A verified, individually-accessible resource discovery (alluvial gold
findable by an individual prospector with minimal capital, at least in the
opening phase) triggers migration at a speed and volume normal economic
opportunity never matches, because the payoff is framed as unusually large
and immediately achievable rather than a steady, comparable-to-others wage —
a genuinely different trigger type from the steady land-hunger driving
frontier colonization (MIG-090), even though the destination and settler
demographic often overlap heavily.

## Social consequence

Gold-rush boomtowns produce an extreme, compressed version of frontier
social conditions — a young, mostly male, transient, high-risk-tolerant
population arriving faster than any governing institution can be built to
manage it, producing a distinctive period of weak formal governance before
either the resource depletes (and the population disperses) or the
extraction industry consolidates into large-scale capitalized mining (and
the individual-prospector social structure is displaced by a wage-labor one).

## Regional specifics

The Witwatersrand case converts unusually fast from the individual-prospector
phase into an entrenched, heavily capitalized deep-mining industry with a
racially stratified migrant labor system, a faster and more consequential
institutional lock-in than the California or Australian cases saw at
comparable points after their own discoveries. Chinese migrant labor's
presence across nearly every major rush alongside escalating legal exclusion
from independent claims and eventually from immigration itself shows the
same resource-rush pull operating on different migrant populations under
sharply different, and worsening, legal tracks within the same rush.

## Hook

A verified individually-accessible resource discovery spikes `migration
trigger intensity` far above steady-opportunity migration levels, producing
a fast-forming, weakly-governed boomtown population that either disperses on
depletion or gets displaced by capitalized industry — with migrant legal
status (as with Chinese exclusion) able to diverge sharply within the same
rush rather than applying uniformly to everyone the trigger pulled in.
