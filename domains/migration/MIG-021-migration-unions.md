The direct inverse of MIG-017's border-friction mechanic: a multilateral
treaty among states mutually agrees to remove passport/visa friction between
their own citizens, converting several previously separate migration spaces
into one for purposes of movement (though not necessarily for full legal or
labor-market equality).

## Timing

The Nordic Passport Union (1954) is an early case; the European Economic
Community's later free-movement-of-workers provisions and the Schengen
Agreement (from 1985) extend and generalize the same mechanic across a much
larger and more diverse group of states; ECOWAS's protocol (1979) applies a
comparable instrument in West Africa.

## Mechanism

A free-movement union is a treaty-level reversal of the border-friction
mechanic in MIG-017: member states mutually recognize each other's
documentation and agree not to apply entry restriction to each other's
citizens, which functionally re-merges separate migration spaces into one
for movement purposes — the underlying administrative capability
(documentation, border control) doesn't disappear, it's just contractually
not applied between the treaty's members.

## Social consequence

Removing border friction between member states doesn't automatically
equalize labor-market access, social benefits, or social integration —
those typically require separate, additional legal harmonization — so a
migration union can produce a real jump in cross-border movement volume well
ahead of, and sometimes without ever fully reaching, comparable social and
economic integration between the populations now able to move freely.

## Regional specifics

The European cases (Nordic, then EEC/EU, then Schengen) build up
progressively more comprehensive integration over decades, each stage adding
movement rights, labor-market access, or border-control abolition somewhat
separately rather than all at once. ECOWAS's protocol establishes comparable
formal movement rights with substantially weaker practical uptake, plausibly
reflecting uneven administrative capacity among member states to actually
implement passport-free crossing rather than any difference in the treaty's
stated commitments. South Asia's large actual labor-migration flows without
any comparable regional free-movement treaty shows the treaty instrument and
the real movement volume are genuinely separable — a region can have
substantial migration without formal free movement, and (per ECOWAS) can have
the formal agreement without a comparable migration volume actually following.

## Hook

A multilateral free-movement treaty is a deliberate, contractual removal of
`border friction` (the mirror image of MIG-017) between specific member
states only — it doesn't reduce the underlying border-control capability
itself, and its practical effect on actual migration volume depends on
member states' administrative follow-through, not just the treaty's text.
