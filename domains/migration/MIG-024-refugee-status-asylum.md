A legal category distinct from ordinary voluntary economic migration: a
claim to protection triggered by persecution or violence rather than by
any destination's ordinary immigration quota or economic criteria, and —
increasingly across this entry's window — administered partly through
international institutional machinery rather than by destination-state
discretion alone.

## Timing

The League of Nations creates the Nansen passport (1921) for stateless
persons displaced by the Russian Revolution and Armenian Genocide, an
early precedent for internationally-recognized refugee documentation;
the 1951 Refugee Convention and UNHCR (established 1950) generalize the
framework, initially limited to European post-war displacement before
its 1967 Protocol removes that geographic and temporal limit; Partition
(MIG-018) predates this framework, while the Bangladesh crisis (1971) and
ongoing decolonization-era displacement test the mature version of it
through 1980.

## Mechanism

An asylum claim adds a track to migration control genuinely different
from either MIG-017's ordinary documentation gate or MIG-018's
state-driven forced movement: rather than a state screening a migrant for
economic or quota criteria, or itself producing the displacement, a
person fleeing persecution asserts a legal claim (non-refoulement — not
being returned to danger) that in principle doesn't depend on the
destination state's usual admission criteria at all, with an
international institution (UNHCR) increasingly involved in registering,
assisting, and in some cases resettling the displaced population rather
than the matter being handled purely bilaterally.

## Social consequence

The refugee category creates a legal population whose claim to remain
doesn't rest on the same grounds as an ordinary migrant's — provoking a
durable, still-live political tension between a state's formal
humanitarian commitment (embodied in treaty ratification) and its
practical willingness to actually admit large numbers of people fleeing a
specific crisis, a gap that recurs across nearly every major
refugee-producing event covered elsewhere in this record.

## Regional specifics

Palestinian refugees being administered under a wholly separate UN
mechanism from the general UNHCR framework, from as early as 1949, shows
the international refugee-institution landscape fragmenting by specific
political circumstance rather than converging on one uniform mechanism,
even as the general framework matures elsewhere. The Bangladesh
Liberation War's roughly ten-million-person displacement into India tests
the young international framework against a Partition-scale event, but
this time explicitly framed and administered as a refugee crisis under
the developing international machinery rather than as a bilateral
population exchange. The United States' comparatively narrow,
foreign-policy-aligned refugee admissions through most of this window,
well short of a general asylum standard until 1980, shows domestic asylum
law and international refugee-institution development can run on
separate, only loosely-connected tracks even within the same era.

## Contested

Where the line falls between a "genuine" persecution-based refugee and an
economic migrant using an asylum claim strategically is a live,
politically contested question in every era this entry covers, not only
the present, and the practical answer has always depended heavily on the
destination state's own foreign-policy alignment and institutional
capacity rather than a fixed, consistently-applied legal test. Model
recognition outcomes as branching on host-state institutional capacity
and political climate at the time of the claim, rather than scripting a
uniform, purely legal-merits-based outcome.

## Hook

A gate parallel to MIG-017's ordinary passport/visa gate but triggered by
persecution rather than economic calculus, and partly administered by an
international institution rather than the destination state alone — with
actual recognition outcomes branching on host-state institutional
capacity and political alignment rather than resolving the same way for
every claim.
