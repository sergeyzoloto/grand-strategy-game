The wheel and axle themselves are ENG's invention; this entry owns what
hitching that invention to a draft animal does to how much a moving household
can carry with it, and therefore how far and how self-sufficiently it can go.

## Timing

Wheeled vehicles and draft-animal traction appear in the broader Middle East
and steppe zone within a few centuries of each other, roughly -4000 to -3500,
converging into ox-drawn wagon nomadism on the Pontic-Caspian steppe by
roughly -3500 to -3000.

## Mechanism

A wagon converts a household's mobility constraint from "however much a
person or pack animal can carry on its back" to "however much a large draft
animal can pull," an order-of-magnitude jump in transportable mass. This is
the specific difference between short-range transhumance (moving herds
between fixed seasonal pastures, base camp intact) and true nomadism (the
base itself moves) — the wagon is what lets an entire household's material
world travel rather than just its herd.

## Social consequence

Full wagon-based nomadism restructures household and kin organization around
mobility as the default state rather than the exception — dwelling
architecture becomes the vehicle itself, and social units organize for
sustained movement rather than periodic movement from a fixed settlement,
a distinct habitus from either sedentary farming or short-range herding.

## Regional specifics

The steppe case is the clearest and best-attested: ox-wagon nomadism appears
to be a genuine precondition for the kind of long-range, family-inclusive
pastoral mobility that later steppe confederations (see MIG-007, MIG-008)
scale up into political and military organization. The Americas' complete
absence of draft-wagon logistics — despite independently inventing the wheel
itself on toys — shows the wheel's transport value is entirely conditional on
having a suitable draft animal, not on the mechanical idea being known or
unknown.

## Hook

Draft-animal wagon traction multiplies sustainable household `migration
range` and cargo capacity by an order of magnitude over pack transport,
converting seasonal short-range herding into full household nomadism — a
capability entirely gated on draft-animal availability, independent of
whether the wheel itself is known.
