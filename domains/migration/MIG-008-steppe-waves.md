Three specific instances of a steppe confederation (MIG-007) successfully
converting its mobility and military advantage into sustained territorial
conquest of settled populations — and three different long-run resolutions of
what happens after.

## Timing

The Magyar migration into the Carpathian Basin (895), the Seljuk migration
into Persia and Anatolia (from 1040), and the Mongol conquests (from the
1200s) span roughly four centuries of the same underlying mechanic recurring
at different scales.

## Mechanism

A steppe confederation with enough cohesion (MIG-007) and mobility range
(MIG-001, MIG-003) directed at a weak point in a settled neighbor's defenses
can convert temporary raiding advantage into durable territorial control —
but holding that control long-term requires a second, different capability
(administering a much larger settled population) the confederation's original
mobile-military organization doesn't automatically provide, which is what
makes the post-conquest trajectory diverge so sharply case by case.

## Social consequence

In every case, a small conquering population ends up ruling a much larger
settled one, forcing a choice (not always made deliberately) between
adopting the settled population's administrative and cultural apparatus
wholesale, imposing a durable separate ruling-caste structure, or eventually
being absorbed and diluted back into the local population — the three
outcomes visible respectively in the Seljuk, (partially) Mongol, and Magyar
cases.

## Regional specifics

The Seljuks convert fastest and most completely into a settled Persianate
administration, essentially disappearing as a distinct mobile steppe polity
within a couple of generations. The Magyars uniquely achieve full permanent
territorial sedentarization within their core conquest region rather than
either dissolving or ruling as a distant mobile minority. China's repeated
experience of the same basic mechanic (steppe-origin dynasties eventually
governing as settled Chinese dynasties) shows a third resolution again,
suggesting the conquered society's own administrative depth and appeal, more
than anything intrinsic to the conquering steppe group, is what determines
which of these outcomes results.

## Hook

Steppe-wave conquest converts confederation mobility into territorial control,
but the conquering group's post-conquest trajectory — full assimilation into
the settled administration, a durable separate ruling caste, or full
sedentarization into the conquered territory — branches on the relative
administrative depth of conqueror versus conquered, not on conquest success
alone (design principle 1).
