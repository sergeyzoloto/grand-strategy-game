This entry owns the transportation event itself as a forced-migration
mechanic — coercion, logistics, mortality, demographic redistribution — kept
separate from the commodity trade that financed and organized it (COM's
territory) and from chattel slavery as a legal status once arrived (LAW's).

## Timing

From the first sustained transport to Spanish American colonies (1526)
through legal abolition of the transatlantic trade itself across the major
participating powers, substantially complete by 1867 even as slavery
persisted longer in some destinations.

## Mechanism

Forced migration removes the voluntary cost/benefit calculus present in
every other migration mechanic in this domain: there is no destination
"pull" the migrant is weighing, only point-of-origin violence (capture,
often via African intermediary polities and networks) and a controlled,
high-mortality transport logistics chain optimized for the transporter's
profit rather than the transported population's survival. Middle Passage
mortality (commonly estimated in the range of 12-15% of those embarked, on
top of substantial pre-embarkation mortality) is a direct, calculated
input to that logistics chain's economics rather than an incidental byproduct
of it.

## Social consequence

Destination societies receiving large numbers of forcibly transported
people build entire social, legal, and economic structures (plantation labor
regimes, chattel-slavery legal codes, and later systems of racial
categorization used to justify and maintain them) around a population that
arrived through total coercion rather than any of this domain's other
migration mechanics — a foundational social rupture with none of the
partial agency present even in indentured servitude (MIG-012).

## Regional specifics

Destination distribution within the Americas is sharply uneven, with sugar
colonies (Brazil, the Caribbean) receiving the overwhelming majority of
transported captives against a much smaller mainland North American share —
a fact that cuts directly against a common popular misconception about where
the institution was concentrated. On the African side, participation as a
supplying region varied enormously by era and by which coastal and interior
polities were drawn into the Atlantic system, meaning the demographic and
social impact was highly unevenly distributed across the continent rather
than a single uniform continental experience.

## Contested

Full-chain mortality (from initial capture through post-arrival "seasoning,"
not just the Middle Passage crossing itself) and the resulting demographic
and social impact on specific African source regions and societies are both
harder to pin down with confidence than shipping-manifest-based Middle
Passage mortality alone, and remain under active revision as more records are
digitized. Model total mortality as a genuinely uncertain range rather than a
single figure, and keep source-region demographic impact as a separate,
even-more-uncertain downstream question rather than assuming it follows
mechanically from transport numbers alone.

## Hook

Forced migration imposes a severe, front-loaded mortality cost with zero
migrant agency at any stage, converting a source region's `displacement
pressure` directly into destination-society labor supply and, downstream, a
new coerced-population `settler footprint` with a legal status (chattel
slavery) that this entry deliberately leaves to LAW rather than modeling here.
