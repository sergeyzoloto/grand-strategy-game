The domesticated animal itself and its use as food or draft power are AGR's;
this entry owns what a reliably rideable horse does to how far, how fast, and
how sustainably a population or raiding party can move — the single largest
jump in unassisted human mobility before the railroad.

## Timing

Domestication for milk and meat begins on the Pontic-Caspian steppe by roughly
-3500; reliable riding at range for sustained travel and mounted warfare is
attested considerably later, plausibly not before -2000 to -1000 depending on
how the tack and wear evidence is read.

## Mechanism

A rideable horse multiplies a person's sustainable daily travel range several
times over what walking or even ox-cart travel allows, and does so without
requiring the fixed infrastructure (roads, way-stations) that made comparable
gains possible elsewhere — the mobility gain is portable, self-fueling
(grazing), and available anywhere with sufficient pasture. This is what
converts pastoralism from a locally mobile subsistence strategy into a
platform for long-range raiding, trading, and eventually large-scale
population movement.

## Social consequence

Societies that acquire horse-mounted mobility gain a standing capability
asymmetry against slower-moving neighbors — faster raiding, faster
retreat, faster response to opportunity or threat — that reshapes social
organization around mobility itself: herding units that can range further
apart, military and political structures built around rapid mustering and
movement (a foundation later steppe confederations build directly on).

## Regional specifics

China's cavalry adoption via steppe contact shows the mobility technology
diffusing into an already-settled administrative system rather than producing
steppe-style pastoralism itself — the same input technology, a completely
different social outcome depending on what kind of society receives it. The
Americas' total absence of horses until 16th-century European reintroduction,
followed by an extraordinarily rapid adoption and adaptation by Plains
peoples, is a clean natural experiment in how fast a society can restructure
around a mobility technology once it's available, unconstrained by any slow
indigenous domestication process.

## Contested

Whether horse domestication and horse riding-at-range were the same event or
separated by a substantial gap is unresolved, with genetic, dental, and
material-culture evidence supporting different timelines. Model domestication
(an AGR-side food/dairy unlock) and riding-at-range (this entry's MIG-side
mobility unlock) as two independently triggerable technologies rather than
one bundled event, so a society can plausibly have one without the other.

## Hook

A reliably rideable horse raises a population's sustainable `migration range`
by a large, discrete multiple — the single biggest pre-railroad jump in this
domain, and the enabling precondition nearly every later steppe-mobility entry
in this record builds on.
