Cyclical, short-range movement of herds and herders between fixed
seasonal pastures — mentioned only as a contrast case inside this
domain's own wheel-and-wagon entry (MIG-003) until now, despite being the
most geographically universal and technologically undemanding migration
pattern in this entire record.

## Timing

Seasonal herd movement between lowland winter and highland summer
pasture is plausibly as old as pastoralism itself, attested from at least
-6000 in the Zagros and Mediterranean uplands; Castile's Mesta
formalizes the practice into a chartered legal institution from 1273, and
comparable transhumant systems persist in parts of the Mediterranean and
Middle East into the industrial era before enclosure, fencing, and
motorized transport erode the open-route model through the 19th century.

## Mechanism

Transhumance requires no mobility-technology breakthrough at all — no
horse, no wagon, no camel — just a herd, a customary or legally-recognized
right of passage, and two pastures at different elevations or latitudes
whose growing seasons don't overlap. This makes it mechanically distinct
from every other entry in this domain: it neither expands a frontier
(MIG-002), converts a household's whole material base into something
mobile (MIG-003), nor unlocks a new sustainable range (MIG-001) — it is a
steady-state, repeating cycle that returns to the same two endpoints
every year rather than pushing outward or resettling anywhere
permanently.

## Social consequence

Because a transhumant route crosses land other populations are
simultaneously trying to cultivate, farming and pastoralist communities
co-exist along the same corridor under an inherent tension — sometimes
managed through mutually recognized customary rights (as with the
Mesta's protected cañadas), sometimes erupting into the more acute
conflict this record's pastoralist-cultivator entry (AGR-014) already
tracks — with the specific form of accommodation reached shaping which
economy dominates a given borderland for generations at a time.

## Regional specifics

The Mesta's centuries-long legal protection of transhumant right-of-way
against local agricultural land claims shows the mechanic can be
formalized into a durable political institution with real power to
override competing local interests, not just a spontaneous seasonal
habit. East Asia's steppe pastoralism running closer to full nomadism's
base-camp-relocating pattern (MIG-003) than to transhumance's narrower
fixed-route seasonality shows the two pastoral mobility types are
genuinely separable choices shaped by local geography and herd
economics, not points on a single continuum every pastoral society moves
along in the same direction.

## Hook

A modifier: a steady-state, cyclical mobility pattern that recurs
independent of horse, wagon, or camel technology (MIG-001, MIG-003, and
this domain's camel entry all sit orthogonal to it), returning to the
same two endpoints every year rather than expanding a frontier — and one
that generates its own durable land-use conflict or accommodation
dynamic wherever its route crosses land under active cultivation.
