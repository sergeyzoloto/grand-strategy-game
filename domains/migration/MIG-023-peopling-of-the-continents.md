Before any domesticated animal, wheel, or boat gives a population a
mobility advantage, humans already walk into every uninhabited landmass
this record's other entries assume is already peopled — the domain's
actual first migration event, and the one every later entry silently
stands on.

## Timing

Anatomically modern human dispersal out of Africa and into the wider
Eurasian landmass is underway by roughly -70000, reaching every region
this record's focus areas cover well before any other entry in this
domain opens; the Beringia land-bridge crossing into the Americas, the
last major populated landmass, occurs from roughly -20000, with the land
bridge itself submerging as sea levels rise by around -11000.

## Mechanism

Unassisted, on-foot dispersal into previously uninhabited territory is
paced by a small founding population's own demographic growth and
foraging-range expansion generation over generation — mechanically the
same shape as MIG-002's later wave-of-advance (a slow frontier pushed
outward by local population pressure, not any single long journey), but
pre-agricultural, driven by hunting-and-gathering carrying capacity
rather than farming surplus, and terminating in the first human presence
on a landmass rather than displacing an already-resident population
there.

## Social consequence

A founding population entering wholly uninhabited territory faces none
of the negotiation, absorption, or conflict dynamics every later
migration mechanic in this domain has to model, because there is no
prior population to negotiate with — the social consequence is entirely
internal to the migrating population itself: how a small founding
group's genetic and cultural diversity, carried forward across every
subsequent generation in the newly settled region, shapes everything
from disease susceptibility (feeding directly into HLT's later
virgin-soil epidemic mechanics) to linguistic and technological
divergence over the following millennia.

## Regional specifics

The out-of-Africa dispersal into the Levant and onward across Eurasia is
the founding event every later Eurasian regional lineage in this record
ultimately traces back to. The Americas' Beringia crossing arrives tens
of thousands of years after Eurasia is already peopled, by a route
(interior ice-free corridor versus a coastal route) that remains
genuinely disputed, and the land bridge itself closes behind the
migrating population as sea levels rise — one of the only migration
routes in this entire record that stops being physically usable shortly
after being used. Remote Oceania is not settled during this window at
all, requiring a categorically different capability (deliberate
open-ocean voyaging) millennia later.

## Contested

Beringia's exact crossing date, whether the founding population moved via
an interior ice-free corridor or a coastal route, and whether there were
one or several distinct migration pulses are all actively revised by
ancient-DNA and archaeological evidence, with pre-Clovis sites
complicating the older "Clovis First" consensus. Model the peopling of
the Americas as arriving within an uncertain date range and an unresolved
route choice, while treating the overall fact and rough timing window of
arrival as solid.

## Hook

An origin gate: this entry establishes the human population in each of
this record's focus regions that every other migration mechanic in this
domain — from MIG-002's farming wave to MIG-005's frontier colonization
of the Americas — silently assumes is already there to move, resettle,
farm, or be displaced.
