This entry owns the plantation as an agricultural production system —
large consolidated landholding, a single export monoculture crop,
on-site processing infrastructure, and a resident coerced gang-labor
force organized around the crop's specific harvest and processing
calendar; MIG-013 owns the forced-migration event that supplied plantation
labor, and COM-017 owns the resulting commodity-trade dependency.

## Timing

The plantation complex's basic template develops on Portuguese Atlantic
islands (Madeira, then São Tomé) from around 1450, transfers to and
scales up dramatically in Brazil and the Caribbean from the mid-1500s,
and persists as an organized production system through slavery's formal
abolition across the major participating economies, substantially
complete by 1888.

## Mechanism

A plantation concentrates land, processing infrastructure (a sugar mill,
for instance, requiring the cut cane be processed within hours of
harvest to prevent sucrose loss), and a large resident coerced labor
force under one integrated management structure specifically because the
crop's own agronomic and processing timing — a narrow harvest window,
immediate on-site processing requirements — rewards tight, centrally-
coordinated control at a scale an independent smallholder farm can't
match; the same monoculture concentration that maximizes short-run
output also drives faster `soil fertility` depletion than diversified
cultivation, since the same nutrients are drawn down from the same soil
every single cycle with no rotation to restore them.

## Social consequence

The plantation's labor regime is organized entirely around the crop's
processing calendar rather than around the workers' own subsistence or
welfare — gang labor scheduled to harvest and mill sugar cane before it
spoils, for instance, produces work rhythms and physical demands with no
equivalent in either subsistence farming or the wage-labor systems
covered elsewhere in this record, a labor organization made possible
only by the total coercive control MIG-013's forced-migration mechanic
supplies.

## Regional specifics

The plantation complex's origin on Portuguese Atlantic islands, before
any American-phase Atlantic trade begins, shows the model's basic
template — monoculture, on-site processing, coerced gang labor — was
established and proven in the eastern Atlantic first, then transferred
and scaled to the American context rather than being invented there. The
Dutch Cultivation System's Java estates achieve a broadly comparable
export-monoculture outcome through a structurally different production
system — compelled peasant smallholder labor within existing village
landholding rather than the plantation's consolidated estate and
resident gang-labor force — showing colonial export-commodity
production doesn't require the plantation's specific labor-organization
template to reach comparable output.

## Hook

The plantation concentrates land, processing infrastructure, and coerced
labor under one management structure specifically because narrow harvest
windows reward centralized control — a production system that maximizes
short-run output at the cost of faster `soil fertility` depletion than
diversified cultivation, running entirely on the total labor coercion
MIG-013's forced-migration mechanic supplies rather than on any wage or
tenancy relationship this record's other agricultural labor systems rely
on.
