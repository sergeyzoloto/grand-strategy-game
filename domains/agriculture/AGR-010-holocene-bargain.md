Agriculture's near-simultaneous, independent emergence across multiple
unconnected world regions within a narrow window after the last Ice Age
ends is best explained by a shared precondition none of those regions
individually created: the Holocene epoch's unusually stable climate,
without which repeated crop-selection investment (AGR-020) could not
reliably pay off across the many consecutive stable growing seasons it
requires.

## Timing

The Holocene epoch begins around -11700 with the end of the Pleistocene's
last glacial period; the first independent agricultural origins (Fertile
Crescent, and shortly after, other centers) appear within a few thousand
years of that transition, clustering suspiciously close to the climate
shift itself across otherwise unconnected regions.

## Mechanism

Pleistocene climate is characterized by frequent, often abrupt, large-
magnitude swings (the Younger Dryas cold snap immediately preceding the
Holocene is a documented example) that would have made the multi-
generation selective investment domestication requires (AGR-020)
extremely high-risk: a crop-selection program invested across many
seasons only pays off if enough of those seasons are climatically similar
enough to let selected traits accumulate and prove their value. Holocene
climate stability removes that risk by providing, for the first time in
the human species' history, a long enough run of predictable growing
seasons for that multi-generation investment to reliably pay off.

## Social consequence

Because climate stability is a removed constraint rather than a positive
trigger, it explains why agriculture becomes possible everywhere roughly
simultaneously without explaining why any specific population actually
takes up the opportunity — some Holocene populations with adequate wild
resource abundance never develop agriculture at all (HLT-030's African
foraging/herding case among them), meaning the bargain's terms became
available globally at the same time even though very different
populations chose, or were pushed toward, taking it.

## Regional specifics

No confirmed independent agricultural origin predates the Holocene
anywhere, despite tens of thousands of years of prior modern-human
occupation across most of the inhabited world — a striking absence that
argues for climate stability functioning as a genuine necessary
precondition rather than coincidental timing. That several regions with
comparably long stretches of stable Holocene climate never independently
develop agriculture at all shows the removed constraint is necessary but
not sufficient — something else, varying by region, still has to supply
the actual push toward the domestication investment (AGR-020) once the
climate barrier is gone.

## Contested

How much explanatory weight Holocene climate stability itself carries,
versus regionally-specific population-pressure, resource-circumscription,
or social-competition explanations for why agriculture emerges when and
where it does, is disputed among archaeologists — climate stability is
close to a necessary condition, since no known agricultural origin
predates the Holocene, but plausibly not a sufficient one, since long
stretches of stable Holocene climate in many regions never produce
independent domestication at all. Model Holocene climate stability as
removing a hard prior constraint rather than as a positive trigger,
leaving the region-specific question of why some Holocene populations
respond to that removed constraint with domestication and others don't
as a separate, unresolved variable.

## Hook

Holocene climate stability functions as an unlocked precondition, not a
trigger — every region gains the *option* of a payoff-worthy multi-
generation crop-selection investment (AGR-020) at roughly the same time,
but whether any specific population actually takes that option, and how
fast, stays a separate, region-specific variable the climate shift alone
doesn't determine.
