Agriculture begins independently in at least seven identifiable centers
worldwide, each domesticating its own unrelated founder-crop package —
not one invention diffusing outward, but the same underlying selective
pressure (higher, more reliable yield from human-favored plant traits)
producing convergent but genetically independent results on different
continents.

## Timing

The Fertile Crescent's wheat, barley, and legume package domesticates from
roughly -9500; East Asian rice and millet domestication proceeds on
independent timelines from around -9000; Mesoamerican maize's slow
selection from wild teosinte begins by roughly -9000 but requires several
further millennia to reach a fully productive form; the New Guinea
highlands' vegetative package domesticates from around -7000.

## Mechanism

Domestication converts a wild plant population's traits — seed shattering
for natural dispersal, small seed size, dormancy — into their opposite:
non-shattering heads that hold their seed for a human harvester, larger
seed size, and reduced dormancy, all traits that make the plant worse at
surviving without human cultivation and better at feeding the humans
cultivating it. This raises the land's `yield ceiling` for the domesticated
crop specifically, but only after the multi-generation selection process
that produces those traits has run its course — an investment with a long,
gradual payoff curve rather than an immediate one.

## Social consequence

A founder-crop package that includes a storable, calorie-dense staple
(cereal grain especially) sets the ceiling on how large and how sedentary
a population the surrounding land can support, converting the later
storage-and-settlement dynamic (HLT-030) and the demic spread of farming
populations outward from each center (MIG-020) into direct downstream
consequences of which specific crop package a given region happened to
domesticate or receive.

## Regional specifics

Maize's domestication from wild teosinte is unusually labor-intensive as
domestications go — teosinte's wild form bears little resemblance to
productive maize, requiring a longer and more deliberately managed
selection process than Fertile Crescent cereals, whose wild ancestors were
already closer to agronomically useful. New Guinea's vegetatively-propagated
package (taro, banana, sugarcane, propagated from cuttings rather than
selected seed) shows domestication itself isn't one single technological
path — propagation-based and seed-selection-based domestication are two
independently viable routes to the same underlying outcome. Aboriginal
Australia's alternative trajectory — landscape-scale fire management and,
regionally, aquaculture, without seed-crop domestication — shows
intensifying land productivity and founder-crop domestication are
separable achievements, not points on one single continuum every
sufficiently long-settled society eventually reaches.

## Contested

Whether domestication proceeded as a rapid, deliberate human choice or a
slow, largely unintentional accumulation of "domestication syndrome"
traits under repeated harvesting pressure is disputed, with dating
evidence increasingly favoring longer, more gradual timelines than
earlier "Neolithic Revolution" framings assumed. Model domestication as a
multi-century-to-multi-millennium accumulation process with no single
triggering decision, rather than a point event any population can be said
to have chosen.

## Hook

Each independent domestication center raises its local `yield ceiling`
only after a long, non-optional multi-generation selection investment
with no shortcut — a founder-crop package's specific traits (storable
grain versus vegetative propagation, fast versus slow domestication
curve) then set hard downstream limits on the settlement, storage, and
population-spread mechanics (HLT-030, MIG-020) built on top of it.
