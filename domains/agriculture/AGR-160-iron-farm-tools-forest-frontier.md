This entry owns iron tools' specific agricultural application — the axe
that clears heavy forest and the plowshare that turns denser, heavier
soils than earlier tool materials could manage — as a distinct
consequence from COM-020's general treatment of metallurgy as a craft
and commodity.

## Timing

Iron tool-making reaches agricultural use across multiple regions from
roughly -1200 onward (following iron metallurgy's broader development,
COM-020), with iron-tool-enabled forest clearance and heavy-soil
cultivation continuing to expand agricultural range across new regions
through the following two millennia.

## Mechanism

Bronze tools are too scarce and too soft-edged (bronze being a
copper-tin alloy requiring imported tin, and dulling faster under heavy
use) to serve as the primary tool material for large-scale forest
clearance or heavy-soil plowing; iron, once smelting technique matures,
is cheaper to produce at scale from more widely available ore and holds
a working edge through far more use, converting axe-and-plowshare
agriculture from a bronze-scarcity-limited activity into one limited
mainly by labor and land availability instead.

## Social consequence

Iron-tool-enabled forest clearance opens temperate forested land —
previously too costly to clear at scale with stone or bronze tools — to
agricultural settlement, directly extending the ecological range farming-
frontier expansion (AGR-080) can reach and converting land that
previously only supported foraging or light grazing into cultivable
farmland at a labor cost low enough for ordinary settler households to
undertake.

## Regional specifics

Bantu-speaking populations carry iron tool-making and farming technique
together as one bundled expansion package rather than adopting the two
technologies separately, plausibly contributing to the Bantu expansion's
distinctive combined pace (AGR-080's Bantu case). The pre-contact
Americas' complete absence of iron tools shows substantial forested-
frontier agricultural expansion remains achievable through stone, wood,
and fire-based clearance alone — a real capability at real additional
labor cost per unit of land cleared, not a hard technological floor
below which forest-frontier farming simply can't happen.

## Hook

Iron tools remove bronze-scarcity's ceiling on forest-clearance and
heavy-soil-cultivation labor cost, directly extending the ecological
range farming-frontier expansion (AGR-080) can reach into previously
uneconomical terrain — a capability gap between iron-tool and no-iron-
tool frontier expansion that shows up as a real labor-cost multiplier,
not an absolute barrier, since stone-and-fire clearance remains a
working, if costlier, substitute.
