This entry owns the specific mapping between which domesticated species a
region keeps and which disease-family reservoir that choice tends to
generate — HLT-001 owns the general storage-and-proximity mechanism by
which any dense livestock keeping raises zoonotic exposure at all.

## Timing

Zoonotic spillover risk rises alongside livestock domestication and
secondary-products intensification (AGR-002) from roughly -8500, with the
disease-reservoir profile of a given region's domesticate package fully
established by the time that package itself matures, around -2000.

## Mechanism

Different domesticated species carry different zoonotic risk profiles:
cattle and other ruminants are linked to disease families including
measles-adjacent morbilliviruses and anthrax; pigs and waterfowl are
particularly associated with influenza-family viruses, whose genetic
reassortment risk rises specifically where pigs and fowl are kept in
close proximity to each other and to humans; a region's total zoonotic
reservoir risk is therefore a function of its specific domesticate mix,
not just of livestock density in the abstract.

## Social consequence

A region with a denser, more diverse domesticate package accumulates a
correspondingly richer set of endemic zoonotic diseases among its human
population over time — a real epidemiological cost of secondary-products-
intensity livestock keeping (AGR-002), but one that, per HLT-001's own
contested framing, also builds population-level `immunological capital`
against that same disease set, which becomes decisive only on contact
with a population that never built the equivalent exposure.

## Regional specifics

China's pig- and fowl-heavy livestock profile generates a disease-
reservoir weighted toward influenza-family zoonoses specifically, a
different risk signature from the Fertile Crescent's cattle/sheep-weighted
package despite both regions independently crossing the same general
storage-and-proximity threshold (HLT-001). The pre-contact Americas'
smaller, lower-reservoir-potential domesticate roster is a direct,
traceable contributor to the stark immunological asymmetry HLT-017
describes at contact — not the only cause, but a real and specific one.

## Contested

The popular thesis that Eurasia's domesticable-mammal roster alone
mechanically explains its disease-reservoir richness is challenged by
scholars who argue population density, settlement duration, and contact
frequency between regions do comparable or greater explanatory work, and
that some specific livestock-to-human disease jumps are less well
documented than commonly assumed. Model domesticate roster as one real,
contributing input to zoonotic reservoir risk alongside these other
factors, not as the single sufficient explanation.

## Hook

A region's specific domesticate mix (cattle-heavy, pig-and-fowl-heavy,
or a smaller lower-reservoir roster) sets a distinct disease-family risk
profile on top of HLT-001's general storage-driven zoonotic mechanism —
richer profiles cost more in ongoing disease burden but bank more
`immunological capital` against exactly that disease set, a trade whose
payoff or cost is only realized on contact with a population carrying a
different profile.
