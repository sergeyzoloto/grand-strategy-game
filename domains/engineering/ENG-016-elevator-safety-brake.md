Elisha Otis's safety brake (demonstrated dramatically in 1852, patented
1861) — a mechanism that automatically catches and holds an elevator car
in place if its hoisting cable snaps, solving the specific trust problem
that had kept elevators a freight-only, rarely-ridden novelty.

## Timing

Freight hoists using ropes or chains predate Otis's innovation by decades
but carry an unacceptable fall risk for passenger use; Otis's safety brake
demonstration in 1852 and subsequent commercial installation from the
1857 Haughwout Building make passenger elevators viable, directly enabling
skyscraper construction that scales through the 1880s.

## Mechanism

Without a reliable fail-safe, an elevator's entire safety depends on a
single hoisting rope or cable never failing — an unacceptable risk profile
for carrying passengers rather than freight. The safety brake mechanism
uses the sudden slack that occurs the instant a cable actually breaks to
trigger spring-loaded gripping bars that jam against the shaft's guide
rails, converting a catastrophic single point of failure into a
mechanically redundant, self-triggering safety system that requires no
human intervention or even any advance warning to activate.

## Social consequence

Removing the passenger elevator's fall risk directly removes the practical
height ceiling on buildings that human stair-climbing endurance had
otherwise imposed — a building's maximum practical height stops being a
function of how many flights of stairs occupants will tolerate climbing and
becomes a function of structural engineering and elevator capacity instead,
the specific precondition for the skyscraper as a building form.

## Regional specifics

This is a genuinely American-originated and American-commercialized
innovation in this window, with the resulting skyscraper building form
developing first and most extensively in American cities where land-value
pressure and building codes created the strongest immediate commercial
incentive; European adoption follows with a lag driven by different urban
development conditions rather than by any independent competing safety-
brake innovation.

## Hook

A self-triggering mechanical safety brake removes the passenger elevator's
fall risk entirely, converting a building's maximum practical height from
a function of stair-climbing endurance into a function of structural
engineering and elevator capacity — the specific, narrow innovation that
makes the skyscraper possible as a building form at all.
