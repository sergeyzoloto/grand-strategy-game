The transistor and, from it, the integrated circuit — solid-state
components that replace fragile, power-hungry vacuum tubes with something
smaller, more reliable, and mass-manufacturable at a precision scale
building directly on the machine-tool precision lineage (ENG-190).

## Timing

The transistor is demonstrated at Bell Labs in 1947; integrated circuits
combining many transistors on a single chip follow from the late 1950s,
with fabrication capability and market leadership shifting substantially
toward East Asian manufacturers by 1990.

## Mechanism

Semiconductor fabrication requires precision at a scale far beyond
mechanical machine tools (ENG-190) — photolithography and chemical
processing replace physical cutting, but the underlying logic is the same
one ENG-190 established: each generation of more precise fabrication
capability is used to build the next, even more precise generation,
compounding recursively in a way that has let transistor density on a
single chip increase by many orders of magnitude over the following
decades.

## Social consequence

Semiconductor manufacturing leadership becomes a direct proxy for
advanced-industrial and, increasingly, military-technology capacity,
concentrating enormous strategic and economic importance in the small
number of firms and countries that can fabricate at the industry's leading
edge — a `skill differential` and capital-intensity barrier steep enough
that very few new entrants can credibly compete at the frontier once
established players have several fabrication generations' head start.

## Regional specifics

East Asian semiconductor development's trajectory from licensed follower
to independent leader and, in several categories, outright global
manufacturing dominance within a couple of decades is a more complete
technological leapfrogging than most other technology-adoption cases in
this record, which more typically show fast adoption without full
frontier leadership following. Europe's comparatively weaker fabrication
capacity despite strong foundational research contributions shows research
strength and manufacturing leadership are separable outcomes — a region
can contribute significantly to a technology's science without becoming a
leading producer of it.

## Hook

Semiconductor fabrication precision compounds recursively exactly like
ENG-190's machine-tool lineage, but the resulting `skill differential` and
capital barrier to entry is steep enough that leadership can fully
transfer between regions (America to East Asia) rather than merely
diffusing — a more complete technological leapfrogging than the partial-
adoption pattern most other technologies in this record show.
