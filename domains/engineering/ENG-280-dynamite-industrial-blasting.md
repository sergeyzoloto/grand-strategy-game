Alfred Nobel's stabilization of nitroglycerin (an explosive too unstable
for safe practical handling on its own) into a manageable, storable, and
controllably-detonated form — extending gunpowder-lineage explosive power
(ENG-140) by an order of magnitude for the specific purpose of moving rock
rather than firing a projectile.

## Timing

Nobel patents dynamite in 1866, stabilizing nitroglycerin by absorbing it
into an inert material; the technology scales rapidly into mining,
tunneling, canal, and railway construction worldwide through the following
half-century, essential to major projects completed by 1914.

## Mechanism

Nitroglycerin's explosive power vastly exceeds gunpowder's, but its
extreme sensitivity to shock and temperature makes it too dangerous to
transport or handle reliably at industrial scale on its own — dynamite
solves this by absorbing the compound into a stabilizing material, trading
a small amount of raw explosive power for a controllable, transportable,
and reliably detonated product usable for planned, precise rock removal
rather than only for weapons.

## Social consequence

Reliable, controllable industrial blasting removes terrain as a fixed
constraint on infrastructure route planning — a mountain range or hard rock
formation that would previously force a road, railway, or canal to route
entirely around it can instead be tunneled or cut through directly, at a
labor and cost tradeoff engineers can now actually calculate rather than
simply accepting geography's dictate.

## Regional specifics

Dynamite's role in American transcontinental railway construction and,
later, the Panama Canal's Culebra Cut make it directly responsible for
removing what would otherwise have been each project's single largest
physical obstacle — the specific engineering achievement each project is
most remembered for. East Asian adoption arriving through direct purchase
and licensed manufacture rather than independent development fits the
broader pattern already established for this record's other chemical and
industrial technology transfers.

## Hook

Stabilized industrial explosives convert terrain from a fixed routing
constraint into a calculable cost-and-labor tradeoff, directly enabling the
mountain and isthmus crossings (COM-200's transcontinental railways,
COM-130's canal chokepoints) that would otherwise force infrastructure to
route entirely around the obstacle rather than through it.
