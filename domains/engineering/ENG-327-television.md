Not an extension of ENG-325. Mechanisms 1 through 4 of broadcast radio do
carry over unchanged, which is the argument for folding the two together —
but two further mechanisms run in the opposite direction to radio's own
conclusions, and one of them reverses radio's defining claim outright.

## Timing

From the first regular high-definition service in 1936, through postwar
mass adoption, the arrival of cable and then direct satellite broadcasting,
to the mid-1990s point at which satellite channels made national control of
television reception broadly unenforceable.

## Mechanism

**Production cost asymmetry, and it reverses ENG-325's central finding.**
Radio's claim is that reach decouples from capital — a transmitter is a
purchase, so the peripheral and the poor can buy reach the wire era priced
as an imperial project, which is what makes the FLN's clandestine
broadcasting and Congress Radio possible at all. Television keeps the cheap
transmission and adds an expensive product. **An hour of television is a
capital project**: cameras, lighting, studio, crew, editing, and a schedule
to be filled every day without gaps. The marginal cost of another listener
is still zero and the marginal cost of another *hour* is not, so the
binding constraint migrates from transmission to content — and the actors
who could afford a transmitter cannot afford a production house. The
medium that was cheap for the peripheral becomes expensive again, and
radio's from-below cases do not replicate. This is the strongest argument
for a separate entry: an entry cannot simultaneously hold that reach is
cheap for weak actors and that it is not.

The political consequence is that a monopoly on the ability to produce is
more durable than a monopoly on the ability to transmit, because it cannot
be broken by buying equipment. Doordarshan and Globo both illustrate it,
from opposite ends of the state-commercial divide.

**Attention exclusivity.** Radio is compatible with work, with driving, and
with other people in the room; it colonises hours without displacing what
fills them. Television is not — it requires the eyes, which means it
competes directly with every other activity rather than layering on top of
them. Household time-use shifts rather than absorbing, and the displacement
falls hardest on the activities television most resembles, which are the
social and associational ones. This is the `time discipline` variable
COM-170 establishes for the factory, appearing in the household and moving
the other way: the factory imposed external scheduling on work, and
television imposes it on leisure, since a broadcast schedule must be met
where a book need not be.

**Register changes what makes a leader viable.** Radio carries the voice;
television carries the face, the body, and the involuntary. Attributes that
were previously invisible to an audience at scale become legible — bearing,
apparent health, reaction under pressure — and attributes that carried
authority in print and audio become less decisive. The mechanism is a change
in which existing qualities are observable, not a change in what an audience
wants, and that distinction is what keeps the claim modellable rather than
mystical.

**Cable and satellite modulate radio's mechanisms rather than repeating
them.** Cable re-imposes the wire, with all of ENG-230's cost structure and
its territorial constraint returning — but bought voluntarily by the
subscriber for quality and choice, which is a genuinely new reason to
accept a wire. Satellite runs the other way and hardens the border-crossing
mechanism: a terrestrial broadcast can be jammed at the border with
sustained expenditure, and a satellite footprint cannot be. Suppression has
to move to confiscating dishes, pressuring the uplink state, or blocking
the transponder contract — political and diplomatic acts rather than
technical ones, each of them visible and attributable in a way that jamming
a frequency never was.

## Social consequence

Television concentrates cultural production in very few hands and then
exports the output, which reverses a flow radio had briefly made
bidirectional. A national broadcaster that cannot fund production schedules
imported programming instead, so the smaller and poorer the market, the more
of its screen time originates elsewhere — and the relationship is structural
rather than a matter of policy or taste, since the arithmetic of amortising
production over an audience does not care who is broadcasting.

Inside the household the set becomes the organising object of the main room
and of the evening, and viewing is initially collective because sets are
expensive and singular. As sets multiply the same medium fragments the
household it had assembled, which is a reversal worth carrying rather than
a footnote — television is a communal technology at low set density and a
solitary one at high density, with no change in the technology itself.

For rulers, the direct channel ENG-325 opened stays open and gets narrower.
Appearing on television is close to obligatory and is also risky in a way
radio never was, because the medium transmits what the speaker did not
intend along with what they did.

## Regional specifics

The Japanese pair is the most economical demonstration available that this
is a distinct medium and not a faster radio. The same population, the same
institution, fifteen years apart — the emperor's voice in 1945 and the
crown prince's wedding in 1959 — with authority moving from remoteness to
visibility. Kennedy-Nixon carries the same register point for an electoral
system and should be given no more weight than the Japanese case, partly
because the evidence behind it is weak.

Latin America and South Asia both show the production-cost mechanism
converting into political power, through a commercial monopoly in one case
and a state monopoly in the other, which is the right kind of convergence
for a mechanic — the same constraint producing the same outcome through
opposite institutions. East Germany supplies the European instance of
information sovereignty lost to a medium rather than to a policy. Al
Jazeera closes the arc that Sawt al-Arab opened in ENG-325, in the same
language community and on the same mechanism, with the jamming defence now
unavailable.

Africa and Oceania are where the reversal bites hardest, and they are the
entry's load-bearing absences rather than decoration. Radio's cheapness for
weak actors does not survive here; the medium that carried Radio Algerie
cannot carry its equivalent, because the constraint is no longer the
transmitter.

## Contested

The register claim is widely believed and thinly evidenced. The
Kennedy-Nixon radio-versus-television finding comes from one small survey
with acknowledged sampling problems and has been challenged repeatedly; it
should be treated as an illustration of a proposed mechanism, never as
support for it. The broader proposition that television systematically
changes which leaders succeed is plausible on the register argument and
close to untestable, because the medium arrives everywhere alongside other
changes in political recruitment and party organisation.

Model it as a change in which existing attributes are legible to an
audience rather than as a change in who wins, and keep the effect small and
uncertain. A strong version would reduce to a slider on political outcomes,
which design principle 2 forbids and which the evidence does not carry. The
production-cost and attention mechanisms above are far better supported and
should do the mechanical work.

## Hook

Reverses ENG-325's defining claim. Transmission stays cheap and content
becomes expensive, so a new `production cost floor` gates the medium — an
actor who could afford a transmitter cannot afford a schedule, radio's
from-below insurgent and clandestine cases fail to replicate, and a monopoly
on producing is more durable than a monopoly on transmitting because
equipment cannot break it. Small and poor markets import their screen time
as an arithmetic consequence rather than a policy choice. Attention becomes
exclusive rather than compatible with work, so `time discipline` enters the
household and displaces associational activity instead of layering over it.
The set is communal at low density and solitary at high density, with no
change in the technology. Cable re-imposes ENG-230's wire, voluntarily and
per-subscriber; satellite makes `information sovereignty` defensible only by
visible political acts — dish confiscation, uplink pressure, transponder
blocking — rather than by the quiet continuous expenditure of jamming.
