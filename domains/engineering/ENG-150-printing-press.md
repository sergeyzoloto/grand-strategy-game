The reading-habit and social consequence of cheap printed text is HLT-190's
territory; this entry owns the mechanism itself — casting durable, reusable
type, setting it accurately, and pressing it against paper with even,
repeatable pressure at production speed.

## Timing

Bi Sheng's ceramic movable type dates to the 1040s; Korea's Jikji
demonstrates metal movable type by 1377; Gutenberg's press, adapting the
mechanical leverage of an existing screw-press design (originally used for
wine and olive pressing) to printing, reaches practical mass production by
the 1450s.

## Mechanism

Movable-type printing requires solving three distinct technical problems
together: casting individual characters in a durable, reusable material at
consistent size, setting many such characters accurately into a stable
page-form, and applying even, repeatable pressure to transfer ink to paper
without smearing or requiring re-inking and re-pressing by hand for every
impression — Gutenberg's specific contribution is largely in the third
problem, adapting an existing mechanical screw-press design already used
for oil and wine, rather than in inventing movable type itself, which both
East Asian traditions had already solved earlier.

## Social consequence

The specific mechanical solution matters enormously for how fast a
technology scales into mass production even once the basic movable-type
concept is proven — Gutenberg's press-based approach, once combined with a
small alphabetic character set, mechanizes output speed dramatically beyond
what East Asian hand-pressed movable type achieved with a character set an
order of magnitude larger, a difference in practical throughput that shapes
how quickly cheap printed text becomes genuinely mass-market (HLT-190's
reading-habit consequence) in each tradition.

## Regional specifics

Both Chinese and Korean movable-type printing solve the casting and
setting problem centuries before Gutenberg, but a Chinese or Korean font's
thousands of distinct characters make the type-casting and compositor's
setting labor per page vastly higher than an alphabetic system's, a
separate constraint from the printing mechanism itself that shapes how fast
mass-market printing actually scales even once the underlying technique is
proven. Middle Eastern resistance to mechanical printing for Arabic script,
driven by calligraphic and religious objections rather than technological
unavailability, is a case where an available technique's adoption is
blocked entirely by institutional and cultural factors rather than by any
engineering gap.

## Hook

Movable-type printing requires solving casting, setting, and repeatable
pressing together — East Asian traditions solve all three earliest, but a
much larger character set keeps per-page labor high regardless, while
Gutenberg's screw-press mechanization combined with a small alphabet
produces a throughput advantage that determines how fast HLT-190's mass-
reading consequence can actually follow from the underlying technology.
