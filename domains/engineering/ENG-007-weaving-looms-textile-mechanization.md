This entry owns the specific mechanical inventions — the flying shuttle,
spinning jenny, water frame, and power loom — as engineering artifacts;
their effect on displaced hand-loom artisans is COM-021's, and their role
in factory labor organization is COM-023's.

## Timing

John Kay's flying shuttle (1733) roughly doubles a single weaver's
productive speed; Hargreaves's spinning jenny (1764) and Arkwright's
water frame (1769) mechanize spinning to keep pace; Cartwright's power
loom (1785), refined and adopted at scale through the following decades,
mechanizes weaving itself by the early 1800s.

## Mechanism

Each invention in this sequence targets a specific production-speed
bottleneck the previous one exposed: the flying shuttle speeds weaving
faster than hand-spinning can supply thread, the spinning jenny and water
frame close that gap by mechanizing spinning, and the power loom then
re-opens a weaving-speed bottleneck relative to the now-abundant machine-
spun thread — a cascading sequence where solving one stage's capacity
constraint routinely just relocates the bottleneck to the next stage.

## Social consequence

Mechanized spinning and weaving each independently collapse the labor-hours
required per unit of cloth, which is the direct engineering fact underlying
COM-021's craft-displacement mechanic and COM-023's factory labor-discipline
mechanic — the machines themselves are indifferent to which existing
workforce they displace or what new one they require; that outcome is
determined by which economy adopts the machinery and under what trade and
labor conditions.

## Regional specifics

Indian handloom weaving's multi-millennia technical sophistication,
producing textiles fine enough to sustain a major long-distance export
trade for centuries before mechanization, means the displacement COM-021
covers hits a highly refined existing craft tradition, not an unsophisticated
one overtaken by obviously superior technique. Chinese and Japanese
pattern-weaving mechanisms (drawlooms and similar) reach real mechanical
sophistication considerably earlier than European power looms while
remaining within a hand- or foot-powered paradigm — the missing ingredient
relative to the European case is the leap to external (water, then steam)
power, not mechanical ingenuity itself.

## Hook

A cascading sequence of textile inventions each solves one production-speed
bottleneck only to relocate it to the next stage, mechanically enabling
(but not itself determining) which existing workforce gets displaced
(COM-021) and what new factory-labor structure replaces it (COM-023) —
outcomes set by adoption context and trade policy, not by the machinery
itself.
