A continuously moving belt or chain that transports material or partially-
assembled goods between production stages automatically, removing the
labor and time cost of manually carrying items from one workstation to the
next.

## Timing

Early conveyor systems for bulk material handling (grain, coal) develop
from the 1790s; the innovation that most transforms manufacturing —
applying a moving conveyor to bring work to stationary assembly workers
rather than the reverse — is Henry Ford's 1913 moving assembly line.

## Mechanism

Manually carrying materials or partially-assembled products between
workstations costs time and labor that adds nothing to the product itself;
a conveyor system eliminates that dead time by moving the work
automatically and continuously, and Ford's specific innovation — keeping
workers stationary at fixed tasks while the product itself moves past them
— lets the line's speed be set deliberately to pace worker output, an
industrial engineering refinement of the same factory-discipline logic
COM-023 already covers, applied to material flow rather than only to labor
task division.

## Social consequence

A conveyor-paced assembly line sets work rhythm even more rigidly than
ordinary factory machine-tending does, since a worker can no longer even
briefly work ahead or fall behind their own pace — the line's speed
directly and continuously dictates the required work rate, a further
intensification of the externally-imposed time discipline COM-023
describes rather than a qualitatively new mechanic.

## Regional specifics

Ford's moving assembly line's dramatic assembly-time reduction — cutting
automobile production time by roughly an order of magnitude within a year
— makes it the paradigm case for conveyor-based mass production globally
influencing subsequent manufacturing organization far beyond the automobile
industry itself. East Asian adoption of comparable continuous material-
handling systems arriving through later direct technology transfer during
industrial modernization is consistent with the broader industrial-
technology adoption pattern documented elsewhere in this domain.

## Hook

Conveyor-based assembly removes manual material-transport dead time and
lets the line's speed directly set the required work pace, a further
intensification of COM-023's factory time-discipline mechanic rather than
a separate one — the same underlying labor-pacing dynamic, tightened one
additional notch by automating the movement between stations too.
