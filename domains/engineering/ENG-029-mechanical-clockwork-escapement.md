The escapement mechanism converts continuous force — a falling weight,
later a coiled spring — into regular, countable, mechanically-released
increments, the foundational precision-timekeeping device whose gear-
cutting tolerance demands directly prefigure `ENG-010`'s much later
machine-tool precision lineage by centuries.

## Timing

Su Song's water-powered astronomical clock tower, completed in China by
1092, is the earliest documented sophisticated escapement mechanism;
al-Jazari's Islamic-world water clocks and automata follow by 1206;
European weight-driven mechanical tower clocks, using the simpler
verge-and-foliot escapement, spread from around 1300.

## Mechanism

An escapement is the specific mechanical component that prevents a
clock's driving force (a falling weight's steady pull, or later a
spring's steady unwinding) from simply releasing all at once — it locks
and releases a toothed wheel in regular, small increments, converting
continuous force into the discrete, countable ticks a clock's hands or
astronomical display actually run on. Achieving this reliably requires
gear-cutting precision well beyond what casual carpentry or blacksmithing
demands, since an inconsistent tooth shape or spacing throws off the
escapement's regular release timing directly.

## Social consequence

A functioning mechanical clock converts timekeeping from something read
approximately off the sun, a water clock's draining level, or a burning
candle's length into something a household, monastery, or civic
authority can display and coordinate collective activity against
precisely and continuously, regardless of weather or daylight —
directly enabling the more rigid, clock-governed schedule coordination
that later factory time discipline elsewhere in this record intensifies
rather than invents.

## Regional specifics

Su Song's water-regulated escapement achieves earlier and, in some
respects, more sophisticated astronomical function than either the
Islamic or European traditions that follow it. Al-Jazari's documented
water clocks and automata reach comparable mechanical sophistication
through evidently independent development. European verge-and-foliot
clockwork, mechanically simpler and less accurate than Su Song's design
but fully independent of a continuous water supply, becomes the
lineage that eventually feeds into `ENG-010`'s precision-machining
tradition specifically, for reasons of subsequent institutional
continuity rather than original technical superiority.

## Contested

Whether European mechanical clockwork developed in genuine isolation or
carries any transmitted influence from Su Song's Chinese astronomical
clock tower, which predates the first European mechanical clocks by
roughly two centuries, is a genuinely unresolved history-of-technology
question — no documented transmission chain has been established, but
the possibility of indirect influence via intermediate contact hasn't
been ruled out either. Model the two traditions as independently
developed by default, while treating a transmission link as a live,
unconfirmed possibility rather than a settled non-connection.

## Hook

Escapement-based clockwork demands gear-cutting precision beyond
ordinary craft tolerance well before `ENG-010`'s industrial machine
tools formalize the same precision-compounding logic — a modifier and
precondition on precision-mechanism capability generally, arriving
independently in at least three regions before any one of them builds
a continuous institutional lineage on top of it.
