The second fossil resource, and the entry that owns what an energy
transition actually does to the incumbent. ENG-165 established that a
stock differs from a flow; this establishes that a substitute does not
add to a market, it destroys one.

## Timing

Commercial hand-dug extraction at Yenangyaung well before 1859, industrial
drilling from Drake's Titusville well in that year, Baku's rise to roughly
half of world output by 1900, Spindletop in 1901, and the first Persian
strike in 1908 — with 1911 as the closing point at which oil becomes a
strategic rather than merely a commercial resource.

## Mechanism

Petroleum differs from coal on two axes that matter more than its
chemistry. It is **fluid**, so it can be pumped, piped, and pumped again
into a ship or a vehicle without handling, which removes the labour that
coal requires at every transfer and is why oil-firing eventually beats
coal-firing at sea regardless of energy content. And it is **far more
geographically concentrated**, which is the property the twentieth-century
strategic entry downstream is built on, and which does not yet bite in this
entry's window because the consequences arrive only once navies and armies
depend on it.

The mechanism this entry owns is **substitution and stranding**. An energy
transition does not add a source to a market. It destroys the incumbent,
and it does so by making the incumbent's capital worthless while that
capital is still physically intact. Kerosene ended the American whale
fishery in roughly twenty years, and what died was not a resource but an
investment — purpose-built whaling ships with no alternative trade, the
port infrastructure of New Bedford and Nantucket, the multi-year voyage
financing arrangements, and a deep body of skill with nowhere to go. The
ships still floated. The men could still hunt whales. There was simply no
longer anyone who wanted what they produced at a price that covered the
voyage.

This is a different and crueller mechanic than depletion, and the corpus
needs both because MAR-100 already models the other one. Depletion gives
warning — catches fall, voyages lengthen, the resource visibly thins, and
capital can be withdrawn gradually. **Substitution gives none.** Demand
for the product disappears on the timescale of the substitute's
distribution, not on any timescale internal to the incumbent industry,
and the assets are worth their scrap value rather than a discounted
fraction of their former earnings. A model that treats energy transitions
as adoption curves will miss the entire cost, which falls on people and
places that did nothing wrong and had no signal to act on.

Two properties make the stranding sharper here than in most
displacements. The substitute was **cheaper and better immediately**
rather than after a learning period, so there was no interval in which
the incumbent could adapt. And the incumbent's capital was **maximally
specific** — a whaler converts to nothing, unlike a textile mill that can
weave something else.

## Social consequence

The stranded are geographically concentrated, because extractive and
maritime industries build towns around themselves. New Bedford was the
richest city per capita in the United States in the 1850s and was not by
the 1880s, and the decline is not a business cycle but a permanent
removal of the reason the place existed. Whole occupational communities
lose not their employer but their occupation, and the skills that had
been the most valuable thing a young man could acquire there become
worthless inside one working life.

On the other side, the new industry's social form is different from
coal's in a way that matters. Coal needs a large permanent workforce
underground; oil needs a small crew and a great deal of capital, so it
generates wealth without generating a comparably large working
population. A coalfield produces pit villages and unions; an oilfield
produces royalties, concessions, and a labour force that is often
imported and temporary. This asymmetry is why oil wealth accrues so
differently to the places it comes from, and it starts here rather than
in the twentieth century.

## Regional specifics

The early industry is not American and should not be written as such.
Baku produced roughly half the world's oil in 1900 and generated the
first tanker and the first major pipeline; Burma's Yenangyaung was
producing commercially under hereditary rights before Drake drilled;
Sumatra founded Royal Dutch; Romania's Ploiesti was organised slightly
ahead of Pennsylvania. The Middle East is the latecomer, with Persia
first producing in 1908, which is worth holding onto precisely because
the region's later identification with oil is invisible in this period.

The American contribution that does generalise is a property rule rather
than a technique. The rule of capture — oil belongs to whoever lifts it —
gave every leaseholder over a common reservoir an incentive to drain it
faster than his neighbours, producing over-drilling, wasted gas drive,
and permanent damage to fields that a unitised rule would have
conserved. It is a clean instance of a legal arrangement determining a
physical outcome, and it recurs wherever a fugitive resource meets
surface-based property.

## Hook

Introduces `stranded capital` — the mechanic for an energy transition,
which destroys the incumbent rather than adding to the market. Demand for
the displaced product collapses on the substitute's distribution
timescale rather than any timescale internal to the incumbent industry,
so unlike depletion it arrives with no warning signal and no interval to
adapt, and the assets fall to scrap value while still fully functional.
The loss should be sharpest where capital is most specific, which is why
a whaling fleet is destroyed where a mill would have been repurposed, and
it should land on a concentrated place rather than diffusely. Fluidity
makes oil handling-free where coal is labour-intensive at every transfer,
which is what eventually wins at sea. And a fugitive resource under a
rule of capture should be physically wasted by competitive extraction —
a property rule producing a worse physical outcome than the same field
under unified ownership.
