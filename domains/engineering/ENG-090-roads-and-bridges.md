All-weather, engineered land routes that convert travel time and reliability
from a function of terrain and season into a function of maintained
infrastructure — the land-transport equivalent of what coastal trade routes
(MAR-020) do for the sea.

## Timing

The Persian Royal Road is established by around -500; the Roman road
network expands over the following centuries; the Inca road system reaches
its full extent by around 1400, shortly before Spanish conquest in 1533
disrupts its Inca-state maintenance.

## Mechanism

A well-engineered road or bridge removes the terrain-dependent variability
that otherwise makes travel time and reliability wildly inconsistent by
season and route — an all-weather surface, graded slopes, and secure river
crossings convert a journey's duration into something predictable enough to
plan military campaigns, administrative communication, and trade logistics
around, at the cost of the sustained labor and material investment needed
to build and, critically, maintain the infrastructure against weather and
wear.

## Social consequence

Road networks concentrate a state's reach and responsiveness in a way that
correlates directly with political and military control — a road system
built primarily for message and troop movement (the Persian and Inca cases
both explicitly prioritize this) extends central authority's practical
reach over distant territory far more than an equivalent unimproved
distance would allow.

## Regional specifics

The Inca road system's scale and engineering sophistication — spanning
extreme elevation changes with rope suspension bridges rebuilt annually —
achieved with no wheeled vehicles, no large draft animals for hauling, and
no iron tools, is one of the most striking examples in this record of a
major infrastructure achievement built from a completely different
technological toolkit than its Old World counterparts. The Persian Royal
Road's explicit design around a relay messenger system predates and
parallels the more commonly cited Roman network's later, broader
multi-purpose design. Chinese infrastructure investment favoring canal and
river transport (MAR-090) over comparable long-distance road building
where navigable water is available shows road construction is one
transport strategy among several, not a universal infrastructure priority.

## Hook

Engineered roads and bridges convert travel time from terrain-and-season-
dependent to predictable and plannable, extending a state's practical
military and administrative reach — a capability the Inca case shows
doesn't require wheeled vehicles or draft animals at all, only sustained
organized labor and maintenance.
