Machine tools (ENG-010) make precise, repeatable manufacturing possible;
this entry owns what that precision is used for once it exists — components
made to a standard tight enough that any unit interchanges with any other,
without individual hand-fitting.

## Timing

Eli Whitney's 1801 demonstration popularizes the idea from that date, though
actual interchangeable-parts capability matures more gradually over the
following decades, maturing into a general "American System" of
manufacturing applied across many product categories through the 1800s.

## Mechanism

Interchangeability requires every copy of a component to fall within a
tolerance tight enough that it fits correctly with any other unit's
mating part, without the individual hand-fitting craft production always
required — this depends entirely on the precision machine tools (ENG-010)
that make consistent tolerance achievable at production speed, and once
achieved, it converts repair and assembly from a skilled craft task
(fitting this specific part to this specific machine) into an unskilled
one (swap in any unit from stock).

## Social consequence

Interchangeable parts shift value from the individual craftsman's
hand-fitting skill toward the precision of the manufacturing process itself
— a factory worker assembling standardized components needs far less
training than a gunsmith hand-fitting a musket lock, directly extending the
skill-deskilling dynamic already covered in COM-023's factory labor
discussion into mechanical assembly specifically, and enabling field repair
and maintenance (particularly for military equipment) that hand-fitted
components never allowed.

## Regional specifics

The American System's rapid domestic adoption and export as a manufacturing
philosophy makes the United States, alongside Britain, a primary source
economy for this specific industrial capability. East Asian adoption
arriving later, through deliberate Meiji-era industrial import rather than
independent development, is consistent with the broader wholesale-
institutional-adoption pattern already documented repeatedly across this
record's other Japanese modernization cases.

## Hook

Interchangeable-part manufacturing converts machine repair and assembly
from a hand-fitting craft skill into an unskilled stock-swap operation,
directly extending COM-023's factory deskilling logic into mechanical
components specifically — a capability entirely gated on the precision
machine tools (ENG-010) that make consistent tight tolerances achievable
at production speed.
