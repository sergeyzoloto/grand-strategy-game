The claim, worked out across the Upanishadic corpus, that the individual
self (atman) is not merely related to but ultimately identical with the
single underlying reality of the cosmos (Brahman) — a metaphysical
argument with a direct soteriological payoff: liberation (moksha) through
recognizing an identity that was true all along, not through acquiring
anything new.

## Timing

The Upanishads are composed across a long, layered period from roughly
-800 to -200, building on and reinterpreting the earlier Vedic corpus
rather than replacing it outright.

## Mechanism

The atman-Brahman identity claim converts the ordinary experience of being
a separate, bounded self into a case of mistaken perception (avidya,
ignorance) rather than an accurate report of how things actually are — the
philosophical work the Upanishads do is arguing for this identity through
analogy, negation (neti neti, "not this, not this," progressively
stripping away everything the self is not), and meditative instruction,
not merely asserting it as revealed dogma.

## Social consequence

A doctrine holding that ultimate liberation comes through correct
philosophical/meditative insight rather than through ritual performance
alone puts real pressure on the Vedic sacrificial priesthood's monopoly on
religious efficacy, part of the same broader ferment (alongside the
shramana movements, PHI-011, PHI-012) that produces renunciate and
forest-dwelling seekers pursuing this insight outside settled priestly
institutions entirely.

## Regional specifics

Nahua teotl philosophy's independent arrival at a single, unifying,
ultimately-real underlying principle behind apparent multiplicity is one of
this record's more striking cases of convergent metaphysics reached with
zero contact between traditions, complicating any assumption that
monist metaphysics is a distinctively Old World or Axial-Age-dependent
achievement. Chinese correlative cosmology's relational, cyclical
categories (NAT-001) pursue a structurally different explanatory project
rather than a weaker version of the same monist move, underscoring that
"a single reality behind appearances" is one distinctive philosophical
option among several coherent ones, not a default destination every
sufficiently developed tradition eventually reaches.

## Contested

Whether the Upanishads themselves commit to one coherent monist reading or
remain genuinely underdetermined between later rival schools' incompatible
interpretations is unresolved — Shankara's non-dualism (PHI-014), Ramanuja's
qualified dualism, and Madhva's full dualism all claim direct textual
grounding. Model the Upanishadic corpus as underdetermined raw material
that later systematic schools resolve in mutually exclusive directions,
rather than as already deciding the question its own later interpreters
still fight over.

## Hook

The atman-Brahman identity claim converts self-knowledge into liberation
itself rather than a precondition for some further achievement — a
distinct model from doctrines requiring ongoing ritual maintenance or
external grace, where realizing a truth that was always already the case
is sufficient, and the corpus's own genuine ambiguity about what exactly
that truth is becomes the branch point later schools (PHI-014) resolve
differently.
