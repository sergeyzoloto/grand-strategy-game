This entry owns kokutai ("national polity") as systematic ideological
argument — the doctrine that Japan's emperor is a direct divine
descendant and the nation itself a sacred, family-like religious
community, making loyalty to the state a religious rather than merely
political duty; Shinto's institutional and ritual practice (shrines,
priesthood, the imperial ritual calendar, and the state's administrative
reorganization of shrine practice) is REL-026's territory.

## Timing

Kokugaku ("National Learning") scholarship, associated especially with
Motoori Norinaga from the 1730s-70s, reconstructs a claimed "pure"
Japanese identity from ancient texts (the Kojiki) predating Buddhist and
Confucian influence; this scholarship's ideological descendants shape
Meiji-era State Shinto and kokutai doctrine from the 1868 Restoration
through its formal disestablishment following Japan's 1945 defeat.

## Mechanism

Kokutai doctrine argues Japan's imperial line descends in an unbroken
divine lineage from the sun goddess Amaterasu, making the emperor not
merely a political sovereign but the head of a sacred national family —
converting political loyalty into something closer to REL-013's
confessional-state loyalty-sorting logic, except organized around
national-familial-religious identity rather than a chosen doctrinal
confession, and requiring no conversion or profession of belief since
membership is by birth into the nation itself.

## Social consequence

Because kokutai frames the state itself as sacred and the emperor's
authority as directly divine, dissent from state policy becomes
recastable as a kind of impiety rather than merely political
disagreement — concentrating substantial `religious authority` behind the
Meiji and subsequent Japanese state in a way ordinary secular political
loyalty doesn't provide, with real consequences for how thoroughly the
doctrine could mobilize wartime sacrifice through the 1930s-40s.

## Regional specifics

Kokutai's sacralization of a specific ruling line's authority is
comparable in function, though not in specific content, to DIP-001's
tribute-legitimacy and REL-013's confessional-state mechanisms elsewhere
in this record — a state converting religious framing into concentrated
political loyalty — while its specific genealogical-divine-descent claim
and family-nation framing has no close equivalent among this record's
other sacralized-authority cases.

## Contested

Whether kokutai represents systematization of a genuinely continuous
ancient tradition or is substantially a 19th-century invented tradition
constructed for modern nation-building purposes is well-established
academic debate. Model kokutai as a specific, datable 18th-19th-century
ideological construction drawing on genuinely ancient textual material
(the Kojiki) while reorganizing it toward a modern legitimation purpose
those texts weren't originally composed for.

## Hook

Kokutai converts national loyalty into a form of `religious authority`
by birth rather than by conversion or doctrinal profession — a
sacralized-sovereignty mechanic distinct from confessional-state
loyalty-sorting (REL-013) precisely because membership requires no chosen
belief, only national birth, making the doctrine's mobilizing reach as
wide as the nation itself rather than bounded by who accepts a specific
creed.
