Nietzsche's genealogical method asks not whether a moral claim is true but
what historical power-relation produced it and whose interest it serves —
treating every other entry's normative ethical argument in this domain as a
further object of historical-psychological investigation, not a rival
first-order claim to be evaluated on its own argumentative terms.

## Timing

Nietzsche's early genealogical moves appear in Human, All Too Human (1878);
the method reaches its fullest and most systematic statement in On the
Genealogy of Morality (1887), with Nietzsche's productive period closing at
his 1889 mental collapse and the tradition's uptake continuing through his
1900 death and well beyond.

## Mechanism

Genealogy traces a moral concept's actual historical origin rather than
assessing its truth or justification directly — the master/slave morality
argument reads conventional "good" as originally meaning something like
noble, strong, and life-affirming (a valuation the powerful apply to
themselves), with "evil" a later, reactive inversion produced by the
powerless: unable to act on their resentment (ressentiment) toward the
powerful directly, they instead recode the powerful's own qualities
(strength, self-assertion, dominance) as morally evil and their own
qualities (humility, patience, meekness) as morally good, a historically
real revaluation Nietzsche argues Christian morality specifically
institutionalizes and universalizes. The method's target is general: any
moral claim, on this account, can in principle be traced to the power
interest of whichever group's revaluation produced it, rather than assessed
purely on its own argumentative merits.

## Social consequence

Applied consistently, genealogy converts every other ethical theory in this
domain into a further case study rather than a settled rival position — Kant's
categorical imperative (PHI-022), the utilitarian calculus (PHI-037), and
natural law's reason-discoverable order (PHI-013) all become, from a
genealogical standpoint, historically specific moral revaluations with
identifiable power-interested origins rather than timeless discoveries,
converting first-order ethical disagreement between those theories into a
second-order question about whose revaluation each one actually encodes.

## Regional specifics

Nietzsche develops the method in direct, explicit reaction against the
Enlightenment rational-universalist project (Kant's autonomy-grounded
morality, PHI-022, and the natural-rights tradition, PHI-019, both prominent
targets) — arguing their claimed universality and rational self-evidence is
itself exactly the kind of unexamined moral inheritance genealogy exists to
unmask, rather than a neutral, culture-independent discovery standing outside
the historical process genealogy traces everything else through.

## Contested

Whether Nietzschean genealogy is itself self-undermining is a standard,
unresolved objection: if all moral claims reduce to power interest, does the
genealogical claim itself escape that same reduction. Model genealogy as a
tool with real explanatory reach against other entries' normative claims,
whose own foundational status remains a separate, genuinely open question,
rather than as a neutral method standing outside the critique it applies to
everything else.

## Hook

A branch: supplies a meta-level "unmask the power interest behind this
claim" move usable against any other ethical-theory entry in the domain — and
is the explicit, previously-uncredited method the following generation's
structuralist and post-structuralist genealogical analysis (PHI-028's own
Foucault citation) directly inherits and radicalizes.
