Before any school forms to defend a doctrine against a named rival (PHI-001),
several major civilizations already produce and transmit systematized ethical
and cosmological reflection — through fixed maxim-collections curated by an
existing priestly or scribal institution, not through competing lineages
founded to out-argue each other.

## Timing

Egyptian instructional literature is attested from at least the Old Kingdom,
with the Instructions of Ptahhotep (traditionally associated with the Fifth
Dynasty, roughly -2400) among the earliest and best-preserved examples;
Mesopotamian scribal wisdom literature develops on an overlapping and
continuing timeline from roughly -1800; Vedic Brahmana ritual-cosmological
speculation elaborates from around -1500, continuing through the layered
composition of the Upanishads (PHI-002) that eventually turns against it.

## Mechanism

A wisdom text's authority rests on the corpus's antiquity and the
interpreting institution's standing — a scribe, priest, or court official
transmits and glosses a fixed, already-authoritative maxim-collection —
rather than on demonstrated dialectical victory over a naming rival, which is
precisely the different mechanism PHI-001's schools introduce once they
appear: a school has to keep training disciples capable of defending a
teaching in live, adversarial argument, where a wisdom-literature scribal
tradition only has to keep copying and glossing correctly.

## Social consequence

Because wisdom-literature authority is institutional and corpus-bound rather
than argument-bound, it produces a durable, low-friction ethical consensus
within the institution that curates it (the Egyptian court, the Mesopotamian
edubba, the Vedic priestly line) at the cost of having no built-in mechanism
for generating or absorbing a genuinely rival position — a wisdom tradition
doesn't refute a challenger the way a school does, it simply has no
established channel for a challenger to enter through in the first place,
which is exactly the gap PHI-001's competitive schools open once multi-state
political fragmentation gives a rival teaching somewhere else to find
patronage.

## Regional specifics

Mesopotamian wisdom literature circulates specifically as scribal-school
(edubba) copying exercise, meaning wisdom-transmission here is embedded
inside bureaucratic administrative training rather than inside any
specialized ethical-teaching institution of its own — a scribe learns Shuruppak's
instructions the same way he learns cuneiform, as professional formation, not
as a separate ethical curriculum. The Vedic Brahmana layer is not a separate
tradition preceding the Upanishads so much as their own direct scaffolding,
elaborating the ritual-correspondence thinking the Upanishads will later turn
inward against ritual performance itself. China's comparatively thin
pre-Confucian wisdom-maxim layer (no comparably datable corpus survives at
Egyptian or Mesopotamian scale) means PHI-001's Hundred Schools era has less
pre-competitive material to displace there than in the Egyptian, Mesopotamian,
or Vedic cases.

## Contested

How much genuine continuity exists between named wisdom-literature maxims and
later systematized ethics is disputed — Egyptian Ma'at's concern with cosmic
and social right-order has sometimes been argued as a distant ancestor of
later natural-law-adjacent reasoning, but the specific transmission pathway,
if any, from Egyptian or Mesopotamian material into Greek or Levantine
thought remains genuinely unresolved rather than established. Model any
specific claimed influence line as a separate, weaker, contested sub-claim,
not as something the corpus's mere existence proves.

## Hook

A gate: establishes pre-competitive, corpus-and-institution-authority ethical
reasoning as this domain's actual starting condition, with PHI-001's
school-formation mechanic readable as a direct response to and departure from
it — competitive argument displacing curated-corpus authority once
multi-state patronage competition gives a rival teaching somewhere to go —
rather than an unexplained beginning.
