The Buddha's teaching takes the diagnostic structure of a cure: suffering
(dukkha) is real and pervasive, it has an identifiable cause (craving,
tanha, rooted in ignorance of anatta, the absence of any fixed self),
that cause can be eliminated, and there is a specific practical path (the
eightfold path) for eliminating it — a systematic argument, not merely a
consoling promise.

## Timing

The Buddha teaches in the eastern Gangetic plain; historians using
corrected chronological methods now generally place his death around -400,
substantially later than the traditional Theravada dating of roughly
-544, within the same broader shramana ferment that produces Jainism
(PHI-011) in the same era and region.

## Mechanism

The Four Noble Truths structure Buddhist doctrine as applied diagnosis:
suffering's existence is the symptom, craving born of ignorance about the
self's true (non-fixed, impermanent, compounded) nature is the cause,
the cause's elimination (nirvana) is the achievable cure, and the
eightfold path (right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood,
effort, mindfulness, concentration) is the treatment regimen — each
element doing specific argumentative work rather than functioning as
loosely related teachings.

## Social consequence

Because liberation in this framework depends on directly realizing anatta
— that the sense of a fixed, continuous self is itself the root
misunderstanding generating craving and suffering — Buddhist teaching
requires sustained meditative and ethical practice to verify experientially,
not merely intellectual assent to a proposition, which is part of why the
doctrine and the institutional practice-community that sustains it (the
Sangha, REL-009) develop as tightly linked from the tradition's earliest
period rather than as separable doctrine-then-institution stages.

## Regional specifics

Buddhism's anatta doctrine directly and explicitly rejects the
Upanishadic tradition's (PHI-002) atman-Brahman identity claim — where
Vedanta locates liberation in correctly identifying the self with ultimate
reality, Buddhism denies there is any such fixed self to identify with
anything in the first place, making the two traditions' both being
products of the same regional philosophical ferment a case of direct
doctrinal rivalry, not parallel variation on a shared premise.

## Contested

Traditional Theravada chronology places the Buddha's death around 544
BCE, while most historians using corrected chronological methods now favor
a date roughly a century and a half later, around 400 BCE — a
significant discrepancy for dating Buddhism's relationship to other South
Asian developments. Model the Buddha's dates using the historians'
corrected chronology while treating traditional Buddhist chronology as a
distinct, internally significant dating tradition rather than simply an
error to be silently corrected.

## Hook

The Four Noble Truths structure suffering as a diagnosable, treatable
condition rather than a brute fact to be endured — nirvana as an
achievable cessation reached through a specified practical regimen (the
eightfold path) grounded in directly realizing anatta, a doctrine in
active, foundational rivalry with the atman-based liberation model
(PHI-002) it develops alongside.
