Shankara provides Upanishadic non-dualism (PHI-040) with its most
systematic and logically rigorous defense — Brahman as the sole
ultimately real existent, with the entire phenomenal world, including
individual selfhood, an appearance (maya) that is not simply nonexistent
but also not ultimately real in the way Brahman alone is.

## Timing

Shankara is generally dated to the early-to-mid 8th century CE, a
relatively short but highly productive life traditionally associated with
extensive travel establishing monastic centers across the Indian
subcontinent.

## Mechanism

Where the Upanishads themselves argue for atman-Brahman identity through
scattered analogy and instruction (PHI-040), Shankara constructs a
sustained logical defense using argumentative tools comparable in rigor
to Nyaya formal logic (NAT-030) — systematically working through and
rebutting rival metaphysical positions (Sankhya dualism, various Buddhist
schools) to argue maya is neither simply real (it doesn't survive correct
philosophical analysis) nor simply unreal (it's experienced and has
practical, if not ultimate, effect), a genuinely difficult intermediate
category the argument's rigor is specifically built to defend.

## Social consequence

Shankara is also credited with founding a network of monastic centers
(mathas) that institutionalize and transmit Advaita teaching across
generations — the specific institutional/monastic-order dimension of
this achievement belongs to REL's fuller territory (not yet written); this
entry's concern is the argument the institution was built to preserve and
transmit, on the same schools-as-transmission-mechanism logic PHI-030
describes generally.

## Regional specifics

Advaita becomes one of several rival Vedanta schools rather than the
uncontested reading of the Upanishadic corpus — Ramanuja's qualified
dualism and Madhva's full dualism both develop as direct responses
arguing Shankara's radical non-dualism misreads the same source texts,
each claiming its own faithful continuity with the underlying Upanishadic
tradition (PHI-040) Shankara also claims to be systematizing correctly.

## Contested

Rival dualist commentators accused Advaita of being "crypto-Buddhism,"
covertly reproducing Madhyamaka Buddhist dialectic about the unreality of
ordinary experience while officially rejecting Buddhism and claiming
Vedic orthodoxy. Whether this identifies genuine argumentative debt or is
primarily rival-school polemic is disputed among scholars. Model Advaita
as making a distinct metaphysical claim (Brahman as sole positively-
existing reality, not Buddhist emptiness of inherent existence) while
treating the historical influence question as separately and genuinely
open.

## Hook

Shankara converts the Upanishads' scattered analogical case for
atman-Brahman identity into a systematically defended logical position
capable of directly out-arguing rival schools (Sankhya dualism, Buddhist
anatta, PHI-090) on their own argumentative terms — raising the
doctrine's `intellectual authority` by meeting rival traditions'
formal rigor rather than simply asserting scriptural priority over them.
