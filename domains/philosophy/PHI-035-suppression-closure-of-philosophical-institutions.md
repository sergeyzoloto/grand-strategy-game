A sufficiently motivated and capable state can destroy accumulated
intellectual-transmission capacity in a single decree — converting
`intellectual authority` (PHI-001's stat) from something that erodes
gradually through competitive failure into something directly,
catastrophically destructible by external political action the school
itself has no say in.

## Timing

The Qin state's burning of non-Legalist texts and execution of scholars
dates to 213–212 BCE, immediately following the dynasty's unification and
directly continuing the Legalist state's own operative policy (PHI-008);
Justinian's edict closing the Athenian philosophical schools dates to 529 CE,
ending the Academy's roughly nine centuries of continuous operation since its
founding by Plato.

## Mechanism

Where PHI-001 models a school's long-term survival as a function of its own
sustained disciple-training capacity — a school can fail to train successors
and fade regardless of its founder's brilliance — this entry models the other
way a school's accumulated `intellectual authority` disappears: an external
state, possessing sufficient administrative and coercive reach, simply
prohibits the school's texts, disperses or executes its scholars, or revokes
its institutional charter, zeroing out centuries of accumulated transmission
capacity in the time it takes to issue and enforce a single order, with no
built-in recovery mechanism the school's own remaining disciples control.

## Social consequence

Because this kind of loss is externally imposed rather than competitively
earned, it can eliminate a philosophically dominant or well-regarded
tradition exactly as readily as a marginal one — the Qin's targets included
established, actively-patronized rival schools (PHI-004's Confucians
prominent among them), not merely fringe or already-declining lineages, and
the Academy at the time of its closure was still an active center of Neo-
Platonic teaching, not a moribund institution already fading on its own —
meaning a state's capacity and willingness to suppress, not any measure of a
school's own argumentative or institutional strength, is what actually
determines whether this kind of loss occurs.

## Regional specifics

The Qin case is the direct, otherwise-unmodeled aftermath of PHI-008's own
Legalist state ideology, occurring immediately after the unification PHI-008's
own Timing section stops at — Legalism's amoral, uniformly-enforced
statecraft extends here to eliminating rival doctrinal authority outright,
not merely outcompeting it for patronage. Justinian's closure of the Athenian
schools on stated grounds of persistent paganism shows the same
capability-destruction mechanism recurring in a religiously-motivated rather
than statecraft-motivated register, nine centuries and an entirely different
civilization removed from the Qin case, with the shared underlying mechanic
(a state simply ends the institution) constant across both.

## Hook

A pool-drain event: `intellectual authority` is not only built and lost
through PHI-001's ordinary competitive-transmission-failure mechanic but is
directly, catastrophically destructible by external state action regardless
of the targeted school's own current strength or standing — with no
guarantee of recovery on any timescale the school's surviving disciples
themselves control.
