This entry owns Daoism as philosophical text and argument — wu wei
(non-action, or effortless action aligned with the Dao) and ziran
(spontaneous naturalness) as a systematic alternative to both Confucian
ritual cultivation (PHI-004) and Legalist state technique (PHI-008); the
later institutional, ritual, and priestly development of organized Daoist
religion is REL's fuller territory, not yet written.

## Timing

The Daodejing and the Zhuangzi both take shape across the Warring States
period, roughly -600 to -200, alongside and in active dialogue with the
same Hundred Schools ferment (PHI-001) that produces Confucianism,
Legalism, and Mohism.

## Mechanism

Wu wei argues that the most effective action is action that doesn't force
or contend against the natural grain of a situation — a ruler who governs
by wu wei achieves order by not imposing elaborate structure (a direct
inversion of both li's structured ritual cultivation, PHI-004, and
Legalism's maximal state intervention, PHI-008), letting outcomes emerge
from things being allowed to follow their own nature (ziran) rather than
from deliberate program or decree.

## Social consequence

Because wu wei explicitly devalues striving, ambition, and the pursuit of
conventional social distinction, it offers a systematic philosophical
refuge for scholars and officials disillusioned with or excluded from
court politics — a socially legible "drop out and align with the Dao
instead" option that Confucian and Legalist officialdom, both fully
invested in active participation in state affairs, don't provide.

## Regional specifics

Greek Cynicism's rejection of convention (PHI-010) lands in a similar
anti-conventional territory through Diogenes's confrontational public
flouting of social norms specifically to expose others' hypocrisy — a
performative, dialectically aggressive stance quite different from wu
wei's quieter cultivation of alignment with an impersonal cosmic process,
showing rejection-of-convention itself can be pursued through opposite
social postures.

## Contested

Whether the Daodejing has one identifiable historical author or is a
composite text compiled and edited across generations, and its precise
chronological relationship to the Zhuangzi, remains genuinely unresolved
in textual-critical scholarship. Model early Daoism as a textual tradition
with real compositional uncertainty rather than assuming a single
founder-philosopher comparable to PHI-003's Socrates or PHI-011's
Mahavira.

## Hook

Wu wei models effective action as withdrawal of forced intervention
rather than its intensification — a direct mechanical inverse of both
Confucian ritual cultivation (PHI-004) and Legalist state technique
(PHI-008), offering a legible "align with natural process rather than
impose structure" alternative strategy for both individual conduct and
governance.
