Across the 18th and 19th centuries, European thought increasingly treats
"society" itself — not just political sovereignty (PHI-005's polis) and
not just aggregated individuals (PHI-019's social contract) — as a
coherent object with its own laws and structure, amenable to systematic
study in its own right.

## Timing

Montesquieu's comparative study of how climate, custom, and law shape
different societies (The Spirit of the Laws, 1748) is an early landmark;
Rousseau's concept of the general will follows in the 1760s; the
trajectory culminates in Durkheim's 1893 argument for "social facts" as a
genuinely distinct category of causal explanation, founding sociology as
a discipline in its own right.

## Mechanism

Durkheim's core claim is that social phenomena (suicide rates, religious
practice, moral norms) have social causes irreducible to the psychology
of the individuals making them up — a suicide rate, for instance, tracks
a society's degree of social integration and regulation in ways no
individual psychological explanation captures on its own, treating
"society" as a real causal-explanatory level between the individual
(PHI-019's social-contract starting point) and the state (PHI-005's polis)
rather than collapsing into either.

## Social consequence

Once society itself becomes a legitimate object of systematic study and,
eventually, deliberate intervention, social problems (crime, poverty,
declining social cohesion) become framed as susceptible to social-
scientific diagnosis and social policy response, rather than being left
entirely to either individual moral failing or a purely political/legal
remedy — a new available register for addressing collective problems
neither PHI-005's civic virtue nor PHI-019's individual-rights framework
supplies on its own.

## Regional specifics

Ubuntu philosophy's relational-personhood starting point — that a person
only becomes a person through relationship to other persons, with
community ontologically prior to the individual — inverts social-
contract theory's individuals-first logic entirely, and sits somewhat to
the side of Durkheim's framing too, since Durkheim's social facts still
presuppose individuals as a separate explanatory level society acts upon,
where Ubuntu denies the individual is even coherently specifiable prior to
relationship in the first place — a genuinely distinct foundational
starting point for social philosophy, not a variant of either European
position.

## Contested

Whether Ubuntu represents continuous ancient tradition or substantially a
modern, post-apartheid-era systematization projected onto older
communal practices is genuinely debated among Africanist philosophers.
Model Ubuntu as a philosophically serious relational-personhood framework
with a specific, datable 20th-century systematization, while treating
claims of deep unbroken historical continuity as a separate, contested
question — consistent with how this project treats comparable "invented
tradition" cases elsewhere (DIP-006, PHI-025).

## Hook

Treating society as its own causal-explanatory level, irreducible to
either individual psychology (PHI-019) or the polis's civic virtue
(PHI-005), opens a distinct social-policy register for collective
problems — and Ubuntu's relational-personhood alternative shows even the
starting assumption that individuals exist prior to and separable from
society is a specific philosophical choice, not a neutral default every
social theory has to share.
