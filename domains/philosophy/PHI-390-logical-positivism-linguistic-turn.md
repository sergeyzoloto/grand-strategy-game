Wittgenstein's early Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (language pictures
facts; philosophy's proper job is clarifying what can meaningfully be
said) supplies the Vienna Circle with a foundation for the verification
principle — a statement is meaningful only if empirically verifiable or
true by logical/definitional necessity, ruling out traditional
metaphysics, theology, and much normative ethics as not false but
literally meaningless — until the principle's own collapse redirects
philosophy toward Wittgenstein's later, very different linguistic
turn.

## Timing

Wittgenstein publishes the Tractatus in 1921; the Vienna Circle
formalizes logical positivism around the verification principle through
the 1920s-30s; Wittgenstein's posthumously published Philosophical
Investigations (1953) marks the fullest statement of his later, reversed
position.

## Mechanism

The verification principle, applied consistently, faces an immediate
self-application problem its critics quickly identified: the principle
itself is neither empirically verifiable nor true by logical necessity,
meaning it fails its own meaningfulness test — a foundational internal
collapse, not merely an external objection, that leaves logical
positivism unable to sustain its central criterion in the form originally
proposed. Wittgenstein's own later philosophy abandons the picture-theory
of language entirely, arguing instead that meaning arises from a word's
use within a "language game" embedded in a form of life, with many of
philosophy's traditional problems arising specifically from language
being taken out of its ordinary working context and requiring
dissolution through careful attention to actual use, not a positive
metaphysical thesis to be proven or disproven.

## Social consequence

The shift from verification-as-meaning-criterion to use-as-meaning
converts philosophy's characteristic activity, for a substantial
following generation of Anglo-American philosophers, from constructing
grand systematic theories toward painstaking, therapeutic clarification of
how specific expressions actually function in ordinary language —
"ordinary language philosophy" becomes its own distinct, influential
mid-20th-century school (J.L. Austin, the later Wittgenstein's direct
successors) built on treating many inherited philosophical puzzles as
symptoms of linguistic confusion rather than as genuine problems requiring
substantive answers.

## Regional specifics

This entry's verification-to-use trajectory runs on the same broad
foundation as NAT-030's formal logic and NAT-090's empirical method —
logical positivism explicitly modeled meaningful discourse on natural-
scientific verification as the gold standard — making its own collapse a
notable case of a movement's founding methodological confidence in
formal/empirical rigor undermining, rather than vindicating, its own
central thesis once that thesis was subjected to the same rigor it
demanded of everything else.

## Hook

Verificationism's self-application failure converts philosophy's own
methodological confidence against itself, redirecting a major
mid-20th-century tradition from constructing grand verifiable systems
toward painstaking clarification of ordinary language use — a "linguistic
turn" whose treatment of inherited philosophical puzzles as often
resolvable (or dissolvable) through attention to language itself, rather
than through further metaphysical argument, marks a lasting change in
what doing philosophy is even understood to involve.
