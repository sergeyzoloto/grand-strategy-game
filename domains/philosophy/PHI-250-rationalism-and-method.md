Rationalism argues substantial knowledge is attainable through reason
alone, independent of and prior to sensory experience — Descartes's
methodological skepticism (doubting everything possible until reaching an
indubitable foundation) inaugurates the approach, with Spinoza's
geometric-method monism and Leibniz's rationalist metaphysics extending
it in the following decades.

## Timing

Descartes publishes his Discourse on Method in 1637, with the cogito
argument ("I think, therefore I am") supplying rationalism's founding
indubitable certainty; Spinoza's Ethics and Leibniz's mature metaphysics
extend the rationalist program through the following decades, to
Leibniz's death in 1716.

## Mechanism

Descartes's method systematically doubts every belief that admits even
the slightest possible doubt — sensory experience, mathematical truth, the
existence of an external world — searching for something that survives
even the most extreme skeptical challenge; the act of doubting itself
turns out to require a doubter, giving Descartes his one indubitable
foundation (the cogito), from which he then attempts to rebuild
certain knowledge of God and the external world by reason alone, treating
sense experience as a secondary, less reliable source of knowledge than
clear and distinct rational perception.

## Social consequence

By grounding certainty in individual, unaided rational reflection rather
than in inherited scriptural or scholastic authority (PHI-220),
rationalism supplies each individual thinker, in principle, with the same
independent authority to reach truth that theological or classical
authority previously held more exclusively — a democratizing epistemic
move in principle, even though rationalism's actual practitioners
remained a narrow, highly educated elite in practice.

## Regional specifics

Zera Yacob's 1667 Hatata, composed in Ethiopia, independently develops a
methodical-doubt argument strikingly parallel to Descartes's own,
subjecting inherited religious and scriptural authority to systematic
rational scrutiny and grounding reliable belief in reason alone — with no
documented contact whatsoever between Yacob and the roughly
contemporaneous Cartesian tradition, making this one of this record's
clearest cases of genuinely convergent rationalist method arising
independently rather than diffusing outward from Europe.

## Contested

Descartes's own foundationalist project faces the unresolved "Cartesian
Circle" objection: he uses clear-and-distinct perception to prove a
non-deceiving God exists, then invokes that God to guarantee clear-and-
distinct perception's general reliability, an apparently circular
argument first raised by his contemporary Arnauld and still debated
without consensus resolution. Model the cogito's own narrow certainty as
secure while treating the wider project of certifying all clear-and-
distinct perception as reliable knowledge as resting on an argument many
specialists consider not fully successful.

## Hook

Cartesian method relocates the foundation of certain knowledge from
inherited authority (PHI-220's scholastic tradition) to individual
rational reflection alone — a move independently reproduced by Zera
Yacob with zero contact between the two traditions, though the wider
claim that reason alone can then certify further knowledge beyond the
cogito's narrow certainty remains a specifically contested extension.
