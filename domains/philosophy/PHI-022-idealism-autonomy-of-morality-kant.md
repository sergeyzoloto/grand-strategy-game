Kant argues the rationalism-versus-empiricism dispute (PHI-018 versus
PHI-019) rests on a shared mistake: both sides assume the mind either
passively receives a ready-made world (empiricism) or accesses one
directly through pure reason (rationalism), when in fact the mind
actively structures all possible experience through built-in categories
(space, time, causation) — and Kant then grounds morality not in
consequence, virtue, or divine command, but in the rational agent's own
self-legislated autonomy.

## Timing

Kant publishes the Critique of Pure Reason in 1781, working through its
implications for ethics (the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals,
1785) and politics in the following two decades, through his death in
Königsberg in 1804.

## Mechanism

Transcendental idealism argues space, time, and causal structure aren't
features of reality as it is "in itself" but are the mind's own
necessary forms for organizing any possible experience — resolving the
rationalism/empiricism dispute by making both sensory content
(empiricism's contribution) and organizing mental structure (rationalism's
contribution) jointly necessary for any experience at all. The categorical
imperative extends the same autonomy-centered logic into ethics: a moral
law is binding only if a rational agent could consistently will it as a
universal law for all rational agents — moral worth comes specifically
from self-legislated rational duty, not from calculating consequences
(directly rivaling PHI-009's consequentialism) or from external command
(rivaling divine-command and natural-law framings, PHI-013).

## Social consequence

Grounding moral worth in autonomous rational self-legislation rather than
in consequence, inherited virtue, or obedience to external authority gives
every rational agent, in principle, equal standing as a source of moral
law regardless of social position — a radically leveling implication in
principle, converting moral dignity into something a rational agent
possesses by virtue of being rational at all, not by virtue of rank,
lineage, or divine favor.

## Regional specifics

Kant's categorical imperative and Mohist consequentialism (PHI-009) reach
sharply opposed conclusions about what actually makes an action right —
universalizable rational duty regardless of outcome, versus aggregate
benefit regardless of the action's form — from comparably rigorous and
systematic starting points, a genuine, load-bearing fork in this record's
ethical theory rather than a difference reducible to one tradition simply
being less developed than the other.

## Hook

Kant relocates moral worth from consequence or external command to
autonomous rational self-legislation — a universalizability test any
rational agent can in principle apply regardless of social position,
standing in direct, symmetric rivalry with consequentialist (PHI-009) and
natural-law/command-based (PHI-013) alternatives rather than superseding
them, and setting up the immediate philosophical target (an autonomous,
self-transparent rational subject) that psychoanalysis's discovery of the
unconscious (PHI-023) will directly put in question.
