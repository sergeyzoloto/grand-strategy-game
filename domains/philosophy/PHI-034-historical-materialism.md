Historical materialism argues a society's mode of production — who controls
productive property, and the resulting class relation — determines its
legal, political, and ideological superstructure, not the reverse,
converting Enlightenment natural-rights and consent theory (PHI-019) from a
timeless discovered truth into a historically specific class position with a
material interest in its own universalization.

## Timing

Marx's early formulation of the materialist conception of history dates from
the mid-1840s (The German Ideology, co-written with Engels, 1845–46); Capital's
first volume (1867) develops the mature critique of political economy this
historical claim rests on; Marx's death in 1883 closes the period of his own
direct authorship, with the framework's elaboration and contestation
continuing well beyond it.

## Mechanism

Where Locke's and Hobbes's social-contract theory (PHI-019) treats political
legitimacy as resting on rational consent independent of any particular
economic arrangement, historical materialism argues the reverse: a society's
underlying mode of production and the class relation it produces (who owns
productive property, who must sell their labor to survive) generates and
sustains a legal and political superstructure — including natural-rights
theory itself — that serves the interest of the class controlling
production, converting PHI-019's claimed universal, reason-discoverable
political truth into something this entry treats as a historically specific
and contestable class position rather than a neutral finding independent of
who benefits from it being believed.

## Social consequence

Because historical materialism frames legal and political ideas as
downstream of economic class interest rather than as free-standing rational
discovery, it converts political and philosophical disagreement itself into
a symptom of underlying class conflict — a ruling class's ideas function, on
this account, as the ruling ideas of the age not because they're more
rationally compelling but because that class controls the material means of
producing and disseminating ideas, a claim with direct, still-live
consequences for how any later institutional response to capitalism (FIN-014)
gets read: as neutral reform, or as the successful containment of a
challenge this framework predicts must recur as long as the underlying class
relation persists.

## Regional specifics

Marx develops the framework in direct engagement with British industrial
capitalism specifically (Capital's empirical material is substantially drawn
from English factory conditions and Parliamentary Blue Book inspection
reports), while writing and organizing from exile across Germany, Belgium,
France, and England — a genuinely transnational European intellectual
production process responding to a specifically British industrial case
study.

## Contested

Whether historical materialism is a strict determinist claim or a looser
"in the last instance" influence claim allowing real superstructural
autonomy is disputed within Marxist scholarship itself, not only by external
critics — a dispute later Frankfurt-School critical theory (PHI-030) is
substantially a response to, since strict determinism struggles to explain
how mass culture could function as effective ideological containment rather
than simple base-driven reflection. Model the determinism strength as the
open, contestable variable, not a settled orthodox reading either direction.

## Hook

A branch and a gate: reframes any other entry's systematic argument in this
domain as potentially ideological superstructure rather than disinterested
reasoning — a genuinely different, symmetric-rivalry-worthy register than
anything else here — and is the explicit theoretical prerequisite the
Frankfurt School's culture-industry critique (PHI-030) and any institutional
response to capitalism (FIN-014) both build directly against or on top of.
