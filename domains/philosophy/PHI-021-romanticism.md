Romanticism reacts against Enlightenment rationalism's (PHI-018, PHI-019)
universalizing, mechanistic picture of humans and nature, insisting
instead on the irreducible importance of emotion, individual genius, and
the organic historical particularity of specific peoples and cultures.

## Timing

Romantic thought develops from roughly 1770, with Herder's writings on
language and cultural particularity especially influential from the
1770s-80s, through the movement's flourishing across German, British, and
French thought into the mid-19th century.

## Mechanism

Herder's central argument is that each people (Volk) possesses its own
distinct spirit (Volksgeist) expressed most fully through its own
language, folklore, and history — a direct rejection of the Enlightenment
assumption (PHI-019) that reason and political principle operate the same
way for any rational agent regardless of cultural particularity, arguing
instead that a people's authentic self-expression can only be properly
understood and valued on its own particular cultural terms, not measured
against one universal rational standard.

## Social consequence

Because Romantic thought treats a people's language and folk culture as
the authentic seat of its distinct spirit, it supplies a ready-made
philosophical vocabulary for treating "the German people" or "the Slavic
peoples," say, as coherent entities with their own legitimate claim to
self-expression and self-governance — vocabulary later cultural and
political nationalist movements draw on directly and explicitly, well
beyond Romanticism's own original literary and philosophical concerns.

## Regional specifics

Romantic nationalism's vocabulary of distinct peoples each possessing an
authentic collective spirit feeds directly into 19th-century political
nationalism's diplomatic recognition claims (DIP-010) — Herder's
philosophical argument for cultural particularity supplies the underlying
intellectual case that a "people" is a real, coherent unit deserving
recognition, prior to and independent of DIP-010's specifically
diplomatic question of when and how that claim gets converted into actual
international recognition and border legitimacy.

## Hook

Romantic Volksgeist supplies the philosophical vocabulary — a people's
authentic spirit expressed through language and culture — that later
political nationalism (DIP-010) converts into a diplomatic recognition
claim, with the philosophical argument for cultural particularity
developing as a rejection of Enlightenment universalism (PHI-018, PHI-019)
well before it acquires any specific political program of its own.
