Formal credit extended to individual households for consumption rather
than production — furniture and appliance installment plans, then
revolving credit, extending purchasing power ahead of income in a way
previously available mainly to merchants and producers.

## Timing

Installment purchase plans for consumer goods develop through the 1800s;
the modern revolving credit card (from 1958) generalizes the concept into
a single standing credit instrument usable across any participating
merchant.

## Mechanism

Installment and revolving consumer credit let a household's purchasing
power exceed current income by borrowing against expected future earnings,
extending the same basic credit logic FIN-004 covers for productive lending
into ordinary household consumption — the lender's risk assessment shifts
from evaluating a specific business venture's prospects to evaluating an
individual's income stability and repayment history, a fundamentally
different and much more distributed underwriting problem once done at mass
consumer scale.

## Social consequence

Widespread consumer credit access changes household consumption timing
fundamentally, letting purchases happen ahead of savings accumulation
rather than only after it — a genuine expansion of material living standard
timing for households with stable income, paired with a real risk of
debt-driven financial fragility for households whose income proves less
stable than the credit terms assumed.

## Regional specifics

The credit card's generalization of installment credit into one
merchant-agnostic revolving instrument is a specifically American
innovation that spreads internationally as a service industry over the
following decades. Rotating savings and credit associations across West
Africa and comparable arrangements elsewhere achieve broad consumer credit
access with no formal lending institution, interest charge, or collateral
requirement at all — social trust and reciprocal obligation substituting
entirely for the underwriting apparatus formal consumer lending depends on,
a genuinely different institutional solution to the same basic need for
credit ahead of savings.

## Hook

Formal consumer credit extends household purchasing power ahead of income
by shifting underwriting from business-venture assessment to individual
income-stability assessment at mass scale — a function informal
reciprocal-obligation institutions like rotating savings associations
achieve through social trust alone, with no formal lending apparatus,
interest charge, or collateral requirement at all.
