The foundational fiscal infrastructure this entire domain sits on top of:
knowing who holds what land, who lives where, and what each owes — without
which credit, currency, and banking have nothing reliable to anchor
sovereign obligation to.

## Timing

Systematic land survey and tax-liability record-keeping is attested from
roughly -3000 in Mesopotamia and Egypt; comparably fine-grained household
census registration develops independently in China from around -350; the
Inca quipu system reaches full territorial scale by the 1450s, shortly
before Spanish conquest ends its independent development.

## Mechanism

A cadastre (land registry) and census convert an ungoverned, un-inventoried
population and territory into a legible resource base a state can reliably
tax, conscript from, and borrow against — this is the same `legibility`
mechanic already central to this record's military and health domains,
applied here as this domain's own foundation: every later financial
instrument (bonds, paper money, central banking) ultimately rests on a
state's credible capacity to make good on obligations, which in turn rests
on knowing what it can actually extract from its own territory.

## Social consequence

Registration cuts both ways for the registered population: it enables more
predictable, negotiable tax burdens than arbitrary ad hoc extraction would,
but it also removes the option of going unnoticed — a household that
successfully stayed off a ruler's radar under a weaker administrative
system loses that option once cadastral survey and census reach it,
regardless of whether the resulting tax burden is fairer or heavier than
before.

## Regional specifics

Han China's household-level registration granularity, tying census directly
to both conscription and land-tax liability, gives the state an unusually
precise fiscal and manpower picture relative to contemporary systems
elsewhere. The Inca quipu system achieves comparable administrative
function — tracking population, tribute, and storehouse inventory across a
vast territory — through an entirely non-written recording technology,
showing state legibility doesn't require a script, only a sufficiently
rigorous and legible recording convention of some kind. Early Mesopotamian
and Egyptian systems predate both but generally operate at coarser
(settlement or estate level rather than individual household) resolution,
a difference in fiscal granularity rather than in the basic administrative
concept.

## Hook

Cadastral survey and census convert territory and population into taxable,
conscriptable, legible state resources — the `legibility` foundation every
later financial instrument in this domain (credit, paper money, sovereign
bonds) ultimately depends on, since none of them mean anything without a
state credibly able to make good on what it owes.
