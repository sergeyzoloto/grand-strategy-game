This entry owns the financial innovation itself — permanent capital raised
from many investors as freely tradable shares, ownership separated from
day-to-day management; `COM-150` owns what a chartered company built on top
of this instrument did as a trade monopoly and quasi-sovereign actor.

## Timing

The Dutch East India Company's 1602 charter is the first major joint-stock
company to issue permanent, continuously tradable shares rather than
capital committed to a single voyage and liquidated afterward; the
instrument matures and occasionally overheats (the 1720 South Sea Bubble,
FIN-080) over the following century.

## Mechanism

Earlier trading ventures raised capital per voyage, paying investors back
(with profit or loss) once that specific voyage concluded — the joint-stock
innovation instead raises permanent capital that never has to be returned,
with investor liquidity provided instead by selling shares to someone else
on a secondary market, decoupling an individual investor's ability to exit
an investment from the underlying venture's own need for continuous,
uninterrupted capital. This also separates ownership from management for
the first time at scale: a shareholder need not run the business or even
understand its operations in detail, only trust the accounting (FIN-060)
reporting on it.

## Social consequence

Tradable shares let capital flow toward productive use from investors who
have no direct expertise in or control over that use, at the cost of a
genuinely new principal-agent problem — the managers actually running the
company have interests that may diverge from shareholders who can't
directly observe or control day-to-day decisions, a tension this
instrument doesn't resolve so much as trade for the liquidity and
capital-pooling benefits it provides.

## Regional specifics

Islamic partnership structures achieve comparable capital-pooling and
risk-sharing without developing a permanent, freely-tradable share
instrument — ventures typically dissolve and reform rather than persisting
as a continuously-owned entity, achieving this entry's risk-distribution
function without its specific liquidity innovation. This is a genuinely
Europe-concentrated financial innovation in its origin; no comparably early
independent parallel is well documented elsewhere in this record.

## Hook

Permanent, freely-tradable share capital decouples an individual investor's
liquidity from a venture's own continuous capital needs and separates
ownership from management for the first time at scale — solving the
capital-pooling problem partnership structures elsewhere already solved
differently, while introducing a durable principal-agent tension neither
approach fully resolves.
