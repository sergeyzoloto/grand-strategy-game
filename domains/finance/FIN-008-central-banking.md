A single institution, typically state-chartered but often operationally
distinct from the treasury, entrusted with issuing currency, managing the
money supply, and — once the role fully matures — acting as lender of last
resort to prevent an ordinary banking panic (FIN-010) from cascading into a
systemic collapse.

## Timing

The Sveriges Riksbank (1668) and Bank of England (1694) are early cases,
initially organized more as government-debt-financing vehicles than as
monetary regulators in the modern sense; the lender-of-last-resort function
matures through the 1800s, with the U.S. Federal Reserve's 1913 founding
among the last major industrial powers to formalize a comparable
institution.

## Mechanism

Concentrating currency issuance and money-supply management in one
institution converts monetary policy from a byproduct of scattered,
uncoordinated commercial bank behavior into a single coordinated lever a
state can use to expand or contract credit conditions — the lender-of-last-
resort function specifically lets the central bank inject liquidity into a
panicking banking system without any single commercial bank needing to
absorb that entire risk itself, directly interrupting the self-reinforcing
collapse dynamic FIN-010 describes.

## Social consequence

A credible central bank's currency and credit backing directly extends a
state's `sovereign credit capacity` — creditors lend to a government (or to
that government's banking system) more readily when a central institution
stands behind the currency and can act as crisis backstop, converting an
otherwise abstract sovereign promise into something with an operational
mechanism behind it.

## Regional specifics

The Bank of Japan's establishment as a deliberate Meiji-era institutional
import fits the broader pattern of wholesale adoption already seen
repeatedly across this record's other Japanese modernization cases. The
Ottoman Bank's foreign capital and management, serving external creditor
interests more than independent domestic monetary authority, shows a
nominally equivalent institution can diverge sharply in actual function and
loyalty from the domestically-controlled model — the label "central bank"
doesn't guarantee the same institutional role everywhere it's applied.

## Hook

A central bank converts scattered monetary conditions into one coordinated
policy lever and, once the lender-of-last-resort function matures, a crisis
backstop that can arrest a banking panic (FIN-010) before it cascades — its
credibility directly extending a state's `sovereign credit capacity`, though
a nominally central institution can just as easily serve external creditors
as its own domestic economy, depending on who actually controls it.
