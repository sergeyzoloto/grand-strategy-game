Recording every transaction twice — once as a debit, once as a matching
credit — so that a bookkeeping error becomes mathematically detectable
(the two sides stop balancing) rather than silently accumulating unnoticed.

## Timing

Chinese four-column accounting methods achieve comparable self-checking
rigor from around 1000; Italian merchant practice develops double-entry
technique through the 1300s-1400s, with Luca Pacioli's 1494 published
treatise the first widely-disseminated codification of the Western method.

## Mechanism

A self-balancing accounting system converts bookkeeping from a record a
merchant simply has to trust into one that mathematically audits itself —
if the two sides of every recorded transaction don't sum to match, an error
exists somewhere in the books, findable and correctable before it
compounds. This is a direct multiplier on `contract enforcement` and
investor trust: a merchant, partner, or creditor can verify a set of
accounts' internal consistency without needing to independently re-derive
every underlying transaction from scratch.

## Social consequence

Reliable, auditable accounts make it practical for owners to delegate
day-to-day business management to employees or partners at a physical and
temporal distance, since performance and honesty become verifiable through
the books rather than requiring the owner's constant direct oversight — a
precondition for the joint-stock and multi-partner commercial structures
(FIN-006) that develop in the following centuries.

## Regional specifics

Chinese four-column accounting reaches comparable arithmetic self-checking
rigor centuries before Pacioli's codification, developing independently
within Song government and merchant practice — a case, similar to gunpowder
and paper money elsewhere in this record, where a major accounting
innovation's usual "Renaissance Italy" attribution understates real prior
achievement elsewhere. Islamic merchant accounting's sophisticated
running-balance and partnership-profit tracking, without clear evidence of
the specific self-balancing dual-entry mechanism, leaves a genuine open
question about whether an equivalent tool existed there in a form that
simply wasn't preserved.

## Contested

Whether Pacioli's Italian codification was independently developed or
influenced by prior contact with more advanced accounting traditions to the
east is disputed, given how much earlier the Chinese method achieves
comparable function. Model the two traditions as independent solutions to
the same underlying problem by default, since direct transmission evidence
is thin, while treating the timeline gap itself as a genuinely open
historical question rather than resolving it either way.

## Hook

A self-balancing accounting method converts bookkeeping into a self-auditing
record, directly multiplying `contract enforcement` and investor trust by
letting a distant owner or partner verify accounts without independently
re-deriving every transaction — the specific precondition that makes
delegated, multi-partner commercial structures (FIN-006) practical at scale.
