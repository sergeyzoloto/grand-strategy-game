Before a state can tax or a lender can charge interest, a population needs a shared, portable, divisible, and verifiable unit everyone accepts as representing value — the precondition every later entry in this domain silently assumes is already solved.

## Timing

Commodity money (cattle, grain, standardized metal ingots) precedes true coinage by millennia and is attested wherever organized exchange exists from at least -3000; Chinese bronze currency develops its own distinctive cast-metal tradition from roughly -1000; Lydian electrum coinage, stamped with a royal guarantee of weight and purity, emerges around -600 and spreads rapidly across the Mediterranean within a century.

## Mechanism

Commodity money solves the barter problem — trading goods directly requires each party to want exactly what the other offers — by settling on some widely-wanted good as an intermediate store of value, but still requires weighing, tasting, or otherwise verifying quality case by case. Stamped coinage solves this more completely: a government's stamp guarantees a specific weight and purity of metal, converting value verification from an ongoing case-by-case judgment into a one-time act of trust in the issuing authority, after which the coin can simply be counted rather than assessed anew in every transaction.

## Social consequence

Once a state issues and guarantees its own coinage, debasement (reducing the actual precious-metal content while maintaining the stamped face value) becomes a standing temptation and a direct, quantifiable measure of a ruler's fiscal desperation or restraint — a population's trust in coinage is trust in a specific issuing authority's honesty, not in the metal alone, the same underlying trust relationship this record's later paper-currency and central-banking entries inherit and scale up.

## Regional specifics

Lydian electrum coinage's rapid adoption across Greek city-states shows how quickly a genuinely superior value-verification technology can spread once demonstrated. Chinese bronze currency's entirely distinct design lineage — cast tool and spade shapes maturing into round coins with square holes — achieves the identical underlying function through a materially unrelated minting tradition, showing coinage's core innovation (government-guaranteed, instantly verifiable value) doesn't require any particular physical form. Pre-contact American economies functioning successfully for centuries on barter, tribute-in-kind, and cacao-bean currency without ever standardizing into stamped metal shows coinage is one successful solution to the value-verification problem, not the only one available to a sophisticated economy.

## Contested

Whether stamped coinage was independently invented in Lydia, India, and China within a few centuries of each other, or diffused outward from a single origin point along trade contact, is genuinely disputed given how thin the early evidence is. Model each regional tradition's specific minting technique and iconography as most likely independent, while treating the underlying idea's possible diffusion across contact zones as a live, unresolved question.

## Hook

An origin gate: establishes the portable, verifiable unit of value FIN-001's tax assessments and FIN-004's interest-bearing loans both silently assume already exists — with a state's own temptation to debase its coinage becoming the first, oldest instance of the issuer-trust problem this domain's every later currency mechanic inherits.
