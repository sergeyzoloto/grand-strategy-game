A shareholder's potential loss is capped at their original investment,
regardless of how much debt or liability the company itself incurs — a
legal innovation that decouples personal financial ruin from business risk-
taking.

## Timing

Restrictive early charters granting the privilege selectively date earlier,
but general, accessible incorporation statutes extending limited liability
without requiring a special legislative act begin with New York's 1811
manufacturing law, with Britain's more comprehensive general Companies Act
following in 1862.

## Mechanism

Without limited liability, an investor in a joint-stock venture (FIN-006)
risks their entire personal wealth if the company fails and creditors come
looking for payment beyond what the company itself can cover — this risk
profile strongly discourages passive, diversified investment from anyone
who isn't prepared to personally absorb catastrophic loss. Capping
potential loss at the original investment converts equity investment into
a bounded, calculable risk, which is what makes it practical for genuinely
passive investors (not just active partners personally managing the
business) to supply capital at scale.

## Social consequence

Limited liability shifts risk from investors onto creditors and other
parties the company deals with, who cannot pursue shareholders' personal
assets if the company's own assets prove insufficient — a redistribution of
risk that makes large-scale, diversified equity investment far more
attractive while correspondingly making it more important for creditors and
regulators to assess a company's own creditworthiness carefully, since
shareholders' personal wealth is no longer a backstop.

## Regional specifics

New York's early, broadly accessible general-incorporation approach —
available to any qualifying manufacturing company rather than requiring
individual legislative charter — extends the privilege more democratically
than Britain's initially more restrictive, charter-by-charter practice,
even though Britain's later general Companies Act becomes the more
influential model globally. Colonial India's adoption of comparable
statutes only through British legal transplant, rather than independent
development, is consistent with the broader pattern of colonial legal
institutions arriving as imports rather than local innovations.

## Hook

Capping investor loss at the original investment converts equity ownership
from an unbounded personal risk into a calculable, bounded one — the
specific legal change that makes passive diversified investment in
joint-stock ventures (FIN-006) practical at real scale, at the cost of
shifting uncovered risk onto whoever extends the company credit instead.
