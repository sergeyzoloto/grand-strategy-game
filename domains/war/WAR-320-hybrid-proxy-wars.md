Where nuclear deterrence (WAR-310) makes direct great-power confrontation
too dangerous to risk, competition displaces into sponsoring, arming, or
otherwise backing local combatants (often practicing WAR-220's guerrilla
doctrine) in someone else's conflict, plus non-military tools (cyber
operations, disinformation, economic pressure) below the threshold of
open war.

## Timing

Proxy conflict scales sharply as a Cold War-era substitute for direct
superpower confrontation from 1945 onward; "hybrid warfare," extending the
same displaced-competition logic into cyber operations and coordinated
disinformation campaigns, becomes a widely used framing from the 2000s
through the present era this entry's window extends to.

## Mechanism

A sponsoring power gains strategic influence and can damage a rival's
position without triggering the direct confrontation nuclear deterrence
(WAR-310) makes catastrophically risky, by funding, arming, training, or
otherwise backing a local combatant force whose own motivations may be only
partly aligned with the sponsor's — this arm's-length structure lets a
sponsor claim deniability and limits its own direct casualties, at the cost
of much less control over how the proxy actually fights or what political
outcome it ultimately pursues.

## Social consequence

Populations in a proxy-war theatre bear the conflict's full direct human and
material cost (including the displacement WAR-220 identifies as a
recurring guerrilla-warfare consequence) while the sponsoring powers whose
rivalry the conflict partly expresses remain physically insulated from it —
a systematic externalization of war's cost from the powers actually
directing broader strategic competition onto populations in the theatres
where that competition plays out instead.

## Regional specifics

The Syrian civil war's dense multi-sponsor configuration, with several
external powers backing different internal factions simultaneously while
carefully avoiding direct confrontation with each other, shows the pattern
scaling beyond the simpler two-sponsor Cold War template into a much more
complex multi-party arrangement. Europe's continued absence of direct
great-power military confrontation throughout this same era, even as
competition continues through proxy and non-military channels elsewhere and
through disinformation and economic pressure at home, is itself evidence
that nuclear deterrence (WAR-310) has specifically succeeded at preventing
the exact direct-confrontation scenario it was designed against, even while
displaced competition continues robustly through every channel deterrence
doesn't cover.

## Hook

Where direct confrontation is deterred (WAR-310), strategic competition
displaces into proxy-backed local conflict and non-military hybrid tools
instead — externalizing cost onto populations in the theatres where that
competition actually plays out, while the sponsoring powers' own deterrence
relationship stays formally intact.
