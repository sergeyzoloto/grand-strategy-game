Military service exchanged for a grant of land rather than a wage — the land-for-service institutional baseline WAR-001's professional standing army converts away from, and one no entry in this domain has modeled on its own terms until now.

## Timing

European feudal knight-service matures from roughly 800 through the high medieval period, gradually eroding as European states professionalize from the 1400s-1500s onward (WAR-001); the Japanese shoen-and-samurai system develops on an independent track from roughly 1000, persisting in modified form until its deliberate legislative abolition in the Meiji conscription reforms (WAR-012); the Islamic iqta system applies a related revenue-assignment logic from around the same date.

## Mechanism

A land-tenure military system ties the obligation to fight directly to hereditary land or revenue rights rather than to a state salary: a lord grants a fief (or a shoen, or an iqta revenue assignment) to a warrior in exchange for military service when called, converting the state's problem of paying and equipping soldiers into a problem of allocating and defending land rights instead. This requires no continuous fiscal administration the way WAR-001's professional soldiery does, but it also ties military obligation to a hereditary landholding class whose loyalty and readiness a distant lord can't always reliably compel.

## Social consequence

Because military service and land tenure are directly linked, the resulting warrior class (European knighthood, the Japanese samurai) develops its own hereditary status, honor code, and social identity independent of any central state's administrative reach — a class whose military obligation is inseparable from its social rank, unlike WAR-001's later salaried professional or WAR-012's later conscripted citizen, for whom military service and social status are at least formally separable.

## Regional specifics

Japan's shoen-and-samurai system reaches a structurally parallel land-for-service logic to European feudalism with no direct contact between the two traditions, each independently tying warrior status to hereditary land rights. The Islamic iqta system's revenue-assignment variant achieves a comparable fiscal-substitution function through a related but distinct mechanism — collecting tax revenue from an assigned territory rather than holding the land outright. South Asian reliance on royal and noble retinue ties rather than a comparable widespread land-grant system shows the underlying problem (compelling military service without continuous state salary) has more than one institutional solution available.

## Hook

A land-for-service institutional baseline that WAR-001's professional standing army converts away from — with its own hereditary, honor-bound social logic (knighthood, bushido) shaping military culture and class identity independent of any state's fiscal capacity, until professionalization or, in Japan's case, deliberate legislative abolition (WAR-012) replaces it.
