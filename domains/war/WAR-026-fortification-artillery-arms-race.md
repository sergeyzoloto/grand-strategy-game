The bastion fortification technique itself is ENG's; this entry owns what it does to the campaign-level siege-attrition calculus WAR-005 describes once WAR-008's siege artillery had seemingly settled it in the besieger's favor.

## Timing

The trace italienne bastion style develops in Italian city-states from around 1500, in direct response to the vulnerability high vertical medieval walls had just shown against gunpowder siege artillery (WAR-008); the style spreads across Mediterranean and European fortification practice through the 1600s, remaining the dominant fortification standard through 1700.

## Mechanism

Low, thick, angled bastion walls absorb and deflect cannon fire far more effectively than the high vertical walls WAR-008's artillery had made newly vulnerable, while their angled, mutually-supporting bastion points eliminate the dead ground a besieger could previously approach unfired-upon — restoring much of the defender's advantage in WAR-005's attrition calculus, at a construction and maintenance cost far exceeding traditional wall-building, affordable only to a well-resourced state or city.

## Social consequence

Because trace italienne fortification is so much more expensive to build and maintain than the walls it replaces, it converts fortification itself into a marker of first-rank state or civic wealth in much the same way WAR-008 already makes artillery park size a legible signal of centralized power — a smaller city or polity priced out of the new fortification standard becomes newly vulnerable to exactly the siege artillery that a generation earlier could not have breached its old walls either.

## Regional specifics

Ottoman fortification practice's continued heavy investment in extending high-wall traditions at key sites, rather than wholesale bastion conversion, reflects a different cost-benefit balance given the empire's own continued offensive siege-artillery strength (WAR-008) rather than any lag in awareness of the new European style — a state can rationally choose not to adopt an available defensive innovation if its own offensive capability already covers the same ground.

## Hook

A defensive-technology response that reverses WAR-008's own claimed effect: bastion fortification restores much of the defender's advantage in WAR-005's siege-attrition calculus at enormous new construction cost, showing this domain's offense/defense race runs in both directions rather than settling permanently toward one side.
