This entry owns the doctrine of organizing mounted troops into a battlefield
arm — the underlying mobility technology (horse riding-at-range) is MIG-001's,
and this entry inherits it as a precondition rather than re-deriving it.

## Timing

Cavalry as an organized battlefield arm, distinct from mounted infantry
simply riding to a fight and dismounting, develops from roughly -900 in
steppe and Middle Eastern contexts, with heavy shock cavalry doctrine
maturing further from around 700 onward, remaining a dominant battlefield
arm through the 1600s before gunpowder infantry (WAR-007) erodes its
relative advantage.

## Mechanism

Cavalry's battlefield value rests on the same mobility-range advantage
MIG-001 describes, applied tactically rather than migratorily: superior
speed lets cavalry choose the time and place of engagement, threaten flanks
and rear areas infantry can't quickly redeploy to cover, and disengage from
a losing engagement that infantry can't — a persistent tactical initiative
advantage that different doctrines (horse archery's stand-off missile fire
versus heavy cavalry's direct shock charge) exploit in different ways.

## Social consequence

Sustaining an effective cavalry arm requires ongoing access to enough
suitable horses and to a population with the equestrian skill and leisure
time to train from childhood, which is why cavalry-heavy militaries
recurringly correlate with pastoralist or landed-aristocratic social
structures rather than with the professional infantry-focused states
covered in WAR-001 and WAR-003 — the arm's social base is structurally
different from mass infantry's.

## Regional specifics

Middle Eastern cavalry doctrine's combination of horse-archery and heavy
shock-cavalry roles in a single tradition (feeding into Mamluk and Ottoman
sipahi practice) shows the two approaches aren't mutually exclusive
specializations but can be integrated within one military culture. Sub-
Saharan Africa's comparative marginalization of cavalry doctrine traces
directly to the tsetse-belt disease constraint on horse survivability
already noted in the migration domain — a hard ecological ceiling on the
underlying animal resource rather than any tactical or cultural preference
against mounted warfare.

## Hook

Cavalry doctrine converts MIG-001's raw mobility advantage into tactical
battlefield initiative — the specific doctrine (missile stand-off versus
shock charge) is a real choice, but the underlying capability ceiling is set
entirely by horse availability, which in turn is set by disease ecology in
regions like the tsetse belt regardless of doctrine or training investment.
