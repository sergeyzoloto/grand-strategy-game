Breaking soldiering itself into standardized, counted, endlessly-repeatable drill sequences — a training-method innovation distinct from WAR-040's fiscal professionalization and WAR-080's formation doctrine, since it's specifically about how training itself gets standardized and taught.

## Timing

Maurice of Nassau's Dutch army reforms, developed from around 1590 and drawing explicitly on rediscovered Roman drill manuals, break musket-loading and firing into a fixed sequence of numbered, individually-drillable motions; the method spreads across European standing armies through the 1600s, becoming a standard component of infantry training by 1700.

## Mechanism

Loading and firing a musket effectively under battlefield stress had previously depended on individual soldiers developing their own competence through experience; breaking the process into a fixed sequence of counted, numbered motions — practiced in unison, repeatedly, under an instructor calling each step — converts that competence into something teachable to a large body of recruits in a matter of months rather than years, and makes battlefield performance more uniform and predictable across an entire unit regardless of any individual soldier's natural aptitude.

## Social consequence

Because drilled competence is teachable and largely aptitude-independent, it lets a state's standing army (WAR-040) absorb and effectively train much larger annual intakes of new recruits than a system reliant on slow individual skill accumulation ever could, directly easing the constraint gunpowder infantry's rapid manpower expansion (WAR-110) would otherwise run into.

## Regional specifics

Maurice of Nassau's reforms draw explicitly on classical Roman drill manuals, a deliberate revival of an ancient training technology rather than a wholly novel invention — the underlying insight, that repetitive, standardized, numbered drill sequences convert individual skill into a teachable, scalable competence, had been available in written form for over a millennium before it was systematically reapplied to musket infantry.

## Hook

A gate that converts training time from an individual-mastery bottleneck into a scalable, teachable process — directly easing the manpower-expansion pressure gunpowder infantry (WAR-110) puts on a standing army's (WAR-040) capacity to train new recruits quickly.
