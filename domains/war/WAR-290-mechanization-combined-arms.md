Tank technology itself is ENG's; this entry owns the doctrine of
coordinating armor, motorized infantry, artillery, and air support as a
single integrated striking force, restoring offensive maneuver capability
against the positional stalemate WAR-240 describes.

## Timing

Tanks first see combat in 1917; the fully integrated combined-arms doctrine
that lets mechanized forces reliably break through and exploit a front
rather than simply supporting infantry assault matures by the German
campaigns of 1939-1941.

## Mechanism

A tank or aircraft in isolation is vulnerable and limited in what it can
accomplish alone; combined-arms doctrine coordinates armor's breakthrough
and shock capability with motorized infantry's ability to hold and exploit
that breakthrough, artillery's suppressive fire, and air support's
reconnaissance and interdiction — restoring the offensive maneuver
capability WAR-240's defensive-firepower stalemate had neutralized, but only
where the coordinating doctrine and communication infrastructure (radio, in
particular) actually exist to synchronize all these arms in real time.

## Social consequence

Effective combined-arms doctrine requires an officer corps trained to
coordinate across traditionally separate branches (infantry, armor,
artillery, air) rather than commanding a single specialized arm, creating
new career and training pressures on the professional officer and NCO corps
(WAR-170) beyond what any single-arm specialization previously demanded.

## Regional specifics

German combined-arms doctrine's faster and more thorough institutionalization
than French or British interwar practice, despite the latter having
comparable or superior tank hardware, is one of this domain's clearest cases
of doctrine and hardware capability moving on genuinely separate tracks —
possessing the technology (ENG's territory) doesn't guarantee possessing the
doctrine to use it decisively. Imperial Japan's comparative lag in army
mechanization despite its navy's parallel technological sophistication
(MAR-350) shows the same doctrine/hardware separation can occur within a
single state across its different military branches, reflecting resource
allocation and institutional priority rather than a uniform national
industrial capacity constraint.

## Contested

Whether German interwar success reflects genuinely superior doctrinal
development or French/British institutional caution shaped by WAR-240's
trauma leading them to underinvest in offensive doctrine despite adequate
hardware is debated. Model doctrine sophistication and hardware capability
as independent, separately-triggerable variables, letting a state hold a
hardware lead and still lose the doctrinal race if its institutions are
oriented toward defensive caution rather than active doctrine innovation.

## Hook

Combined-arms doctrine restores offensive maneuver against positional
stalemate (WAR-240) by synchronizing armor, infantry, artillery, and air
support in real time — a doctrinal achievement entirely separable from
hardware capability (ENG's territory), meaning a state can hold superior
tank technology and still lose a war of maneuver to worse-equipped forces
with better-coordinated doctrine.
