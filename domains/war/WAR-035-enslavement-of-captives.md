This entry owns the captive-disposal decision and the institution that
follows from it — what an armed victor does with a defeated population, and
what holding them instead of killing them does to the holder's economy. The
transport of the enslaved as a migration event is MIG-160's, the plantation
as a production system is AGR-210's, and the codified hereditary legal
status is LAW's. The decision itself, and its four preconditions, are WAR's.

## Timing

Captive labour appears in the earliest administrative records of Mesopotamia
and Egypt, from roughly -3000, already as an existing practice rather than
an innovation being described. The institution persists continuously
somewhere in every focus region until the nineteenth-century abolitions,
with Brazil's 1888 emancipation as the conventional terminal date for the
Atlantic system. It does not develop along a single line — it appears,
contracts, and reappears in different forms as the four conditions below
move.

## Mechanism

A victor standing over captives has four options — kill, ransom, release,
or retain — and retention is chosen only when four conditions hold at once.
First, the captive's output must exceed their upkeep, which requires an
agrarian or otherwise storable-surplus economy in which a person can produce
more food than they eat; foraging economies mostly cannot clear this bar,
which is why captive-taking in them terminates in killing, adoption, or
sale rather than retention. Second, supervision must be cheap. This is the
Nieboer-Domar term, and it is geographic as much as economic — nucleated
fields, mines, galleys, and quarries are watchable; dispersed herding and
scattered arable are not. Third, the captive must be visibly
distinguishable, by language, phenotype, marking, or origin, so that
recapture is possible and so that the surrounding free population does not
identify with them. Fourth, the victor must want labour rather than
territory; a campaign fought to take land from a population it intends to
replace has no use for the population.

Where those conditions fail, mass execution of captives persists. It is
attested throughout antiquity and beyond wherever holding is unprofitable —
after sieges, on campaigns far from any market, and against populations
that cannot be marched. Enslavement is not an ethical advance on killing
and did not displace it; it is a calculation that runs in parallel with it
and wins when the arithmetic favours it.

What the calculation produces is not merely bad treatment. Following
Patterson, the constitutive feature is natal alienation — the enslaved
person is severed from ancestors and descendants, holds no claim on kin, and
has no standing in the community's system of obligation. Kinlessness is the
status, not a description of how harshly the status is administered. This is
why a well-fed household slave and a mine slave occupy the same category
while a badly-treated free tenant does not, and why manumission everywhere
requires a formal act of social rebirth rather than merely the end of a
work obligation.

## Social consequence

The wealth is real. Slave labour built and ran the Laurion silver mines that
underwrote Athenian naval power, the Roman villa system, the Caribbean sugar
complex, and the Ottoman and Abbasid military-administrative apparatus. Any
model that treats the institution as merely brutal and therefore
economically marginal will misprice the societies that ran on it.

The counter-finding is equally standing, and both halves belong in the same
entry. Cheap coerced labour suppresses the incentive to develop
labour-saving technique, because the labour constraint that drives
mechanisation elsewhere is met by acquiring more people instead. It also
hollows out domestic market depth, since a workforce that consumes at
subsistence and holds no property generates no consumer demand, which caps
the internal market a manufacturing sector could have grown into. The
wealth is real and the growth path is a dead end — a society can be rich on
this basis and still be structurally unable to convert that wealth into
sustained productivity growth.

The demographic term closes the loop back to WAR. Slave populations under
gang-labour regimes typically fail to reproduce themselves, through
mortality, sex-ratio skew, and suppressed family formation. The stock is
therefore depleting and must be replenished from outside, which couples the
institution permanently to warfare, raiding, and long-distance purchase.
A slave economy that stops winning wars, or loses access to a supply market,
faces a labour crisis on a generational timer — and one that solves the
problem by shifting to internal reproduction has thereby changed the
institution into something else.

## Regional specifics

The five focus regions produce genuinely different institutions rather than
one institution at different intensities. The Mediterranean chattel form —
the enslaved person as alienable property, concentrated at scale — is the
historical outlier, not the template, and Finley's point is that only a
small number of societies ever ran on it. The Islamic military-slavery
variant inverts the usual position of the enslaved entirely, placing
purchased and levied men in command of armies and provinces precisely
because their kinlessness makes them structurally incapable of founding
rival dynasties; this is the same natal-alienation mechanism producing an
opposite social outcome, and it should be modelled as a real branch rather
than an oddity. The South Asian and Chinese cases both show the institution
running primarily through debt and juridical channels rather than a market
in war captives, which keeps it entangled with existing status hierarchies
instead of resolving into a property category. The African lineage form runs
absorptively, accumulating dependents where people rather than land are the
scarce factor, until external demand reorients it. And the Americas supply
the sharpest contrast pair — Aztec *tlacotin*, revocable and non-hereditary
with free-born children, against the Atlantic chattel system that arrives
later on the same continent, with Andean *mit'a* standing outside the
category altogether as rotating corvée owed by intact communities.

The Pacific Northwest Coast case is the one that most usefully strains the
model. Hereditary slaveholding at substantial population proportions, on a
salmon-and-storage economy with no agriculture, indicates the first
condition is not tracking farming but storable concentrated surplus plus a
supervision geography — fixed winter villages on a coastline where flight
means crossing the territory of other slaveholding groups.

## Contested

Finley's typology is the standard framework and is under sustained attack
for drawing a binary where a gradient exists; use it, but do not treat the
threshold as a real discontinuity. The place of slavery in Roman decline has
been substantially revised away from the older supply-shock account, and
this entry takes no position on it. The Nieboer-Domar land-labour ratio
predicts the institution's appearance well in frontier cases and visibly
fails in dense ones, so it is one input to a supervision-cost calculation
rather than a sufficient condition. A fourth, quieter dispute worth
flagging — whether ancient and medieval servile statuses across Asia are
usefully called slavery at all, or whether the term imports chattel
assumptions that distort them — bears directly on how many of the regional
variants above should share a single mechanic.

## Hook

A branch on the captive stack after any won engagement, gated on four
conditions read from the winner's economy rather than chosen freely — output
above upkeep, supervision cost below the output margin, a recapture-enabling
visible distinction, and a war aim of labour rather than land. Failing the
gate does not produce release; it produces execution, which stays on the
menu throughout. Passing it converts captives into a `coerced labour stock`
that pays out real output while applying a standing penalty to
`labour-saving incentive` and to domestic market depth, and that depletes on
its own through failed reproduction — so the holder must keep winning,
keep buying, or watch the stock run down. `supervision cost` is read from
terrain and settlement pattern, not set by policy, which is what makes the
same institution profitable in a mine and impossible on open pasture.
