Demobilisation turns an organised, armed, trained body of men into unemployed
civilians, and the state loses the power to direct them at the moment it stops
paying them. Whether the released force dissolves into individuals or persists
as a formation decides whether the result is banditry or a coup, and that is
settled by the state's prior institutional position, not by a choice made at
the moment of release.

## Timing

The mechanism is as old as paid or rewarded force. New Kingdom Egypt grants
land to soldiers after campaigns from around -1550, and settled soldiery founds
an Egyptian dynasty by -943. The Roman Republic's discharge-land politics runs
from -103 to Augustus's cash settlement in 6 CE, and every later era repeats the
pattern. Examples include Tang China's inability to disband rebel armies in 763,
the Free Companies after 1360, Ottoman sekban in the Celali revolts from 1598,
and the New Model Army in 1647. The Napoleonic half-pay officers after 1815, the
Freikorps after 1918, the colonial veterans of 1944-48, and the dissolved Iraqi
army of 2003 carry it forward. The 2020 end date marks the present, not a
closure. Mass conscription after 1793 (WAR-200) and total mobilisation
(WAR-290) multiply the numbers released at once without altering how the
release works.

## Mechanism

**An army is worth most to the state that raised it and least to the economy
that must take it back.** A released soldier carries military skill, a learned
tolerance for violence, experience of working inside an organisation, a claim
on the state for what he was promised, and often no livelihood. What the war
built is only useful for war. A peacetime economy has no demand for most of it,
and the gap between what the force is good for and what the economy can absorb
is the whole mechanism. Any force whose members lose their prior life by
serving produces it. A long-service professional of WAR-060's standing army
has no civilian life to return to at all. A conscript usually does (WAR-200), which lowers the pressure per
man. Conscription then releases so many men at once that the total pressure
rises anyway.

**The claim falls due when the treasury is emptiest.** The counter-mechanism
is an absorbing institution: land grants, veteran colonies, pensions, a
preference in state employment, continued enlistment, or settlement on a
frontier. Every one of them costs money or land, and the bill arrives at the
end of a war, the moment when the state has spent its reserves, exhausted its
credit, and stopped needing the men. Paying for the war and paying off the army
compete for the same revenue. A state that borrowed to fight (FIN-110) now owes
its creditors and its soldiers at once. The creditors can refuse the next loan,
but the soldiers are armed. That is the trap: the state is least able to pay at
the moment it most needs to. Some ways out only move the cost onto someone
else. Octavian paid his veterans in 41 with land confiscated from Italian towns,
and the Perusine War followed. Rome's veteran colonies on the frontier put the
cost on the conquered, and the colonists at Camulodunum drove the Trinovantes
into Boudica's revolt. The Free Companies were sent to Castile. In each case the
problem went somewhere else, not away.

**The branch is whether the force dissolves or persists.** That is decided
before the release, by the institution the state already has.

- **Dispersed.** Soldiers are released one by one, unpaid or underpaid, into a
  labour market that cannot take them. The command link to the state is cut,
  and nothing replaces it at scale. The results are vagrancy, banditry, and
  emigration as mercenaries. Criminal organisation follows, along with a pool
  of trained men available to anyone who can pay: rebels, provincial
  governors, foreign recruiters, and the market for violence WAR-140
  describes. Dispersal does not mean men stay isolated. Ottoman sekban
  re-formed as bands under their own captains within a season. What
  dispersal means is that they re-form around a private paymaster instead of
  the state. A band that settles on a territory and starts charging for its
  own restraint is running WAR-020's roving-to-stationary conversion from a
  standing start.
- **Cohesive.** Units demobilise together with their officers in place, and
  they keep their command structure, a shared grievance, and a collective claim
  on the state. The result is an organised political actor. At its mildest it
  is a pension lobby. At its strongest it is the militarisation of politics or
  a coup. The primary group WAR-220 describes does not dissolve at discharge.
  It becomes the veterans' association, the Freikorps unit, or the regiment
  that elects Agitators.

Four variables decide which branch runs, and each can be read from the state
before the war ends:

1. **Can the state pay what it promised?** When the claim is honoured, most
   of the political charge in a returning army goes away, even a very
   cohesive one. The New Model Army of 1660, paid its arrears, disbanded
   quietly. The same army in 1647, unpaid, took over the country.
2. **Is the officer corps co-opted or discarded?** Officers are the part of
   an army that can hold it together after release. Co-opted officers take
   pensions, posts, estates, or continued commands, and the force dissolves
   quietly. The early Song buying out its founding generals and Charles VII
   taking the best captains into the compagnies d'ordonnance are examples.
   Discarded officers turn the dispersed rank and file into an organisation,
   as in Iraq in 2003 and the French demi-solde conspiracies of the 1820s.
   **This is the single most decisive variable,** because it can turn a
   dispersed release into a cohesive one after the fact.
3. **Does an absorbing institution exist already?** A veteran colony, a
   pension chest, or an employment preference that is already in place
   works. One improvised after the war has to be paid for from the empty
   treasury.
4. **Was the war won or lost?** A victorious army returns with a claim against
   a government that owes it. A defeated army returns with a grievance against
   the government that sent it, which is a different political object. The
   first wants to be paid. The second wants to change who governs. A victory
   the soldiers regard as unrewarded behaves like a defeat, as in Italy
   in 1919.

## Social consequence

At household scale, the returning soldier finds that the war has removed the
life he left. Under a Republican Rome that kept legions in Spain for years, the
smallholding had been sold, mortgaged, or absorbed by a neighbour. The Hindustani
naukar's lord had been annexed. The 1919 conscript's job had gone to someone
who had not served. What he keeps is the unit. For a man with no livelihood, his
comrades are the only network he has, the officer who led him is the only
patron with a reason to help, and the claim on the state is the only property
he holds. That is why cohesion survives discharge. It is the returning soldier's
main social asset, and he holds onto it.

The claim then changes what the state is to him. A pension paid through the
treasury makes the state the soldier's creditor and gives him a stake in its
survival. Augustus's aerarium militare, the Union pension rolls, and the GI Bill
all did this. Union veterans' pensions became, in Skocpol's reading, the first
large American entitlement programme, and military pensions are an early root
of state social insurance (LAW-210). A discharge that depends on a general's
political success makes the general the soldier's creditor instead, and gives
him a stake in that general's power. This is the core of the Roman case. The
late Republic did not fall because its soldiers were disloyal. They were loyal
to whoever could settle them, and the Senate had declined to be that party.
WAR-180 is the counterpart inside the institution. A standing corps that is
never released turns its permanence into a veto over reform. A force that is
released unpaid turns its dismissal into a grievance. Both are armed bodies
that the state has failed to keep as instruments, one because it kept them
and one because it let them go.

The released men also change the community around them. Where they disperse,
villages learn to pay or arm, and the line between an unpaid soldier and a
bandit disappears: Ottoman Anatolia in the Great Flight of 1603, Shaanxi in the
late Ming, and the Gelaohui lodges that spread among the Xiang Army soldiers
Zeng Guofan disbanded in 1864. Where they persist, politics acquires a
constituency that shares a uniform and a way of settling disputes. Colonial
veterans present the sharpest form. They were trained by an empire that does
not intend to honour their claim, and they have learned exactly how that empire
fights. Demobilised soldiers recur as organisers and cadres in anticolonial
guerrilla movements (WAR-240) for that reason.

## Regional specifics

Rome anchors the entry because it runs the cohesive branch to its end and then
builds the fix. The question of whether a discrete "Marian reform" of 107 ever
happened is itself doubted (Cadiou, _L'armée imaginaire_, 2018). That matters
less than it appears, because the mechanism rests on the discharge-land politics
that follows, not on a recruitment change. Augustus's solution is a change of
debtor. He records paying cash for settlement land in 30 and 14 BCE, and in
6 CE he endowed a military treasury funded by an inheritance tax and a sales
tax, which paid a fixed discharge bounty on completion of service (Res Gestae
16-17; Cassius Dio 55.23-25). The soldier's pension no longer depended on any
general.

China runs the design-it-away approach and then shows it failing. The fubing
system tied service to land so that release returned a man to his own allotment.
When that failed, long-service frontier armies under jiedushi replaced it, and
in 763 the Tang court had to accept surrendered rebel armies intact as provincial
governments because it could not afford to disband them (Graff, _Medieval
Chinese Warfare 300-900_, 2002). Later dynasties pull every lever. The Song buy
out their generals, and the anecdote of the "cup of wine" in 961 is later, but
the policy is not. The Song also recruit famine refugees into the army so that
the desperate become soldiers rather than bandits, at a fiscal cost that swells
the army past a million men. The Ming build hereditary military farm colonies
that decay into desertion. The late Qing deliberately disbands its most
effective army and seeds the secret societies with its veterans, and the Beiyang
Army's formations survive Yuan Shikai's death as the warlord armies of 1916-28.

The Ottoman sekban are close to a textbook case of the dispersed branch. They
were raised for a campaign and released without pay or land between campaigns,
then re-formed around whoever could pay. Barkey (_Bandits and Bureaucrats_, 1994)
shows the state absorbing band leaders into provincial office instead of
destroying them. That is officer co-optation after the fact, and it partly
worked. İnalcık carries the fiscal side ("Military and Fiscal Transformation",
_Archivum Ottomanicum_ 6, 1980). White (_The Climate of Rebellion_, 2011) supplies
the climate side that AGR-230 covers. The two causes compound: harvest failure
pushed peasants toward sekban recruitment, and released sekban turned harvest
failure into revolt.

South Asia shows what happens when a society has absorption built into a labour
market. Kolff (_Naukar, Rajput and Sepoy_, 1990) describes an armed peasantry
for whom service was a seasonal livelihood. Release was routine there, and the
danger came when the Company monopolised military employment and closed the
market (Alavi, _The Sepoys and the Company_, 1995). The Pindaris and the
disbanded army of Awadh are the result. The Indian National Army trials of
1945-46 and the Royal Indian Navy mutiny of 1946 show the colonial variant
arriving at the end of the empire's own largest volunteer army.

In Europe and the Americas the full range appears. The Landsknecht problem
of unemployed mercenaries garting between contracts is WAR-140's market seen
from the unemployed side. The Peace of Westphalia could only be carried out
after the Nuremberg Execution Diet of 1649-50 raised the money to pay the
Swedish army to leave (Wilson, _Europe's Tragedy_, 2009), which means peace had
to be bought from the army. The New Model Army gives the cohesive branch in
full, and the Freikorps give it in defeat (Gerwarth, _The Vanquished_, 2016).
Spanish American caudillos give it where the victors could not be paid (Lynch,
_Caudillos in Spanish America_, 1992), and the Chaco War gives it in two
countries at once. The GI Bill is the successful absorption case, and it keeps
this entry from pointing in one direction only. It was designed by veterans'
organisations explicitly to prevent a second Bonus Army. It did so, and it
turned a demobilised army into a generation of homeowners and graduates.
Katznelson (_When Affirmative Action Was White_, 2005) documents the veterans it
excluded. Colombia's paramilitary demobilisation of 2003-06 and the
post-Soviet 1990s (Volkov, _Violent Entrepreneurs_, 2002) show the dispersed
branch producing criminal organisation in the present. In Colombia, mid-level
commanders who were never absorbed rebuilt their units as criminal bands.

The absences all point the same way. The fubing farmer, the feudal vassal, the
steppe herder and the age-set warrior had no separate civilian life that service
had taken away, so nothing needed absorbing. Each absence ends where paid force
separated from status. The routiers condemned in 1179 and the Tang frontier
armies of 737 mark those endings (LAW-080 for the tenure bond, MIG-090 for the
colonies built to recreate it, MIG-110 for the steppe's version of the failure).

## Contested

The coup literature disagrees about whether returning soldiers are a cause of
instability or a symptom. On the grievance and corporate-interest reading,
associated with Nordlinger and Thompson, an unpaid or discarded military body is
an actor in its own right whose interests trigger intervention. Huntington
answers that the causes of military intervention are political and not military.
A state that cannot pay its veterans is already failing, and the veterans are
simply the first people with the organisation and weapons to act on it. After
1918 the same argument recurs in another form. Mosse's brutalisation thesis
holds that front experience turned men into paramilitaries. Prost's French
veterans and Ziemann's rural German returnees mostly went home, sought pensions,
and distrusted war. Gerwarth moves the explanation from front experience to
defeat and revolution, which is variable 4 above rather than a property of
soldiers.

Model it conditionally. State fiscal failure is the common cause, and it raises
instability whether or not an army is released. A veteran formation that
persists adds a further contribution on top, as a per-scenario weight drawn from
a range, not as a fixed effect. The weight stays uncertain because the history
cannot separate the two readings. The states that paid their veterans (Augustan
Rome, Restoration England, the United States in 1944) were also the states with
the strongest institutions, so the cases that would distinguish cause from
symptom do not exist in the record. The branch structure itself is not in
dispute: dispersal produces banditry and cohesion produces political pressure,
and the four variables decide which. Only how much independent force a
persisting formation adds is contested.

## Hook

Ending a war creates a `veteran claim`, the compensation the released force was
promised, and it falls due when `sovereign credit capacity` is at its lowest.
Release then branches on prior institutional state, per design principle 1. The
player built the conditions beforehand and does not choose the branch at the
moment of release.

- **Inputs, read at the moment of release:**
  - How much of the claim can be paid now.
  - Whether an absorbing institution already exists: veteran colonies, a
    pension chest, an employment preference, continued enlistment, or frontier
    settlement.
  - Whether the officer corps is co-opted or discarded.
  - Whether the war was won or lost.
  - The share of the force that is professional rather than conscript.
- **Dispersed branch:**
  - Released men become `mobilisable manpower` for any payer, including
    rebels, rival states and provincial strongmen.
  - Bandit groups form, and some settle into charging `protection rent`.
- **Cohesive branch:**
  - The released force becomes an organised faction holding the claim.
  - While the claim is paid, it acts as a lobby.
  - While the claim is unpaid, pressure escalates.
  - It escalates to a coup only when the claim is unpaid, the officers are
    discarded, and the war was lost or seen as unrewarded.
- **Claim persistence:** unpaid claims do not expire. The Bonus Army pressed
  a claim fourteen years after the war that created it.
- **Displacement:** paying the claim with confiscated land or a foreign
  expedition moves the grievance onto the dispossessed or the invaded, rather
  than removing it.
- **Absorption dividend:** a state that pays through a standing treasury
  makes its veterans stakeholders in its survival. One that lets generals
  settle their own men creates armies loyal to the generals.
