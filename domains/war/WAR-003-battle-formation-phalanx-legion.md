Mass-equipped infantry (WAR-002) is only as effective as the formation
organizing it — a formation converts individually-armed soldiers into a
single tactical unit whose combat effectiveness depends on cohesion and
training as much as on individual weapon quality.

## Timing

The Greek phalanx matures from roughly -700; the Roman legion's more
flexible maniple-based system develops and displaces phalanx dominance in
Mediterranean warfare by roughly -200 to -100; comparable independent
formation innovations in China and South Asia develop on separate timelines
within the same broad era.

## Mechanism

A rigid close-order formation like the phalanx maximizes frontal shock power
through sheer density and shared shield-wall protection, at the cost of
inflexibility (a phalanx that loses cohesion or gets flanked collapses
badly); the Roman legion's smaller, more independently-maneuverable
sub-units trade some of that frontal density for the ability to reposition,
rotate exhausted lines, and respond to terrain and flanking threats mid-
battle — a tactical flexibility upgrade that compounds advantage over many
engagements even where a single set-piece phalanx-versus-phalanx clash might
be closer to even.

## Social consequence

Formation warfare of this kind requires extensive collective drill and
training time, which only a professionalized or at least regularly-mustered
force (WAR-001) can sustain — formation quality becomes a direct, visible
signal of a state's investment in sustained military training, not just its
population size or weapon quality.

## Regional specifics

Warring States Chinese formations achieve comparable battlefield dominance
through crossbow-and-spear combined-arms coordination under centralized
command, a structurally different solution built on missile-and-shock
integration rather than the Mediterranean's shock-only close order, arrived
at with no plausible direct contact between the two traditions. Mauryan
India's reliance on combined chariot, elephant, cavalry, and infantry arms
rather than a single dominant infantry formation type shows mass-battle
effectiveness doesn't require infantry formation standardization at all —
arm diversity can substitute for formation discipline as a path to the same
tactical result.

## Contested

Whether the legion's tactical flexibility was the decisive factor in Roman
dominance over Hellenistic phalanx armies, or one contributing factor among
several (manpower depth, political resilience, logistics), is genuinely
disputed. Model formation flexibility as a real, independently-weighted
tactical modifier that improves outcomes across a campaign of many
engagements, without scripting it as sufficient on its own to guarantee
victory in any single battle.

## Hook

Formation type converts trained, disciplined mass infantry (WAR-002) into a
tactical multiplier independent of individual soldier quality — flexible,
repositionable formations compound advantage over a sustained campaign even
where they don't guarantee superiority in any single set-piece engagement.
