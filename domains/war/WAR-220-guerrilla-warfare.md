A militarily weaker force avoids decisive open engagement entirely, instead
using mobility, local knowledge, and population support to impose sustained
attrition (WAR-100) on a conventionally stronger opponent it cannot defeat
in a direct confrontation.

## Timing

The term originates from Spanish resistance to Napoleonic occupation (from
1808); the strategy itself recurs throughout this record wherever a weaker
force faces a stronger conventional army, reaching its most systematically
theorized form in 20th-century anti-colonial and revolutionary conflicts
through the 1970s.

## Mechanism

Guerrilla warfare deliberately inverts the attritional calculus of WAR-100:
rather than matching a conventional opponent's endurance directly, it
imposes a much higher relative cost-per-casualty on the stronger side (which
must maintain expensive conventional forces, garrisons, and supply lines
across contested territory) than it costs the guerrilla force to inflict —
a cost-asymmetry strategy that depends critically on sustained local
population support for supply, concealment, and intelligence, without which
the weaker force has neither the mobility nor the information advantage the
strategy requires.

## Social consequence

Because guerrilla warfare depends on population support to function at all,
it makes civilian populations in contested areas a direct strategic target
for both sides — the guerrilla force for supply and concealment, the
counterinsurgent force for intelligence and to deny the guerrillas that same
support — a dynamic that recurringly produces the population displacement
this domain's migration entries track (MIG's territory) as controlling or
denying population becomes as strategically important as any battlefield
engagement.

## Regional specifics

Maoist protracted-war theory treats guerrilla resistance as the deliberate
first phase of a longer sequential strategy building toward eventual
conventional confrontation, a systematic doctrinal framework rather than
merely an improvised weaker-side tactic — a significant theoretical
contribution distinct from earlier, less systematized guerrilla practice.
African anti-colonial guerrilla movements tie the strategy's ultimate
objective directly to decolonization diplomacy and international
recognition (DIP's territory) rather than to displacing a rival domestic
regime, showing the same military technique can serve structurally different
political end goals depending on context.

## Hook

Guerrilla warfare imposes cost-asymmetric attrition on a conventionally
stronger opponent, but only functions where the weaker side sustains real
local population support — making civilian population control, not
territory or battlefield engagement, the actual contested resource both
sides are fighting over.
