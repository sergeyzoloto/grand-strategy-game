Conscription (WAR-012) mobilizes manpower; total mobilization extends the
same logic to an entire economy — converting civilian industrial capacity,
labor, and resource allocation wholesale to war production, erasing the
prior distinction between a nation's peacetime economy and its war effort.

## Timing

Total mobilization develops substantially from World War One (1914) onward,
reaching its fullest expression in the industrial conversion efforts of the
major powers through World War Two (to 1945).

## Mechanism

Total mobilization treats a state's entire industrial, agricultural, and
labor capacity as a single resource pool to be centrally allocated toward
war production, requiring extensive new state control over civilian economic
activity (rationing, production quotas, labor direction) that peacetime
governance never previously exercised — the war's material outcome becomes
directly proportional to the size and conversion efficiency of a
state's entire economy, not just its standing military capacity, tying this
domain's outcome directly to COM and FIN's underlying economic capacity.

## Social consequence

Total mobilization draws civilian populations, including groups previously
excluded from direct war-economy participation, into essential war-
production roles (most visibly, large-scale female industrial labor
mobilization in several combatant states), creating social and economic
changes that frequently outlast the war itself and reshape postwar labor
and gender relations in ways the mobilization effort never intended as a
war aim.

## Regional specifics

American industrial mobilization converts civilian manufacturing to
military production at a scale that becomes the decisive Allied material
advantage of the war, achieved without the domestic mainland ever becoming
a battlefield itself — a case where total mobilization's economic effect
matters more to the war's outcome than any single campaign. Japan's total
mobilization effort, while extensive and total in its domestic social
disruption, runs up against a hard ceiling its territory and trade access
simply cannot supply once Allied sea-lane interdiction (MAR-022, MAR-024)
cuts off critical raw material imports — mobilization doctrine intensity
cannot substitute for resources a state's underlying economic geography
doesn't provide.

## Hook

Total mobilization converts a state's entire economy (COM, FIN's territory)
into a single war-production resource pool, making the war's material
outcome a direct function of economic scale and conversion efficiency rather
than of standing military capacity alone — a lever with a hard ceiling set
by the state's actual resource base and trade access, which no amount of
mobilization doctrine can raise on its own.
