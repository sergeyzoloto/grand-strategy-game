Aircraft as a land-warfare and strategic arm — reconnaissance, air
superiority, close air support, and strategic bombing — covering everything
except the carrier-based naval aviation MAR-028 already owns under this
project's sea/land theatre split.

## Timing

Military aviation begins with reconnaissance and light bombing roles from
1911; the full range of doctrinal roles this entry covers (air superiority,
close air support integrated into combined-arms doctrine per WAR-016, and
independent strategic bombing) matures and is tested at large scale through
World War Two, to 1945.

## Mechanism

Aircraft extend a military's reach and reconnaissance (WAR-004) beyond
ground-based limits in three increasingly ambitious doctrinal roles:
supporting ground forces directly (close air support, integrating with
WAR-016's combined arms), contesting control of the airspace itself (air
superiority, a precondition for the other two roles to function safely),
and striking an enemy's industrial and civilian infrastructure directly
without needing to defeat its army first (strategic bombing) — each role
demands different aircraft design priorities and doctrine, and a military
that masters one doesn't automatically master the others.

## Social consequence

Strategic bombing in particular deliberately extends military targeting to
civilian infrastructure and, in practice, civilian populations, collapsing
the earlier distinction between front-line combatant risk and home-front
safety that most of this domain's earlier entries still assumed — a
population's physical safety from war becomes a function of its state's air
defense capability rather than of distance from any front line.

## Regional specifics

American daylight precision-bombing doctrine and British night-area-bombing
practice, pursued by two closely allied powers with comparable aircraft
technology over the same theatre in the same war, reach different doctrinal
conclusions from essentially the same starting capability — a clean
illustration that aircraft technology (ENG's territory) doesn't determine
one single correct doctrine for using it. Naval carrier aviation, where
Japan held clear early doctrinal leadership over the same period, is
deliberately kept in MAR-028 rather than covered here, following this
project's sea-theatre carve-out.

## Contested

Strategic bombing's actual effectiveness — did it meaningfully shorten wars
by breaking industrial capacity and civilian morale, proportionate to its
enormous cost — is disputed, with postwar bombing-survey findings more
mixed than wartime expectations. Model material-industrial damage and
morale/political effect as two separate, independently-measured outcomes of
a bombing campaign rather than assuming one follows automatically from the
other, since the historical record suggests they frequently didn't move
together.

## Hook

Military aviation doctrine splits into air superiority, close air support,
and strategic bombing roles that each require separate doctrinal mastery
beyond simply possessing capable aircraft (ENG's territory) — and strategic
bombing specifically collapses the previous front-line/home-front safety
distinction, making civilian populations directly targetable for the first
time in this domain's record.
