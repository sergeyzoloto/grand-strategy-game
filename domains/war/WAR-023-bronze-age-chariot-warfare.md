Warfare organized around a small, expensively-equipped elite rather than a numbers contest — bronze armor and weapons, and the chariot itself, all require capital and a long-distance tin-trade network only a narrow aristocratic class can sustain, the precise monopoly WAR-002's cheap iron later breaks.

## Timing

Chariot warfare matures across the Near East and eastern Mediterranean from roughly -1700 onward, reaching its best-documented peak in the major Bronze Age powers' campaigns (Egyptian, Hittite, Mycenaean) through the Bronze Age collapse around -1200; Shang China develops chariot warfare independently from around -1500.

## Mechanism

A bronze-equipped chariot warrior requires tin, which for most of this era means importing it across a long-distance trade network vulnerable to disruption, plus the chariot itself, a trained team of horses, and years of driving and combat practice — a bundle of capital and training costs only a narrow hereditary elite can sustain. This converts military effectiveness into a direct function of how much of that elite a state or chiefdom can equip and field, rather than of total population, the exact monopoly structure WAR-002's cheap, locally-available iron later dissolves.

## Social consequence

Bronze Age elite warfare ties military capability directly to aristocratic lineage and inherited wealth, since only a household with land, herds, and trade access sufficient to outfit a chariot and its crew can participate at all — a warrior class defined by birth and capital rather than by either state salary (WAR-001) or legal obligation (WAR-012), neither of which yet exists as an alternative.

## Regional specifics

Shang Chinese chariot warfare develops on a wholly independent track from Near Eastern practice, with the chariot serving as much as a mobile command-and-prestige platform for the ruling lineage as a direct combat weapon — a role emphasis shared, per the Contested note, with several Near Eastern cases as well. South Asian elite warfare of this era develops more continuously into the later combined chariot-elephant-cavalry doctrine WAR-003 already notes for the Mauryan period, rather than passing through as sharp a chariot-monopoly-to-mass-infantry transition as the Mediterranean case.

## Contested

How much of chariot warfare's actual battlefield role was direct shock or skirmish combat, versus a mobile command-and-prestige platform for an already-established warrior elite, is debated given how thin direct tactical accounts from the period are — Egyptian and Near Eastern reliefs depict chariots prominently in battle scenes, but the depictions may overstate combat function relative to display and command value. Model chariot warfare's battlefield contribution as a mix of both functions whose exact ratio is uncertain, rather than committing to either a pure-weapon or pure-status reading.

## Hook

An origin gate: establishes the elite, capital-and-trade-dependent warrior monopoly WAR-002's cheap iron needs something to erode — the "before" state every later mass-infantry entry in this domain implicitly measures itself against.
