This entry owns the organizational and logistical innovation of getting a
wounded soldier off the battlefield and through a staged evacuation chain
fast enough to matter — the underlying medical efficacy that determines
whether treatment at the end of that chain actually saves them is the health
domain's territory (see HLT-011, HLT-021).

## Timing

Baron Dominique Larrey's "flying ambulance" (ambulance volante) rapid-
evacuation corps, developed during the French Revolutionary Wars from 1793,
is the foundational innovation; the staged evacuation-chain model matures
through the following century and a half, into the mobile forward-surgical
hospital systems of the mid-1900s.

## Mechanism

Time between wounding and treatment is often the single largest determinant
of survival for an otherwise treatable battlefield injury, independent of
how advanced the treatment itself is — organizing dedicated, fast evacuation
transport and staged triage (treating the most time-sensitive cases first,
sorting who can wait) converts casualty survival from a matter of individual
luck and proximity to a treatable rear-area facility into a logistics and
organizational design problem a state can deliberately invest in solving.

## Social consequence

A functioning evacuation-and-triage system changes soldiers' own
willingness to accept combat risk, since credible expectation of prompt
casualty care measurably affects morale independent of the system's actual
average outcomes — an army with a visible, trusted evacuation chain fights
differently from one where a serious wound is understood to mean near-
certain abandonment.

## Regional specifics

Letterman's Civil War-era three-stage evacuation chain becomes a durable
organizational template referenced well beyond the conflict that produced
it, showing a battlefield-tested logistics innovation can outlive its
original context by decades. East Asian militaries' later and more
import-driven adoption of comparable evacuation doctrine, particularly
Meiji Japan's direct adoption of Western models alongside its general-staff
import (WAR-009), fits the broader pattern of institutional capability being
adoptable as a complete package once demonstrated, rather than requiring
independent organic development.

## Hook

A staged evacuation-and-triage system converts casualty survival from
individual battlefield luck into a logistics-investment outcome, largely
independent of the underlying medical efficacy (HLT's territory) available
at the end of the chain — and measurably affects combat morale on its own,
separate from its actual average survival results.
