The shift from a seasonally-levied, self-equipped citizen or landholder militia
to a year-round, state-paid, state-equipped professional soldiery — a fiscal
and administrative threshold at least as much as a tactical one.

## Timing

Professional or semi-professional standing forces appear independently across
several Iron Age states between roughly -900 and -100, converging on a
broadly similar institution (paid, year-round, state-equipped soldiery)
without a single shared point of origin.

## Mechanism

A professional soldier requires continuous state pay, equipment, and
provisioning rather than showing up self-armed for a single campaign season —
a state can only sustain this if it has the fiscal legibility to extract and
budget the necessary continuous revenue, converting military capability from
a function of how many landholders can be levied into a function of how much
sustained tax revenue the state can reliably collect and administer.

## Social consequence

Professionalization severs the old tie between land tenure and military
obligation, replacing a warrior class defined by property ownership with one
defined by state salary — a shift that relocates political leverage away
from landholding elites (who no longer monopolize who shows up armed) and
toward whichever institution controls the pay and provisioning apparatus.

## Regional specifics

Neo-Assyrian standing regiments predate the more commonly cited Greek and
Roman professionalization cases by roughly two centuries, unsettling any
Mediterranean-centered origin story for this institution. Qin China's
performance-and-reward-based professionalization achieves a comparable
standing-force outcome through an incentive structure structurally distinct
from the citizen-obligation model underlying early Roman service, showing
more than one institutional route reaches the same professionalized end
state. Mauryan India's unusually large standing professional core, resting
on an unusually centralized fiscal base, is the clearest direct illustration
that professionalization scales with state fiscal capacity rather than with
any one culture's military tradition.

## Hook

A standing professional army converts sustained tax `legibility` directly
into year-round military capability, replacing the old land-tenure-for-
service bargain — a state's army size and readiness becomes a function of
its fiscal administration rather than of how many landholders it can call up
each season.
