Taking a surplus somebody else has already produced is cheaper than producing
an equivalent surplus, wherever that surplus is concentrated enough to find
and the taker can leave faster than the owner can respond — a correct answer
to a specific configuration rather than a stage societies pass through before
learning to farm.

## Timing

Raiding becomes viable as soon as there is accumulation worth the trip —
herds from roughly -8500 (AGR-030), granaried grain from roughly -8000
(AGR-050) — and recurs continuously wherever that condition holds, on foot
and later mounted, through Barbary corsair raiding's suppression in the
1830s. It is not superseded by organised state warfare; it runs alongside it
for the whole of this record.

## Mechanism

Three conditions have to hold together. The target's surplus must be storable
and visible, which is why foragers are never raided for subsistence and why a
granary is a better target than a field. The raider must be able to arrive and
leave inside the target's response time, an advantage that is already large
against an early farming village with no standing defence at all and that
horse (MIG-050) and camel (MIG-080) mobility later widen enormously without
having been required to open it. And retaliation must cost the target more
than absorbing the loss, which is why highland-to-lowland raiding recurs for
millennia — the lowland state can win any given engagement and still not
afford to pursue into terrain where its advantages invert. The same property
that satisfies the first condition is the one a tax system needs: a surplus
legible enough to assess is legible enough to locate, so `legibility`
investment builds a revenue base and a target map in the same motion.

## Social consequence

Livestock is the ideal object because it carries itself home — no porters, no
carts, no second trip — which is why the cattle raid rather than the granary
raid is the form that recurs independently across unconnected traditions.
Captive-taking is the same logic applied to people, and is the original form
of the slave trade, the point-of-origin violence that MIG-160's transatlantic
logistics later organises into a sustained commercial system rather than
invents. But the half that explains raiding's persistence is not the haul at
all — it is that a raid is a route to adult standing for a young man with no
inheritance to wait for, converting participation directly into the
bridewealth and reputation that marriage and full membership require. A
society with more such men than its land can absorb has a raiding supply
generated by its own age structure, independent of whether any particular
raid pays. The defensive answer is correspondingly expensive even when no raid
comes: settlement nucleates, sites move uphill, refuge towers get built and
maintained, and fields end up further from dwellings than agronomy alone
would ever put them — so living within reach of raiders costs a standing
productivity discount in every year, including the quiet ones.

## Regional specifics

Mesopotamian highland-lowland raiding is the mechanism's clearest structural
case — the alluvium's concentration is precisely what makes it worth raiding
and its openness precisely what makes it hard to defend, an asymmetry stable
enough to recur under three separate named highland groups. The Vedic
gaviṣṭi and the Irish Táin preserve, in two unrelated literatures, the same
identification of war with cattle-taking, which is convergence on a
configuration rather than evidence of contact. Chinese frontier raiding
predates organised steppe confederation (MIG-110) and motivates walling long
before WAR-160's artillery-era fortification. East African stock raiding
shows the institution running continuously with its own recruitment and
payoff structure rather than as a stage. The Aztec flower war is the case
that strains the economic reading hardest and is treated as such below rather
than assimilated to it.

## Contested

Whether the material configuration explains raiding, or whether status,
revenge, and ritual capture drive it independently of any return, is
disputed. Model the configuration as the viability condition and the status
and ritual drivers as separate, independently-triggerable persistence
conditions — a society can then be raiding at a net material loss without the
model treating that as an error, which is the outcome a purely economic
reading cannot produce and the flower war plainly requires.

## Hook

A branch, not a technology: raiding is available wherever surplus is storable
and visible, the raider holds a timing advantage over the response, and
retaliation costs more than absorption — and it charges the target twice,
once for whatever is actually taken and continuously for the defensive
settlement pattern that living in range imposes, so a region pays for raiders
in every year including the ones with no raid in them.
