The physical construction of trenches and field fortifications is ENG's;
this entry owns the strategic and doctrinal consequence once massed
artillery (WAR-130) and automatic weapons make field fortification
defensively dominant enough to grind offensive maneuver to a standstill.

## Timing

The dynamic previews at Port Arthur during the Russo-Japanese War
(1904-1905); it becomes the dominant strategic reality of the Western Front
from 1914 through 1918, once both sides' field fortifications mutually
neutralize offensive maneuver across an extended, continuous front line.

## Mechanism

Massed artillery and automatic weapons (an extension of WAR-110's and
WAR-130's firepower trajectory) make an entrenched defender extraordinarily
costly to dislodge through direct assault, while the same firepower makes
any attacker crossing open ground between trench lines extremely vulnerable
— once both sides possess comparable firepower and fortification capacity
along a continuous front with no open flank to maneuver around, the result
is a strategic stalemate where WAR-100's attrition dynamic dominates because
decisive maneuver has become nearly impossible.

## Social consequence

Positional stalemate imposes enormous, visible, sustained casualties for
minimal territorial gain, directly intensifying the domestic political
strain and morale-management pressure WAR-100 associates with prolonged
attrition — trench warfare's specific horror (visible to soldiers and,
through casualty statistics, WAR-230's territory, to the home front) becomes
a major driver of the propaganda and mobilization innovations covered in
WAR-260 and WAR-310.

## Regional specifics

The Port Arthur siege gives European military observers direct, documented
advance warning of exactly the defensive-firepower dynamic that would
dominate the Western Front a decade later, making 1914's continued
commitment to offensive maneuver doctrine a case of an available lesson
going substantially unlearned rather than an unforeseeable surprise — the
information existed, but institutional doctrine didn't sufficiently update
in response before the cost of not updating became catastrophic.

## Hook

Once artillery and automatic-weapon firepower (WAR-130) outpaces offensive
maneuver capability along a continuous front with no open flank, the result
is a positional stalemate that locks both sides into WAR-100's attrition
dynamic — a doctrinal trap that, per the Port Arthur precedent, can be
observed and warned about years before a military establishment actually
revises its offensive doctrine to account for it.
