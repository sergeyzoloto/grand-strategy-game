A mercenary company sells organized, trained violence to whichever payer offers the best contract — a third army-manpower channel alongside WAR-001's state-salaried professional and WAR-012's legally-conscripted citizen, with loyalty conditional on payment rather than on obligation to any one state.

## Timing

Italian condottieri companies contract their services to city-states from around 1300; Swiss pikemen and, following their model, German Landsknechte become the dominant mercenary infantry type across European battlefields from the 1400s through the 1500s; large-scale entrepreneurial mercenary armies (Wallenstein's Thirty Years' War force being the paradigm case) persist through the mid-1600s before standing professional and conscript armies (WAR-001, WAR-012) progressively displace the model.

## Mechanism

A mercenary company converts military labor supply into a market transaction: a captain raises, trains, and equips a force independently, then contracts its services to whichever state or city offers the best terms for a given campaign or season, letting a payer raise capable trained troops far faster than building a standing army (WAR-001) from scratch and without the legal and administrative apparatus conscription (WAR-012) requires — at the cost of a force whose loyalty is to its contract and its own pay, not to the hiring state, and which can defect, extort better terms mid-campaign, or simply stop fighting the moment payment lapses.

## Social consequence

Mercenary captains who accumulate enough successful contracts and reputation can convert that military standing directly into independent political power, sometimes over the very polities that hired them — a route to power available to a skilled military entrepreneur that neither WAR-001's salaried career officer nor WAR-012's conscripted citizen-soldier has, since a mercenary captain's personal command over a loyal, self-contained force is itself a political asset independent of any state office.

## Regional specifics

Italian condottieri companies illustrate the mechanic's most extreme political consequence: a successful captain's own force and reputation could become powerful enough to seize the contracting city itself, as the Sforza dynasty's rise in Milan shows directly. East Asian militaries of the same era rely far more consistently on state levy, professionalization, or land-tenure obligation than on an open cross-polity mercenary market, showing the market-for-violence model isn't a universal stage every military system passes through, but one specific institutional choice among several viable alternatives.

## Hook

A third manpower-supply channel alongside WAR-001's state-salaried professional and WAR-012's legal conscript: contracted market violence buys speed and skill without the fiscal or legal apparatus either alternative requires, at the cost of loyalty that lasts exactly as long as payment does — and, in its most successful cases, can convert into the mercenary captain's own independent political power.
