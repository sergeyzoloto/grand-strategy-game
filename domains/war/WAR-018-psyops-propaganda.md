Deliberately shaping belief and morale — among one's own population,
neutral observers, and the enemy — as an organized instrument of war,
converting the casualty visibility and home-front strain that attritional
and positional warfare (WAR-013, WAR-015) generate into something a state
can actively try to manage rather than simply absorb.

## Timing

Systematic, centrally-organized state propaganda apparatus develops sharply
from World War One (1914) onward, reaching its most extensive institutional
form across multiple combatant states through World War Two (to 1945).

## Mechanism

Sustained warfare, especially attritional or positional warfare, generates
casualty visibility and material strain that can erode home-front morale and
neutral-power sympathy faster than military outcomes alone would justify —
propaganda directly targets that gap, using mass media (newsreels, radio,
print) to shape interpretation of the same underlying facts (WAR-010's
casualty statistics among them) in the state's favor, extending a state's
effective control over the war's political meaning well beyond its control
over the battlefield itself.

## Social consequence

Once propaganda becomes a normal, expected instrument of state wartime
communication, it changes the public's baseline relationship to official
information generally — populations exposed to obvious wartime propaganda
often become more broadly skeptical of state communication even in
peacetime, a durable trust effect that outlasts the conflict that produced
it.

## Regional specifics

The American Committee on Public Information's rapid build-out from
essentially nothing shows the institutional capacity for large-scale
propaganda can be assembled quickly once a state prioritizes it, rather than
requiring long organic development. Imperial Japanese wartime propaganda's
distinct thematic content — Pan-Asianism and anti-Western-imperialism
framing rather than the European Allied or Axis narrative templates —
demonstrates the underlying mechanic (using mass media to manage morale and
belief for strategic effect) is separable from any specific narrative
content, adaptable to whatever themes actually resonate with a given
audience.

## Hook

Propaganda extends a state's effective control over the political meaning of
a war beyond its actual battlefield or attritional position, converting
`information speed` and mass-media reach into a home-front and diplomatic
morale-management tool — with a durable side effect of eroding public trust
in state communication that persists well past the conflict itself.
