The metallurgy of iron smelting and working is ENG's; this entry owns what
cheap, abundant iron (relative to bronze's tin-dependent, trade-network-
reliant supply chain) does to who can be armed, and therefore who fights.

## Timing

Ironworking spreads and scales from roughly -1200 through -500 across the
Mediterranean, Middle East, and independently in East Asia and parts of
Africa, overlapping with and partly driving the Bronze Age collapse in the
eastern Mediterranean.

## Mechanism

Bronze requires tin, which for most Bronze Age Mediterranean and Middle
Eastern states means importing it across long, vulnerable trade networks —
a supply chain fragile enough that disruption (as in the Bronze Age
collapse) can cut off weapon production entirely. Iron ore, by contrast, is
widely locally available almost everywhere, so once smelting technique
matures, the cost of arming a soldier drops sharply and stops depending on
maintaining a long-distance trade network at all — converting weapons
supply from a trade-network-dependent bottleneck into a widely
distributable local capability.

## Social consequence

Where bronze weaponry required enough capital and trade access that only a
narrow elite could outfit themselves (and, often, their smaller elite
retinues), cheap iron lets a much larger share of a population be armed to a
comparable standard — directly feeding the mass-infantry formations (see
WAR-003) that displace small elite-warrior-dominated battle forms across
several independent regions in the following centuries.

## Regional specifics

Chinese blast-furnace cast-iron technology reaches mass-production capability
considerably earlier than the Mediterranean's wrought-iron-dominant tradition,
producing standardized equipment at a scale and speed the Mediterranean world
doesn't match for centuries. Independent African ironworking feeding directly
into the Bantu expansion's own material toolkit (MIG-002) shows the
technology's spread and a major migration wave developing hand in hand rather
than one simply following the other. South Asian wootz steel's orientation
toward exceptional quality over bulk volume shows mass-equipping wasn't the
only rational use of advanced ironworking capability — a region could excel
at the metallurgy and still choose a different output profile entirely.

## Contested

Iron's causal role in the Bronze Age collapse and the associated decline of
bronze-dependent elite warrior castes is disputed against climate stress,
the Sea Peoples migrations, and cascading trade-network failure as
competing or complementary explanations. Model mass iron availability as one
independently-weighted pressure among several contributing to elite-warrior-
monopoly collapse in any given region, rather than scripting it as the sole
or primary cause everywhere it occurs.

## Hook

Cheap, locally-available iron breaks the trade-network dependency that
previously let a narrow elite monopolize weapons access, directly enabling
the shift toward mass-equipped formations (WAR-003) wherever the metallurgy
matures — a supply-chain unlock more than a battlefield-technology one.
