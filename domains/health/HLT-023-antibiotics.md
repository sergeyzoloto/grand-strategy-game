The clearest `medical efficacy` step change in this entire record, and the
first entry that models as a depleting pool rather than a permanent
capability gain — the drug that works today works less well tomorrow, by the
same mechanism that made it work at all.

## Timing

Fleming's observation of penicillin's antibacterial effect dates to 1928;
wartime mass production, driven by Allied military demand, scales it into a
widely available treatment by 1945.

## Mechanism

Antibiotics directly kill or halt bacterial growth via a specific biochemical
mechanism, the first treatment in this record with unambiguous, large,
measurable efficacy against a broad class of previously often-fatal
infections. The same selective pressure that makes the drug effective also
selects for resistant bacterial strains with every use, so the intervention's
effectiveness is a consumable resource, not a fixed capability — heavy or
careless use draws the pool down for everyone, including future users,
exactly as design principle 4 describes.

## Social consequence

Mass antibiotic access changes ordinary risk calculus at every scale — minor
wounds, childbirth, routine surgery — that had carried meaningful mortality
risk for the entirety of this record up to this point, compressing centuries
of `medical efficacy` gap into a period of a few decades in the regions with
early access, while regions with delayed access continue experiencing
pre-antibiotic mortality patterns for those same conditions well into the
20th century and beyond.

## Regional specifics

Wartime American industrial capacity, not European laboratory priority, is
what converts the discovery into a mass-deployable resource — Fleming's
discovery and the production scale-up that makes it historically significant
happen in two different regions for two different structural reasons.
Africa's persistent access lag, well after the drug exists and works
everywhere, is a distribution and purchasing-power story layered entirely on
top of the underlying medical-efficacy story — the same drug, the same
efficacy, a completely different regional outcome driven by supply chains.

## Hook

Antibiotics grant a large, immediate `medical efficacy` jump, but instantiate
as a depleting `antibiotic pool` (design principle 4): every use draws down
future effectiveness via resistance, so sustained benefit requires ongoing
stewardship investment rather than a one-time unlock.
