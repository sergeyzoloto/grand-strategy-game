Milk is a calorie- and protein-dense food adults can only fully digest if
either their genes or their processing technique compensates for lactase
production normally switching off after infancy.

## Timing

Dairying itself begins with animal domestication (~-7000, Fertile Crescent);
the lactase-persistence allele that lets European and some African/Middle
Eastern populations digest raw milk into adulthood only reaches high frequency
much later (broadly -4000 to -2000), well after dairying practice itself.

## Mechanism

Two independent solutions to the same digestive constraint: a genetic one
(the lactase-persistence allele, which arose independently at least twice —
Europe/Middle East and East Africa) and a technological one (fermenting milk
into yogurt or cheese, which breaks down lactose before consumption and
requires no genetic adaptation at all). Populations with neither still get
calories from milk as an occasional or processed food; populations with either
get a reliable, renewable, non-slaughter protein and fat source that ordinary
meat livestock doesn't provide.

## Social consequence

Where dairying is intensive, it restructures pastoralist life around herd
management as continuous work rather than periodic slaughter, and around milk
and its processed forms as a status-inflected, tradeable, storable good —
steppe cultures in particular build substantial social and dietary structure
around fermented milk products that require no genetic adaptation to access at
all.

## Regional specifics

The independent evolution of lactase persistence in East Africa and in Europe
is a clean case of convergent biological adaptation to the same cultural
practice arising twice. Steppe pastoralists reaching viable milk consumption
through fermentation rather than genetics show the technological route can
fully substitute for the biological one. East Asia's persistent low lactase
persistence despite long-standing access to dairy animals, and the total
absence of dairying in the pre-contact Americas for lack of a domesticable milk
animal, are both genuine, stable regional differences — not delayed versions of
the same eventual outcome.
