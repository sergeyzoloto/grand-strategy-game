A confraternity, guild, or friendly society pools regular member
contributions specifically to cover fellow members' illness, injury, and
burial costs — a peer-funded risk pool distinct from both household folk
care's complete absence of pooling and a charitable hospital's
donor-funded endowment.

## Timing

European craft-guild and religious confraternity mutual-obligation funds
are attested from around 1250, maturing into dedicated friendly societies
with formal actuarial-adjacent contribution schedules by the 18th and
19th centuries; comparable Ottoman guild and Chinese native-place
association structures develop on overlapping but independent timelines
through the same broad era.

## Mechanism

Members pay a regular, budgeted contribution into a shared fund
specifically earmarked to cover a member's illness-related lost income,
medical cost, or burial expense when the need arises — converting an
individually catastrophic and unpredictable cost into a predictable,
shared, recurring one, requiring no religious or state sponsorship to
function and no member to individually accumulate savings against a risk
that might never materialize for them specifically.

## Social consequence

Mutual-aid membership extends real financial protection specifically to
artisan and laboring households too well-off to qualify for religious
charity (HLT-014) but too exposed to any single illness or death to
self-insure through savings alone — filling a coverage gap neither the
household folk-care substrate nor the donor-funded hospital reaches, and
doing so through the same craft or communal ties that already structure
members' working lives rather than through any external institution.

## Regional specifics

Ottoman esnaf guilds bundle comparable member-funded mutual obligation
directly into their broader craft-regulatory function rather than
maintaining it as a separate dedicated fund. Chinese huiguan associations
reach a structurally similar risk-pooling outcome through shared
geographic origin or lineage rather than shared craft, showing the
underlying mechanism — a bounded, mutually-obligated membership pooling
contributions against individually unpredictable but collectively
predictable risk — recurs independently of which specific tie (craft,
origin, kinship) defines the qualifying membership.

## Hook

A modifier/pool: converts individually catastrophic illness or burial
cost into a shared, budgeted, recurring member contribution, extending
`medical access` to exactly the households too poor to self-insure and
not poor enough to qualify for religious or state charity (HLT-014).
