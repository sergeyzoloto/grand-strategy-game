Marking status or belonging on the body or its covering is close to universal;
what varies enormously, and matters mechanically, is how much of that marking
is reversible display versus a permanent, health-costed alteration of the body
itself.

## Timing

Symbolic beads and ochre marking are attested from the Middle Stone Age in
Africa (~-75000); the specific severe-modification and sumptuary-law cases
compared here are much later and separately dated in their own regions.

## Mechanism

Two distinct cost structures achieve the same signaling function. Reversible
display (dyed cloth, restricted colors or materials, jewelry) marks status at
near-zero health cost but is easy to counterfeit or acquire, which is exactly
why it invites legal restriction (sumptuary law) to keep it scarce enough to
keep signaling anything. Permanent body modification (foot binding,
scarification, some forms of tattooing) marks status at a real, sometimes
severe, biomechanical or health cost, but that cost is precisely what makes it
uncounterfeitable — you cannot fake having paid it.

## Social consequence

Where marking is cheap and reversible, maintaining the status hierarchy
requires ongoing legal enforcement (who may wear what) rather than any
lasting mark on the body — the LAW-side sumptuary mechanic. Where marking is
severe and permanent, the enforcement is front-loaded into the body itself:
no ongoing policing is needed because the marked person cannot un-pay the
cost, but the household or family absorbing a lifelong mobility or health
penalty (as with foot binding) pays a standing, compounding cost far beyond
the initial marking.

## Regional specifics

Chinese foot binding is the clearest severe-and-permanent case: a specific,
datable elite practice (Song dynasty onward) that becomes a broad status marker
with a serious, lifelong biomechanical cost. West and Central African
scarification traditions independently arrive at body-marking-as-uncounterfeitable-
signal, at a much lower and largely-healed health cost, showing the
severe/reversible axis is a spectrum with more than one stable point on it, not
a two-option choice. European status-marking sits at the opposite pole from
foot binding: heavily regulated reversible display (sumptuary law) substituting
almost entirely for costly permanent modification.

## Hook

Status-marking practices sit on a reversible-display-to-permanent-modification
spectrum: cheap-signal societies need ongoing legal enforcement to keep the
signal scarce (ties to LAW), while costly-signal societies pay the enforcement
cost upfront as a standing health/mobility penalty on the marked population instead.
