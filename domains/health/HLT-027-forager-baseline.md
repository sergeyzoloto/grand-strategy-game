Before any settlement, storage pit, or domesticated animal exists to
concentrate people, food, and pathogens together, a mobile forager's diet,
social contact pattern, and physical activity already produce a specific,
non-arbitrary health and body baseline — the actual zero-point every later
entry in this domain measures its costs and gains against, currently
assumed rather than established.

## Timing

Anatomically modern human foraging populations are attested from roughly
-300000 in Africa, with the pattern this entry describes persisting
essentially everywhere until a given population's own transition to
sedentism and storage — as late as -10000 in the earliest Fertile Crescent
case, and considerably later still in regions HLT-001 already notes never
cross the threshold within this record's timeframe at all.

## Mechanism

A forager population's diet draws on dozens of wild food sources across a
season rather than a handful of stored staples, its social units disperse
and recombine rather than staying fixed in continuous proximity, and its
physical activity varies day to day (walking, digging, climbing, carrying)
rather than repeating one narrow labor pattern — three structural features
that jointly produce low chronic pathogen exposure (no standing grain
reserve to draw vermin, no year-round crowding), broad nutritional
adequacy (dietary variety buffers any single food source's failure), and a
varied rather than repetitive-strain skeletal load profile, all without
anyone involved experiencing it as a deliberate health strategy.

## Social consequence

Because storage, surplus, and fixed dwelling don't yet exist as
organizing structures, wealth and status differentiation has no
comparable physical anchor to what HLT-001's household-as-storage-unit
later provides — inequality in a forager band is real but structurally
limited by the absence of an accumulable, hoardable surplus, a baseline
social flatness (relative, not absolute) that sedentism's storage
economy is what actually displaces.

## Regional specifics

Africa's much longer occupation history gives this baseline its deepest
and clearest evidentiary record, but the pattern itself held equally
wherever and for as long as a population stayed foraging — the Fertile
Crescent's own forager populations lived this same baseline for tens of
thousands of years before their own transition to storage-based
settlement (HLT-001) displaced it locally.

## Contested

How much the "Neolithic decline" reading depends on an idealized,
insufficiently regionally-specific forager baseline is disputed among
bioarchaeologists — savanna, coastal, and circumpolar foraging carried
genuinely different risk profiles, and some forager populations plausibly
fared worse than some farming populations on specific measures. Model the
baseline's generally favorable profile (diet diversity, low crowd-disease
exposure, varied physical load) as real, while treating any specific
numeric comparison to a specific farming population as a separate,
regionally-contingent claim rather than a fixed universal gap.

## Hook

A gate: establishes the pre-sedentism body and disease-exposure baseline
as this domain's actual starting condition — the explicit, previously
unmodeled contrast point HLT-001's toxic-burden accumulation and HLT-007's
stature/dental penalty are both measured against, and the state HLT-002's
"healthier, lower-density countryside" silently assumes is still available
nearby once cities begin drawing on it.
