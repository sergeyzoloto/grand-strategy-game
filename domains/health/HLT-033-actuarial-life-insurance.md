Edmond Halley's 1693 mortality table converts age-specific death
probability into a calculable, sellable price, letting a commercial
insurer profitably underwrite individual mortality risk for a premium
rather than requiring a bounded community of mutually obligated members.

## Timing

Halley's mortality table, built from Breslau parish records, is published
in 1693; commercially viable life insurance and annuity markets built on
increasingly refined actuarial tables develop across the following two
centuries, maturing into a major financial sector by 1900.

## Mechanism

Actuarial pricing takes the same kind of population-level mortality
pattern public health statistics (HLT-032) first extracts and puts it to
a different use: rather than targeting a public health intervention, an
insurer prices an individual policy against the known age-specific death
probability across a large enough pool of policyholders that individual
uncertainty averages out into a predictable, profitable aggregate — a
market solution to the same underlying risk problem HLT-029's mutual-aid
funds solve through membership obligation instead.

## Social consequence

Because actuarial life insurance is priced and sold rather than earned
through community membership, it extends risk-transfer to anyone who can
afford the premium regardless of guild, craft, or communal tie — a wider
but more strictly cash-gated form of coverage than HLT-029's mutual funds,
available to the wealthy independent of any qualifying social bond and
unavailable to the poor regardless of how strong their community ties
are.

## Regional specifics

This entry's development is concentrated in England and the Netherlands,
where a combination of available mortality data, a maturing commercial
insurance market, and receptive capital markets let actuarial pricing
scale into a major financial institution faster than in regions with any
one of those three preconditions missing.

## Hook

A modifier: converts population-level mortality data (HLT-032) directly
into a tradeable financial instrument, extending mortality-risk transfer
to anyone who can afford a premium rather than only to members of a
qualifying community (HLT-029) — the same underlying risk pooled by
price instead of by membership obligation.
