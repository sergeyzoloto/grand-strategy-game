The first entry in this record where `medical efficacy` starts moving upward
independent of, and eventually because of a break with, existing `medical
legitimacy` — a genuinely European-centered development this time, not a
false-balance case.

## Timing

Semmelweis's handwashing intervention in Vienna (1847) is the first
well-documented case; Lister's carbolic-acid antiseptic surgical technique
(from 1867), grounded in Pasteur's germ theory, is the point at which the
practice acquires a causal theory and begins spreading as a professional
standard rather than a single doctor's contested claim.

## Mechanism

Antisepsis directly targets the transmission mechanism (pathogen transfer via
unwashed hands, unsterilized instruments) rather than treating symptoms after
the fact, which is what makes it a genuine efficacy improvement rather than
one more theory-driven intervention layered onto existing humoral practice —
it works because it interrupts the actual causal chain, independent of
whether the physician applying it even accepts germ theory.

## Social consequence

Semmelweis's own career is destroyed by professional rejection of his
findings — the medical establishment's authority structure actively resisting
a genuine efficacy gain because it implicated existing practitioners'
unwashed hands in patient deaths, a stark illustration of `medical legitimacy`
actively opposing a `medical efficacy` improvement rather than merely being
uncorrelated with it. Lister's later success owes as much to arriving after
germ theory gave the intervention a legitimating causal story as to the
intervention itself being any different in substance.

## Regional specifics

This is a genuinely Europe-concentrated development in its origin and initial
institutionalization, unlike most of this domain's entries — there is no
comparable independent antisepsis breakthrough elsewhere in this period to
region-balance against, though the older piecemeal antimicrobial folk
practices noted in `absence` show other traditions had stumbled onto partial,
untheorized versions of the same effect long before.

## Hook

Antisepsis is the first entry where `medical efficacy` starts a sustained
climb decoupled from `medical legitimacy` — a precondition this domain's later
entries (antibiotics) build directly on, and a rare case of the authority
structure actively fighting the efficacy gain rather than just ignoring it.
