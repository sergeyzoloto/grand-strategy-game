Every major transregional pandemic in this record rides an existing trade or
transport network — the disease doesn't create the connectivity, the
connectivity creates the pandemic's reach.

## Timing

The Plague of Justinian (541) via Mediterranean/Red Sea grain shipping, the
Black Death (from 1331) via the Mongol-integrated steppe and Mediterranean
networks, and the Third Plague Pandemic (from 1855) via steamship routes are
the three clearest instances across roughly thirteen centuries.

## Mechanism

Pandemic reach and speed scale directly with the connectivity and throughput
of whatever trade or transport network is at its peak in a given era — the
same route knowledge and infrastructure that carries goods and lowers
transaction costs also carries pathogens, at a speed set by the fastest
common transport mode of the day (camel caravan, sailing ship, steamship). The
cost is symmetric and impossible to unbundle at the time: there is no version
of the trade network that carries silk and spice but not disease.

## Social consequence

Regions and trading communities most integrated into the network — exactly
the ones most enriched by it in ordinary times — pay the highest exposure cost
when a connectivity plague arrives, while genuinely isolated populations are
protected right up until the moment they aren't (see the Americas absence
case), at which point isolation's protective effect inverts into catastrophic
vulnerability from zero acquired immunity.

## Regional specifics

The Black Death's path traces the Mongol steppe network with striking
fidelity, arriving fastest exactly where the preceding century of Mongol-peace
trade integration had been deepest. The Third Plague Pandemic reproduces the
same logic three centuries later at steamship speed, reaching the Americas,
Africa, and Oceania within a couple of decades of its Yunnan origin — a
connectivity-driven reach the Black Death, bounded by Eurasia and caravan
speed, never achieved. The pre-Columbian Americas' total exclusion from every
one of these events until 1492 is the cleanest natural experiment available
for what network integration itself costs in disease exposure.

## Hook

Trade/transport `route knowledge` and network integration are the same stat
that determines both peacetime commercial throughput and pandemic
transmission speed and reach — raising one raises the other, with no way to
have the benefit without the exposure.
