A dedicated institution confines and, depending on era and philosophy,
either restrains or attempts to treat the mentally ill as a distinct
medical population — converting madness from a diffuse family and
community problem into an institutionally managed, physically separated
category, with custodial control and genuine care pulling in the same
direction only inconsistently.

## Timing

London's Bethlem Hospital, founded in 1247, gradually specializes toward
confining the mentally ill specifically over the following centuries; the
York Retreat's 1796 "moral treatment" reform (structured routine, minimal
restraint, an explicitly therapeutic rather than purely custodial model)
marks a significant philosophical shift; the 19th-century county-asylum-
building wave generalizes dedicated institutional confinement across
Europe through 1890, immediately preceding psychoanalysis's 1895 emergence
(HLT-022).

## Mechanism

An asylum substitutes institutional confinement for family or community
management of a population whose behavior is read as requiring specialist
control — the same charitable-endowment logic HLT-014 already covers, but
applied to a population where "care" and "restraint" are frequently the
same physical act, and where the institution's actual therapeutic
`medical efficacy` varies enormously by era and specific administration
rather than tracking any steady improvement curve, unlike HLT-021's later,
genuinely progressive antisepsis case.

## Social consequence

Confinement removes a difficult-to-manage individual from their family and
community at real cost to the confined person's autonomy, converting a
socially disruptive or frightening condition into an institutionally
contained one — a real relief for families and communities whether or not
the confined individual received anything resembling effective treatment,
which is exactly what makes it hard to separate an asylum's genuine
therapeutic function from its custodial-removal function in any specific
historical case.

## Regional specifics

Bimaristans include specialized mental-health wards from the Abbasid
period, centuries before comparable European institutional attention,
treating madness as warranting genuine clinical care within the same
broader charitable-hospital framework HLT-014 covers rather than as a
dedicated standalone confinement institution. The dedicated European
asylum, by contrast, is a specific, datable innovation — before Bethlem's
gradual specialization, most medieval European management of madness ran
through family or religious rather than medical channels at all.

## Contested

How much genuine therapeutic improvement moral treatment and its
successors actually delivered, versus how much asylum institutions
functioned primarily as social confinement for unwanted populations
regardless of stated philosophy, is a live historiographical dispute.
Model custodial-control function and genuine therapeutic function as two
separately-tracked variables rather than assuming either fully explains
asylum-building in any given era or institution.

## Hook

A modifier/branch: the asylum grants medical authority over madness at a
`medical efficacy` level that stays low and highly era-dependent —
sometimes actively harmful confinement, sometimes genuine moral-treatment-
era improvement — the same authority/efficacy decoupling HLT-011
established, applied to a population whose confinement is itself
frequently the actual social function being purchased.
