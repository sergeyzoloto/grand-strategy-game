Deliberate smallpox immunization is independently developed and folk-practiced
in China, the Ottoman world, and West Africa well before Jenner's cowpox
refinement — a rare case in this record of a high-efficacy, low-authority folk
technique reaching Western Europe from outside its own medical establishment
rather than the reverse.

## Timing

Chinese variolation is documented from the 16th century; Ottoman/Circassian
practice reaches British awareness through Lady Mary Wortley Montagu in 1717;
Onesimus's account of West African practice reaches Cotton Mather in Boston by
1706; Jenner's safer cowpox-based method follows in 1796.

## Mechanism

Variolation (deliberate exposure to matter from a mild smallpox case) trades a
real, non-trivial mortality risk (roughly 1-2%) for near-certain future
immunity against a disease that otherwise kills 20-30% of those it infects —
a favorable but not risk-free bet, which is exactly why it spreads first
through folk and enslaved/colonized knowledge networks with direct experience
of smallpox's alternative, rather than through the codified medical
establishments that generally distrust unlicensed foreign practice. Jenner's
innovation swaps the source material (cowpox rather than smallpox itself),
removing the mortality-risk side of the bet entirely while keeping the
immunity payoff — a genuine efficacy improvement on an imported base
technique, not an independent invention.

## Social consequence

The technique's transmission route into Western elite awareness runs
specifically through a woman (Lady Mary Wortley Montagu) observing a practice
inside Ottoman women's social networks, and through an enslaved man
(Onesimus) whose knowledge a prominent clergyman chose, unusually, to trust
and publicize — both routes cut directly against the era's usual authority
hierarchy of whose medical knowledge counted, and both faced significant
resistance from formally credentialed physicians specifically because of
where the knowledge came from, not because of its measured effectiveness.

## Regional specifics

China's documented practice is the earliest of the three independent or
semi-independent traditions compared here. The Ottoman and West African
transmission routes into Britain and its colonies are separate events with
separate carriers, arriving at comparable practical technique from different
starting traditions. Formal European medicine's total lack of a comparable
prior practice, followed by its initial resistance to adopting a foreign,
low-status source technique even once its effectiveness was well attested
locally, is itself a clean illustration of `medical legitimacy` and `medical
efficacy` moving independently (design principle 3) — here, briefly, with
efficacy having to fight uphill against an establishment's authority
prejudice rather than the more usual reverse.

## Hook

A working, high-efficacy folk immunization technique can sit outside a
society's high-`medical legitimacy` institutions for centuries before being
adopted — efficacy and institutional authority don't just fail to track each
other, low-authority sources can hold a real, usable efficacy advantage the
high-authority system has to be shown before it will take it.
