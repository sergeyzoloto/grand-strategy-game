Fixed storage and year-round settlement concentrate people, stored grain, and
the vermin and zoonotic vectors that ride along with both — a chronic exposure
pattern the mobile-forager lifestyle didn't carry in the same form.

## Timing

From roughly -10000 in the Fertile Crescent (earliest storage pits precede
domesticated cereals themselves), spreading via independently-originated
sedentism in East Asia (~-9000) and, later and more gradually, the Americas
(~-5000). Treated here as an era-length structural shift, not a dated event.

## Mechanism

Storage architecture — pits, granaries, sealed vessels — fixes a population in
place and creates a standing reservoir of calories that also feeds commensal
rodents and insects, and the pathogens they carry. Year-round proximity to
penned or corralled domestic animals adds zoonotic spillover on top. The
economist's read: the cost of disease exposure shifts from acute and dispersed
(a forager band's occasional bad luck) to chronic and concentrated (a
settlement's continuous background rate), paid in small amounts constantly
rather than in rare spikes.

## Social consequence

The household becomes the storage unit, and therefore the unit of wealth and of
risk — storable surplus is surplus that can be unequally hoarded, in a way a
forager's daily catch cannot be. Closer, longer cohabitation with stored food,
vermin, and animals becomes ordinary background life rather than an occasional
hazard: a new baseline that nobody chose and few would have recognized as a
trade-off at the time (habitus, not decision).

## Regional specifics

East Asian millet and rice sedentism reproduces the storage-and-proximity
pattern on an entirely different crop and animal base, suggesting the dynamic
is generic to storage-based settlement rather than specific to Fertile Crescent
species. The Americas complicate the pattern: later sedentism plus a much
thinner roster of domesticable animals (no cattle, sheep, goats, or pigs at
scale) means the storage effect on toxic burden shows up without the same
zoonotic-spillover layer — a genuine regional asymmetry, not a lag. Large parts
of Africa never cross this threshold in the same way at all: mobile mixed
foraging/herding persists as a stable, load-bearing alternative shaped by local
constraints (tsetse ecology, flood-recession farming), not an unfinished
transition.

## Contested

Skeletal evidence (reduced stature, increased dental caries and enamel
hypoplasia, higher infection markers) supports a straightforward health decline
at the individual level with the shift to sedentism and cereal dependence —
the "Neolithic health decline" reading. Against this, population growth and
rising social complexity are read by some as revealed-preference evidence that
whatever was lost individually was outweighed collectively, or that the
skeletal signal is itself an artifact of larger, more visible cemetery samples.
Model this as two independent dials rather than a single resolved question:
`toxic burden` and pathogen exposure rise unconditionally with storage-based
settlement (not contested), while the net welfare effect is a separate,
downstream function of food security and stratification that can go either way
depending on what else is true of the society.

## Hook

Settlement plus storage silently accumulates a `toxic burden` / pathogen-exposure
stock from turn one — invisible in the moment, revealed only much later when a
health-adjacent technology (sanitation, germ theory) finally lets the player see
what it's been costing all along.
