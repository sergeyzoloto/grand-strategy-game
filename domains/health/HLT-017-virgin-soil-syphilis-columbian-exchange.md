Two disease transfers run in opposite directions across the same contact
event, at wildly different scales: a catastrophic, one-directional wave of
Old World crowd diseases into a population with no prior exposure, and a
smaller, contested, possibly-reciprocal exchange the other way.

## Timing

From 1492, with the Old-World-to-Americas epidemic wave unfolding in repeated
waves over the following century-plus (rather than as a single event), and
the first clearly documented European syphilis outbreak in 1494.

## Mechanism

"Virgin soil" epidemic mortality is a direct function of `immunological
capital`: a population with zero prior generational exposure to a pathogen
has no acquired or selected-for resistance at all, producing mortality rates
(plausibly 50-90% in the worst-hit regions over the following century) far
beyond what the same pathogens produced in Old World populations with long
co-exposure history. The underlying asymmetry traces back to the disease pool
itself: Eurasia's dense livestock-domestication package (HLT-005) generated a
much larger set of endemic crowd/zoonotic diseases than the thinner American
domesticate roster ever could, so the exchange was never going to be
symmetric regardless of which side made first contact.

## Social consequence

Population collapse on this scale doesn't just kill individuals, it breaks
labor systems, political structures, and knowledge transmission (elders,
specialists, ritual authorities die alongside everyone else) fast enough that
recovery isn't just repopulation but a genuine institutional rupture — and it
directly enables the subsequent colonial labor-extraction systems (including
the African slave trade this population collapse helps motivate) that follow
in its wake.

## Regional specifics

The mortality asymmetry is close to total in direction: there is no
comparably severe pre-contact American disease that devastates European
populations on return crossing. Syphilis is the one candidate for a
disease moving the other way, and even that is disputed rather than
confirmed. This one-sided severity is itself explained by the `immunological
capital` mechanism rather than by any difference in the resilience or care
practices of the populations involved — it's a direct, near-mechanical
consequence of which side had the animal-dense disease pool going in.

## Contested

Syphilis's geographic origin — genuinely a Columbian-exchange import, versus
a previously-present but under-recognized or differently-diagnosed European
disease — remains an open dispute, with treponemal-disease skeletal evidence
read differently by different researchers. Model this specific question as
its own branching/probabilistic sub-event, kept fully separate from the
Old-World-to-Americas crowd-disease transfer, whose overall direction, scale,
and mechanism (immunological capital asymmetry) are not seriously in dispute.

## Hook

Contact between populations with radically unequal `immunological capital`
against each other's endemic disease pools triggers a mortality wave scaled
to that capital gap, in whichever direction the gap runs — here, overwhelmingly
one direction, with the reverse-direction case (syphilis) modeled as a much
smaller, uncertain probabilistic event rather than a mirror-image one.
