Cheap, plentiful text turns reading from a spoken, communal, clergy-mediated
act into a private, silent, individually-paced one — a change in daily habit
with claimed downstream effects on selfhood that are more argued over than
settled.

## Timing

Movable type originates in China (1040s) and reaches Korea's Jikji by 1377;
Gutenberg's press (1450) is a later, independent development in movable-type
mechanics that catalyzes a much faster scaling into mass vernacular output in
Europe over the following two centuries.

## Mechanism

Print collapses the marginal cost of a copy far enough that book ownership and
private reading move from an institutional or elite privilege to something
an individual household can plausibly afford, which shifts reading from a
usually-communal, often read-aloud practice to a private, silent, self-paced
one. The habit — not the technology itself — is the actual lever: it changes
how much unsupervised, unmediated time an ordinary literate person spends
alone with a text.

## Social consequence

Private silent reading is a genuinely new mass behavior wherever it takes
hold, and it recurringly correlates with — though causation is contested —
new claims to individual conscience and interpretation that unsettle
institutions (religious and secular) used to mediating text for a mostly
oral-reception audience; the Reformation's vernacular Bible reading is the
paradigm case, but not the only one.

## Regional specifics

East Asia has the technological head start by centuries but a much slower
scale-up into mass private vernacular reading, plausibly a function of
character-set size and a print culture weighted toward state and scholarly
reproduction rather than commercial mass output — a case where inventing the
technology and mass-adopting the resulting habitus are genuinely separable
events, not two names for the same thing. Ottoman resistance to movable
Arabic-script print delays the same habitus shift for institutional and
religious reasons that have nothing to do with printing capability, which
existed and was in active use nearby for other scripts. Europe is the outlier
in speed of social uptake, not in technological priority.

## Contested

Whether silent private reading actually produces a new kind of interior,
individuated selfhood, or merely makes a pre-existing interior life newly
visible in the historical record (letters, diaries, confessional literature
all become more common at the same time reading habits change, which could be
a recording effect rather than a causal one), is unresolved among historians
of reading. Model as a slow, cumulative `privacy`/introspection-adjacent
habitus effect proportional to literacy rate and book affordability, not a
one-time discrete unlock — leaving room for the effect to be real but modest
rather than the sweeping mentality change sometimes claimed for it.

## Hook

Print affordability and literacy rate jointly and gradually raise a
`privacy`/interior-life stat via accumulated private-reading habit — a slow
habitus drift (ties to design principle 2), not a switch that flips on
invention of the press.
