The specific engineering — pipes, drains, gradient, materials — belongs to
ENG; this entry owns what that infrastructure does to a city's disease
dynamics once built, and what its absence does when it isn't.

## Timing

Earliest integrated systems in Indus Valley cities (~-2500: covered drains,
near-universal household connections). Roman engineering (~-600 onward)
achieves comparable or greater physical scale with less even household reach.
European capability collapses post-Rome and isn't rebuilt to comparable
population-adjusted coverage until the 19th-century sanitary movement.

## Mechanism

Separating drinking water intake from waste discharge, and moving waste away
from population fast enough that it doesn't recontaminate the water supply,
breaks the fecal-oral transmission route for a large share of endemic enteric
disease. The cost that moves is capital and maintenance labor for
infrastructure against a return that's statistical and invisible (fewer
deaths that would have happened, not a visible product) — which is exactly why
it's chronically underbuilt relative to its actual payoff.

## Social consequence

Where it exists, integrated water/waste infrastructure is a state- or
elite-capacity signal as much as a health intervention — it requires
sustained coordination and maintenance that only a reasonably legible,
resourced authority sustains, and its collapse (as in post-Roman Europe) is as
much a marker of state capacity collapse as of any single technological loss.

## Regional specifics

The Indus cities' near-universal household drains stand out precisely because
coverage, not just engineering sophistication, is what determines the health
payoff — a scaled-up version serving only elite quarters gets a much smaller
population-level effect. Tenochtitlan's dual-aqueduct fresh/brackish
separation and causeway system shows the same systematic logic arrived at
independently in the Americas, at a scale that surprised the first European
observers of the city. Post-Roman Europe's roughly thousand-year gap between
losing and rebuilding integrated infrastructure is the clearest absence case
in this domain: the knowledge existed once, was lost with the institutional
capacity to maintain it, and had to be reconstructed rather than merely
diffused back in.

## Hook

Water/waste infrastructure converts state legibility and capital investment
into a standing reduction of a settlement's disease-load rate — and, distinctively,
decays if maintenance investment lapses rather than persisting once built,
so a polity can lose this capability outright rather than just failing to gain it.
