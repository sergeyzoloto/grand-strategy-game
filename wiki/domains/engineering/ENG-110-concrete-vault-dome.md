Spanning open interior space without a forest of interior supporting
columns — solved independently by several traditions through genuinely
different structural principles, each with its own span limits and
material requirements.

## Timing

Roman concrete construction, culminating in the Pantheon's dome (completed
around 128 CE), matures from roughly -100; Islamic squinch and muqarnas
solutions develop from around 600; Maya corbel vaulting is attested from
around 250, all developing on independent or only loosely connected
technical trajectories.

## Mechanism

A true arch or dome works by converting downward load into outward thrust
distributed around a continuous curve, requiring either massive
buttressing to contain that thrust or, as with Roman concrete, a material
that can bear compressive load across a genuinely monolithic poured
structure — corbel vaulting instead simply steps stone courses inward
until they meet, which requires no thrust-management engineering at all but
correspondingly can't span nearly as wide an opening before running out of
structural stability.

## Social consequence

Large domed or vaulted interior spaces function as a durable, visible
statement of a patron's — usually a state's or a religious institution's —
resources and technical command, becoming a preferred form for the most
prestigious religious and civic buildings across essentially every
tradition that develops the capability, independent of the specific
structural technique used to achieve the span.

## Regional specifics

Islamic muqarnas solve the square-to-round transition problem independently
of Byzantine pendentives, developing into both a structural solution and a
distinctive decorative tradition that shapes Persian and later Mughal
domed architecture. Maya corbel vaulting achieves a working, if
structurally more limited, alternative to true vaulting with zero contact
with Old World engineering knowledge. Chinese architecture's sustained
preference for wood post-and-beam and bracket systems over masonry vaulting
at comparable scale represents a deliberate, long-maintained alternative
structural philosophy rather than an unrealized ambition — different
traditions can and did choose different large-span solutions rather than
converging on the same technique.

## Hook

Spanning open interior space without a full monolithic material like Roman
concrete requires either thrust-managing arch geometry or, as with corbel
vaulting, accepting a much narrower practical span — several structurally
distinct solutions to the same architectural problem, chosen independently
by traditions that each treat a wide interior span as a preferred marker of
patron prestige regardless of which technique gets them there.
