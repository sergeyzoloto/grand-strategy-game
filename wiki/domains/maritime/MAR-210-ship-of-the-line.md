A heavily-gunned sailing warship built and organized specifically to fight
in a single-file line alongside others of its class, broadside guns facing
outward — the doctrine that defines "capital ship" status for the next two
centuries of naval warfare.

## Timing

Line-of-battle tactics and the purpose-built ships to fight them develop
from roughly the mid-1600s (formalized in English and Dutch naval "Fighting
Instructions"), remaining the dominant capital-ship doctrine through the
early-to-mid 1800s before steam and armor (MAR-200) make the wooden line-of-
battle ship obsolete.

## Mechanism

Fighting in a rigid line lets each ship's broadside guns bear on the enemy
line without friendly ships blocking each other's field of fire, converting
naval combat from a chaotic melee of individual ship duels into a
formation-level tactical problem — a ship's value is now defined by how
many guns it can bring to bear in that line (its "rate"), and a state's
naval power becomes countable and comparable in a way that ad hoc combat
never allowed.

## Social consequence

Line-of-battle doctrine creates a strict hierarchy of ship rates and,
correspondingly, of the captains and admirals who command them — naval
career advancement becomes tightly coupled to formation position and
seniority within this rated system, reinforcing naval officer corps as a
distinct, rule-bound professional hierarchy layered on top of the standing
navy institution (MAR-060) that makes maintaining such ships possible at
all.

## Regional specifics

This is a specifically European naval doctrine in this era, with no directly
comparable line-of-battle tradition developing independently elsewhere —
East Asian naval theatres in the same centuries favor different tactics
suited to their own strait- and coast-dominated engagements rather than
open-ocean gunnery duels between large battle fleets, a difference in
theatre and tactical requirement rather than in shipbuilding or gunnery
capability generally.

## Hook

Line-of-battle doctrine converts individual warship gunnery into a countable,
comparable formation-level naval power metric (ship rate and line strength),
directly enabling the standing-navy administrative logic of MAR-060 to
translate into a legible, rankable fleet-strength stat — and setting the
doctrinal template that MAR-200's ironclads inherit and then break.
