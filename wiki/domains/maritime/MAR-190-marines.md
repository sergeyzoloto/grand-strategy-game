Infantry permanently organized and trained specifically for shipboard
service and landing operations — a distinct institutional branch rather
than regular army troops temporarily embarked for a single voyage.

## Timing

Spanish naval infantry (the Infantería de Marina, from 1537) is among the
earliest permanently organized marine corps; comparable dedicated
institutions develop across other European naval powers over the following
centuries, remaining a standard component of major navies through 1900 and
beyond.

## Mechanism

A dedicated marine corps solves two distinct problems regular embarked army
troops handle less well: shipboard discipline and small-arms combat suited
to a vessel's confined spaces (boarding actions, suppressing mutiny, ship
security), and landing-party operations requiring troops used to operating
directly off naval transport rather than after a conventional overland
approach march — specializing a standing force for this role pays off once
naval campaigns are frequent and large enough to justify a permanent
institution rather than ad hoc army detachments each time.

## Social consequence

Marine units typically develop a distinct institutional culture and chain of
command straddling naval and army command structures, often with more
insular esprit de corps than either parent service, precisely because their
role doesn't fit cleanly into either a purely naval or purely land-army
identity.

## Regional specifics

Ming and Japanese naval forces achieve comparable shipboard-infantry
function using regular embarked soldiers rather than a permanently distinct
institutional branch, showing the underlying capability (soldiers trained
and equipped for boarding and landing operations) doesn't strictly require
the dedicated-corps organizational form Europe settles on. The Barbary and
Swahili coast cases show the same is true even for polities with extensive
and sophisticated naval operations otherwise — a standing marine corps is an
organizational choice about how to provide shipboard infantry, not the only
way to have it.

## Hook

A dedicated marine corps provides shipboard-infantry and landing-operation
capability as a standing institutional branch rather than an ad hoc levy,
buying readiness and specialized training at the cost of permanent upkeep —
functionally substitutable, per the East Asian and African cases, by simply
embarking regular land troops when the campaign requires it.
