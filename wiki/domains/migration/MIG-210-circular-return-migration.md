A migrant who travels intending a savings target and voluntary return
rather than permanent settlement — a household economic strategy this
domain's one-way emigration framing (MIG-180) and state-driven forced
population movement framing (MIG-250) both miss, made viable specifically
by cheap, fast, round-trip-capable steamship service.

## Timing

Documented at real scale from the 1880s through the 1910s among Italian,
Balkan, and other Southern and Eastern European emigrant populations to
the Americas, tapering with the immigration-restriction era and the
disruption of transatlantic travel during and after the First World War.

## Mechanism

Return migration requires a round trip to be cheap and fast enough to
plan a specific savings-target strategy around — a migrant who could only
ever afford one crossing, or whose crossing took months rather than
weeks, has no realistic option to treat migration as temporary. Once
scheduled steamship service (MIG-180) makes repeat crossings affordable
and predictable, a substantial share of migrants — contemporary estimates
for some Southern and Eastern European origin groups run well over a
third — travel with an explicit intention to earn, save, and return
rather than settle permanently, a fundamentally different household
economic calculus from either frontier settlement's (MIG-090) or the
mass-emigration narrative's (MIG-180) implicit assumption of permanent
relocation.

## Social consequence

Circular migration lets a household treat emigration as a temporary
labor strategy — financing a specific goal (land purchase, debt
repayment, a dowry) back home — rather than an irreversible break with
the origin community, sustaining tighter ongoing ties between migrant and
origin household than permanent emigration typically does, and feeding
the same network effects (`MIG-200`) that lower the cost of the next
departure.

## Regional specifics

Indian indentured labor migration under MIG-180's regularized shipping
lines builds a return passage directly into the indenture contract
itself, a legally-structured and involuntary-adjacent version of
temporary migration quite distinct from the voluntary, self-timed pattern
free European emigrants pursued on the same underlying steamship
infrastructure — showing "temporary" migration can be either a
household's own strategic choice or a contractual term imposed on it,
using the identical transport technology either way.

## Hook

A branch: an individual-choice return-migration pattern running at real
scale alongside permanent emigration within the same passenger-line era,
complicating any simple one-way emigration count this domain's other
entries implicitly assume, and only viable once round-trip steamship
service (MIG-180) makes a planned return affordable.
