Diogenes pursues independence from social convention through deliberate,
confrontational public performance — living without property or shelter by
choice, flouting norms of modesty and decorum specifically to expose
onlookers' hypocrisy — a distinct school in its own right, not a delayed
product of the polis's later political collapse (PHI-150) it's sometimes
grouped alongside.

## Timing

Diogenes of Sinope, Cynicism's most closely associated figure, is active in
Athens and Corinth from roughly -412 to -323 — squarely within the still-
functioning, still-politically-autonomous Athenian polis system, predating
Alexander's death and the polis's subsequent loss of independent political
agency (PHI-150) by decades, not following from it.

## Mechanism

Cynicism argues conventional social distinctions — wealth, reputation,
citizenship, even shelter and modesty — are arbitrary constructs actively
obstructing a genuinely virtuous, self-sufficient life, and pursues freedom
from them not through quiet withdrawal but through deliberate, provocative
public flouting: living in a barrel, begging openly, publicly performing acts
convention deems shameless, each a staged demonstration meant to expose the
audience's own conventional judgments as arbitrary rather than natural or
necessary.

## Social consequence

Because Cynic practice is confrontational and public rather than private or
quietist, it functions as ongoing social theater with a real audience effect
distinct from a philosophy practiced privately — Diogenes becomes a
recognized public figure precisely through provocations designed to be
witnessed and discussed, converting philosophical argument about
convention's arbitrariness into a performed demonstration rather than a
treatise to be read.

## Regional specifics

Cynicism's rejection of convention lands in broadly similar anti-
conventionalist territory to Daoist wu wei (PHI-060) — both reject
striving after conventional social distinction — but through an entirely
different social posture: Diogenes's confrontational public performance,
staged specifically to expose others' hypocrisy, versus wu wei's quieter
cultivation of alignment with an impersonal cosmic process, showing
rejection of convention itself can be pursued through genuinely opposite
public postures.

## Hook

A modifier: convention-rejection pursued as confrontational public
performance rather than quiet withdrawal, generating social effect (audience
reaction, reputation, provocation) as a direct part of the practice itself —
structurally distinct from, and chronologically prior to, the later
Hellenistic schools' (PHI-150) very different, circumstance-independent
route to the same underlying independence from convention.
