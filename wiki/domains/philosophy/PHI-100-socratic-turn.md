Philosophy's center of gravity shifts from asking what the natural world
is fundamentally made of (the pre-Socratics' physei questions, NAT-020) to
asking how a person ought to live — a redirection Cicero later summarizes
as bringing philosophy down from the heavens into the cities and houses of
ordinary people.

## Timing

Socrates is active in Athens from roughly -470 (his approximate birth)
until his 399 BCE trial and execution, with the shift he's credited with
crystallizing continuing to shape the following generation's philosophy
(Plato, the other "Socratics") for decades afterward.

## Mechanism

Socrates's method, the elenchus, works by publicly cross-examining a
fellow citizen's confident claim to know something (courage, piety,
justice) until the claim's internal contradictions surface — the method
produces no positive doctrine of its own, only the demonstrated
insufficiency of the interlocutor's prior confidence, which is precisely
what makes it so socially provocative: it converts an ordinary
conversation into a public demonstration that a respected figure doesn't
actually know what he claims to.

## Social consequence

Publicly exposing prominent Athenians' ignorance on questions they
assumed they had answered wins Socrates a devoted following among younger
citizens and, simultaneously, enough elite resentment to contribute
directly to his prosecution and execution on charges of impiety and
corrupting the youth — the same method that makes him philosophically
influential is what gets him killed.

## Regional specifics

The roughly contemporaneous ethical turns in South Asian shramana
movements (Buddhism, PHI-090; Jainism, PHI-070) reach a similarly central
concern with how to live and what suffering or ignorance actually consists
of, but through renunciate teachers addressing disciples seeking release
from worldly existence, not through a citizen publicly interrogating other
citizens within an ongoing civic community he has no intention of leaving
— a different social form arriving at a related redirection of philosophy's
core questions independently.

## Contested

Whether any specific doctrine can be reliably attributed to the historical
Socrates, as opposed to Plato's own philosophy developing and using
"Socrates" as a literary mouthpiece, is the long-standing and unresolved
"Socratic problem" — the surviving sources disagree with each other and
each serves its author's distinct purposes. Model "the Socratic turn" as a
genuine, datable shift in Athenian philosophy's central questions, while
treating specific positive doctrines attributed to Socrates personally as
carrying real sourcing uncertainty.

## Hook

The elenchus converts confident, unexamined belief into publicly
demonstrated ignorance without supplying a replacement doctrine — a method
that generates philosophical influence and civic resentment through the
same act, setting up the systematic positive political philosophy (PHI-140)
the following generation builds to answer the questions Socratic
cross-examination could only ever expose as unresolved.
