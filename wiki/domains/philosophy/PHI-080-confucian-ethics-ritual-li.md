Confucius's central claim is that ethical cultivation happens through
repeated, correctly-performed ritual practice (li) within one's concrete
social roles, not through abstract moral rule-following — a systematic
argument that habituated practice, not doctrine memorized and applied, is
what actually produces a virtuous person (ren).

## Timing

Confucius teaches in the Chinese state of Lu from roughly -551 to his
death in -479; his disciples compile the Analects in the following
generations, and rival and elaborating interpretations (Mencius's
optimistic view of innate human goodness, Xunzi's pessimistic view
requiring more deliberate ritual correction) develop through the Warring
States period to -221.

## Mechanism

Li names both the specific ritual forms (mourning practices, court
etiquette, family deference) and the general disposition of performing
one's social role correctly and with genuine feeling rather than empty
formality — the mechanism is that repeatedly performing a role correctly
gradually reshapes the performer's actual character to match the role,
directly implementing habitus-as-practice (design principle 2) rather than
treating virtue as a slider a person simply decides to set.

## Social consequence

Because li is enacted through specific hierarchical relationships (ruler-
subject, parent-child, elder-younger, husband-wife, friend-friend), correct
ritual performance simultaneously produces personal virtue and reinforces
the surrounding social hierarchy those relationships encode — ethical
cultivation and social order are, in this framework, the same activity
viewed from two angles, not two separable goals in tension with each
other.

## Regional specifics

Aristotelian virtue ethics reaches a structurally similar conclusion about
habituation producing character through an entirely independent argument
grounded in rational deliberation toward a mean between extremes, rather
than through li's specific vocabulary of correctly performed role-based
ritual — two traditions converging on "practice, not rule-following, forms
character" from genuinely different starting premises, which is part of
why comparative virtue-ethics scholarship treats the parallel as
significant rather than coincidental.

## Contested

Whether li functions mainly as externally-imposed social control that
happens to be dressed in the language of virtue, or as a practice whose
repetition genuinely internalizes the disposition (ren) it expresses, is a
long-running interpretive dispute running through the history of
Confucian scholarship. Model li as functioning as social control when
disconnected from any actually internalized ren, and as genuine
habitus-formation when practice and cultivated disposition stay linked —
treating the disconnection itself, not a fixed answer, as the thing worth
tracking.

## Hook

Li converts `habitus formation` into an explicit ethical program — ritual
practice correctly performed within a concrete social role gradually
reshapes the performer's actual disposition to match it, making virtue a
downstream consequence of sustained correct practice rather than a trait
a person simply chooses to have.
