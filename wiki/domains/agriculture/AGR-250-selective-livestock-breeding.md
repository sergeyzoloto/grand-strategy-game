Deliberate, records-based selection for specific livestock traits —
distinct from AGR-240's soil-chemistry mechanism, with which it shares
only an era and a general "deliberate selection" family resemblance, not
an actual causal or chemical relationship — converts animal improvement
from passively accumulated variation into an actively managed breeding
program with measurable, repeatable results.

## Timing

Robert Bakewell's systematic English livestock breeding program develops
from the 1760s, with comparable records-based selective-breeding practice
spreading across British agriculture through the century's end; the
underlying mechanism — controlled mating enabling deliberate trait
selection — recurs independently and far earlier in other traditions,
including Andean camelid breeding from roughly -3000.

## Mechanism

This entry applies the same deliberate-selection logic that originally
produced domesticated species in the first place (AGR-020) to
programmatic, ongoing improvement of an already-domesticated population —
but doing so at all requires the controlled mating fixed containment
infrastructure supplies (AGR-070), since selecting specific sires and
dams for desired traits is impossible if any two animals in a herd can
breed with each other unsupervised. Once that precondition is met,
recording outcomes across generations and selecting breeding stock
accordingly converts what was previously slow, passive, largely
unintentional trait drift into a directed, comparatively fast-acting
improvement process.

## Social consequence

Programmatic breeding concentrates real economic value in specific
named bloodlines and the breeders who control them, converting livestock
improvement into an activity with its own reputational economy — a
breeder's records and claimed results become a tradeable, reputation-
bearing asset in their own right, distinct from the value of any single
animal, and correspondingly vulnerable to exaggeration and dispute.

## Regional specifics

Andean pastoralists' millennia-deep, entirely independent tradition of
selective camelid breeding for fiber quality shows the underlying
mechanism — controlled mating plus deliberate trait selection — is not
a uniquely European achievement, even though the European case (Bakewell
specifically) is the one this record's popular framing usually credits.
Sub-Saharan Africa's tsetse-constrained domesticate roster caps the
mechanism at the same ecological ceiling already limiting livestock
domestication and secondary-products intensity there (AGR-030), showing
this entry's precondition chain (domestication, then containment, then
breeding) fails at the same early link regardless of which later stage
is being asked about.

## Contested

Bakewell's own specific claimed achievements and methods were
substantially mythologized by contemporaries and later writers, with
modern reassessment finding his documented results less dramatic and
less methodologically systematic than the popular "father of scientific
breeding" narrative credits him with — though the underlying shift
toward deliberate, records-based selection in this era is itself
genuine. Model the underlying practice as real and datable, while
treating Bakewell's personal centrality and the scale of his specific
results as separately contested and likely overstated.

## Hook

Programmatic selective breeding is gated entirely on controlled mating
(AGR-070), applying AGR-020's original domestication-selection logic to
an already-domesticated population at a much faster, deliberately
directed pace — with the mechanism's real-world credit distributed more
widely, and its most famous individual case less singularly dramatic,
than the popular framing this record's own source material inherited
suggests.
