Sealed ceramic vessels make boiling and controlled fermentation routine rather
than opportunistic, turning cooking into a pathogen-reduction and
nutrient-unlocking technology, and fermentation specifically into a
storable, mildly antimicrobial food and drink supply.

## Timing

Earliest in East Asia (pottery in use for fermentation by ~-9000), with
independent grain-beer fermentation in the Fertile Crescent from shortly after
cereal domestication, and independent African and American traditions
following on sorghum/millet and maize respectively by -5000 and -3000.

## Mechanism

Boiling in a sealed vessel kills a wide range of pathogens and unlocks
nutrients (starches, some proteins) that raw or fire-roasted preparation
doesn't fully release. Fermentation goes further: it pre-digests
otherwise-marginal foodstuffs, lowers pH enough to suppress many competing
pathogens, and produces a storable, calorie-dense, low-risk liquid in
environments where raw water often is not low-risk. The mechanism is a
straight substitution — pathogen exposure traded down for processing labor and
fuel.

## Social consequence

A storable, safe, mildly intoxicating, easily-portioned substance is
tailor-made for controlled group distribution, and the feast becomes the
occasion where that distribution enacts status: who is served first, from
which vessel, of which quality, in whose company. Commensality — who eats and
drinks with whom — becomes a legible, performable boundary marker in a way raw
foraged food sharing rarely was.

## Regional specifics

East Asian and Fertile Crescent fermentation traditions arrive at structurally
similar feast-and-status functions from unrelated starting crops and vessel
traditions, suggesting the social mechanism is close to inevitable once
sealed-vessel fermentation exists at all, not culturally specific. Mesoamerican
nixtamalization (treating maize with alkali) is a partial outlier: it's a
processing technology solving a nutritional deficiency problem (niacin
bioavailability, preventing pellagra) as much or more than a feast/status
technology, showing the same pottery-cooking toolkit can be selected for
different primary payoffs depending on the base crop's weaknesses.

## Hook

Fermentation/cooking technology seeds a `commensality` stat and quietly lowers
background pathogen exposure from food and drink — the mechanical payoff is
invisible until a later entry (crowd disease, water engineering) needs a
baseline to compare against.
