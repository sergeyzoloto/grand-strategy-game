Childbirth is the single most common serious medical event in any premodern
population, and who attends it, with what knowledge and what tools, is a
mostly separate historical track from the codified medical systems that get
written down and credentialed.

## Timing

Attested midwifery practice across every literate tradition from the earliest
written medical sources (~-1800, Egyptian and Mesopotamian texts); the
European shift toward instrument-assisted, physician-attended birth
concentrates from the 17th century.

## Mechanism

Successful birth management is overwhelmingly about managing malposition,
bleeding, and infection risk with hands-on technique and accumulated
experiential knowledge — a domain where formal theoretical medical systems
(humoral theory, qi-balance) contribute comparatively little, and where a
skilled traditional attendant's tacit, orally-transmitted expertise can
outperform a formally credentialed physician with more prestige and less
hands-on obstetric experience. The introduction of instruments (forceps)
changes the calculus only for a narrow set of otherwise-fatal obstructed
deliveries, while doing nothing for the much larger risk of puerperal
infection until antisepsis (a much later entry) addresses that separately.

## Social consequence

Where midwifery stays a female-controlled knowledge domain, it sits partly
outside the prestige and authority structures of formal (usually
male-credentialed) medicine, which makes it low-status by the standards of
those structures despite frequently superior practical outcomes — a clean
authority/efficacy split (design principle 3) running in the opposite
direction from the usual one, with the lower-authority practice not
infrequently having a better hands-on record for straightforward births.

## Regional specifics

The European transition to male-physician-attended birth is a specific,
datable professionalization event that displaces rather than absorbs prior
female midwife knowledge networks, and does so before instrument-assisted
delivery or antisepsis actually deliver better outcomes on average — the
authority shift precedes any efficacy justification for it. African and
indigenous American traditional-birth-attendant systems persist as
independent, sophisticated, orally-transmitted knowledge bases throughout and
beyond this period, without meaningful exposure to or dependency on
Old World texts or tools.

## Hook

Obstetric outcomes track accumulated hands-on `medical efficacy` specific to
childbirth management, which is only loosely correlated with — and in the
European case is actively displaced by — a region's general `medical
legitimacy`/credentialing structure.
