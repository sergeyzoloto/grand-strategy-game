Lead does not decay and does not leave. The burden a population carries is the
integral of everything ever smelted around it, and it produces no symptom that
names its cause. A society can pay for it for centuries without knowing it
exists.

## Timing

Cupellation begins in Anatolia and Mesopotamia in the late fourth millennium BCE.
Greek and Roman silver mining raises hemispheric lead pollution for most of a
millennium. Alchemical medicine in China and South Asia and colonial mercury
amalgamation in the Andes from the 1570s follow. The craft diseases of early
modern Europe are catalogued by Ramazzini in 1700 and Pott in 1775. Tetraethyl
lead enters petrol in 1921, and Patterson's contamination findings follow from
the 1950s. The entry closes around 1970, when phase-out begins, although the
stock the period built is still in the soil.

## Mechanism

**It is a stock, not a flow.** Most health mechanisms in this record are
flows. An epidemic passes, a famine ends, a pathogen is suppressed, and mortality
returns toward its baseline. Lead behaves differently. It does not degrade, so
every tonne smelted adds to a `persistent contaminant stock` held in soil,
sediment, household dust, old pipes and paint, and in human bone. Each generation
is exposed to the whole inherited stock, not only to its own additions. The
burden therefore grows with the cumulative history of mining, metalwork, glazing
and later industry, and it falls only very slowly after the additions stop.
Mercury behaves the same way. That makes this stock the reservoir feeding `toxic
burden`, the population-level harm that HLT-030 already shows accumulating
silently under settlement. Most of the other uses of `toxic burden` in the corpus
clear when exposure ends. This one does not.

**It is invisible without instruments.** Acute poisoning of heavily exposed
workers was noticed early. Vitruvius saw pallid lead workers, and Huancavelica was
called the mine of death. Chronic low-dose exposure, the kind a whole population
carries, looks like nothing specific: a few points of intelligence lost, lower
fertility, more stillbirths and child deaths, more gout and kidney disease in
adults. These cannot be told apart from ordinary misfortune without blood and
bone assays, and a society without those instruments cannot find the cause even
if it looks for one. Detection, not the harm, is what arrives late.

**The origin is silver.** Most silver in the ancient and early modern world came
from argentiferous lead ore. Cupellation separates the silver by driving the lead
off as litharge and fume, so every unit of the most valuable extractive product
generated a larger mass of lead and released part of it into the air. This is
metallurgy (ENG-010) whose byproduct is a health cost. Greenland ice records it
year by year: rising with Roman mining and the Pax Romana, falling with plague and
crisis, and later rising again with medieval silver. The Andes add mercury to the
same equation. Amalgamation made silver cheaper to extract by consuming mercury
and the men who mined it. **A state's silver income and its mercury casualty rate
are the same variable seen from two sides**, and COM-140's `bullion stock` is
bought with the Huancavelica mita's `coerced labour stock`.

**The highest-value crafts are disproportionately the toxic ones.** This is the
entry's broad claim, and the catalogue supports it:

- **Mercury.** Fire-gilding bonded gold to church domes and metalwork by
  boiling mercury off, and hatmaking used mercury to felt fur.
- **White phosphorus** made the cheap strike-anywhere match and destroyed the
  jaws of the young women who dipped them.
- **Silica dust** killed the grinders who put edges on Sheffield steel and the
  potters and stonemasons of every ceramic and building tradition.
- **Soot** gave chimney sweeps the first occupational cancer ever identified.
- **Lead** glazed pottery, whitened paint, sweetened wine and made type metal.

The pattern follows from the same economics each time. These materials were used
because they did something nothing else did as cheaply, and the cost fell on the
worker and the household, where it was invisible to the market that priced the
product.

**Medicine can be the exposure route.** In Chinese alchemy and Ayurvedic rasa
shastra, mercury and lead were among the most prestigious medicines available. Their
`medical legitimacy` was high and their `medical efficacy` negative. This is design
principle 3 in its most dangerous form, and HLT-150 carries the codified systems
that granted such substances their authority. The consequence inverts the usual
social gradient. The Tang emperors who could afford the purest elixirs were the
most thoroughly poisoned people in the empire.

## Social consequence

At household scale the exposure is domestic and ordinary. It comes from a wine
sweetened in a lead pot, a child's eyes lined with surma, a family drawing water
through lead pipe, a mother's sindoor, flaking paint on a windowsill, and later
the petrol exhaust of the road outside. None of these looks like a hazard, and
several were marks of status or care. The harm is spread thinly across everyone,
which is why no one attributes it.

In the workshop the gradient runs the other way. The worker at the cupel, the
gilder, the hatter, the match dipper and the grinder carried doses high enough to
kill. Ramazzini's contribution was to see this as a class of disease rather than a
series of individual misfortunes. His practical innovation was to add one question
to the clinical history, "what work do you do?", which turned occupation into a
diagnostic variable for the first time. Pott's chimney sweeps, typically boys
climbing flues, show whose bodies the knowledge was first learned from.

Once instruments exist, the discovery comes too late to prevent anything. Leaded
petrol was introduced over a documented worker death toll and defended for half a
century. The scientist who proved its effect did so by accident, while trying to
measure something else. By then the stock had been laid down in every city's soil,
and removing it is slow, expensive and never complete.

## Regional specifics

Rome is the best-measured premodern case, with ice cores, Tiber sediment,
Vitruvius's warning, and sapa as a lead additive consumed deliberately for its
sweetness. It is also the most over-interpreted. The exposure was real and the
civilisational consequence is unproven. The Middle East supplies cupellation's
origin and the longest-running cosmetic route, galena kohl.

China and South Asia supply the inversion in which medicine and prestige deliver
the dose. Chinese elixirs killed at the top of the social order. Rasa shastra
preparations and household sindoor and surma show South Asia running the same
logic through both medicine and adornment.

The Andes supply the coupling of silver revenue and mercury casualties, run
through forced labour at Huancavelica and legible in lake sediments that also
record pre-Hispanic smelting. Europe supplies the systematic craft catalogue and
the founding of occupational medicine. The twentieth-century United States
supplies leaded petrol, the Bayway deaths, and Patterson's accidental proof.

The absences show where the burden never started. Oceania did no smelting before
contact, and pre-contact North America worked metal cold. What creates the
burden is smelting argentiferous ore, not using metal. Elsewhere in the record,
ENG-165's coal smoke and ENG-275's refinery economics sit alongside this entry.
Coal smoke is a burden paid by non-users. Tetraethyl lead was the cheap
alternative to the refining improvements ENG-275 describes: it bought octane from
an additive instead of from a better refinery.

## Contested

Nriagu's claim that lead poisoning degraded the Roman elite and helped bring down
the empire is not accepted. Its rebuttal, that mineral scale sealed the pipes,
fails on the Tiber sediment evidence. Real chronic exposure is established.
Civilisational consequence is not.

The lead-crime hypothesis is suggestive across several datasets and is weakened
by evidence of publication bias and a much smaller pooled effect.

Model lead as a hidden accumulating stock with modest, diffuse penalties to
cognition, fertility and child survival. Never script a collapse from it, and keep
any crime effect small and uncertain.

## Hook

`persistent contaminant stock` is incremented by smelting (above all silver
cupellation and amalgamation), lead glazing and pigment, craft processes and later
industry and leaded fuel. It never decays. It falls only through slow, expensive,
incomplete remediation. It drives `toxic burden` as diffuse penalties to
cognition, fertility and child survival.

**Hidden until detected.** The penalties are applied from the first smelt, but the
cause is hidden from the player until a detection technology is researched.
Research then gives previously unexplained penalties a retroactive explanation.
The strongest version of the mechanic is that the player has been paying the cost
for centuries and learns why only once it is too late to avoid it.

**Tied to income.** Silver and mercury extraction increment the stock and
`coerced labour stock` losses in step with `bullion stock` gains, so extraction
revenue and toxic cost are one variable.

**Prestige as a delivery route.** Medical and cosmetic uses deliver the dose through
high `medical legitimacy` and negative `medical efficacy`, concentrating it where
wealth and prestige buy the most exposure.

**The workshop.** The craft catalogue concentrates acute doses on specific
occupations. Occupational medicine becomes available once someone thinks to ask
what work a patient does.
