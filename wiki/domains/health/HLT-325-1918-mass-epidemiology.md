A pandemic that killed healthy working-age adults, and in doing so forced public
health to move from the city to the nation and from the nation to a standing
international body. The deaths are the event. The institutions it left behind are
the entry.

## Timing

A mild spring wave in 1918, the lethal autumn wave from September to November
1918, and a third wave in early 1919, with scattered recurrences into 1920. The
institutional response follows over the next decade:

- **National ministries:** Britain's Ministry of Health in 1919, Canada's
  federal department in 1919, New Zealand's Health Act in 1920, and France's
  ministry of hygiene in 1920.
- **International intelligence:** the League of Nations Health Organisation in
  1923 and its Singapore bureau in 1925.
- **Complete registration:** US national death registration completed in 1933.

The consolidation arrives with the World Health Organization in 1948 and its
global influenza surveillance network in 1952. HLT-170's connectivity plagues end
at 1894, and nothing else in the record covers this pandemic.

## Mechanism

**The mortality curve is W-shaped, and that is the worst possible shape.**
Ordinary influenza kills the very young and the very old, producing a U-shaped
curve. The 1918 strain added a third peak among adults aged roughly 20 to 40, most
of them killed by secondary bacterial pneumonia (Taubenberger and Morens, 2006,
Emerging Infectious Diseases). A state therefore loses working adults, soldiers,
and the parents of young children, rather than those who were already outside the
labour force. The demographic and economic damage per death is far above what the
same death toll would do in the normal age pattern. It removes `mobilisable
manpower` directly and leaves orphaned households behind. Troop concentrations
in camps and on transports amplified exactly the age group the virus was already
killing. That wartime overlap is why military casualty accounting (WAR-250)
recorded the pandemic as closely as any civilian system did.

**The information variable decides whether a state can respond at all.**
Belligerent governments suppressed or downplayed reporting to protect morale. A
state that censors news of an epidemic blinds its own administration as well as
its public: officials lose the independent signal of the press, local health
officers cannot tell whether what they see is local or general, and the public
does not change its behaviour. Neutral Spain, which reported freely, gave the
pandemic its name. The lesson is structural. Detection is a precondition of
response, and it depends on two separate capabilities. One is registration, the
ability to count deaths (HLT-270). The other is an open channel for reporting them.

**The core: public health changes scale.** Quarantine (HLT-230) was a port-and-city
instrument, run by municipalities and harbour authorities at chokepoints. The 1918
pandemic crossed every chokepoint in months and killed on a national scale, and it
exposed what a municipal system could not do. It could not compare districts,
detect a wave arriving, standardise cause-of-death reporting, or direct resources
between regions. In the decade that followed, states built four things:

- **A central health ministry.** Public health moves out of local government
  and becomes a department of state.
- **Compulsory notifiable-disease reporting.** Doctors and local officers are
  obliged to report specified diseases to the centre on a fixed schedule.
- **Standardised mortality statistics.** Deaths are coded to a common
  international cause list, so that a death in one district is comparable to
  a death in another and one year to the next.
- **A standing international body.** The earlier sanitary conferences met
  occasionally. The League's Health Organisation ran continuously, collected
  weekly epidemiological reports, and from Singapore broadcast outbreak
  intelligence by wireless to ports across Asia.

This is the pandemic converting vital registration (HLT-270) from a record into a
surveillance system. Its later estimation methods, excess mortality measured
against a seasonal baseline and representative sampling where complete counts
were impossible, draw on the inference methods of MTH-062.

**Chokepoints still matter where they exist.** Quarantine lost against a
continental pandemic and won completely at a single harbour. The Samoan pair shows
the same intervention producing zero deaths or a fifth of the population depending
on whether the chokepoint was closed. That makes the effect of quarantine depend on
geography and timing, not on doctrine.

## Social consequence

At household scale the pandemic took parents. A family lost its breadwinner or
mother in the prime of working life, and the surviving children were absorbed by
kin, by orphanages, or not at all. In India the autumn wave arrived during harvest
failure, so hunger and influenza worked on the same households. The famine
amplification HLT-110 describes ran through a new pathogen. Deaths concentrated
where nutrition, housing and caste position were worst.

The pandemic was also strangely quiet in public memory for its size. It arrived as
the war ended and was overshadowed by it, and in belligerent countries it had been
under-reported while it happened. The institutional consequences outlasted the
memory. A generation later every industrial state had a health ministry, a
notifiable-disease list, and statistics coded to international standards, and
most of the officials who ran them could trace the arrangement to 1918-19.

## Regional specifics

India is the centre of the story. It lost between 12 and 18 million people, more
than any other country and more than Europe as a whole. Any account centred on
Europe and the United States describes the minority of the deaths. China is the
largest unknown, because no national registration existed. Japan shows a state
with working vital statistics responding centrally from the first wave. Persia
shows influenza, famine and occupation fusing into one mortality event that cannot
be decomposed. Sub-Saharan Africa received the lethal wave at the same moment as
Europe and North America, through Freetown, and lost perhaps two million people
almost entirely unrecorded.

**The data problem must be carried, not smoothed over.** Colonial mortality
reporting was worst exactly where mortality was highest. That is true of village
registration in India, of the near absence of registration across most of Africa,
and of China, which had no national system at all. As a result:

- The largest national tolls are the least certain.
- The global total ranges from under twenty to a hundred million.
- The regional distribution of that uncertainty is itself a finding about
  which states could see their populations.

A model that treats the Indian or African figures as precise is modelling the
colonial record's silence as a number.

The two natural experiments carry the policy argument. Western and American Samoa
show quarantine's total effect at a closable chokepoint. Philadelphia and St. Louis
show the effect of early intervention in open cities, which is real and more
moderate. Spain supplies the information finding. Britain, Canada, New Zealand and
France supply the institutional conversion, and the League's Health Organisation
supplies its international form (DIP-180).

## Contested

Three things are unresolved and should stay unresolved in the model. The first
is the origin: Kansas, northern France and the Chinese Labour Corps all have
advocates. The second is the total. The standard 50 to 100 million has been
challenged down to around 17 million, and most of the uncertainty sits in the
regions with the weakest registration. The third is the size of the intervention
effect in open cities, which is inferred from observational comparison. The
direction of all three is well established. The magnitudes are not.

Model the origin as unscripted and the total as a wide distribution whose width is
driven by the registration gap. The effect of intervention should depend on its
timing and stay moderate in open populations. It should be total only where a
single chokepoint can be closed before arrival.

## Hook

**The event.** A pandemic whose mortality falls on working-age adults, removing
`mobilisable manpower` and parents rather than the dependent old and young.
Severity is amplified by three things: troop concentration, famine stress, and
low `immunological capital` in isolated populations.

**The response depends on seeing it.** A state's response is gated on two separate
capabilities: vital registration to count deaths (`legibility`), and an uncensored
reporting channel. A state that suppresses reporting loses its own ability to
detect and respond. Quarantine's effect depends on geography: total at a closable
single-harbour chokepoint, timing-dependent and moderate in open populations.

**The institutional conversion.** A state that has vital registration converts it
into a surveillance system: a national health ministry, compulsory notifiable-disease
reporting, and standardised cause-of-death statistics. A state without registration
cannot make the conversion and stays blind to the next epidemic. Internationally,
the event turns sporadic sanitary conferences into a standing epidemiological body.

**Uncertainty is part of the model.** Mortality estimates should be wide in
proportion to the registration gap, so the regions that suffered most are the ones
the player, like the historian, knows least about.
