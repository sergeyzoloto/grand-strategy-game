Endemic malaria kills non-immune outsiders at rates that make sustained presence
impossible, regardless of military or economic strength. The constraint is
biological. Force, capital and organisation don't move it, and only prophylaxis
lifts it.

## Timing

Cinchona bark reaches Europe from Peru in the 1630s. Quinine is isolated in 1820,
and routine prophylactic use spreads from the 1840s. The 1854 Pleiad expedition
returns from the Niger without a European death, and the partition of Africa
follows from the 1880s. The Dutch and British plantations of the 1850s-60s break
the Andean monopoly on the bark. The entry runs to the mid-twentieth century,
when DDT and synthetic antimalarials replace quinine. Its coda is the eradication
campaign of 1955-69 and its retreat. Malaria in the Mediterranean, South Asia and
China predates the entire window.

## Mechanism

**A hard gate, not a modifier.** In West Africa European mortality ran at around
half a garrison per year. Expeditions were not made more expensive by it. They
were destroyed. The coast was traded with for three hundred years through
fortified posts that survived by constant replacement, and the interior was not
held at all. No amount of money or firepower changes that arithmetic, because the
losses do not depend on resistance from anyone. The model should reflect this:
without prophylaxis, a tropical colony of non-immune settlers or garrisons fails
to establish, rather than establishing at a higher cost. With prophylaxis, it can
be established but still costs lives. The gate is necessary, not sufficient.
Opening it did not by itself produce the Scramble. That took another three
decades and steamers and repeating rifles as well.

**The unlock is a supply chain as well as a drug.** Cinchona grew only on the
eastern slopes of the Andes, and the exporting republics banned the export of
seeds. The medicine that opened Africa was therefore for decades a commodity
from territory no colonising power controlled. That makes it a textbook `strategic
input dependency`: a militarily essential input that cannot be produced at home at
any price. Dutch and British transplantation to Java and the Nilgiris broke the
monopoly in the 1850s and 1860s. Botanical transfer converted a scarce import into
a colonial plantation crop, and it moved the dependency rather than ending it.
Java came to dominate world supply so completely that its loss to Japan in 1942
cut the Allies off and forced synthetic substitutes into mass production. COM-160
carries the monoculture dependency that plantation crops create. The
crop-transfer act itself, a state seizing a monopoly by moving the plant, is not
yet carried anywhere in the corpus.

**The differential is what makes malaria strategic.** A disease that killed
everyone equally would be a uniform burden. Malaria kills outsiders and spares,
partially, those with long exposure, and that asymmetry turns it into a
territorial variable. It acts like `immunological capital` in reverse, with the
same logic as HLT-240's virgin soil but the incoming population as the vulnerable
one. There are two sources of resistance, and both are costly.

- **Acquired immunity** comes from repeated infection in childhood, and it is
  paid for with very high child mortality among the children who do not
  survive to acquire it.
- **Genetic variants** are the other. The sickle-cell trait protects carriers
  against falciparum malaria at the price of sickle-cell disease in children
  who inherit two copies. G6PD deficiency and thalassaemias follow the same
  logic, and the Duffy-negative blood group that protects against vivax is
  why vivax is almost absent from West Africa.

Populations living with malaria carry a permanent genetic and mortality cost that
outsiders do not, and outsiders carry an acute mortality cost that locals do not.

**Land use raises the burden.** Anopheles breed in standing water. Irrigation
canals, rice paddies, cleared forest, borrow pits and blocked drainage all create
habitat, so agricultural intensification raises malaria alongside yield (AGR-100).
The irrigation that raises a plot's `yield ceiling` can lower the working capacity
of the people farming it. Drainage runs the other way, which is why Roman
land-reclamation, Italian bonifica and southern US drainage all count as malaria
policy.

**Eradication shows that the barrier can be depleted and regrows.** DDT spraying
and chloroquine drove malaria out of southern Europe, the United States and, for a
time, Sri Lanka and much of India. The WHO programme of 1955-69 then met
resistance in both the vector and the parasite, lost funding, largely left out
sub-Saharan Africa, and was abandoned for control. Where spraying stopped, malaria
came back. This is design principle 4. Suppression is standing maintenance spend
against a pool that refills, and the tools themselves lose effect with use.

## Social consequence

At household scale, in a malarial region, malaria is part of the calendar: fever
seasons, a child mortality rate set by the parasite, adults with recurrent fevers
that cut working days at harvest, and settlement drawn uphill away from the
valley floors. Italian peasants in the Campagna commuted down to fields they
could not safely sleep beside. In the Terai the forest was left to the one
population that could live in it.

The immunity differential was converted into a labour doctrine. Planters in the
Caribbean, Brazil and the American South argued that Africans could work malarial
and yellow-fever lowlands where Europeans could not, and used the argument to
justify enslavement and coerced gang labour on rice and sugar plantations
(AGR-210). The biology was partly real and the doctrine far exceeded it. Enslaved
people died of these diseases in large numbers, and the claim of natural
suitability did ideological work that no mortality table supported.

The gate also sorted colonial societies. Where outsiders could not survive, as in
West Africa before prophylaxis, colonies became trading and extraction regimes
with few settlers. Where they could, as in the highland zones and the malaria-free
Pacific, settler colonies formed. The prevalence of malaria helps explain why
Kenya's highlands and not its coast drew European settlement, and why the
effective occupation principle the Berlin Conference required (DIP-090) could be
met only once quinine made interior garrisons survivable.

DDT's reversal from near-eradication of malaria to the emblem of chemical harm after
Silent Spring is one of the cases PHI-305 carries of expertise losing public trust
through a solution's own side effects.

## Regional specifics

West Africa is the barrier at its strongest and the clearest case of it being
lifted. Curtin's garrison mortality figures and the contrast between the 1841 and
1854 Niger expeditions are the core evidence. HLT-280 carries the colonial medical
institutions built once the gate was open.

The Mediterranean carries the argument that this is not a tropical-periphery
entry. Rome, the Campagna, Sardinia and the Greek plains were malarial from
antiquity to the twentieth century. The first state antimalarial programme
anywhere is Italy's quinine distribution of 1900. Southern Europe was also the
first region eradication succeeded in.

South Asia shows the barrier as a defence and the land-use mechanism at work. The
Terai protected the Nepali hills and sheltered the Tharu, and Punjab's canal
colonies raised malaria alongside yield. China's southern and southwestern
frontiers were held at the mortality line for centuries, most visibly in the
Qing's disease-wrecked Burma campaigns.

The Americas are the negative case turned positive. They had no malaria before
contact and gained it through contact and slavery. It then shaped the plantation
South's labour system and was eliminated there only in the 1940s, by the agency
that became the CDC.

Southeast Asia is where the quinine monopoly changed hands, and Oceania is the
cleanest absence: no vector east of Vanuatu, so no barrier.

HLT-110 carries the famine side of the same interaction seen in colonial
Punjab. Hunger raises malaria mortality among people who would otherwise survive
infection.

## Contested

Two disputes. The first is how much the partition of Africa owed to quinine.
Headrick makes prophylaxis the enabling technology. Curtin shows mortality falling
through several simultaneous improvements, and the thirty-year lag before the
Scramble shows other technologies were needed too. Model prophylaxis as a
necessary gate, not a sufficient trigger.

The second is how far differential immunity was biological and how far it was
planter ideology. Resistance is documented, but enslaved Africans still died of
malaria and yellow fever in large numbers, and the claim of natural suitability
went far beyond the evidence. Model the immunity differential as a real partial
reduction in mortality for exposed populations, and keep the coerced-labour
doctrine separate as something built on top of it, not derived from it.

## Hook

- **Hard gate.** In endemic malarial regions, sustained presence by
  non-immune populations fails without prophylaxis. It fails outright rather
  than costing more, and no military, fiscal or organisational investment
  substitutes. Prophylaxis opens the gate without triggering expansion, so
  mortality falls to a cost instead of a wall.
- **Supply chain.** Prophylaxis depends on a `strategic input dependency`: the
  bark grows only on territory the colonising power does not control, until
  botanical transfer to a plantation colony moves the dependency, and losing
  that colony cuts supply and forces synthetic substitutes.
- **Differential immunity.** Exposed populations carry a partial reduction in
  mortality paid for in child deaths and genetic disease, and outsiders carry
  acute mortality. The differential, not the burden, makes malaria a
  territorial variable.
- **Land use.** Irrigation, paddy and clearance raise malaria burden
  alongside `yield ceiling`, and drainage lowers it.
- **Suppression is a depleting pool.** Spraying and drugs suppress
  transmission only while funded and only until vector and parasite
  resistance erode them, and the barrier returns where the spend stops.
- **Geography of absence.** No vector east of Vanuatu, no parasite in the
  Americas before contact, no transmission at altitude. These absences are
  what decide where settler colonies are possible at all.
