What farming does to the body, read directly off the skeleton: shorter,
worse teeth, different joints, from a narrower diet and new physical labor.

## Timing

Visible in skeletal series within a few centuries of a population's shift to
cereal-dependent farming, essentially everywhere the shift happens, from
~-10000 in the earliest Fertile Crescent samples through comparable East Asian
evidence by ~-7000.

## Mechanism

Three separate signals stack: reduced stature and long-bone robustness
(narrower, more carbohydrate-heavy diet than a varied foraged one), sharply
increased dental caries and enamel hypoplasia (sticky, fermentable
starches replacing tougher, less cariogenic foraged foods), and altered joint
and enthesis wear patterns (new, repetitive labor: grinding grain, tilling,
carrying water) replacing the more varied load profile of foraging and
hunting.

## Social consequence

None of this is chosen or noticed as a trade-off by the people living it —
it's the embodied, physical residue of a set of economic decisions (what to
grow, how to process it, how to divide labor) made for entirely different
reasons, showing up a generation later as a population's default body rather
than as anyone's visible sacrifice.

## Regional specifics

The same signature — shorter stature, worse teeth, altered joint wear —
appears in Fertile Crescent and East Asian farming populations independently,
on unrelated crops, which is strong evidence the mechanism is generic to the
dietary and labor shift rather than tied to wheat or barley specifically.
Populations that remain foraging or mixed-mobile longer, well attested in parts
of Africa, show the inverse skeletal profile surviving alongside farming
neighbors rather than preceding them chronologically — a standing contrast
case, not merely an earlier stage on the same road.

## Hook

Cereal-dependent farming quietly writes a stature/health penalty and a labor-
pattern signature into a population's baseline body — legible only in
retrospect (skeletal record), and a candidate lever for whatever later
"nutrition transition" mechanics eventually reverse or compound it.
