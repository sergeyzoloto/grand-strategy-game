A genuine capability-loss-then-recovery case: Roman law's sophisticated legal apparatus effectively disappears across most of Western Europe for roughly five centuries before being deliberately revived from surviving texts — the same institution and decade LAW-130 already cites for corporate personhood, without ever explaining what had been lost or why that specific place became the hub of its recovery.

## Timing

Roman law's systematic legal apparatus fragments across the former Western Empire following its collapse (from around 476), replaced for roughly five centuries by localized, orally-transmitted Germanic customary law; the rediscovery and systematic teaching of Justinian's Corpus Juris Civilis begins at Bologna from 1088, with the resulting "reception" of Roman legal concepts into local law spreading across the continent through the following century.

## Mechanism

Western Europe's post-Roman successor kingdoms largely lack the administrative continuity, trained legal professionals, and systematic legal education Roman law had depended on, so its accumulated doctrine — property, contract, procedure, legal reasoning itself — falls out of active use even where manuscript copies physically survive in scattered monastic libraries. The Bolognese glossators' systematic recovery and teaching of the Corpus Juris Civilis from 1088 converts those surviving but dormant texts back into an actively taught, applied body of law, restoring a conceptual and procedural sophistication no intervening five centuries of local custom had independently redeveloped.

## Social consequence

The reception of Roman law creates Europe's first specialized, formally-trained legal profession since antiquity — scholars and practitioners credentialed through systematic study of a shared textual tradition rather than through local custom or personal apprenticeship alone — directly feeding the same universitas institutional innovation (LAW-130) that gives Bologna itself its own durable legal personhood as a corporate teaching body.

## Regional specifics

The Byzantine-ruled eastern Mediterranean, where Justinian's compilation was actually produced, never experiences a comparable disappearance-and-revival cycle at all — Roman-derived law continues in unbroken use there, later feeding into regional legal synthesis in some areas, making the Western European case specifically one of loss and deliberate recovery rather than a universal pattern every heir to Roman law had to pass through.

## Hook

An event: an entire sophisticated legal system disappears for roughly half a millennium before being deliberately revived from surviving texts at Bologna — the same institution LAW-130 already credits for corporate personhood, restoring a conceptual and procedural depth no intervening custom had independently developed.
