A permanent, professionally-trained planning institution standing between
overall strategy and individual battlefield tactics — coordinating supply,
movement, and multi-corps maneuver as its own specialized discipline rather
than leaving it to a single commander's personal judgment and improvisation.

## Timing

The Prussian General Staff system develops substantially from the reforms
following defeat at Jena (1806), maturing into the professionalized planning
institution that produces the Prussian/German victories of 1866 and 1870-71,
with the model spreading to other major powers through the rest of the
1800s.

## Mechanism

Operational art — coordinating the movement, supply, and timing of multiple
army corps toward a single campaign objective — exceeds what any single
commander can hold in their head alongside battlefield tactical command, so
a general staff institutionalizes that planning function as a standing,
professionally-trained body with its own career track, doctrine, and
war-gaming and mapping practice, converting campaign planning from an
individual talent into an institutional, teachable, repeatable capability.

## Social consequence

The general staff creates a new kind of military career and prestige
hierarchy oriented around planning and administrative skill rather than
battlefield heroics or aristocratic birth, gradually shifting real military
influence toward officers who may never personally lead a battlefield charge
— a professionalization of command function analogous to, but a further step
beyond, the professional soldiery of WAR-060.

## Regional specifics

Prussia's development of the institution is the paradigm case, emerging
directly from post-defeat institutional reform rather than steady
peacetime evolution. Meiji Japan's wholesale import of the Prussian model,
rather than independent development, shows the institution itself — once
its value is demonstrated by Prussia's own battlefield results — can be
adopted essentially as a complete package by another state, compressing
what took Prussia decades to build into a much shorter adoption timeline.

## Hook

A general staff converts campaign-level operational planning from
individual-commander talent into an institutional, teachable capability —
raising a state's effective military `legibility` and, per the Japanese
case, adoptable wholesale by another state once its results are proven,
rather than requiring the same organic development time to replicate.
