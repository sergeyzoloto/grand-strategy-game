# Stats Observed

Append-only capture log of every mechanical variable invented while writing
entries. **Not a reconciled glossary** — no structure, no consistency enforcement,
no deduplication pass. If two entries independently invent near-synonyms, both
get logged as-is. Consolidation into an actual stat glossary happens once after
the full coverage pass, not while writing.

One line per invented variable, in the order encountered.

| stat name              | entry ID | definition                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| legibility               | —        | state's ability to see/measure/tax a population or activity it governs.     |
| commensality             | —        | who eats with whom; a proxy for social boundary permeability.               |
| immunological capital    | —        | accumulated population-level disease resistance from prior exposure.        |
| toxic burden             | —        | cumulative harm from environmental/occupational exposure.                   |
| medical legitimacy       | —        | social authority granted to a medical practice, independent of its efficacy.|
| medical efficacy         | —        | actual health outcome improvement a medical practice produces.              |
| antibiotic pool          | —        | depleting stock of effective antimicrobial treatment, drawn down by use and resistance. |
| information speed        | ENG-085  | rate at which news/knowledge propagates across distance — defined by ENG-085 as a purchasable state capability rather than a constant of geography, bought at a standing cost in relay stations, staff, and remounts, yielding nothing when idle and collapsing within a season if the spend stops. ENG-230 later converts it from a transport-bound variable into an electrical one. |
| protection rent          | WAR-020  | value extracted by an actor in exchange for (real or imposed) protection — episodic predation converted into standing extraction once the predator's time horizon lengthens and it holds something close to a monopoly on the threat it charges against, which is what makes the payer prefer it to the unpredictable destruction it replaces. |
| route knowledge          | —        | accumulated, learnable knowledge of a trade or travel route.                |
| time discipline          | —        | internalized scheduling of labor/life to clock or calendar rather than task.|
| privacy                  | —        | degree to which household/individual activity is shielded from communal observation. |
| contract enforcement     | —        | reliability with which an agreement is upheld absent personal trust.        |
| bullion stock            | COM-140  | accumulated specie held by a polity or market, independent of its fiscal use. |
| price level              | COM-140  | aggregate price index for a market/region, movable by monetary and non-monetary shocks. |
| sovereign credit capacity | COM-140 | a state's ability to borrow against future revenue without defaulting.      |
| migration range          | MIG-050  | sustainable travel distance for a population or raiding party, per mobility technology available. |
| settler footprint        | MIG-060  | founding/settling population size a colonization event requires relative to its resulting control. |
| displacement pressure    | MIG-120  | accumulated forced-movement stress propagating through a population or contact chain. |
| confederation cohesion   | MIG-110  | how strongly a loose multi-group political-military alliance holds together, tied to ongoing success. |
| diaspora reach           | MIG-070  | size/connectivity of an established diaspora's trust and trade network abroad. |
| migration trigger intensity | MIG-190 | how sharply a discovery or opportunity spikes migration above steady-state levels. |
| migration cost           | MIG-180  | price/risk/time a migrant must pay to relocate, before any legal barrier is applied. |
| urban absorption capacity | MIG-170 | a city's ability to house/employ/service incoming migrants without strain. |
| border friction          | MIG-230  | legal/administrative barrier to movement imposed independent of migration cost. |
| skill differential       | MIG-260  | gap in institutional/career opportunity driving skilled-labor migration.   |
| naval prestige           | MAR-200  | a state's naval standing as measured by internationally-comparable capital-ship metrics, resettable to near-zero by a single capital-ship design leap. |
| religious authority      | REL-020  | trust/legitimacy a religious institution commands, usable as a resource for banking-like trust, tithe/court authority, and canon-enforcement — distinct from medical or state legitimacy. |
| intellectual authority   | PHI-030  | prestige/trust a school of thought or reasoning tradition commands, tracking sustained patronage and disciple-training capacity rather than a founder's original argument alone. |
| habitus formation        | PHI-080  | degree to which repeated ritual/practice reliably reshapes a performer's internalized disposition — a stat implementation of design principle 2, reused across ritual, ascetic, and meditative practice traditions. |
| yield ceiling             | AGR-020  | the per-land-unit agricultural output cap set by prevailing crop genetics, water control, traction, and nutrient technology — raised by domestication, irrigation, rotation, synthetic nitrogen, and high-yield varieties in turn. |
| soil fertility            | AGR-080  | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of land's actual nutrient productivity, drawn down by continuous cultivation and monoculture, restored by fallow, rotation, manure, or synthetic fertilizer investment. |
| market trust radius       | COM-010  | the maximum social/geographic distance a given trust-guarantee mechanism (neutral ground, market judge, brand) lets stranger-to-stranger trade safely reach. |
| caravan viability threshold | COM-050 | the minimum pooled merchant participation/capital below which a long-distance overland crossing isn't viable at any price, and above which marginal joiners are cheap. |
| network fragility         | COM-040  | the degree to which a trade network's peak throughput and value are coupled to its own specialization, so that a shock of a given size produces cascading rather than proportional loss. |
| route capacity            | COM-070  | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of a physical trade route's carrying capacity, drawn down by silting/weathering by default and restored only by standing maintenance investment. |
| crop failure correlation  | AGR-270  | the degree to which a pathogen or shock striking a genetically-uniform monoculture damages the whole dependent food supply simultaneously rather than being contained to a fraction of it. |
| engineering knowledge pool | ENG-130 | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of institutionally-sustained technical capability that decays when the sustaining institution collapses, recoverable only through renewed investment and often via a different technical path than the original. |
| accumulated scientific corpus | NAT-110 | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of a knowledge-producing institution's manuscripts, trained scholars, and standing patronage, built up over generations and destructible outright by a single violent event rather than only fading through neglect. |
| fishery stock | MAR-100 | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of a fish or whale population, renewable under moderate extraction pressure but drawn down faster than it replenishes under sustained heavy effort. |
| remittance flow | MIG-200 | a standing income-and-information tie from a settled migrant back to an origin household, funding and directing further chain migration — a building rather than depleting pool (design principle 4 run in reverse). |
| asabiyyah | PHI-240 | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of group solidarity/cohesion that lets a tribal or dynastic coalition seize and hold power, peaking at conquest and predictably eroding across roughly three to four generations of settled, urbanized success. |
| alliance cohesion | DIP-170 | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of accumulated inter-power trust and coordination capacity within a standing alliance or concert system, built up gradually over decades but catastrophically destructible in a single triggering crisis via the same commitment architecture meant to prevent that outcome. |
| medical access | HLT-210 | population-level coverage by any organized channel of medical or burial-cost support (mutual-aid fund, charitable endowment, insurance), tracked separately from the underlying medical efficacy that coverage actually buys. |
| coerced labour stock | WAR-035 | a depleting pool (design principle 4) of enslaved or otherwise bound workers held by a polity or estate, paying out real output but failing to reproduce itself under gang-labour conditions, so it must be replenished by war, raid, or purchase — which couples the holding economy permanently to an external supply. |
| supervision cost | WAR-035 | the per-worker cost of preventing flight and extracting work from coerced labour, read from terrain and settlement pattern (nucleated fields, mines, galleys, and fixed coastal villages are cheap; dispersed herding and scattered arable are not) rather than set by policy — the Nieboer-Domar term, and the variable that decides whether captives are retained, ransomed, sold on, or killed. |
| labour-saving incentive | WAR-035 | the pressure on an economy to develop or adopt labour-saving technique, suppressed where a labour constraint can be met by acquiring more coerced people instead — the standing counter-finding to coerced labour's real wealth, paired with a cap on domestic market depth from a workforce that consumes at subsistence. |
| raiding prestige | WAR-010 | standing and bridewealth won by participating in a raid — a route to adult status for young men with no inheritance to wait for, which persists as a raiding driver independent of whether any particular raid's material haul repays it. |
| arbitrage rent | ENG-230 | merchant income earned purely from knowing a price before a competitor does, extinguished on any route the telegraph closes — a rent on delay rather than on risk-bearing, warehousing, or judgement, and therefore the one commercial income stream that a communications improvement destroys outright instead of enlarging. |
| maritime safety floor | ENG-315 | the minimum survivable-incident standard a shipping regime guarantees, raised by a continuous listening watch rather than by installed apparatus — a capability that is entirely absent, not merely degraded, whenever the watch lapses. |
| broadcast reach | ENG-325 | population within receivable range — a function of transmitter power, terrain, and receiver density inside the footprint, explicitly NOT of territory controlled, which is what separates it from ENG-230's wire network and lets a peripheral actor buy reach the wire era priced as an imperial capital project. |
| information sovereignty | ENG-325 | degree to which a state controls what its own population hears. Contestable from outside the border for the first time with broadcast, and defensible only by continuous expenditure (jamming, range-limited receivers, set licensing) that yields no output of its own and costs more as the adversary adds transmitters. The inverse direction from `legibility` — that is the state seeing its population, this is the state being the only one speaking to it. |
| intermediary dependence | ENG-325 | a ruler's reliance on editors, notables, and clergy to reach the population — raised by print, which needs distributors and readers, and collapsed by broadcast, which needs neither. The mechanism behind radio's centralising effect, and the leverage those intermediaries lose is simply that of having been unavoidable. |
