# War Domain Audit

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Scope:** `domains/war/` — 22 entries, WAR-040 through WAR-320.
**Method:** adversarial structural review against `_meta/schema.md`, `_meta/conventions.md`, `_meta/design-principles.md`, `_meta/boundary-rules.md`, every entry in `domains/war/`, and cross-checks against `domains/engineering/`, `domains/migration/`, `domains/maritime/`, `domains/health/`, `domains/finance/`.

## Summary of the pattern

War is the most extensively self-documented domain audited this cycle: nine separate entries (`WAR-020`, `WAR-050`, `WAR-060`, `WAR-110`, `WAR-130`, `WAR-190`, `WAR-240`, `WAR-290`, `WAR-250`) narrate their own split from ENG, MIG, HLT, or MAR in their opening paragraph — more than Maritime's six or Migration's six. Region balance is solid (32.9% Europe+Mediterranean), the contested ratio is healthy (6 of 22, 27%), and the prereq chains from professionalization through gunpowder to the nuclear era are genuinely well-built.

The domain's real problem is a specific, repeated pattern rather than a scattershot one: **its two most foundational entries each narrate a transition away from an institutional "before" state that is never itself modeled.** `WAR-040`'s entire Hook is "the shift from a seasonally-levied, self-equipped citizen or landholder militia to a year-round, state-paid... professional soldiery" — but neither the land-tenure militia nor its later medieval descendant (feudal knight-service, the samurai) is ever covered as its own entry. `WAR-020`'s entire Hook is what cheap iron does to "who can be armed" relative to a narrow elite that previously monopolized weapons access — but that elite, bronze-equipped, chariot-warfare era is never covered either. The domain is exceptionally good at describing what changed; twice at its own foundation, it never shows what it changed *from*. Layered on top of that is a concrete, checkable bug: `WAR-040`'s own top-level `date_start` (-750) postdates its own cited Neo-Assyrian region_variant (-900) by 150 years, which in turn makes three downstream entries (`WAR-020`, `WAR-050`, `WAR-060`) either chronologically strained or outright inverted against the entry they prereq.

## PART A — Diagnostic Sweep

### 1. EMERGENCE GAP — **FAIL**

The earliest entry by date, `WAR-020` (Mass Iron, -1200), opens by describing what happens once cheap iron breaks "a narrow elite['s]" prior monopoly on weapons access — a monopoly the domain never itself models. That monopoly is the Bronze Age chariot-warrior era (roughly -3000 to -1200): small, expensively-equipped elite combatants whose battlefield dominance rested on bronze armor and weapons requiring a long-distance tin-trade network, the exact "before" state `WAR-020`'s own Mechanism section describes ending. Separately, `WAR-040`'s own Hook describes a "shift" away from land-tenure militia service — but neither that early levy system nor its later, better-documented medieval form (feudal knight-service in Europe, the samurai in Japan, both land-grant-for-service institutions) is covered anywhere. The domain narrates two transitions at its own foundation without ever showing either starting state.

### 2. SUBSTRATE GAP — **FAIL**

Three concrete absences:

- **Military logistics and supply.** `WAR-050`'s own Mechanism explicitly calls feeding and maintaining a besieging army "a sustained logistics problem at least as demanding as the defenders'"; `WAR-210`'s operational art is defined as coordinating "the movement, supply, and timing" of army corps; `WAR-100`'s attrition strategy runs on "sustained production capacity." All three lean on an army's logistics ceiling as a load-bearing assumption, and none of them, nor any other entry, owns it as its own mechanic.
- **The market for military labor.** Confirmed by grep: zero hits anywhere in the wiki for `mercenar|condottier|landsknecht`. `WAR-040` covers state-salaried professional soldiery and `WAR-180` covers legally-conscripted citizen obligation — but the third historically major labor-supply channel, a soldier or company selling organized violence on contract to whichever payer offers the best terms (Swiss pikemen, Italian condottieri, Wallenstein's entrepreneurial army), is absent entirely.
- **Standardized drill as a training technology.** Confirmed by grep: zero hits for `close.order drill|maurice of nassau|standardized drill`. `WAR-040` covers the fiscal threshold for professional soldiery and `WAR-080` covers formation tactics, but the specific innovation of breaking soldiering itself into standardized, repeatable, rapidly-teachable drill sequences — usually credited to Maurice of Nassau's reforms — is never covered as its own mechanism.

### 3. SCALE GAP — **FAIL**

Sorting the 22: world-system (`WAR-310`, `WAR-320`), state (`WAR-040`, `WAR-210`, `WAR-180`, `WAR-270`, most entries), city/theatre (`WAR-050`, `WAR-240`), but the individual/small-group scale is nearly absent. `WAR-180` covers the state's legal claim on manpower and `WAR-170` covers institutionalized small-unit leadership, but nothing covers the soldier's own motivation to actually fight once in combat — the well-documented gap between a unit's formal training/equipment and whether it holds together under fire, driven by loyalty to the handful of men immediately beside a soldier more than to any larger legal or ideological framing. This is a scale this domain is unusually well-positioned to have (it already models legibility, cohesion, and morale as stats elsewhere) and doesn't.

### 4. REVERSE-DIRECTION GAP — **PARTIAL FAIL**

Better than most domains in absolute terms: `WAR-240` is itself a doctrinal-trap entry (offense loses to defense for over a decade before doctrine catches up), and `WAR-310`'s South African case is "the only case in this record of a state actually giving up an existing weapons capability entirely." But two reversal types are missing. First, `WAR-050` explicitly ends its own window at 1500 "before... gunpowder-artillery... makes traditional wall-based fortification obsolete," and `WAR-130`'s own Hook claims artillery "rewrit[es] WAR-050's attrition calculus in the besieger's favor" — full stop, with no entry ever covering the well-documented fortification response (the bastioned trace italienne) that partially reversed that advantage back toward the defender from the 1500s onward. Second, nothing models a military institution decaying from *within* — every capability loss in this domain comes from being out-teched or out-doctrined by a rival (`WAR-110` eroding cavalry primacy, `WAR-290` restoring maneuver against `WAR-240`); no entry covers a standing institution's own success converting it into a hereditary, reform-resistant interest group that has to be destroyed rather than modernized (the Ottoman Janissary corps' 1826 abolition being the paradigm case).

### 5. PREREQ ORPHANS — **FAIL**, and a concrete data bug

Every `prereqs` ID resolves to a real entry — no dead links. But there is a genuine, checkable chronology problem: **`WAR-040`'s own top-level `date_start` (-750) is later than its own cited Neo-Assyrian region_variant (-900)**, and three other entries prereq `WAR-040` while starting at or before that earlier date:

- `WAR-020` (Mass Iron, `date_start: -1200`) lists `WAR-040` as a prereq — a **450-year inversion** even against the corrected -900 date, and a dubious dependency regardless of date: `WAR-020`'s own Mechanism is about metal supply chains democratizing arms access, which doesn't require standing professional armies to exist first (if anything, cheap iron more plausibly supports the citizen-levy mass infantry `WAR-080` describes, not something downstream of professionalization).
- `WAR-050` (Siege Warfare, `date_start: -900`) and `WAR-060` (Cavalry Doctrine, `date_start: -900`) both prereq `WAR-040` while starting in the same year as `WAR-040`'s own Neo-Assyrian case — resolved cleanly once `WAR-040`'s top-level date is corrected to match its own earliest cited instance, but currently reads as backward against `WAR-040`'s stated top-level -750.

Separately, three entries cite a specific cross-domain ID by name in their own prose without ever promoting it to `domains_touched` or `see_also` — the same bug pattern found in the last two domains audited, here aimed specifically and repeatedly at Maritime: `WAR-060`'s absence note cites `MIG-050` and `MIG-090` (domains_touched already includes `migration`, but neither ID is in `see_also`); `WAR-290`'s absence note cites `MAR-350` by name with **no** `maritime` in `domains_touched` and nothing in `see_also`; `WAR-270`'s regional text cites `MAR-220` and `MAR-320` by name with the same complete absence. (`WAR-250`, by contrast, gets this exactly right — `MAR-350` is correctly in its `see_also` — showing the domain knows how to do this when it remembers to.)

### 6. MECHANISM DIVERSITY — **PASS, with the domain's usual missing pool**

A real spread: gates (`WAR-040`, `WAR-020`, `WAR-180`), civic institutions (`WAR-130`, `WAR-210`, `WAR-230`), genuine branches that explicitly reuse design principles 1 and 6 (`WAR-050`, `WAR-100`, `WAR-290`, `WAR-250`, `WAR-310`), and sharp events (`WAR-240`). Roughly half lean "modifier," consistent with every other domain audited this cycle and not by itself disqualifying given the diversity elsewhere. What's absent, matching every domain checked so far: a genuine **depleting pool** (design principle 4). The closest candidate hiding in plain sight is `WAR-170`'s professional NCO/officer cadre — a slow-to-build, catastrophically-fast-to-lose trained-leadership stock (the Soviet officer corps after the 1937-38 purges is the textbook case) — but `WAR-170` doesn't frame it as a pool, and no other entry supplies one either.

### 7. BOUNDARY INTEGRITY — **PASS**

The cleanest and most extensively self-documented boundary discipline of any domain audited: `WAR-020`, `WAR-050`, `WAR-110`, `WAR-130`, `WAR-240`, `WAR-290` all narrate their own ENG/WAR split explicitly ("the metallurgy/engines/chemistry/construction/tank technology itself is ENG's; this entry owns..."); `WAR-060` narrates its MIG/WAR split; `WAR-190` narrates its HLT/WAR split; `WAR-250` narrates its MAR/WAR split and correctly cross-references `MAR-350`. No entry's canonical domain is wrong, and no content duplicates something canonical elsewhere — WAR consistently owns doctrine and organization while correctly ceding artifact, chemistry, mobility technology, and medical efficacy to their proper domains.

## PART B — Proposed Additions

### LOAD-BEARING (5)

**1. Bronze Age Warfare and the Elite Chariot Monopoly**
- Regions: middle-east, mediterranean, east-asia — era, roughly -3000 to -1200
- Answers: Probe 1 (Emergence) directly — the "before" state `WAR-020`'s own Hook describes ending
- Mechanism: warfare organized around a small, expensively-equipped elite — chariot-owning warriors whose bronze armor and weapons require a long-distance tin-trade network only a narrow aristocratic class can access and afford — a capital-and-training monopoly rather than a numbers contest, and the precise social and material structure `WAR-020`'s cheap iron then breaks.
- Hook (seed): an origin gate — establishes the elite-monopoly baseline `WAR-020` needs to have something to erode, and the "before" state every later mass-infantry entry in this domain implicitly measures itself against.
- Prereqs: none. Enables: `WAR-020` (replacing the dubious `WAR-040` dependency there — see Part C).
- Contested: true — how much of chariot warfare's battlefield role was genuine shock/skirmish combat versus a mobile command-and-prestige platform is debated given how thin direct tactical accounts from the period are.
- Confidence: medium.

**2. Feudal Land-Tenure Military Service**
- Regions: europe, east-asia, middle-east — era, roughly 800 to 1500
- Answers: Probe 1 (Emergence) directly — `WAR-040`'s own "before" state
- Mechanism: military service exchanged for a grant of land (a European fief, a Japanese shoen) rather than a wage, tying the obligation to hold and defend territory directly to hereditary status and land tenure — the exact institution `WAR-040`'s Hook describes itself as superseding, but which no entry in this domain actually models.
- Hook (seed): a gate — a land-for-service institutional baseline `WAR-040`'s professionalization converts away from, with its own hereditary, honor-bound social logic (chivalric knighthood, samurai bushido) shaping military culture independent of any state's fiscal capacity.
- Prereqs: none. See_also: `WAR-040` (a succession/contrast relationship, not a building-block one).
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

**3. Military Logistics and Supply Lines**
- Regions: middle-east, mediterranean, europe, east-asia — era, roughly -900 to 1800
- Answers: Probe 2 (Substrate) directly
- Mechanism: an army's sustainable campaign range and duration is capped by how much food, fodder, and ammunition it can carry, forage along the way, or have delivered by a supply train — a hard logistics ceiling independent of tactical skill or army size, which is why siege viability (`WAR-050`), campaign planning (`WAR-210`), and attritional endurance (`WAR-100`) all silently lean on a capacity this domain never separately owns.
- Hook (seed): a gate — a resource-and-range ceiling every campaign-level entry in this domain assumes is already solved.
- Prereqs: none. Enables: `WAR-050`, `WAR-210`, `WAR-100` (all currently missing this link).
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

**4. Fortification Response and the Artillery-Siege Arms Race**
- Regions: mediterranean, europe — century, roughly 1500 to 1700
- Answers: Probe 4 (Reverse-direction) directly, continuing `WAR-050`'s own declared story past the 1500 end-point where it stops
- Mechanism: low, thick, angled bastion fortifications (the trace italienne) absorb and deflect artillery fire far better than the traditional high vertical walls `WAR-130`'s siege guns had just made obsolete, restoring much of the defender's advantage in `WAR-050`'s attrition calculus — at an enormous new construction cost only a well-resourced state can sustain.
- Hook (seed): an event that reverses `WAR-130`'s own claimed effect — a defensive-technology response showing this domain's offense/defense race runs in both directions, not toward one side permanently.
- Prereqs: `WAR-050`, `WAR-130`. Enables: none.
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

**5. Mercenary Companies and the Market for Violence**
- Regions: europe, mediterranean, east-asia — era, roughly 1300 to 1650
- Answers: Probe 3 (Scale gap) directly — the third army-manpower channel this domain lacks
- Mechanism: a mercenary company sells organized, trained violence to whichever payer offers the best contract, converting military labor supply into a market transaction independent of either a soldier's own citizenship obligation (`WAR-180`) or a state's own payroll (`WAR-040`) — cheaper and faster to raise than a standing army, and structurally unreliable in exactly the situations (unpaid wages, a better offer from the enemy) a citizen or salaried professional soldier wouldn't defect under.
- Hook (seed): a third manpower-supply channel alongside `WAR-040`'s state-salaried professional and `WAR-180`'s legal conscript — buying speed and skill at the cost of loyalty being conditional on payment rather than obligation.
- Prereqs: none. Enables: none.
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

### VALUABLE (4)

**6. Military Institutional Decay from Within**
Regions: middle-east. Era, roughly 1600-1826. Answers Probe 4 (reverse-direction) with a mechanism genuinely distinct from every other capability-loss case in this domain: the Ottoman Janissary corps, founded as a meritocratic, centrally-controlled elite force, converts over generations into a hereditary, politically entrenched interest group that actively resists the modernization needed to keep it militarily effective, ending only in the state's violent destruction of the institution (the 1826 "Auspicious Incident") rather than in gradual reform or defeat by a rival. Hook: an event/branch — a standing institution's own permanence and success can convert it into a political veto player the state eventually judges more dangerous to reform than to destroy outright. Prereqs: `WAR-040`. Contested: false. Confidence: high.

**7. Combat Motivation and Small-Unit Cohesion**
Regions: europe, americas. Century, roughly 1800-1950. Answers Probe 3 directly: soldiers in combat are motivated far more by loyalty to the handful of men immediately beside them than by the larger legal (`WAR-180`) or institutional (`WAR-170`) framing of their service — a primary-group cohesion effect that determines whether a unit actually fights effectively under fire, independent of its formal training or equipment, and one that can fail even in a well-equipped, well-led force that hasn't built this specific bond. Hook: a modifier operating beneath `WAR-180`'s legal-obligation layer and `WAR-170`'s institutional-leadership layer, capable of producing combat failure neither of those entries would predict. Prereqs: none. See_also: `WAR-180`, `WAR-170`. Contested: true — S.L.A. Marshall's widely-cited claim that only 15-25% of WWII infantrymen actually fired their weapons in combat has been seriously challenged on methodological grounds since its original publication; model the underlying cohesion effect as real while treating that specific statistic as disputed. Confidence: medium.

**8. Close-Order Drill as a Training Technology**
Regions: europe. Century, roughly 1590-1700. Answers Probe 2 and Probe 6: breaking musket-loading and firing into standardized, counted, endlessly-repeatable drill sequences (Maurice of Nassau's Dutch reforms) converts soldiering from a skill requiring years of individual mastery into one teachable to a large, comparatively unskilled body of men in months — a training-method innovation distinct from `WAR-040`'s fiscal threshold and `WAR-080`'s formation doctrine, since it's specifically about how training itself gets standardized and taught. Hook: a gate that converts training time from an individual-mastery bottleneck into a scalable, teachable process, feeding directly into both `WAR-040`'s professional soldiery and `WAR-110`'s gunpowder-infantry adoption speed. Prereqs: `WAR-110`. Contested: false. Confidence: high.

**9. Chemical Warfare and International Weapons Taboos**
Regions: europe. Decade, 1915-1925. Answers Probe 6 with a mechanism found nowhere else in this domain: poison gas proves militarily effective but so uniquely horrifying that it generates a durable international taboo (the 1925 Geneva Protocol) suppressing its use for the remainder of this record's timeline far more thoroughly than its actual tactical utility alone would predict — a weapon neutralized by diplomatic norm-enforcement rather than by counter-technology or doctrine, unlike every other weapon this domain covers. Hook: a branch whose outcome turns on whether a given actor judges the taboo's reputational cost higher than the weapon's tactical value in a specific conflict, not on the weapon's raw effectiveness. Prereqs: none. See_also: `WAR-310` (a comparable norm-governed-weapon logic). Contested: false. Confidence: high.

### OPTIONAL (2)

**10. War Elephants**
Regions: south-asia, middle-east, mediterranean. Era, roughly -1000 to 200. Named explicitly in `WAR-080`'s own South Asia absence note ("chariot, elephant, cavalry") but never covered on its own terms — a real, historically significant arm (Hannibal's crossing, Seleucid and Mauryan elephant corps) whose psychological-shock-and-logistics-cost profile is close enough to a specialized variant of shock cavalry (`WAR-060`) that it doesn't clearly need independent tier weight. Honestly flavour.

**11. Special Forces and Commando Raiding**
Regions: europe, americas. Decade, 1940-1980. WWII commando units (British SOE, American OSS) developing into permanent Cold War special-forces institutions — real and specific, but mechanically an extension of `WAR-220`'s small-unit irregular doctrine plus `WAR-250`'s aviation-insertion capability rather than a genuinely new mechanism. Honestly flavour.

## PART C — Proposed Modifications

### SPLIT

None found. `WAR-250` deliberately bundles three doctrinal roles (close air support, air superiority, strategic bombing) under one Mechanism section, checked against the Multi-domain rule's "two `## Mechanism` sections" test and found to genuinely have one (aircraft extending reach in three coordinated ways), the same pattern several other domains' multi-instance entries use correctly. No entry in this domain is straining against its own single-Mechanism structure.

### MERGE

None found. The closest candidates — `WAR-100` (attrition) and `WAR-240` (trench warfare), or `WAR-210` (staff/operational art) and `WAR-230` (military statistics) — were checked seriously and kept separate: `WAR-240` is a specific technological trap that *forces* both sides into `WAR-100`'s dynamic, not a restatement of it, and `WAR-210` is about planning while `WAR-230` is about measurement, a real functional distinction the entries' own Mechanism sections maintain independently of each other. No pair in this domain shares one mechanism closely enough to justify combining.

### RE-DOMAIN

- **`WAR-060`** cites `MIG-050` and `MIG-090` by ID in its own absence note without either appearing in `see_also` (`domains_touched` already correctly lists `migration`). Fix: add both IDs to `see_also`, reciprocally.
- **`WAR-290`** cites `MAR-350` by ID in its own absence note with no `maritime` in `domains_touched` and nothing in `see_also`. Fix: add `maritime` to `domains_touched`; add `MAR-350` to `see_also`, reciprocally.
- **`WAR-270`** cites `MAR-220` and `MAR-320` by ID in its own regional text with no `maritime` in `domains_touched` and nothing in `see_also`. Fix: add `maritime` to `domains_touched`; add both IDs to `see_also`, reciprocally.
- No entry's canonical domain needs to change.

### RETIER

None recommended, though the ratio itself is worth naming: 20 of 22 entries are `core`, the most extreme core-heavy split of any domain audited this cycle by percentage. Checked entry-by-entry for a `WAR-210`/`WAR-170`-shaped candidate (isolated, single-region, `enables: []`, structurally identical to an already-`standard` entry) and didn't find one that survives comparison: every `core` entry contributes either a real downstream `enables` chain or is the domain's sole representative of a distinct mechanism type, the same standard applied in the last two domains audited. The two existing `standard` entries (`WAR-230`, `WAR-170`) are correctly humble already. Worth periodically re-examining as more entries accumulate, but not a defect today.

### CUT

**None.** Read every entry specifically hunting for a self-admitted redundancy or a Hook with no real mechanic and didn't find one — the third domain in a row in this audit series where a genuinely adversarial pass turns up no clean cut. `WAR-090` (Reconnaissance) and `WAR-170` (NCO Corps) are the domain's thinnest entries by downstream connection, but both carry a mechanism (a purchasable uncertainty-reduction variable; institutionalized small-unit leadership continuity) that nothing else in the domain duplicates.

## PART D — Verdict

**Worst structural problem:** the domain's two most foundational entries (`WAR-040`, `WAR-020`) each narrate a transition away from an institutional "before" state — land-tenure militia service, and elite bronze-chariot warfare — that is never itself modeled anywhere in this domain, compounded by a checkable data bug where `WAR-040`'s own top-level date undersells its own cited earliest case and produces a genuine chronological inversion in `WAR-020`'s prereq chain.

**Entry count:** 22 before. With all five LOAD-BEARING proposals: 27. With VALUABLE also adopted: 31. With OPTIONAL: 33.

**Proposed out of symmetry, not mechanical need:** both OPTIONAL entries (#10 War Elephants, #11 Special Forces) are named as such — real, specific history contributing no mechanism `WAR-060`/`WAR-220`/`WAR-250` don't already supply. Of the VALUABLE tier, **Combat Motivation and Small-Unit Cohesion (#7)** is the softest call: a genuine scale gap, but no existing entry structurally requires it the way `WAR-020` requires its missing Bronze Age predecessor.

**Does War justify separate existence?** Yes, more clearly than any domain audited this cycle on boundary grounds specifically — its ENG/MIG/HLT/MAR splits are narrated explicitly and correctly nine separate times, more self-documentation than any other domain checked, and its region balance, contested ratio, and prereq-chain construction (once the `WAR-040` date bug is fixed) are all sound. The problems found here are precise and fixable: two missing origin stories at the domain's own foundation, one data bug with a clear correction, and a recurring habit of citing Maritime by name without crediting it in frontmatter.
