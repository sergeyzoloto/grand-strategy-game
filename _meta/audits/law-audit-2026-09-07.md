# Law Domain Audit

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Scope:** `domains/law/` — 15 entries, LAW-020 through LAW-210.
**Method:** adversarial structural review against `_meta/schema.md`, `_meta/conventions.md`, `_meta/design-principles.md`, `_meta/boundary-rules.md`, every entry in `domains/law/`, and cross-checks against `domains/war/`, `domains/finance/`, `domains/commerce/`, `domains/migration/`, `domains/diplomacy/`.

## Summary of the pattern

Law is the smallest domain audited this cycle (15 entries) and, on region balance, a solid one (34.8% Europe+Mediterranean, under the flag line). But it has the most extreme tier problem of any domain checked: **all 15 entries are `core`. Zero `standard`, zero `stub`.** More strikingly, once you read all 15 back to back, a single-sentence description of the domain's actual scope becomes obvious and it isn't "law" — it's **who holds power, how it's constrained, and who counts as a full member of the polity.** Every entry is public and constitutional law: codification, citizenship, councils, bureaucratic recruitment, feudal obligation, tax-consent assemblies, city charters, corporate personhood, human rights, parties, constitutions, police, unions, suffrage, welfare. Not one entry covers the law that actually governs an ordinary transaction between two private parties — property, contract, inheritance, marriage — the exact substrate every "contract enforcement" stat cited elsewhere in this wiki (`COM-010`, `LAW-120` itself) silently assumes already exists somewhere. And the domain's own earliest entry commits, almost verbatim, the exact failure mode this audit template names as its lead example: a law list starting at Hammurabi, never explaining dispute resolution before writing.

A second, smaller pattern: `LAW-080` and `LAW-110` both cite `WAR-060` by name, twice each, in their own prose — and neither ever promotes that citation to `see_also`, despite `domains_touched` already listing `war` on both. The same specific bug found in every domain audited this cycle, here aimed at War specifically.

## PART A — Diagnostic Sweep

### 1. EMERGENCE GAP — **FAIL**

The earliest entry, `LAW-020` (Written Law, -1754), opens with the Code of Hammurabi — this audit template's own named example of the failure mode ("a law list starting at Hammurabi skips dispute resolution before writing"). `LAW-020`'s own Mechanism section explicitly acknowledges what came before it exists ("an unwritten legal system leaves interpretation and application entirely in the hands of whoever currently holds authority") but never models it. Confirmed by grep: zero hits anywhere in the wiki for `wergild|blood feud|customary law|trial by ordeal|oath.?helper`. Dispute resolution through oath-swearing, ordeal, fixed compensation schedules, and elder/kin-group arbitration functioned as real, socially legitimate law for millennia before any code was written down, and none of it is modeled.

### 2. SUBSTRATE GAP — **FAIL**

Two severe absences:

- **Private law.** Confirmed by grep: zero hits anywhere in the entire wiki for `property law|contract law|inheritance law|marriage law|family law|personal status law`. Every one of this domain's 15 entries is public/constitutional law — who rules, who votes, who is a member, how power is checked. Nothing covers who owns what, what makes an agreement enforceable, or how property passes at death. This is the single most consequential gap found in this audit: `COM-010` and other entries elsewhere in this wiki already invoke a `contract enforcement` stat as though its legal foundation exists somewhere, and it doesn't — not in this domain, not anywhere.
- **Courts and trial procedure.** Confirmed by grep: zero relevant hits for `adversarial|inquisitorial|trial by jury|rules of evidence|judicial procedure`. This domain covers extensively _who makes law_ (codes, senates, parliaments, constitutions) and, in `LAW-110`, _who enforces order_ (police) — but never _how a specific dispute or accusation actually gets adjudicated_ once it reaches a decision-maker. No entry owns judges, juries, evidentiary standards, or the adversarial/inquisitorial procedural choice.

### 3. SCALE GAP — **FAIL**

Sorting the 15: world-system (`LAW-230`), state (the overwhelming majority — `LAW-020` through `LAW-140`, `LAW-230` through `LAW-210`), city (`LAW-120`). The household/individual scale is entirely absent — not one entry touches marriage, inheritance, guardianship, or personal legal status below the level of citizenship. This is a domain about the state's relationship to itself and to categories of person (citizen, corporation, voter) with zero coverage of the law governing a household's own internal legal relationships, despite this being one of the most historically rich and comparatively varied areas of law in the actual historical record (Islamic personal status law, Hindu personal law, Roman patria potestas, canon marriage law).

### 4. REVERSE-DIRECTION GAP — **PARTIAL FAIL**

Real reversal content exists: `LAW-180`'s Ottoman case (a written constitution suspended within two years) and `LAW-160`'s parallel Ottoman parliamentary case are genuine institutional-failure entries. But the domain's most obvious and best-documented capability-loss-then-recovery story is entirely missing, and it's one the domain's own text gestures directly at without ever explaining: `LAW-130` cites the University of Bologna (1088) as the origin of corporate legal personhood — the same institution and the same decade where Roman law itself, having essentially disappeared across Western Europe for roughly five centuries after the Western Empire's collapse (replaced by fragmented, orally-transmitted Germanic custom), was rediscovered and systematically taught from surviving texts of Justinian's Corpus Juris Civilis. Confirmed by grep: zero hits anywhere for `corpus juris|reception of roman law|justinian`. `LAW-130` names the place and the decade this happened without ever saying why Bologna specifically became the hub, or that anything had been lost at all.

### 5. PREREQ ORPHANS — **FAIL**, one concrete chronological bug

Every `prereqs` ID resolves to a real entry. But `LAW-210` (The Welfare State, `date_start: 1883`, Bismarck's legislation) lists `FIN-230` (The Great Depression, `date_start: 1929`) as a prereq — a **46-year inversion**: the entry requires something to exist that doesn't begin until nearly half a century after the entry's own starting point. `LAW-210`'s own body text is explicit about what it means (the Depression as "the crisis pressure that motivated much of this architecture's _expansion_"), which is a real relationship but not a foundational prereq — the fix is to keep `FIN-230` in `see_also` rather than `prereqs`, since Bismarck's system demonstrably didn't require a Depression that hadn't happened yet.

Separately, the now-familiar prose-to-frontmatter bug recurs twice, both times aimed at War: `LAW-080` cites `WAR-060` by name twice (in its own opening paragraph and Mechanism section — "a decentralized alternative to the salaried standing-army... models (WAR-060, LAW-100)"; "a paid standing military (WAR-060)") with `domains_touched` already listing `war` but `WAR-060` never appearing in `see_also`. `LAW-110` does the identical thing ("distinct from the military (WAR-060)") with the identical gap.

### 6. MECHANISM DIVERSITY — **PASS, with a real redundancy**

A genuinely rich spread for a 15-entry domain: gates (`LAW-100`, `LAW-080`, `LAW-120`, `LAW-130`), civic institutions (`LAW-060`, `LAW-140`), and branches that explicitly reject a single-timeline framing (`LAW-020`'s "same mechanism, opposite political purposes"; `LAW-050`'s "three genuinely different institutional strategies, not points on one timeline"). But there's a real, specific redundancy worth naming rather than papering over: `LAW-230` (Human Rights) and `LAW-220` (Universal Suffrage) build on nearly identical mechanic shapes — both are explicitly a "formal claim versus actually-enforced right" gap (`LAW-230`'s Hook: "a large, durable gap between universal textual claim and actual enforceable protection"; `LAW-220`'s Hook: "removing a formal suffrage restriction and actually securing the exercised right are separable achievements... the gap between them"), both explicitly reusing design principle 3. Checked seriously under Part C's MERGE heading below.

### 7. BOUNDARY INTEGRITY — **PASS, with one soft case**

Good self-documentation for a domain this size: `LAW-130` narrates its own FIN split explicitly ("this entry owns the general medieval legal innovation... `FIN-070` owns the later, more specific commercial application"); `LAW-200` and `LAW-210` do the same against `FIN-200` and `FIN-230` respectively; `LAW-220` narrates its own internal split from `LAW-050`. No entry's canonical domain is wrong. The one soft case worth naming: `LAW-230` (Human Rights) is substantially about international treaty instruments (the UDHR, the covenants), and `boundary-rules.md` states plainly that "a treaty is always DIP, even though it's technically a legal instrument." The entry already handles this correctly in practice — its own Mechanism section focuses specifically on domestic ratification and incorporation rather than the treaty-drafting process itself, and `domains_touched` already credits `diplomacy` — but it's a boundary worth remembering exists, the same way `MAR-090`'s river-network case was flagged as arguable-but-defensible in an earlier audit this cycle.

## PART B — Proposed Additions

### LOAD-BEARING (5)

**1. Customary Law and Pre-Written Dispute Resolution**

- Regions: middle-east, europe, africa, americas — era, roughly -10000 to -1754
- Answers: Probe 1 (Emergence) directly — this audit template's own named example
- Mechanism: dispute resolution before writing relies on socially-visible, memorized procedure — oath-swearing before witnesses, physical ordeal, fixed compensation schedules (wergild-type payments), restorative arrangements brokered by elders or kin-group leaders — substituting shared ritual and procedural consensus for a citable written text, a fully functioning legal system requiring no literacy at all.
- Hook (seed): an origin gate — establishes that dispute resolution and legal legitimacy exist and function before `LAW-020`'s written codification, showing writing converts an already-working mechanism into a citable one rather than inventing law from nothing.
- Prereqs: none. Enables: `LAW-020`.
- Contested: true — how much continuity exists between specific customary practices and the actual content of the earliest written codes (recording pre-existing custom versus reshaping it) is genuinely debated by legal historians.
- Confidence: medium.

**2. Civil Law: Property, Contract, and Inheritance**

- Regions: mediterranean, europe, middle-east, east-asia — era, roughly -450 to 1900
- Answers: Probe 2 (Substrate) directly — the single most consequential gap found in this audit
- Mechanism: distinct from `LAW-020`'s public/political law, private law governs everyday transactions between individuals — who owns what, what makes an agreement enforceable, how property passes at death — and its systematic codification (Roman law's Corpus Juris; the Napoleonic Code's comprehensive, exportable rationalization of an entire nation's property, contract, and family law into one internally consistent text) is the exact substrate every commercial-trust and contract-enforcement mechanic elsewhere in this wiki silently assumes already exists.
- Hook (seed): a gate — the private-law substrate this wiki's own `contract enforcement` stat has nowhere been formally grounded, distinct from `LAW-020`'s public/political law.
- Prereqs: `LAW-020`. Enables: none.
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

**3. Courts, Judges, and Trial Procedure**

- Regions: mediterranean, europe, middle-east, east-asia, south-asia — era, roughly -1754 to 1800
- Answers: Probe 2 (Substrate) directly
- Mechanism: the actual institution and procedure by which a specific dispute or accusation gets adjudicated once law exists — who judges (a magistrate, a panel, a lay jury), what counts as valid evidence, and whether the process is adversarial (opposing advocates before a neutral judge) or inquisitorial (a judge actively investigating) — distinct from `LAW-020`'s codification of what the rules are and `LAW-110`'s enforcement of order in the streets.
- Hook (seed): a gate every other entry in this domain assumes exists once law is written and enforcement occurs, with a real institutional choice (jury versus judge-only, adversarial versus inquisitorial) shaping outcomes independent of what the underlying law's text actually says.
- Prereqs: `LAW-020`. Enables: none.
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

**4. Marriage, Inheritance, and Personal Status Law**

- Regions: middle-east, south-asia, europe, east-asia — era, roughly -1754 to 1950
- Answers: Probe 3 (Scale gap) directly — the household/individual-scale category this domain entirely lacks
- Mechanism: law governing marriage validity, divorce, inheritance shares, and guardianship operates at a household scale no other entry in this domain touches, and is frequently administered through a separate legal track from general civil or criminal law — often religious or customary rather than state-secular (Islamic personal status law, Hindu personal law, canon marriage jurisdiction) — meaning a single polity can run multiple, religiously-differentiated personal-status regimes simultaneously for different subject populations.
- Hook (seed): a gate at the household/individual scale, frequently run as a separate, religiously-differentiated track parallel to general civil law rather than unified under one code.
- Prereqs: `LAW-020`. Enables: none.
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

**5. The Reception of Roman Law**

- Regions: europe — era, roughly 500 to 1200
- Answers: Probe 4 (Reverse-direction) directly, continuing `LAW-130`'s own unexplained Bologna citation
- Mechanism: Roman law's systematic legal apparatus effectively disappears across most of Western Europe following the Western Empire's collapse, replaced for roughly five centuries by fragmented, orally-transmitted local Germanic custom, until the rediscovery and systematic study of Justinian's Corpus Juris Civilis at Bologna (from 1088 — the same institution `LAW-130` already cites for corporate personhood) triggers a continent-wide "reception" of Roman legal concepts into local law, restoring a sophistication and systematic breadth local custom alone had never independently developed.
- Hook (seed): an event — a genuine capability-loss-then-recovery case, an entire sophisticated legal system disappearing for half a millennium before being deliberately revived from surviving texts.
- Prereqs: `LAW-020`. Enables: `LAW-130` (the same Bologna institution, currently missing this link).
- Contested: false.
- Confidence: high.

### VALUABLE (3)

**6. Witch Trials and Legal Panic**
Regions: europe, americas. Century, roughly 1450-1750. Answers Probe 4 and Probe 6 with a mechanism found nowhere else in this domain: the same procedural machinery (courts, evidence rules, confession-extraction) legitimate justice depends on can be captured by a moral panic and turned toward systematically producing false convictions, showing legal procedure's _formal_ legitimacy and its actual truth-finding accuracy are separable and can diverge catastrophically under social pressure without the underlying law's text changing at all. Hook: a branch — the same courts and evidentiary standards produce wildly different outcomes depending on surrounding social pressure, not on the law itself. Prereqs: the proposed Courts entry (#3). Contested: true — how much the phenomenon reflects popular belief driving prosecution from below versus elite or clerical direction from above is debated. Confidence: high.

**7. Judicial Review and Constitutional Courts**
Regions: americas, europe. Century, roughly 1803-1950. Answers Probe 6 by supplying the specific institutional mechanism `LAW-180`'s own Hook identifies as the missing variable: a court gains the power to strike down a legislative or executive act as unconstitutional, converting a written constitution from a structural or aspirational text into one with a concrete, court-centered enforcement mechanism — an answer many, but not all (the UK notably lacks it), constitutional systems adopt. Hook: a gate that closes the exact enforcement gap `LAW-180` names but doesn't resolve. Prereqs: `LAW-180`. Contested: false. Confidence: high.

**8. Codified Criminal Law and Punishment Reform**
Regions: europe, americas. Century, roughly 1764-1900. Answers Probe 6: Cesare Beccaria's 1764 argument against torture, arbitrary and disproportionate punishment, and the routine use of capital punishment shifts the underlying theory of what punishment is for — deterrence and proportionality rather than spectacle and retribution — converting criminal sentencing from a display of sovereign power into a calculated, increasingly incarceration-centered system, a mechanism distinct from `LAW-020`'s codification of what's illegal or `LAW-110`'s street-level enforcement of order. Hook: a branch/modifier converting punishment philosophy itself, with the rise of the penitentiary as its institutional consequence. Prereqs: `LAW-020`. Contested: false. Confidence: high.

### OPTIONAL (2)

**9. Trial by Ordeal and Its 1215 Abolition** [REJECTED]
Regions: europe. Century, roughly 800-1215. A specific, vivid medieval procedure — divine judgment through physical trial, hot iron or cold water — formally ended by the Fourth Lateran Council's 1215 ban on clerical participation, directly forcing England toward jury trial as a replacement. Real and consequential, but its mechanism (procedural legitimacy shifting when a key participant withdraws) is close enough to what the proposed Courts entry (#3) would already cover as a region_variant that it doesn't clearly need independent tier weight. Honestly flavour.

**10. The Ombudsman** [REJECTED]
Regions: europe. Decade, from 1809. Sweden's Justitieombudsman, origin of the modern administrative-grievance-oversight office — a real, widely-copied institutional innovation, but mechanically a narrower, lower-stakes variant of `LAW-060`'s coalition/oversight mechanism applied specifically to bureaucratic grievances rather than a genuinely new mechanism. Honestly flavour.

## PART C — Proposed Modifications

### SPLIT

None found. `LAW-080` and `LAW-130` each bundle several independent regional cases (Roman colonate, European, Japanese, and Ethiopian feudalism; the universitas, the waqf, and eventually the chartered company) under one Mechanism section, checked against the Multi-domain rule's "two `## Mechanism` sections" test and found to genuinely have one each — multiple instances of the same underlying bargain, correctly handled through `region_variants` rather than needing separate entries.

### MERGE

**Considered and rejected: `LAW-230` (Human Rights) and `LAW-220` (Universal Suffrage).** Both entries build on the same "formal claim versus actually-enforced right" gap, both explicitly reusing design principle 3, and their Hooks are close enough in structure to read as restatements of each other. Kept separate because the actual institutional machinery differs in a load-bearing way: `LAW-230`'s gap is closed (or not) by international treaty ratification and domestic incorporation across a diffuse, uneven set of states; `LAW-220`'s gap is closed (or not) by domestic administrative and electoral enforcement within a single jurisdiction — different actors, different mechanisms, different historical cases (the UDHR drafting committee versus the US Voting Rights Act). Design principle 3 recurring across entries is an intended feature of this project's grammar, not evidence the entries duplicate each other — but the resemblance is real enough that it factors into the RETIER recommendation below rather than being ignored.

### RE-DOMAIN

- **`LAW-080`** cites `WAR-060` by name twice in its own prose with `domains_touched` already listing `war`, but `WAR-060` never appears in `see_also`. Fix: add it, reciprocally.
- **`LAW-110`** cites `WAR-060` by name in its own prose with the identical gap. Fix: add it, reciprocally.
- **`LAW-210`**'s `FIN-230` prereq (see Part A, Probe 5) should move to `see_also` — the Depression-era relationship it describes is real but not foundational, and keeping it as a `prereq` produces a 46-year chronological inversion.
- No entry's canonical domain needs to change.

### RETIER

**`LAW-220` (Universal Suffrage): core → standard.** Of the domain's 15 entries, this is the one whose mechanic contributes the least genuinely new structure: it applies the same claim-versus-enforcement gap `LAW-230` already establishes (see MERGE above) to a narrower historical process, with `enables: []` and no downstream entries built on it. The concrete history (property qualifications, women's suffrage, the US Voting Rights Act, India's compressed constitutional case) is real and worth keeping, but the entry is riding on a mechanism this domain already has rather than contributing a new one — a `standard`-weight entry, not a `core` one.

Not touching the other 14: this domain's other thin-downstream entries (`LAW-060`, `LAW-100`, `LAW-200`, each with `enables: []`) each still contribute a mechanism nothing else in the domain supplies — coalition-forcing councils, credentialed bureaucratic recruitment, and legalized collective bargaining respectively — earning their weight qualitatively rather than through outbound links, the same standard applied in every domain audited this cycle.

### CUT

**None.** Read all 15 entries specifically hunting for a self-admitted redundancy or a Hook with no real mechanic and didn't find a clean one — `LAW-220`'s resemblance to `LAW-230` was the closest candidate, resolved as a RETIER rather than a CUT because its concrete historical content (a genuinely important process covering multiple continents and a century and a half) earns it a place in the domain even at reduced editorial weight.

## PART D — Verdict

**Worst structural problem:** this domain's name promises "law" but its actual coverage is "constitutional and political-institutional law" — every one of its 15 entries is about who holds power, how it's constrained, or who counts as a member, with zero coverage of the private law (property, contract, inheritance, marriage) that governs the transactions between ordinary people this wiki's own commerce and finance entries already assume has a legal foundation somewhere.

**Entry count:** 15 before. With all five LOAD-BEARING proposals: 20. With VALUABLE also adopted: 23. With OPTIONAL: 25.

**Proposed out of symmetry, not mechanical need:** both OPTIONAL entries (#9 Trial by Ordeal, #10 The Ombudsman) are named as such — real, specific history whose mechanism the Courts entry (#3) and `LAW-060` respectively already substantially cover. Of the VALUABLE tier, **Codified Criminal Law and Punishment Reform (#8)** is the softest call: a genuine gap, but less load-bearing than Judicial Review's direct answer to `LAW-180`'s own stated problem or Witch Trials' genuinely novel branch mechanic.

**Does Law justify separate existence?** Yes, but with a real caveat this audit should say plainly rather than paper over: as currently scoped, this domain is doing the job of a "Political Institutions" domain, not a "Law" domain, and the gap is wide enough (zero private law, zero adjudication procedure, zero household-scale content) that the current name overpromises relative to the actual coverage. The proposed additions aren't decoration — they're what would make the domain's own name match what it contains.
